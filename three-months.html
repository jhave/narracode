Three Month Report: 90 days using Narracode

9 May – 9 August 2026

25 stories  |  124,081 words  |  92 days

Narracode launched on 9 May 2026 as a single markdown file: a harness that unfolds into a system of separated passes — Initiator, Reading, Structural, Compositional, Reflexive. The premise was that literary composition could be handled the way Claude Code handles software. Externalise state to files. Separate generation from critique. Let the critical pass govern the generative one.

Ninety-two days later there are 25 finished stories and 124,081 words, published as standalone pages with their full working memory beside them — poetics, drafts, structural state, critiques, version snapshots, attribution. Every story ships its own provenance.

The harness changed as much as the corpus did. It began with three structural memory files (graph, time-constants, history) and now carries eight, adding obligations, motifs, scene-ledger, character interiority, and reader-state. AUTO_MODE arrived on 25 May, allowing full-pipeline runs without interaction. A registry of AI tells — seventeen classes, each with a detector and a target rate — was added on 30 July, along with a tell-scan pass that runs after drafting. Composition and editing drew on models from three labs: Opus 4.6 through Opus 5, Sonnet 4.6 and Fable 5 (Anthropic); Gemini 3.1 Pro through 3.6 Flash (Google); GPT-5.5 and Codex (OpenAI).

Nothing here was written by prompt alone. The Seamless Edit method treats the author's manual edits as the teaching signal: each snapshot diffs the draft against what the model produced, and recurring patterns are promoted into the project's poetics. The corpus that resulted is also a dataset — paired pre-edit and post-edit drafts of the same content, across 25 stories.

Bio

David Jhave Johnston is a digital poet working in emergent domains. Author of ReRites (Anteism, 2019) and Aesthetic Animism (MIT Press, 2016). He is currently an AI-narrative researcher at the UiB Centre for Digital Narrative (2023–27) with the Extending Digital Narrative project.
