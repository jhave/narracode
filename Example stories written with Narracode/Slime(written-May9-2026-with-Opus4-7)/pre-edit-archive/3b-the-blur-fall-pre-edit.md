# the blur — fall

*Loop 3 draft (ii). ~640 words. Free indirect across the five, ground-level. The end of the six months; the reunion at the start of fall semester.*

---

They met at the bar on a Wednesday. The kitchen was frying the same fish and the same dark oil, but the weather had turned and the air on the basement steps was different; a softer cold. The tea on the table arrived as it had always arrived. The cup was the same cup. The chip on its rim was a little wider now, from some afternoon they had not been there for.

Anwen got there first. She had cut her hair too — separately from Ines, at a different angle. Júlio came in after her, and they looked at each other for a fraction of a second longer than they would have looked at each other a year ago, and then for a fraction of a second shorter, and then they were sitting. Ines came in third. She was not drinking the tea. Her face had a quietness none of them remarked on, in the way they had learned not to remark on things.

Kasimi arrived without a coat, in a different jacket. *You,* said Anwen, and Kasimi said *yes,* and Anwen did not say anything else. Tomek was last, with the look of a man who had run somewhere on the way. He sat down heavily. He said *hi.* He did not say *the point.*

What they said, for the first hour, was almost nothing. Júlio asked Kasimi how his mother was. Kasimi said *she's the same,* and there was a small thing in the way he said *the same* that made the others not ask further. Anwen showed them a sketch she had not been able to finish. Tomek said *that's almost—,* and stopped, and Anwen said *I know,* and they drank.

Ines, when she spoke, spoke about the Tuesday markets. Specifically: the price of a small fish she had eaten the night before, which was up in a way she could not account for. None of the others knew the fish. She described it. Júlio, half-laughing — the data-science laugh, the old one — said, *price discovery on a six-week lag, basically,* and then said, *I mean, the city is still figuring out what to charge for itself.* Ines said *yes, that.*

The wall-screen above the bar was on, soundlessly. A slime-passage notification crossed the corner. The notification was a different shape than it had been six months ago: a longer band, with a small spiraling glyph the city's overlay system had not used before. Tomek saw it. Kasimi saw Tomek see it. Anwen saw Kasimi see Tomek. Júlio saw Anwen see Kasimi see Tomek. Ines saw nothing and saw all of it. None of them said anything.

After a while Anwen said *it's longer.* She did not need to say what was longer. *Yes,* said Tomek. Júlio said *the band,* and stopped, and then said, *yeah. Longer.* Kasimi said *the city is metabolizing differently.* Ines said *I know.*

They ordered more food. They ate. The tea cooled. The kitchen began to fry something else. Outside, somewhere, a bus came, a bus left.

When they paid, Tomek paid for them all, against custom. Nobody objected. Anwen, putting on her jacket, looked at each of the four of them in turn and said *thank you,* not specifying for what, and the *thank you* sat on the table between them after she said it the way the tea had sat. They walked up the basement steps. The new cold was on them. The streetlights were on. The slime, somewhere — somewhere longer, slightly — was on.
