Narracode is a harness for literary writing created on May 10, 2026. It is currently a single markdown file capable of being used with any LLM.

Two recent branches of Narracode research — the Sakana/Fugu orchestration assessment and the AI-tells review pass — suggest potential improvements.

Narracode has assumed that because literary quality cannot be scored, nothing about literary prose can be measured. The instruments that actually discriminate between human and machine fiction measure predictability and are not yet capable of assessing literary quality. The harness should be rebuilt around that distinction.

Background

This section defines the terms used in the rest of the document. Readers already familiar with Narracode and with the research literature on text generation can go directly to §1.

What a harness is

A language model generates text one token at a time, each token conditioned on the tokens before it. The model produces, approximately, the most probable continuation of its input. For summarisation, translation and code completion this is the desired behaviour. For fiction it is the central difficulty: the most probable continuation of a story is the one most similar to stories the model has already been trained on.

A harness is the software layer between a user and a language model. It determines what text is placed in the model's context window, in what order, and what is done with the output. Harnesses are established practice in software engineering — Claude Code, Cursor and Aider are examples. They work by holding state outside the model, in files, and supplying the model at each step with only the part of that state the step requires. The model retains nothing between calls. The harness is what persists.

What Narracode is

Narracode is a harness for fiction, first written on 10 May 2026. It is a single Markdown file, narracode.md, which any language model can read and follow. It has no runtime and no dependencies. Its components are:

  • Separated roles. The harness requires the model to operate in one role per pass: Initiator (establishes the project's stylistic commitments), Reading (analyses uploaded source texts), Structural (updates the record of narrative state), Compositional (drafts prose), and Reflexive (critiques a draft against the stated commitments). One model performs all roles, but never two in the same pass.
  • Externalised state. A structural/ directory holds narrative memory as separate plain-text files: graph.md (entities and relations), history.md (what has been established, including exact phrasings already used), obligations.md (questions raised and not yet answered), motifs.md (recurring images and objects), scene-ledger.md (each scene's function), character-interiority.md (private states and concealed knowledge), and reader-state.md (what a first-time reader is likely to know and expect).
  • A commitments file. POETICS.md records what a given project is attempting and, in a separate section, what it refuses to do.
  • Hooks. Named points at which specified actions occur or are prohibited. For example, post_draft forbids the model from following a draft with self-critique in the same pass.

The project's most consistent internal finding, recorded in 2026-05-14_architectural_harness_observations-CLAUDE.md, is that the harness's prohibitions do more work than its organising structure. Constraints stated negatively — this project will not explain the child's origin — reduce the range of continuations the model treats as available more effectively than constraints stated positively. This result does not appear in the published literature on controlled generation. §7.2 returns to it.

Median collapse

Throughout this document, median collapse means the convergence of model-generated fiction on the most conventional available version of a story. The term describes a measured phenomenon, not a judgement of taste, and the measurements are consistent across studies.

Wang et al., 2026 (Elias in the Lighthouse, Again? Diagnosing Low Diversity in LLM Stories) generated large samples of short fiction from four current models and found that although individual stories differed in wording, their plot elements were highly repetitive: 88.3% of all stories contained at least one of eleven recurring core words. Wenger and Kenett, 2026 (Magic, Madness, Heaven, Sin: LLM Output Diversity is Everything, Everywhere, All at Once) report the same compression across output types. Two further studies find the effect at the level of knowledge and of personal voice respectively: Wright et al., 2026 (Epistemic Diversity and Knowledge Collapse in Large Language Models) and Voice Under Revision: LLMs and the Normalization of Personal Narrative (2026).

Lexical variety is therefore not evidence against median collapse. A model can produce prose that is word-for-word unlike anything in its training data while reproducing the same underlying sequence of events. Narracode exists to interrupt the second kind of repetition. Every proposal in this document is directed at that target.

AI tells and the tell registry

An AI tell is a construction that occurs in model-generated prose at a rate noticeably different from its rate in human-written prose of the same kind. Tells are not errors, and most are ordinary constructions that human writers also use. What distinguishes machine text is frequency. Examples from the Narracode registry include the appositive em-dash; the sentence whose second clause evaluates the first (The room was empty, which was worse); the three-item list in ascending order of intensity; and the naming clause (a kind of grief he had no word for).

Since 30 July 2026 the repository has maintained master_ai_tells.md, a registry of seventeen such classes. Each class records:

  • a detector — a rule or pattern sufficient to locate instances in a draft;
  • a target rate — the frequency of the construction in human literary prose, in occurrences per thousand words;
  • a survival rate — the proportion of flagged instances the author chose to keep;
  • a remedy, and where the remedy is itself a construction, a cap limiting how often it may be used.

A hook, post_draft_tell_scan, runs the detectors over a completed draft and returns a list of located instances. Throughout this document a span means a contiguous stretch of text, usually a phrase or a single sentence — the unit the scan reports. The scan proposes; it does not rewrite.

What this document proposes

Six changes to the harness, listed in §8 in the order they should be built. Three are additions of a single field or table to files the harness already maintains. Two extend work already committed to and not yet implemented. One, described in §5.3, is a research proposal with a stated test for abandoning it.

1. Where the harness stands as of Aug 3, 2026

1.1 Can Sakana's Fugu Orchestrator Improve Narrative Prose? No.

The June assessment of Sakana AI's Fugu (2026; Sakana Fugu Technical Report) (full consideration here, and the underlying analysis in plans/2026-06-28_1353Z_fugu-orchestration-evaluation-and-routing-proposal.md, PR #8) concluded that orchestration offers Narracode nothing it does not already have. Adopting it would cost routing-table maintenance, context budget, and editorial judgment. That conclusion holds.

One finding deserves emphasis. The Fugu refusal rested on the claim that literary merit resists a reward function — that any learned coordination trained against a proxy metric would collapse the prose toward whatever the proxy rewards. Direct evidence has emerged in Sui et al., 2026 (Spoiler Alert: Narrative Forecasting as a Metric for Tension in LLM Storytelling) that the obvious proxy — an LLM judging prose quality — is not merely uninformative at the top of the range but inverted (§3).

LLM evaluators consistently score zero-shot AI-generated fiction higher than published New Yorker short stories. Standard rubric-based LLM judges reward predictable, highly structured, cliché-dense prose and penalize subtle, subtextual human literary craft. Using an LLM to evaluate or route prose quality acts as an adversarial signal that degrades literary merit.

1.2 The AI-tells branch: a potentially strong improvement to harness

The tell-scan pass on main (master_ai_tells.md, the post_draft_tell_scan hook, and the design note at plans/2026-07-30_ai-tells-benchmark.md) proposes the following extensions of the published literature on style control in four ways:

  • Target rates are non-zero. Each class is assigned a frequency measured from human-written literary prose, expressed in occurrences per thousand words, rather than a prohibition. A target of zero would remove the construction from the text entirely. Human literary prose contains all seventeen constructions at measurable non-zero rates. Prose containing none of them would differ from human writing as clearly as prose containing too many, in the opposite direction. The registry therefore records rates, and the design note states the reason explicitly.
  • Survival rate as a calibration signal. For each class the registry records the proportion of flagged instances the author chose to keep. A class whose flags are usually rejected is not identifying a machine artefact; it is encoding a preference the author does not hold. The design note sets the threshold at approximately 0.3: below that a class is marked contested and then retired. Without this field a registry can only grow. With it, individual classes can be shown to be wrong and removed.
  • Rate caps on the remedies themselves. Goodhart's law holds that a measure adopted as a target ceases to function as a measure (Goodhart, 1975; Strathern, 1997, 'Improving Ratings': Audit in the British University System). The registry applies this to its own corrections: each device adopted as a fix receives a detector and a frequency cap on the day it is adopted, because a correction applied consistently becomes as regular as the construction it replaced. Five devices adopted from a single editing pass are capped in this way, and the cap on parenthetical self-correction is set at one per two thousand words.
  • Aligned spans rather than summaries. The design note specifies storing paired before → after text rather than the model's description of what changed. A description is a compression, and compression discards the specific wording, which is the evidence. This matches the conclusion the context-engineering literature has since reached about summarisation loss (§2.3).

These four decisions are sound and this document does not revise them. §2 sets out where the design is incomplete.

2. What the tell-scan architecture does not do

Four limitations. All four are structural: they follow from the design of the pass rather than from errors in its execution.

2.1 A span-level instrument cannot detect a story-level failure

All seventeen registry classes operate on spans. The pass is scoped accordingly: locate the span, name the class, propose a remedy of one word or a deletion. This scope is what makes the pass inexpensive and what keeps it from rewriting the author's prose.

It also means that the failures which most damage a work of fiction cannot be detected by it. A character who never acts contrary to the reader's expectation, an ending that follows from the preceding text as its most probable continuation, a scene whose turn depends on an emotion the previous scene has already stated — none of these produces a locatable span. A draft can satisfy every detector in the registry while exhibiting all three. The registry operates on sentences; median collapse (see Background) occurs at the level of structure.

The correct response is not to widen the scan. Span-level scope is why the pass is usable. The response is a second instrument measuring a different quantity, which §3 identifies and §5.3 specifies.

2.2 The requirement list is limited to syntax

The design note distinguishes between a ban list, which reduces the frequency of over-produced forms, and a requirement list, which raises the frequency of under-produced ones, and observes that only the first currently exists. The distinction is correct, and the evidence for under-production is specific: models suppress erotema — the rhetorical question — relative to human writers, a finding from A Counterfactual LLM-based Framework for Measuring Rhetorical Style (2025), which also documents over-production of the three-part list.

The candidate requirements listed — erotema, sentence-initial conjunctions, anacoluthon, unresolved deixis, flat repetition — are all syntactic forms. Literary fiction also under-produces structures that are not syntactic: ambivalence sustained past the point where the text could resolve it, withheld catharsis, a scene that ends without a change of state, and information kept from the reader without the text signalling that something is being kept. A requirement list restricted to syntax will change the sentence rhythm and leave these untouched.

2.3 The registry has no removal policy

The registry grows without bound by design: any construction the author cuts twice is added, with a quarterly survey of published findings supplementing it. It currently holds seventeen classes. The design note proposes injecting the highest-priority live classes into the pre_draft hook as generation constraints, so that drafts arrive without the construction rather than having it removed afterwards. That change is correct — removal leaves a gap where the excision occurred, whereas prevention does not — and it makes the number of injected classes the operative decision, because context capacity is finite.

Zhang et al., 2025 (Agentic Context Engineering: Evolving Contexts for Self-Improving Language Models) identify the two ways a growing context degrades. Brevity bias is the loss of domain-specific detail when a context is compressed toward a shorter summary. Context collapse is the cumulative loss of detail when a context is repeatedly rewritten rather than amended. Their remedy is to store context as discrete, individually addressable items and to update them incrementally. A registry of numbered classes, each with its own detector and rates, is already that structure. What the harness lacks is the selection policy the structure exists to support.

The design note's priority formula is severity × liveness × (1 − survival_penalty), where liveness decays exponentially from the date a class was last confirmed with a nine-month constant. This ranks classes against one another correctly. It does not take account of the scene being drafted. A domestic interior and a scene of institutional procedure do not exhibit the same tells, and injecting the same fixed set into both allocates context capacity poorly.

Proposed change. Condition the priority score on scene type, read from scene-ledger.md, so that the classes injected at pre_draft are those relevant to the scene about to be written. This requires one additional field per registry class recording the scene types in which the class has been observed, and no new machinery.

2.4 Prose written against a tell list has its own regularities

The registry is calibrated against one author's decisions. This is appropriate for a system whose purpose is to support a particular voice, and it produces a specific risk. Text optimised against a list of machine markers is identifiable as text optimised against a list of machine markers. The design note anticipates this for individual devices: the five adopted from the first editing pass — parenthetical self-correction, noun-collision, subject-dropped verb chains, single-word fragment runs, and colon-apposition — each carry a cap.

The case not covered is the combination. Each device can remain below its individual cap while the set of them, appearing together, constitutes a regularity that no single detector measures. A per-device cap constrains each marginal frequency; it does not constrain the joint distribution.

Proposed change. Implement the two-corpus scan the design note lists as §7.6: check each draft against the tell registry and, separately, against the last three published stories, reporting phrases and constructions that recur across projects. Recurrence across separate works is the only available evidence that an authorial preference has become a fixed and predictable feature, and it cannot be observed within a single draft. Two near-repetitions between Open Loops and The Chute were caught manually; there is no reason to expect manual checking to continue to succeed as the corpus grows.

3. What can and cannot be measured

Narracode's founding assumption is that literary quality cannot be scored, and that any automated quality gate would drive the prose toward whatever the gate rewards. The evidence for the first part of that assumption is now substantially stronger than it was in May 2026, and it arrives together with a result that changes what should follow from it.

3.1 Automated quality judgement fails, and fails in a specific direction

Fein et al., 2026 (LitBench: A Benchmark and Dataset for Reliable Evaluation of Creative Writing) assembled a corpus of creative writing with human preference annotations, controlled for known annotation biases, and tested automated judges against it. The best general-purpose language model used as a judge agreed with human preference 73% of the time; reward models trained specifically for the task reached 78%. These figures are upper bounds under favourable conditions, and they are measured against the preferences of general readers rather than against expert literary judgement.

The more consequential result is reported by Sui et al., 2026 (Spoiler Alert: Narrative Forecasting as a Metric for Tension in LLM Storytelling), already cited in §1.1. Evaluated on EQ-Bench, a widely used creative-writing benchmark that scores stories against a rubric using a language model as judge, zero-shot model-generated stories are ranked above published New Yorker short fiction.

The consequence for harness design is direct. An automated quality gate would not merely fail to help; within the range where Narracode operates it would provide a signal pointing away from published literary fiction, and the score would rise as the prose moved further from it. This is the strongest available argument against the orchestration architecture assessed in §1.1, which requires exactly such a signal in order to be trained.

3.2 Predictability can be measured

The same paper describes a measurement that does discriminate correctly. The 100-Endings procedure works as follows. A story is processed sentence by sentence. At each position, a language model is given only the text up to that point and asked to predict how the story ends. The prediction is sampled one hundred times. The proportion of those predictions that fail to match the actual ending is recorded as the value at that position. The result is a curve across the length of the story. The rate at which the curve changes direction is reported as the inflection rate, and corresponds to points where the text revises what the reader can expect.

Measured this way, New Yorker stories rank far above model-generated stories — the reverse of the rubric-based ranking, and in the direction that matches expert judgement.

The procedure works because it does not evaluate the prose. It measures whether the continuation of the text was foreseeable from the text preceding it. Foreseeability is a relation between a document and a predictor. It requires no human annotation, no reward model, and no rubric.

The distinction the harness should adopt: literary quality is not computable; predictability is. Median collapse is not a deficit of quality that happens to be hard to measure. It is an excess of foreseeability, and foreseeability can be measured directly.

The three proposals that follow are instruments for foreseeability and for its components. None of them scores prose.

4. Proposal: measure cadence as a sequence

The registry measures the frequency of constructions: occurrences per thousand words. Frequency is the wrong quantity for rhythm. Two drafts can contain identical counts of every registry class while differing entirely in how those constructions are distributed through the text, because rhythm is a property of order and a frequency is computed without reference to order. Three changes, in ascending order of cost.

4.1 Record sentence length as an ordered series

For each scene, record the sequence of sentence lengths rather than their mean. The quantities of interest are the variance of the series, the largest difference between adjacent sentences, and the distribution of run lengths — how many consecutive short sentences occur before a long one.

This quantity is called burstiness in the machine-text detection literature, where it is used as a detection feature: human-written text varies sentence length more than model-written text (Gehrmann et al., 2019, GLTR: Statistical Detection and Visualization of Generated Text). Narracode does not need it for detection, since the provenance of its drafts is known. It is proposed here for a different purpose: variation in sentence length is the principal carrier of prose rhythm, and the current registry does not measure it at all. A scene whose sentence-length series is close to constant will read as monotonous irrespective of how many em-dashes it contains.

4.2 Record the series separately for each focal character

This is the least expensive of the proposals in this document and, in the assessment offered here, the one most likely to change the output.

Compute the sentence-length series separately for each focal character — that is, for dialogue attributed to that character, and for passages of narration focalised through them, including free indirect discourse, in which third-person narration adopts a character's diction and perspective without marking the shift (Genette, 1980, Narrative Discourse: An Essay in Method).

Distinct characters should produce measurably distinct distributions. Where they do not, the characters are not linguistically differentiated, and the text has a single narrating voice distributed across several names. This is among the most frequent structural failures in model-generated fiction, and readers register it as flatness without identifying it as a property of sentence length.

The change converts an instruction that cannot be checked into one that can. Vera's interiority runs long and subordinated; the child's runs short and unsubordinated becomes a statement verifiable against the draft, and a target that can be supplied to the Compositional role at pre_draft rather than assessed afterwards.

4.3 Record the operation, not only the revised text

The design note already specifies storing aligned before-and-after spans. The proposal here is to add one field: the operation the edit performed. The available values are small in number — the edit split a sentence, joined two sentences, shortened within a sentence, or reordered clauses. Thirteen such operations were extracted from the first editing pass on Interim Edge.

The ratio of splits to joins across the whole versions/ corpus is a measurable property of the author's revision behaviour, computable from a single project and more stable than any individual rule derived from it. Rules stated in prose become obsolete as models change; counts of operations do not.

The registry measures which constructions indicate machine authorship. The sentence-length series measures whether the prose varies. These are separate properties, and the harness currently measures only the first.

5. Proposal: derive plot reversals from recorded character knowledge

5.1 The distinction to be operationalised

A plot reversal is prepared when the reader could in principle have assembled it from information the text has already supplied, typically information a character possessed and concealed. It is unprepared when it depends on information introduced at the point of the reversal and justified retrospectively. The distinction does not concern the magnitude of the reversal. It concerns whether the material was present in the text beforehand.

Model default produces unprepared reversals, because a reversal composed at the moment it is required can draw only on what is present in the context window at that moment. Two findings describe the resulting failure. Tian et al., 2024 (Are Large Language Models Capable of Generating Human-Level Narratives?) report that narrative planning involving character intention and sustained conflict remains unreliable even in models that maintain causal consistency. Wang et al., 2026 (Elias in the Lighthouse, cited in the Background) show that the underlying plot structures repeat even when the wording does not.

5.2 The knowledge-asymmetry ledger

character-interiority.md currently records private states as an unstructured list. The relevant research is on temporal representations of what characters know: Zhang et al., 2025 (EvolvTrip: Enhancing Literary Character Understanding with Temporal Theory-of-Mind Graphs) constructs graphs recording how a character's knowledge state changes across a narrative, and Guess What I am Thinking: A Benchmark for Inner Thought Reasoning of Role-Playing Language Agents (2025) evaluates the separation between what a character knows and what they disclose.

The proposal is to hold the same information in the form the harness already uses — a table in a Markdown file, updated by the Structural role, one row per fact:

fact character stance reader access materially present in
the wristband was reissued Vera: conceals none sc. 2 (object), sc. 5 (gesture)
the child's name is administrative clerk: knows · Vera: misreads partial sc. 3 (form)

The columns are: the fact; each character's relation to it, drawn from a fixed set (knows, suspects, conceals, misreads); how much of it the reader has been given; and which earlier scenes made it present as a physical object or an action rather than as a statement.

A candidate reversal is then the result of a query over this table rather than an act of invention:

candidates = facts where
    reader_access is low
    AND some character stance is (conceals | misreads)
    AND count(materially_present_in) >= 2

Every row returned is a reversal for which the preparatory material is already in the text. The Compositional role is not asked to invent a turn; it is given the set of turns the existing text supports, and selects from it. Unprepared reversals fail the third condition, which is a precise statement of what is missing when a reversal does not persuade. The mechanism is a query over state the harness already maintains, and it requires one table, not a new role or an additional pass.

It also supplies a counterpart to obligations.md. That file records what the reader has been shown to be waiting for. The proposed table records what the reader has not yet been shown they should be waiting for. These are different, and only the first is currently recorded.

5.3 Applying the 100-Endings measurement at scene boundaries

Run the procedure described in §3.2 at each scene boundary, using a model other than the one that composed the draft, supplied only with the text written so far. The output is a foreseeability curve across the work.

Two readings are useful. A curve that remains low throughout indicates that the ending is derivable from early in the text, which is median collapse in measurable form. A reversal that produces no change in the curve was foreseeable, and is not performing the function it appears to perform.

This is the most expensive proposal in the document: it requires one hundred generations per measured position. The cost is bounded by measuring at act boundaries rather than at every scene.

Test before adoption. Run the procedure once on a published short story and once on a model-generated draft of comparable length. If the reported separation between them does not reproduce, the measurement does not transfer to this setting and nothing that depends on it should be built.

6. Proposal: record the direction of pressure per scene

The tendency toward escalation is not a matter of the author's taste. Rigby et al., 2026 (The Storyteller in the Model: Narrative Pattern Inheritance, Escalation Dynamics, and Alignment Governance in LLMs) argue that the structural conventions of published narrative — recurring role types, rising-action-and-resolution structure — are acquired during training and reappear as a measurable drift toward escalation over the course of extended interaction. The effect strengthens as a session lengthens, which is the condition under which Narracode is used.

6.1 An operational definition

Sensationalism is defined here as escalation without accumulation: the magnitude of events increases while the reader's uncertainty about the outcome does not. Defined this way it separates from literary fiction along two independent and separately measurable axes — the magnitude of what occurs, and the predictability of where the text is going.

outcome foreseeable outcome not foreseeable
event magnitude rising sensational thriller
event magnitude low median — the default failure literary — the target

The target is the lower-right cell: low event magnitude with an outcome that remains unpredictable. Both axes are measurable. The vertical axis is a count of event magnitude per scene, which scene-ledger.md is already structured to hold. The horizontal axis is the curve from §3.2. Neither requires a judgement of quality.

This also constrains the use of the inflection rate defined in §3.2. Inflection rate should not be maximised: sensational fiction has a high inflection rate. The target is a combination of high uncertainty about the outcome with low variation in event magnitude. Stated as a single design objective: reduce the predictability of the outcome without increasing the magnitude of events.

6.2 The change

Add a signed pressure field to each entry in scene-ledger.md, recording whether the scene raised, held, or lowered the stakes, and add a corresponding commitment in POETICS.md to a pressure curve that is not monotonically increasing. A work in which every scene raises the stakes is a thriller as a matter of structure. Permitting a scene to lower them is a constraint stated negatively, which is the form of constraint this harness has found most effective (see Background).

7. The research position

7.1 Difficulties

  • Automated quality judgement points the wrong way. Rubric-based evaluation using language models ranks model output above published fiction (§3.1). Any process that optimises such a signal moves away from the target while reporting improvement.
  • The annotator sample is one. A single author's decisions define the direction. This is appropriate for a system supporting a particular voice, and it means the registry cannot distinguish characteristic of machine output from contrary to this author's preference. The design note records this and offers no solution beyond disclosure. Survival rate limits the damage but does not remove the limitation.
  • Goodhart effects are structural. Every adopted correction becomes a regularity if applied consistently. The registry addresses this per device and not in aggregate (§2.4).
  • Constraint density has an unknown upper limit. Beyond some density of constraint, composition becomes constraint satisfaction and the prose degrades. The design note recommends locating this limit deliberately, in work that is not intended for publication. This has not been done.
  • Context capacity is finite and shared with the manuscript. Each instrument proposed here consumes context, and model performance degrades measurably as input length grows (Diagnosing and Mitigating Context Rot in Long-horizon Search, 2026). Additions must replace existing content rather than accumulate alongside it, which is why §2.3 proposes conditional selection rather than a larger injected set.

7.2 Opportunities

  • A paired corpus already exists. The versions/ directories hold a pre-revision model draft and a post-revision human draft of the same content, across twenty-five completed stories, with full provenance and one consistent annotator. Research on detection and on style transfer generally lacks all three properties. The corpus was not created for this purpose.
  • Predictability is computable without annotation. The 100-Endings procedure provides a measurement that ranks published fiction above model output. It is the first available instrument capable of indicating that a draft has failed without a human reading it.
  • The file-based design matches independent findings. Holding state in inspectable files outside the model is the architecture the context-engineering literature has converged on — see the ACE item structure discussed in §2.3, and Everything is Context: Agentic File System Abstraction for Context Engineering (2025). Narracode adopted it in May 2026 on literary grounds.
  • Negative constraint is not well studied. The finding that prohibitions reduce the available continuation space more effectively than positive instructions (see Background) does not appear in the published literature on controlled generation or creative-writing systems. It is the most transferable result this project has produced and it has not been written up.

7.3 The contribution

Narracode is a single-subject longitudinal study of harness design for a domain that has no reward function. It has accumulated the materials such a study requires: a versioned corpus with consistent single-annotator ground truth, a registry carrying survival statistics, and a dated record of the harness being revised in response to its own output. The published literature contains benchmarks for creative writing and no methodology for sustained single-author collaboration with a model over a period of months. That absence is where this work is placed.

Items 1 to 3 are changes to file formats and to the instructions the harness issues; none requires new infrastructure. Item 4 is a research proposal and carries the abandonment test stated in §5.3. Items 5 and 6 are already recorded as intended work in the design note and have not been implemented.

9. Summary

The assessment in §1.1 concluded that the harness had more to gain from removing machinery than from adding it. This document proposes six additions, and the two positions are compatible for a specific reason. Orchestration inserts a decision-making layer between the author and the model. The changes proposed here add fields to files the harness already reads and a query over data it already stores. None introduces a scoring pass, a synthesis step, or an autonomous sequence of model calls.

One genuinely new capability is proposed: a measurement of how foreseeable a text is from its own opening. Its value is that it can indicate that a draft has become predictable without requiring a person to read the draft and determine this. That is the condition Narracode was built to detect, and until now it could only be detected by reading.

Bio

David Jhave Johnston is a digital poet working in emergent domains. Author of ReRites (Anteism, 2019) and Aesthetic Animism (MIT Press, 2016). He is currently an AI-narrative researcher at the UiB Centre for Digital Narrative (2023–27) with the Extending Digital Narrative project.
