## Where the drift is

### 1. The "White ___" cluster

Three consecutive titles with *White* in them:

- *White Girls,* Hilton Als (2013) — Raven's crew bag
- *The White Album,* Joan Didion (1979) — Cherry Cellophane reads it
- *The White Book,* Han Kang (2016) — Anouk's bedside book

Coincidence at the title level, but reads as pattern. Two of the three are load-bearing (Als signals Raven's reading habit; Didion is the recognition mechanism for Trygve). The Han Kang is the most replaceable, and the prompter specifically flagged it.

**Swap.** **Astrid Roemer's *Off-White* (1985 Dutch original; English translation 2024).** The Roemer pick:
- does cultural work for Anouk's Surinamese-Dutch heritage rather than borrowing from elsewhere,
- is genuinely niche-yet-readable in 2026 (recent English-language breakout, Dutch literature's preeminent Surinamese voice),
- still has *white* in the title, which lets the prose acknowledge the small ironic pleasure or not.

### 2. The Knausgård line (draft 3)

> "You are giving Knausgård, but if Knausgård were a small object that could be slipped into a tote bag."

Knausgård is the obvious-prestigious-Norwegian reach. For a 27-year-old Black gay NYC cabin steward whose other references include Vaginal Davis, Hilton Als, and Caroline Polachek, Knausgård reads as insider-without-discovery. The user flagged this directly.

**Swap.** **Tor Ulven (1953–1995)** — Norwegian poet, suicide at 41, post-war canonical-in-Norway, almost-unknown in English outside specialist circles. The substitution gains:
- a real underground-niche reference with crossover for literary readers
- a death-year for the technique
- specifically-Norwegian texture (the joke is Raven making a Norwegian-literature joke about an actual Norwegian)
- a sharper class-cohort skewer if landed right

Try: *"You are giving — okay, hear me out — you are giving Tor Ulven (1953–1995). You are giving Tor Ulven if Tor Ulven had been invited to Davos."*

### 3. Lena Shapiro drifts toward LA-traveler type

Patagonia quarter-zip + redundancy payout + Echo Park + Substack-via-Florian + fish-sauce-face. Each detail is fine; the totality reads as composite stereotype. She needs ONE specific that breaks the type.

**Add.** A small genuine niche detail her surface wouldn't predict: she carries a paperback of **Natalia Ginzburg's *The Little Virtues* (1962)** in her hostel bag, has read it in three continents, and has not, as far as anyone she has met knows, ever mentioned Ginzburg to anyone. Italian, niche, breakout-via-recent-reissues, deeply unexpected for the surface. The unspoken reading habit is also a fact: most people carry private literary attachments their surface does not predict.

### 4. The "Gulf-monarchy intern" cliche (draft 0)

**Swap.** **"designed by a digital agency in Limassol that had since rebranded twice as a Web3 consultancy and then ceased to exist."** Limassol (Cyprus) is the actual offshore-crypto-design hub; the joke gains specificity and loses the cliche.

### 5. Two characters have drifted toward function

**Anan Phromchai** (draft 2). We know his age, tenure, and that he pocketed the keychain. Otherwise: hinge, not person.

**Add.** One detail. *He is two months into learning Japanese on Duolingo because his older sister married a man from Sapporo two years ago and he has decided he is going to visit them this year and not look like a tourist.* This (a) gives him a particular reason to be the kind of person who pockets a foreign-language-speaking small object without panicking, (b) lets the magic-recognition register slightly differently for him than for Marielle, (c) lands one small humour beat in the warehouse paragraph.

**Tee Saengthong** (draft 2). "Engineering student, normally on his phone." Looked up at the magic moment, then drifts into the background.

**Add.** One detail. *He watches cabin-crew videos on TikTok in secret because he has decided, against the entire family's expectations, that what he actually wants to do is fly long-haul for KLM, and he is going to apply next year, but he has not told anyone.* The detail makes his looking-up at the talking keychain into a slightly different beat — he is more invested than the others realise — and seeds a small private parallel between Tee and Anouk (whose hands he will never see).

### 6. The Joost backstory pulls heavy (draft 4)

> "He had — what they call, in the freediving community, a samba — a small loss-of-consciousness — at a depth of thirty-eight metres in the Blue Hole on a Tuesday afternoon in May. The recovery did not work. He died on the surface six minutes later."

The forensic register (*samba* jargon, the six-minute clock) tips Anouk toward survivor-of-trauma. The prompter asked for *kind, simple* — the silence should carry more than the clinical detail.

**Tighten.** Drop *samba* and the six-minute beat. Keep the depth and the Tuesday. *"He had a blackout at thirty-eight metres in the Blue Hole on a Tuesday afternoon in May. The surface recovery did not work."* Quieter; lets the silence after the sentence do its work.

### 7. Marielle is reverent without ever being funny

Marielle is one of the most carefully drawn workers — Tagalog priest, fig tree, the dead-speak-through-radio-static childhood, *Hello, sir* — and the prose treats her with sober dignity throughout. POETICS' Lagerlöf register includes *playful joyous*. She should carry one quiet humour beat without compromising her gravity.

**Add.** One detail: she keeps a small holy card of **San Lorenzo Ruiz (1594–1637)** — first Filipino canonised saint, martyred in Nagasaki — in her uniform pocket, *that the contractor does not know is there.* The smuggled card adds humour, specificity (San Lorenzo Ruiz is genuinely niche-readable Catholic trivia), and a small subversive pleasure.
