## Established in prose

### About Trygve
- 76 in his prior body, lives alone in Bergen, on Skuteviken — apartment with a brass-handled door, a 1972 Saarinen Womb chair reupholstered in 2007, a kitchen window where the November light at 14:30 hits the spot his mother cut bread fifty-four years ago.
- Wealth: bought a man-in-Oslo's company in 1998; shipping interests in 1989; estate planning in 2014; has read complex bureaucratic forms for forty years across twelve jurisdictions.
- Curmudgeon-as-posture, not as outburst.
- Norwegian native; switches to English when annoyed.
- Spoke Norwegian in his first speech (Schiphol). Spoke Thai (his second speech, at the Saengthong cart) when Mali pressed his button. Has read English aloud (the visa form) at length.
- Hears everything; speaks any language; voice is at the mercy of others' button-presses.
- Has been carried in: a chest pocket (Marielle), a lanyard (Anouk), an interior jacket pocket (Anouk), a magnet (Imani), a high-vis breast pocket (Karim), a wooden cargo crate (Bang Na transit), a worker's shirt pocket (Anan), a child's lap (Mali), and now a backpack side pocket (Lena).
- Bears a one-inch piece of pink ribbon with a faded gold edge tied to his metal ring (Mali's gift). It accompanies him forward.
- **Change-of-heart accumulation so far:**
  - At the Schiphol magnet: noticed Imani's wrist-tic, the unattended NAOMI SCHOOL notifications, the Loro Piana customer's ignoring-look, and *noticed that he was not enjoying it.*
  - In the cargo crate: thought, for fourteen hours, about Skuteviken in a register he had not used in his life.
  - At the Saengthong cart: read the visa form well, felt the auditor's satisfaction *different* from the version he had used watching strangers cry on *Watch them all cry*; *decided, for the moment, not to investigate further.*
  - On Lena's backpack-pocket transit: registered the ribbon as a different kind of weight; thought, against his will, of the brass handle on his door; remembered Imani's question *Are you in pain* and noted the answer was no longer the answer he would have given.
