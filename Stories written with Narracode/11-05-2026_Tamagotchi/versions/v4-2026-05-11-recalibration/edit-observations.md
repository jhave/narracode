# Edit observations — Loop 4 (recalibration)

The prompter did not edit any draft this loop. The recalibration was prompter-directed, not prompter-edited. The signal sources are:
- Their direct instruction to widen, refine, replace insider references with niche-yet-readable ones.
- Their two specific call-outs: *don't use the white book too much* and *the knaussgard reference feels a little too insider.*
- Their permission: *don't touch the excess.*

What this implies for future loops:

## 1. The reference layer is a separate axis of revision

This loop introduced a clean separation: structural / character / scene work happens in drafting; **reference choice** is its own revision pass and can be addressed surgically without touching anything else. The drift critique mapped the reference inventory; the edits swapped only the references.

Useful pattern for future projects: do reference-layer drift checks periodically rather than only at the end. A standing test: for each named reference, ask *is this niche-yet-readable, or just niche, or just safe?* If the answer is "safe," consider a swap.

## 2. The "white" cluster taught a small craft rule

I had not tracked the title-pattern across drafts. Three "White ___" books in two drafts is the kind of accidental pattern only a drift pass catches. Worth instituting going forward: a periodic grep across drafts for repeated lexical patterns (titles, opening words, named years, character ages) that reveal accidental rhymes the prose is making with itself.

## 3. Niche-yet-readable as a craft category

The prompter's instruction names a specific zone. The substitutions that worked:

- **Astrid Roemer** (Surinamese-Dutch literary fiction; recent English breakout via 2024 translation; specific to Anouk's heritage)
- **Tor Ulven** (Norwegian poet; specialist-known; the joke gains from being specifically Norwegian rather than generally literary)
- **San Lorenzo Ruiz** (first Filipino canonised saint; Catholic-Filipino trivia; specific to Marielle's culture)
- **Natalia Ginzburg's *The Little Virtues*** (Italian; canonical-in-Italy; available-but-not-trendy in English; deeply unexpected for Lena's surface)
- **Limassol** (real Cypriot crypto-design hub; specific replacement for the regional cliche)

The pattern: each pick does cultural work for its character (not borrowed), is genuinely specialist (rewards the reader who recognises), and is breakout enough that a non-specialist can be carried by context. References that DON'T do this: anything an LRB-reading literary friend would mention without prompting (Knausgård, *White Book*, Frank Ocean as a recognition-test). The latter category is fine in moderation but should not dominate.

## 4. Two characters were rescued from function

Anan and Tee both got a one-detail conversion. The rule that emerged: a character with three named attributes (age, role, gesture) is a function; a character with one *secret* — a habit, an aspiration, a smuggled card — becomes a person. The secret does not need to be revealed to anyone in the prose; the prose only needs to register that the secret exists.

Marielle, Imani, Karim, Anouk, Raven, Yai already had this. Anan and Tee did not. The fix was minimal.

## 5. The Joost backstory tightening

The clinical detail (*samba* / six minutes) read as performed-trauma. The simplified version lets the reader fill the silence. The prompter's *kind, simple* spec was load-bearing — the simpler version is closer to it.

## Recommendations for POETICS

None. The recalibration validated POETICS as written. Possibly worth a small future addition under *Refusals*:

> *No reference for safety. If a reference is the obvious-prestigious-name in its category, swap it for a real underground-niche one. The aim is the niche-yet-readable zone — specialist enough to reward recognition, breakout enough to carry a reader who does not.*

But this is a craft rule that emerged from the writing; whether to encode it in POETICS or leave it as a learned habit is a prompter decision. Recommending; not writing.

## On the "kind elderly female near retirement" (POETICS open-question 6)

Unfulfilled across four drafts. The story closed without her. Three options for future:

1. **Revise POETICS** to remove the commitment (it was a Loop-0 plan that the writing did not need).
2. **Add a coda scene** in a future loop where she appears (e.g., the next morning at Bergen Flesland; or the moment Trygve gets home to Skuteviken; or in a sequel piece).
3. **Leave the gap** as a deliberate trace of the difference between plan and execution — a Lagerlöf-ish lesson about how stories find what they need rather than what they were told to find.

Recommending option 1 or 3, not 2. Not writing.
