# POETICS.md — project commitments

*Initiated 2026-05-11. Working title: Tamagotchi (working subtitle: "The Wonderful Adventures of Trygve Aas"). Display title: The Wonderful Adventures of Trygve Aas. Loop: 0.*

## Display synopsis

An old, bitter, rich man in Bergen is converted into an animated Tamagotchi-keychain at the airport. Carried in pockets and on lanyards by airline workers across Bergen, Schiphol, Bangkok, LA, NYC, and home again, he sees the world from a different scale — and a change of heart slowly, magically accumulates.

## Premise

A short story. An old, bitter, rude rich man living alone in Bergen — Trygve Aas, working name — is, while browsing an illicit crypto website at the airport, converted without warning by a super-intelligent AI browser plugin magic (inexplicably) into an animated (living like a little elf that can used as a keychain) Tamagotchi (the name etched over the device is 'Trygve Aas' not 'Tamagotchi). From that moment on his adventures are because he keeps getting picked up by people who work on different airlines (and occasionally airport janitors, baggage handlers, lounge cleaners, cabin crew, ground staff) who carry him into a larger world and inadvertently offer tiny parables about what it is to live with socioeconomic constraint. Across these encounters a change of heart slowly, magically accumulates in him.

The story is about what happens to a sensibility built for refusal, endowed with privilege, when the world starts handling it physically — when a man who has spent decades being un-touched is now, of necessity, picked up.

## Uploads (named, source: training-data)

- **Selma Lagerlöf, *The Wonderful Adventures of Nils*** — primary structural and moral DNA. The folk-pedagogical journey across geography. The small protagonist whose smallness *is* the moral instrument. The patient, low-affect prose that lets landscape, weather, and the lives of nearby creatures carry the meaning. Magic-as-the-grammar-of-attention, not magic-as-spectacle. The willingness to take a long, quiet route to a soft ending.

- **Dena Yago, "Rotten Dot Com" (Paris Review, 2026-05-06)** — intermittent rupture-style. Art-world acerbic, theory-pop, sentence-level wit, dry put-down, sharp cultural-diagnostic deflation. These come as *bursts* — a sentence or two — never as the prose's settled register. ## Tonal compound

Most of the prose lives in the Lagerlöf register: patient, attentive, low-key, willing to dwell on a janitor's hand or the temperature of a luggage carousel for a paragraph at a time. Yet also insightful magical playful joyous. Then — irregularly, never on a schedule, never telegraphed — a Yago-burst lands: a sentence or two that diagnoses the cultural moment, takes a shot, names something coldly. Then the Lagerlöf register resumes, slightly chastened, slightly sharper for the interruption.

The interruption folds into the prose idiom. Do not pace it. Do not announce it.

## The 21st-century idiom

Specific. Brands, apps, screens, infrastructures. Airline tier names. Loyalty programs. The smell of the Bergen Flesland jetway in November (wet wool, kerosene, hand sanitizer, snus). The PA voices of a 24-hour concourse. The micro-economy of shift work — break rooms, the cigarette outside the staff exit, the locker. Smartphones. Earbuds. The battery icon. WhatsApp groups for cabin crew. Wise, Revolut, the airline scheduling app. The lighting of a 4am terminal and what it does to the skin of someone working a twelve-hour shift.

No 90s-Tamagotchi-as-kitsch nostalgia. The Tamagotchi-state is a real abjection. He is now a 3D screen-creature requiring care.

## The transformation (the unspoken mechanism)

He browses an illicit site (offering transformative wealth crypto-wins beating everybody, "watch them all cry") at the airport. The illicitness is a vehicle, not the lesson. There is no morality-tale framing of the website. The transformation arrives without warning, with the specificity of a software push notification — except it is biological, except it is not biological, except the categories no longer hold. The story does not explain how he became a Tamagotchi. The reader learns what the cabin crew member who picks him up learns: that this small object on the airport floor, this thing the size of a keychain, is making faces, is tracking eye contact, hears everything, has weather on it. IF you press a button on his side he can speak in any language. People who carry him just think he is an irate fun chatbot being to be let out. Occasionally he is muted or unmuted.

## Inversions of Nils (held lightly, never explained inside the prose)

- Nils is a child who needs taming → Trygve is an old man who needs softening.
- Nils is punished for cruelty by an elf → Trygve is transformed by a screen, by a click, by a private appetite.
- Nils flies on a wild goose → Trygve is carried in pockets, lanyards, the inside of a cabin crew jacket.
- Nils sees Sweden's geography from above → Trygve sees the airline economy from inside, at the level of staff doors and break rooms.
- Nils learns by encounter with animals → Trygve learns by encounter with workers.
- Nils returns home transformed → Landing in Bergen, the story loops, somehow he is transformed back to real size.

## Refusals

- No Tamagotchi-as-kitsch. No 90s nostalgia voice. The transformation is real, and the abjection is real.
- No moralism about the illicit website. No shame-arc. The browsing is a hinge, not a sermon.
- No class tourism. The airport workers are not types. Each is named, particular, has a specific shift, a specific accent, a specific phone, a specific reason for being at work. They are not Trygve's teachers; they are people who happen to be near.
- No clean redemption. The change of heart accumulates; it is never declared.
- No sentimentality about being held. The pocket is warm, yes — and also: it is the inside of a uniform. It is not a home.
- No Lagerlöf-pastiche. We are not writing Edwardian children's-book voice. We are writing what Lagerlöf attended to, in the 2026 idiom.
- No Yago-pastiche either. The bursts come from inside the prose's own pressure, not as imported style.
- No naming the uploads inside the prose. Lagerlöf is not invoked. Yago is not invoked. The Tamagotchi is not explained as a parable.
- No magic-realist signposting. The magic does not announce itself. It is the texture by which the prose starts to honor what Trygve starts to notice.

## Characteristic noticings

What this sensibility attends to:

- the specific small cruelty (some recalled after transform) in how the pre-transformation Trygve speaks to a barista, a check-in agent, a cabin crew member offering water;
- the smell of a airport and cities around the world in November;
- the lighting in a 24-hour concourse and what it does to the skin of someone working a long shift;
- the gesture of a worker bending down to pick up a small object near a charging station;
- the moment a baggage handler realises the keychain on the floor has a face, and what they do in the next 1.5 seconds;
- the lanyard chest or warmth of a cabin crew jacket pocket against the temperature of a screen;
- the texture of a cleaning trolley, an airline lanyard, a lounge attendant's apron;
- the specific apps a worker opens during a break — TikTok, WhatsApp, Wise, Revolut, the rota app;
- what music a janitor is listening to at 4am, and how Trygve, as a screen-creature, can hear the bleed from the headphones;
- the small ordinary attentiveness of being looked after, against Trygve's lifelong refusal to be looked after;
- weather, weather, weather — Bergen rain, a Doha layover at 43°, the cold dry pressurised cabin air at 35,000 feet.

## The change of heart (held open)

The story does not declare the change. The reader feels it as the prose starts noticing what Trygve has begun to notice. The first time he looks at the cabin crew member who has carried him in her pocket for six hours and does not think a cruel thing. The first time he is fed (in the Tamagotchi sense — a button press, an offered crumb of attention) and does not resent the dependence. The accumulation is the magic.

The eventual return to Bergen — if there is one — is held open at the time of this writing. He thinks if only he can get back...
