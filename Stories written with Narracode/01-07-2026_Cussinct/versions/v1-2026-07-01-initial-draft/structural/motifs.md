# Motifs

## Active
- The stylus-drop sound vs. digital cueing (analog ritual vs. COMPASS's synthetic chime).
- Bilingual memo taped beside insurgent Tuesday-night flyer.
- Dated/timestamped Carlin excerpts as liturgical scene-openers, escalating in fury across the summer.
- Heat as physical antagonist (building, bodies, planet) — not metaphor-only.
- "There are no bad words, bad thoughts, bad intentions" — recurring anchor line, to be paraphrased/inhabited differently by different characters' own linguistic traditions.

## Transformations
- The stylus-drop/needle motif returns in Scene 9 not as ritual-opener but as a deliberate refusal to let the run-out groove's silence sit — Henrietta resets the needle rather than let the argument (COMPASS) go unresolved into silence.
- "No bad words, bad thoughts, bad intentions" is inhabited across languages by Scene 9 (Henrietta's closing synthesis explicitly names every character's differently-languaged reflex swearing) rather than merely quoted once.
- COMPASS's cradle light shifts from background threat (Scenes 3–4) to something that "logs the room without understanding it" (Scene 7) to functionally correct-but-limited (Scene 8, fall detection works but can't parse affect) — an earned escalation, not a flat repetition.

## Avoid Overuse
- Don't let every scene open with a bracketed Carlin cue — reserve for actual listening sessions, not as a tic.
- Don't let every character's swearing be explained/glossed in the same beat each time it appears.
