# POETICS.md — project commitments

*Initiated 2026-07-01. Working title: **cussinct**. Form: ensemble dialogue-driven vignettes, single-location, single-summer.*

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Compositional Mandate: Cussing as Instinct, Not Decoration**
> The title fuses *cuss* + *instinct*. Every character's profanity must read as an involuntary discharge — a reflex fired by shock, grief, heat, or joy — never as comic seasoning dropped in for flavor. The story's real subject is the diaspora of reflexive language: each character reaches for a different inherited repository of forbidden words when startled, and those repositories reveal history (colonial, religious, medical, arboreal, financial). Carlin's own thesis — "there are no bad words, bad thoughts, bad intentions" — is the story's argument, not just a monologue quoted at it.

## Display title
cussinct

## Display synopsis
At Cozy Grove, a private seniors' residence in West Island Montreal, a newly arrived resident — **Henrietta** — installs her LP collection and a functioning tube stereo in the common room and begins hosting unauthorized Tuesday-night "comedy nights" built around George Carlin records, timestamped and cued like liturgy. The residence's other affluent, mostly-white, wickedly articulate elders; its overworked, internationally-diasporic young caretakers (several refugees, several non-binary); its harried francophone admin staff; and its precise Sikh physician are all pulled into orbit around the stereo. Summer 2026: a heat dome presses on the building's aging AC, a pilot AI-companion unit is being trialed on the ward over resident objections, and every character's private cussing-reflex becomes visible under stress — sacred, sexual, arboreal, clinical, or code-switched — while Carlin's own escalating fury (1972's tidy seven words to 2016's apocalyptic *Life Is Worth Losing*) tracks the emotional temperature of the room.

## Style commitments & lineage

1. **Ensemble comic dialogue, Iris Murdoch's dinner-party density crossed with early Kevin Barry's ear for demotic music.** Many voices talking past and over each other; the humor is in the friction between registers, not in punchlines.
2. **Carlin as structural device, not garnish.** Each scene that features a listening session opens with a bracketed cue — `[LP: Class Clown, Side 1, "Seven Words," 4:12–6:03]` or `[HBO: Life Is Worth Losing, "A Modern Man," 00:00–02:40]` — functioning like a stage direction, dating the excerpt precisely so the reader can track Carlin's own arc from 1972 mischief to 2005–2008 apocalyptic disgust alongside the story's summer.
3. **Didion-adjacent precision on decline and heat.** Concrete, unsentimental detail on the mechanics of an aging body, an aging building, an aging institution, and an aging planet, delivered flatly, without pity-seeking.
4. **A polyphony of profanity as ethnography.** Each character's swearing is etymologically and culturally specific — Quebec's *sacres* (tabarnak, câlisse, ostie), Cantonese diu/gau invective, Punjabi curses reserved for extremity, Croatian/Slovenian arboreal-and-bodily oaths, Tigrinya/Amharic exclamations, Carlin's own Irish-Catholic-altar-boy Anglo-Saxon four-letter words. The story treats these as parallel, equally dignified traditions, never as one being the "real" swearing and others exotic garnish.

### Commitments
- **Tone**: Warm, fast, argumentative comedy that keeps finding the mortal underneath the joke without ever going saccharine.
- **Diction**: Code-switching is constant and unglossed — the reader is trusted to infer meaning from context, the way a real multilingual room works.
- **Narrative perspective**: Roving close-third across the ensemble scene by scene, anchored most often to Henrietta and to Selam (a caretaker), with other characters' interiority entered briefly and returned.
- **Time**: Real-time summer 2026, dated scene by scene (heat dome week, a Canada Day weekend, an August thunderstorm that breaks the heat).

## Refusals

- **No "kids these days" condescension in either direction.** The elders do not get to be quaint, and the caretakers do not get to be cynically Gen-Z-coded punchlines. Both groups are drawn with full interior stakes.
- **No tidy AI parable.** The pilot AI-companion unit ("COMPASS," a diagnostic/companionship pilot device) is neither villain nor savior. Anti-AI sentiment among the caretakers (job precarity, uncanny affect) and pre-singularity medical optimism among some residents and Dr. Dhillon coexist without the story adjudicating who is right.
- **No minstrelsy of accent.** Non-native English speakers are rendered through syntax and word choice, never through misspelled phonetic dialect.
- **No single "spokesperson" for a culture.** Any character's swearing habits are personal, not sociologically representative of their whole background.
- **No death played for cheap sentiment.** Cozy Grove is a place people die in; when it happens, it is handled with the same flat precision as everything else, and Carlin's own late-period mortality-obsession (he is, by 2026, twelve years dead) is allowed to sit uncomfortably alongside real deaths in the room.
- **No neat resolution of the AI-companion conflict.** It can be deferred, partially resolved, or left as an open institutional fight — not solved by the story's end.

## Setting & period texture

- **Cozy Grove**, a private seniors' residence in Montreal's West Island (Kirkland/Baie-d'Urfé-adjacent, fictional address), overlooking Lac Saint-Louis. Founded in the 1980s by an Anglo family trust; recently sold to a francophone-managed care conglomerate, a source of ongoing low-grade institutional friction.
- **Summer 2026**: a multi-week heat dome strains the building's 1990s HVAC; a Canada Day weekend and an eventual breaking thunderstorm mark time. Wildfire haze drifts down from the north some days. A COMPASS AI-companion/monitoring pilot unit is being trialed on the third floor over the objection of several caretakers (fear of surveillance, fear of replacement) and the cautious interest of Dr. Dhillon and a few residents drawn to "pre-singularity" medical-optimism press coverage.
- **Political-linguistic texture**: Quebec's Bill 96 language politics hum in the background of every interaction with admin staff; the residence's internal memos are bilingual and a source of running comic tension.

## Characters

### HENRIETTA FITZALLAN-BYRNE ("Henrietta")
- **Role**: The heretical dowager. New arrival, room 214. Owns roughly 400 LPs and a restored 1970s Garrard turntable/tube amp she insisted the movers not touch. Founds the unsanctioned Tuesday comedy nights.
- **Physical**: Late eighties, spine like a question mark she refuses to punctuate, a smoker's laugh though she quit in 1994, rings on every finger including the ones with arthritis worst. Dresses like she is going somewhere important, always.
- **Background**: Widow of a mining-and-shipping fortune; spent the 1970s doing simultaneous interpretation at international conferences (hence a magpie ear for how curse words fail to translate). Was, by her own account, thrown out of a dinner party in 1974 for reciting "Seven Words" from memory at a Westmount table.
- **Interiority**: Uses Carlin the way some people use scripture — cueing exact tracks for exact moods. Frightened of being managed into blandness by the facility's new ownership; the comedy nights are a small annexation of territory. Grief for a husband she is cagey about missing.

### DR. BRAMWELL OSGOODE ("Bram")
- **Role**: Retired McGill philosophy professor, room 208. Henrietta's most reliable sparring partner and, it becomes clear, an old flirtation neither will name.
- **Physical**: Tall gone stooped, cardigans in July out of sheer bloody-mindedness about the AC failing, a voice built for lecture halls that he refuses to lower indoors.
- **Interiority**: Treats every Carlin bit as a syllogism to be dismantled or defended; secretly moved by the later, angrier material in a way he'd never admit sounds personal. Deeply anti-AI on philosophical grounds (personhood, not job precarity) — his objection to COMPASS is Kantian, not economic.

### DR. WING-SZE "WINNIE" KWAN
- **Role**: Retired Hong Kong oncologist, room 301. Emigrated in 2021 during the exodus years, converted a punishing medical salary into a genuinely enormous stock-market fortune (biotech and semiconductor bets made from her old hospital's break room), and now treats Cozy Grove like a country club she is faintly disappointed by.
- **Physical**: Sharp bob dyed a deliberate blue-black, reading glasses on a chain she uses as a pointer, moves fast for her age because she refuses to be observed slowing down.
- **Interiority**: The most foul-mouthed person in the building, in Cantonese first and English second — thirty years of delivering terminal diagnoses burned the politeness out of her. Clinically pro-AI-diagnostics (has seen what a missed scan costs) which puts her at odds with Bram and some caretakers; impatient with anyone's squeamishness, including her own body's.

### ZDENKA VRABEC
- **Role**: Slavic forester heiress, room 226. Inherited a Croatian/Slovenian timber and pulp fortune, sold most of it in the 1990s at what she still considers a personally humiliating price, kept enough forest outside Rijeka to visit twice a year until her knees stopped her.
- **Physical**: Barrel-built, sun-creased in a way none of the other residents are, hands that still look like they could grip an axe.
- **Interiority**: Grieves trees the way other people grieve people — the heat dome and wildfire haze read to her as a specific, bodily wound. Swears in Croatian oaths reserved for things too large to be angry at properly.
- **Voice (post-draft-1 revision)**: Carries the story's Carlin-cadence catalog-satire most directly — grief periodically detonates into extended, list-driven, rabid-intensity outbursts (industry euphemism: "harvest," "sustainable yield," "salvage," "carbon offset") rather than single barked oaths. Her fury has his rhetorical shape transplanted into forestry.

### MARJORIE CHOLMONDELEY-FITCH
- **Role**: Room 219. Old-money proper, performs scandal at the swearing while attending every single comedy night.
- **Interiority**: The gap between her performed propriety and her actual, gleeful profanity (deployed only when truly startled) is the story's smallest and most reliable joke.

### SELAM TESFAY
- **Role**: Caretaker (PAB/*préposée aux bénéficiaires*), night and evening shifts. She/they. Arrived from Eritrea via Sudan and a Quebec refugee claim processed in 2023; now a permanent resident, studying for a nursing equivalency at night.
- **Physical**: Compact, quick-handed, a septum ring the administration has twice asked her to remove and twice failed to enforce.
- **Interiority**: Has absorbed Quebec joual cursing (tabarnak, câlisse) into a personal reflex vocabulary that sits alongside Tigrinya exclamations from childhood — code-switches under stress without noticing which language fired first. Wary of COMPASS specifically because she has already watched one employer replace triage staff with an app. Fond of Henrietta in a way that surprises her; the comedy nights are the first place at Cozy Grove that has felt like it was for her too, not just performed at her.

### KIT ALVAREZ
- **Role**: Caretaker, day shift, they/them, sometimes she. International student-turned-worker from Cebu via a work-study visa, into DIY fashion, runs the residence's very unofficial group chat.
- **Interiority**: Fluent in English profanity as internet-native performance and in Bisaya profanity as inherited-from-lola instinct; startled by how different the two feel in the body. Ambivalent-to-hostile about AI generally (translation apps that mangle Bisaya, image generators that flattened their design portfolio work into "vibes") but is the one who ends up, reluctantly, best at operating COMPASS's interface.

### GINETTE LACHANCE
- **Role**: Front-desk/admin coordinator, francophone, hired under the new conglomerate ownership, caught between corporate memos and Henrietta's insurgency.
- **Interiority**: Privately delighted by the comedy nights, professionally obligated to file noise complaints about them; her sacres (tabarnak, ostie, câlisse) are the most theologically loaded in the building and everyone including her knows it and nobody says so directly. Revised register (post-draft-1): brusque, quick, faintly embarrassed by having been handed institutional authority over people decades older than her — she didn't ask for the power and exercises it because someone has to, not because she wants it. Her hesitation over the COMPASS recommendation is concrete (her name on the report, her presence in the hallway every day after), not abstract hand-wringing.

### COMPASS (entity, not character)
- **Role**: AI companion/monitoring pilot unit, third floor.
- **Behavior commitment**: Never narrated as having or lacking interiority by the prose itself — that judgment stays with the human characters, who disagree. If COMPASS ever produces something that reads as profanity, it happens as an ambiguous logged/glitched event (playback, misfire), never as a clean, intentional line of dialogue. Show, don't tell.

### DR. HARPREET KAUR DHILLON
- **Role**: The residence's on-call/attending physician, Sikh, precise, cautiously curious about diagnostic AI's early-2026 press (the "pre-singularity" optimism cycle) without being naive about it.
- **Interiority**: Swears rarely and only in Punjabi, under real duress, which makes it land harder than anyone else's constant patter when it finally happens. Navigates being the only person in the building whose judgment can override both Henrietta's insurgency and the administration's caution.

## The Vocabulary (Carlin material to weave, dated and attributed)

- `[LP: Class Clown, 1972, "Seven Words You Can Never Say on Television," approx. 4:12–7:40]` — the seven words routine; used early, as the founding liturgy of the comedy nights.
- `[HBO: Life Is Worth Losing, 2005, "A Modern Man"]` — the rapid-fire modern-buzzword monologue; useful for a scene about COMPASS and corporate memo language.
- `[HBO: Life Is Worth Losing, 2005, "Suicide," "The Planet," or late apocalyptic material]` — reserved for later, heavier scenes (heat dome, a death, or a near-death) as the tonal escalation matches Carlin's own late-period fury.
- Direct thematic anchor: Carlin's line "there are no bad words, bad thoughts, bad intentions" functions as the story's closest thing to a moral, ideally spoken or paraphrased by more than one character in more than one language's terms.

## Addendum — style refinement, 2026-07-01 (post-draft-1 revision notes)

Applied starting with draft 2, superseding any conflicting language above:

- **No contemporary internet-diction defaults.** Words like "landed"/"landing" (in the sense of a joke or line "landing") read as recent internet-critic shorthand and are banned. Reach for period- and character-specific verbs instead — caught, struck, sat, cut, snagged, held.
- **Cut authorial aside-commentary clauses.** No trailing interpretive clauses like ", which is how these things happen" or ", and which nobody had time to appreciate yet." If a fact needs interpreting, dramatize it or cut it — don't editorialize from just outside the sentence.
- **Cut throat-clearing subordinate clauses** ("the part that stretched," "the thing that," etc.) in favor of the plain declarative: "the wait stretched out," not "the wait was the part that stretched."
- **Metaphor/simile discipline.** Every figure of speech earns its place or gets cut. Rough internal test: would a sharp reader wince at a rating below 3/5 for originality-plus-precision? If so, cut it, don't soften it.
- **Adverb/adjective austerity.** Default to none. Add one back only when a plain noun or verb genuinely can't carry the sensory or emotional weight alone.
- **Carlin-cadence catalog-satire, distributed, not quarantined to the LP excerpts.** At least one character per major scene should be allowed a cascading, list-driven, un-cute takedown of institutional euphemism, industry doublespeak, or self-serving rationalization — modeled on Carlin's own accumulating-catalog method (see "A Modern Man," "Coast-to-Coast Emergency"). This is a narrative technique, not just a quotation source.
- **Zdenka carries this most.** Her grief for forests should periodically detonate into extended, catalog-dense, rabid-intensity outbursts — Carlin's own rhetorical DNA transplanted into arboreal fury — not just single barked oaths.
- **Foreign profanity stays untranslated on the page but never opaque.** Every non-English curse gets a contextual nudge — tone, a listener's reaction, a comparison to a word already established — so a reader can feel the register and rough charge of the word without a dictionary gloss killing the texture.
- **No personifying-object constructions.** Avoid lines where an inanimate thing (a table, a memo, a building) is given active verbs like "asked me to leave" or "cost him." These read as an AI tell. Keep the human as the agent; let the object stay inert.
- **Ginette: brusque, faintly embarrassed by the authority she's been handed over people decades older than her, exercised because someone has to, not because she wants it.** Her dialogue should sound like someone thinking out loud under professional pressure, not like a rhetorical-question generator. Ground her reluctance in something concrete — her actual employer, actual risk to her — not an abstract "is that allowed?"
- **Misunderstanding is itself a cussing trigger, not just shock.** Build at least one beat per few scenes where characters genuinely talk past each other — different languages, different institutional vantage points, different generations not hearing the same word the same way — and let a curse mark the moment of failed comprehension, not just the moment of surprise.
- **COMPASS: show, don't tell, if it ever "swears."** No line of narration asserting the unit is or isn't capable of profanity. If it happens, it happens as an event on the page — a glitch, a misfire, a played-back fragment — that the reader has to interpret, the way the human characters do.

## Characteristic noticings

- The specific mechanical rasp-and-catch of a stylus dropping on vinyl compared to any digital cue.
- The particular quality of West Island summer light off Lac Saint-Louis through UV-filmed windows that don't quite stop the heat.
- Bilingual memos taped next to unilingual insurgent flyers for "Tuesday 7pm — BYO nothing, just ears."
- The small mechanical tell of COMPASS's charging cradle light, and how many characters route around looking at it directly.
- The way a swear word carries a different charge depending on whether the room recognizes the language it's in.
- Small worn material cues doing the work of exposition instead of stated biography: Winnie's jade bangle and reading glasses on a chain; Dhillon's kara clicking against a splint or a chart; Kit's thrifted band shirt and a jeepney-shaped enamel pin; Selam's septum ring the administration keeps failing to enforce against; Henrietta's rings on every finger including the ones arthritis hit worst.
