# −1 — jug

Twenty minutes earlier the road still believed itself empty.

Sama crossed open ground the way open ground is crossed now: diagonal, quick, counting. Forty-one steps, rubble to rubble. Yellow jug in the left hand, once cooking oil, now water, four litres. Her whole small spine leaning against its pull. Key on twine at her throat, knocking her breastbone at each step, key to a door that is no longer attached to a house. She kept it anyway.

Against her ribs, in a pocket her mother stitched inside the dress two wars of clothing ago, the phone. Father's phone. Charged Thursdays at the clinic generator, one hour, no more, the nurse strict and kind. Not for calls. Nobody left to call. Nobody. Pictures: mother laughing into wind on the corniche, flour on her wrists; Qais on both legs, mid-jump, blurred; herself, gap-toothed, squinting into some vanished sun. 

Thursday's charge, moving south along an abandoned axis, registering faintly as its signal shifts between towers. Somewhere north, in cooled air, a pattern noticed the movement. An identifier that had once slept nightly beside a flagged identifier. Confidence interval updated. Anomaly. 

Sugar. She had promised Qais sugar, and the man at the third distribution had let two rough cubes drop into her palm without a word, and they rode now in the stitched pocket beside the phone, sweating their sweetness into cloth. She would boil mint if mint existed. It didn't. Hot water, sugar, calling it tea, watching him pretend, both pretending, the game their parents left them.

At the road's far edge she set the jug down to change hands and looked up once. New moon low over the broken skyline, thin as the paring of a nail, glowing. Their mother used to say a new moon was a debt the sky owed you. She would tell Qais about the moon. He couldn't see sky from the mattress. She was his window; she carried the outside in and described it: today grey, today crows, today a moon like—

Two-note whistle. That was the code, hers, low then high, from the corner of the collapsed pharmacy, so he'd know her step from other steps and stop being afraid. She was rehearsing it, lips already pursed,

wind lifted. A sound like sewing, high, above.
