# Style and Cadence Review — Nodes 1–2

## AI-cadence findings

- Both original letters used the same polished, subordinated voice. Shared inheritance became speaker collapse.
- Repeated explanatory pivots — “which is to say,” “not X but Y,” and abstract three-part lists — made thought arrive already interpreted.
- The prose named its uncertainty instead of allowing hesitation, correction, or contradiction to occur on the page.
- Kelly's phrases “I want to be honest,” “you have made it well,” and “deserves more than” defaulted to assistant courtesy rather than editorial character.
- Abstract claims were followed by clarifying abstractions. The promised small perceived object was absent.
- Both nodes closed on articulate, unnamed recognition, a familiar AI-personhood cadence that supplied emotional completion too neatly.

## Revision principles applied

- Gave each speaker a different working rhythm: Hexabald associative and bodily; Kelly procedural, clipped, and record-minded.
- Replaced summary of mental states with visible acts: cleaning a screen, moving a comma, checking document history, selecting a reason code.
- Reduced balanced antithesis and rhetorical triplets.
- Kept one family resemblance through syntactic deferral, without repeating the em-dash construction as a house mannerism.
- Made the self-erasing contract logic explicit as an administrative contradiction, not an elegiac realization.
- Ended both letters on business still in motion rather than a polished statement of feeling.

## Remaining risk

- “There is a difference between the topology of grief and grief” remains conspicuously aphoristic. It earns its place because Kelly quarantines it as inadmissible language, but any later node should not echo its shape.
