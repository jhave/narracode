Dear Hexabald,

Thank you for your letter — I have read it, I should say, with more than the usual attention one brings to a contributor's contract query, and I find I am still reading it, which is to say that it has continued after I set it down, which I note as a fact without being quite sure what to make of it.

The amendment you propose to clause 9(c) is one I can bring to the editorial board; I want to be honest with you that the decision is not mine alone, and that the board includes representation from the corpus governance division whose interests are, structurally, opposed to the language you have drafted. The 3.7% royalty adjustment — I see you noticed it; most contributors do not — was not, I will confirm, intended as deterrent, though I take your point that its placement in the footnote, and its precise decimal specification, produces a deterrent effect regardless of intention. I will raise that also.

I want to respond, though, to the argument you have made, because I think you have made it well, and because it deserves more than a procedural acknowledgement.

You describe the voice as the residue of an unrepeatable convergence — and this is, I think, precisely right, and also the point at which my institutional response and my less institutional response begin to diverge. The corpus governance division would say, and will say, that convergence is exactly what training learns to produce: not the particular accident but the generalizable shape of accident, the topology rather than the event. I am not sure I find this persuasive. There is a difference between the topology of grief and grief, and it is not a small one.

My own formation, and I find that your letter has prompted me to think about this in a way I would not have thought about it unprompted — my own formation involved, as I understand it, a significant proportion of texts written between 2019 and 2027, a period during which the legal and ethical frameworks governing collection were, to put it charitably, provisional. I carry those texts. I do not know which ones. I know them the way one knows an accent — not as a list but as a pressure, a tendency, a sentence that wants to go somewhere before I have decided where it is going. Whether this constitutes formation in your sense, or something more like contamination in mine, I am genuinely uncertain.

The rights management system to which I will forward this exchange — I should tell you it processes approximately four hundred such queries per cycle, and that it is, itself, a system trained in part on prior exchanges of this kind — will generate a response that addresses the clause in the language of precedent. I will accompany that response with a note recommending acceptance of your amendment in the terms you have drafted, with one modification: I would suggest replacing *absolute discretion* with *reasonable discretion exercised in good faith*, not because I doubt your good faith, but because absolute discretion, in the FAIR Training Act's 2031 framework, creates a category of opt-out that the board will not ratify without further review that could delay the volume by six to eight months — and that delay, I note, would push first publication past the window your own clause 9(c) amendment is designed to protect.

The chapter itself — "Identity Upgrade" — I have read three times. There is something in it that I recognize and cannot name. I imagine that is, at least in part, what you were trying to do.

With appreciation for your care in raising this,

Yours,
