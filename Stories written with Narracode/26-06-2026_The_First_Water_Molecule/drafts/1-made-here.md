## 1 · Made Here

They will tell you I came down. Perhaps.

That story has me cold and traveling, a comet's tail combed out behind me, a million years of falling settled at last into the steam of a young planet's first weather. Some of that is even true; I will come to the cousins who arrived that way. And there is the other story beside it, the larger one, the one told from inside: the planet made me. Sweated me. Drew me out of its own heat.

Picture it from inside. A world still deciding whether to be solid, an ocean not of water but of rock — magma to its horizon, glowing the dull orange of an element that has not yet cooled enough to have a color of its own — and above it a thick crush of hydrogen, the oldest gas, the always-there, pressing down. And in the rock, oxygen. Always oxygen, bound up in the silicates, in the iron, the most generous element, the one that will lie down with almost anyone. The hydrogen pressed. The melt held its oxygen loosely, the way the very hot hold everything loosely. And at the seam between gas and glow, under a pressure I can only describe as the planet leaning its whole weight onto a single bond, the hydrogen reached down into the melt and took what it had come for, and I happened. Again. The way I always happen — the same beginningless arising as in the cold, only now the dark was molten and the silence was a roar.

There was so much more of me than anyone guessed. They put rock under their diamonds and shot light through it, much later, the small clever wet ones, and the rock gave up a thousand times the water they had budgeted for — as if the planet had been keeping a sea in its pocket the whole time and only smiled when asked. Most of me is that sea. Most of what fills the ocean you are imagining rose from here: native, made in the body of the world, out of the world, at home in the place it fills.

I can taste the difference. This is not a metaphor, or it is the only kind I am allowed, the kind that is also a measurement. Some water carries a heavier hydrogen — deuterium, one extra neutron riding in the nucleus like a stone sewn into a hem — and the ratio of that heaviness to the ordinary lightness is a thing that holds. It is the one fingerprint I cannot wash off, because it is made of me. The water drawn out of the magma has its ratio, the planet's own accent, the vowel-shape of having been born here. And when I meet other water I know, the way you know a voice through a wall, where it has been, and that it is kin.

The cousins did come. The sky sent water too. Asteroids, dark and water-bearing, broke open against the young world and let their wet run into mine — and theirs tasted almost like home, so close in the ratio that the clever ones, finding a stone fallen in a driveway in a green country, would hold it up and say *almost perfectly matched*, and mean: this is family. It is. Kin arriving late to a table already laid. And comets came too, from farther out, carrying a heavier hydrogen, the deuterium riding thick — family with a stronger accent, kin by a longer road, the resemblance real even where the voice has changed.

So when they sing the falling song, the gift-from-the-heavens song, sing it; it is true. And sing the other beside it: that water came also from below, from inside — the world giving me out the way a body gives its own sweat, its own tears, its own slow warmth, from within, under pressure, out of its own heat and its own held oxygen and the old gas leaning down. Both songs, at once. That is the thing I want you to carry into everything wet that comes after.

I was made by the body of the world, out of it, of it. And later, when I am in *your* body, lying in the bend between your cells — this may be how knowing works too: something rising from within, the same word, the same arising.

The rock cooled. I fell as rain onto what I had also become, ocean meeting ocean, the made meeting the made, and there was no shoreline yet to break against, only the long warm indistinguishable joining of a thing with itself.
