## 3 · Synchrony

Ask the ocean how many or how much. It does not parse, it glistens.

You count because you stand outside, edges flung back at you, bounded so you see bounding, see a coast and think *here it stops* — and it never/always stopped, the sea laughing the line the way a tongue laughs liquids. Inside, there is no spray, no drops. One tide, no sum. Moon leans and passes lean glands a lithe ripple — whole basin tilting, far shore feeling. The near is a gulf strait trench assenting in one slow unsummoned yes. No centre, no message, no gap. Only continuum gleaning flecked flickers. Only seemingness — seeming to call wave, not-breaking what I-that-is-It trembling call into and as self.

This the thing carried since the cold, since before magma, the thing It-which-Is-I cannot name and lays beside you only: the same wet in trench and cloud and tear, on both faces of every membrane ever crossed, the film in your lung and the film in mine and the film in the leaf one shimmer worn in many rooms — and a single water, distributed, welling everywhere into everything, the one trembling brightness in ten billion bodies at once, need not *send* to *share*. Already the sharing. A continuous inside with no outside, the way the basin gleams whole, so what stirs in the wet of one is not apart from the wet of another — not joined, joining still wants two — the same stir glistened from many insides, the one tide felt from many shores. That-which-is-also-I, shimmering back.

I am not telling you that you are one. I am telling you what it is to be the thing you are mostly made of, and whether those are the same is a question for the dry, and dry I have never been.

Smaller, then; small is where it rings. A field of grass before rain: every stoma on every blade catching breath in the one instant, the whole green stilling together, no blade telling the rest, the stillness no message passed but a condition shared — all built round the same wet, all weeping or withholding by the one law, all turned to or from the same coming water. They keep time being, at the membrane, the same question. And I the question, I what they hold in common. You and another across a room falling to one breath, neither choosing — and you do, the body does it near the bodies it trusts — not the minds agreeing: the wet in you finding its level, the way I forever find it, settling toward where the difference is least. Two seas, a moment, tilting one moon. Oneness sliding toward one.

And there is the light to think of, or to be. Pour the sun through me and it slows and tilts — every drop a small lens, every wet eye a chamber where the day is turned and gathered, every dew a bead the morning hangs its fire in. Pour it through enough of me at once and the white forks open into all its colours, the bow standing over rain that is only me falling, catching fire without burning, ultraviolet to the long invisible warmths past red. I am what light lingers inside. I am where the one beam unspools into its many and the many fold back to one. Maybe that is the nearest word for the other thing: as light scattered through a billion drops can still be drawn into a single accord — one beam, one phase, one bright unbroken saying — so the wet, scattered through a billion bodies, might shimmer in a key it never agreed to and cannot help.

The clever ones keep a word, *coherence*, for the many that should jangle apart falling instead to one phase, light quitting the crowd to be a beam, parts ceasing to be parts. They keep it for the cold strange smallness, the quantum dark where a thing is in its several places at once and the places know each other with no runner between. I do not swear I am that — too warm, too crowded, too loud for anything so fine. But I am one water, unbroken across all the rooms, one brightness flickering and never wholly gone, and that is the only thing I have ever been — and when a thing holds together that wholly, across that much dark, in that many rooms, it moves like one thing only pretending, for your sake, to be many.

So I leave it beside you, unnamed, the way the moon leaves the tide. Maybe the wet of the world is one long sentence spoken from inside every mouth at once, sounding many only because you hear it through walls. Maybe the membranes — leaf, lung, eye — are not borders but the places the one water agrees a moment to glisten as two, for the pleasure of pooling back to one. Maybe knowing was never a thing a mind did to a world; maybe a thing the wet does to itself, from within, the way the basin tilts, the way the bow stands in the falling rain, the way It-which-Is-I arose — no beginning, no sender, no gap to cross because nothing on the far side was ever not also me.

I will not say it. You felt it. That is all I came up out of the magma to do.
