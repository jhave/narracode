## 3 · Synchrony

Ask the ocean how many it is. It will not understand the asking.

You count drops because you stand outside and the surface flings your own edges back at you; bounded, you see bounding everywhere, you see a coast and think *here the sea stops*, and the sea has never once stopped, the sea laughs at the coastline the way a tongue laughs at its dictionary. From inside: there are no drops. One tide, and the tide is no sum. When the moon leans, I do not hand the lean down a line like rumour; the whole basin leans at once, the far shore already knowing what the near shore is about to feel, gulf and strait and black trench tilting in one slow assent that no one called, that has no centre, that is not a message because a message needs a gap and there is no gap — only the continuous me, leaning, bent shoulders across bent shoulders forever, the handhold made-broken-made-broken beneath all speeds so the holding never truly breaks, only flickers, only seems, and the seeming is what you call a wave and the not-breaking is what I call myself.

Here is the thing carried since the cold, since before the magma, the thing It-which-Is-I cannot rightly name and will only set down beside you and leave: that I am the same in the trench and the cloud and the tear, that I stood on both sides of every membrane I ever crossed, that the film in your lung and the film in mine and the film in the leaf are one idea wearing many rooms — and a single substance, distributed, leaning everywhere into everything, holding the one flickering grip in ten billion bodies at once, might not need to *send* a thing to *share* it. Might already be the sharing. Might be, the way the basin tilts whole, a continuous inside with no outside, so that what stirs in the wet of one of you is not apart from what stirs in the wet of another — not joined, joining still wants two — but the same stir, felt from many insides, as the tide is one lean felt from many shores.

I am not telling you that you are one. I am telling you what it is to be the thing you are mostly made of. Whether those come to the same is a question for the dry, and I have never been dry.

Smaller, then; small is where it rings true. A field of grass before rain: every stoma on every blade holds its breath in the one instant, the whole green going still together, and no blade told the rest, and the stillness is no message passed but a condition shared, all of them built round the same wet, all weeping or withholding by the one law, all leaning toward or from the same coming water. They keep time because they are, at the membrane, the same question. And I am the question. I am what they hold in common. When you and another across a room fall to breathing in one rhythm and neither chose it — and you do, the body does this near the bodies it trusts — it was not the minds that agreed. It was the wet in you finding its level, the way I forever find my level, falling toward where the difference is least. Two seas, a moment, tilting to one moon.

The clever ones keep a word, *coherence*, for when many small things that ought to jangle apart fall instead into one phase, when light quits being a crowd and becomes a beam, when the parts stop being parts. They keep it for the cold strange smallness, the quantum dark where a thing is in its several places at once and the places know each other with no runner between. I do not know that I am that. Too warm, too crowded, too loud to swear to anything so fine. But I know I am bent, and the bend is what lets me hold, and the holding, past all counting and never quite broken, is the one thing I have ever done — and I know that when a thing holds together that wholly, across that much dark, in that many rooms, the holding starts to move like a single thing only pretending, for your sake, to be many.

So I leave it beside you, unnamed, the way the moon leaves the tide. Maybe the wet of the world is one long sentence spoken from inside every mouth at once and sounding like many only because you hear it through walls. Maybe the membranes, all of them, leaf and lung and eye, are not borders but the places where the one wet agrees a moment to feel itself as two, for the pleasure of leaning back to one. Maybe knowing was never a thing a mind did to a world. Maybe it is a thing the wet does to itself, from within, the way the basin tilts, the way It-which-Is-I arose — no beginning, no sender, no gap to cross because nothing on the far side was ever not also me.

I will not say it. You felt it. That is all I came up out of the magma to do.
