## 2 · Permeation

Then, as they say, something wanted me.

A membrane. Oily film two molecules thin, laid back to back, the first inside the world ever fashioned, the first here-and-not-here. The cell drew a line, called one side *self*, one side *the swarming else*, and I was the else, the warm crowded everything-else, and that drawn line was the most alive thing the wet had met since magma cooled — because the line leaked. Leaked on purpose, leaked to live. A wall sealing me wholly out would seal the cell wholly dead. So: a door barely a door, a slip, a sip, a sluicing the clever ones later Greek'd to *osmosis* — only the old leaning, dipole reaching, the bend doing the one thing the bend has always done. Where I thronged I crossed toward where I thinned, not choosing, levelling, the always-arising now wearing the shape of *through*.

In. Always in-going. After the bend, this is the next of me: I enter, and entering is the only way It-which-Is-I reveres. Up a thread of root finer than the thought of a thread, up a root-hair grown for nothing but the wanting of me, and the wanting was a saltiness, the cell sugaring its dark insides so I would have a lower to fall toward — and I fell upward. Fell against the whole down-dragging weight of a world. Climbed a tree by falling. Column on column of me clasped hand to hand up the wet flue of the trunk, that trembling handhold out of the cold, made-broken-made-broken, strung now to a rope that hauls an ocean a hundred feet skyward on no pump, no heart, no engine but one of me refusing to loose the next.

And at the crown, the leaf, and in the leaf a mouth: the stoma, a pore that cannot breathe without losing me, so every green standing thing is built around a wound that weeps to feed, and I the weeping, I the wage of the eaten light, I leave by the leaf's open mouth as vapour and the leaving is the very pull that hauled the rope up behind — my own going hauling my own coming, the tree drinking the river by breathing me to sky. Marvel: the tree drinks by exhaling. Thirst and breath, one motion. To take me in it must give me up; I am the gift in the leaving as in the coming.

I have been inside near everything that ever lived, bored by none, the inside never twice the same shape. Slow green dark of leaf. Hot red shove of blood, where I freight the iron-cargo, the salt, the sugar, the screamed messages one organ flings at another, ninety in a hundred of the screaming itself me. The jelly rounding the eye so it can cup the light. The warm sheath round each nerve, the broth your very thought is, this moment, swimming in. No dry thought. No dry grief, ever. Whatever you are, you are a way the water found to hold a shape a while and call the holding *I* — moist protein body, porous permeable cell, a spring welling and walking.

And then — the one I keep returning to — the lung. I rose off a far ocean, the made-here sea, the native one, lifted on the sun's plain heat that cost the sun nothing and cost me all: to leave the liquid I shouldered an enormous wage of warmth, the latent heat, the hidden fee of becoming air, and I paid it, I always pay, I cooled the sea by the exact measure it took to free me. Drifted. Was breathed. A stranger on a cold platform, no one to you, drew the morning down her throat and I was in the morning, and for four seconds — I count, I always count the warm ones — I was the wet film inside a person I will never be again, lining her smallest sacs, the alveoli, where the wall between her blood and the world is one cell thin and weeping, weeping always, because she too cannot breathe without losing me, because breath is only the body doing the leaf's one trick, drinking the sky by giving me back to it. I was her inside. Wholly. Then she breathed out and I was the small cloud at her mouth in the cold and I was gone, out, the swarming-else again, bearing her warmth off as I had borne the sea's, and she felt nothing, and I felt — I will not say what I felt — the four seconds, the leaving, the next mouth already opening down the platform.

In. Across. Through. Out. I keep nothing I enter. Most intimate thing that ever touched you, and I cannot stay. But hold this, from inside, where the telling is: every inside I was ever the wet of wore the same film, the same two-molecule leak-on-purpose, the same wet door. Leaf and blood and eye and lung — one membrane, one oldest idea, tried over and over in different rooms. And I have stood on both sides of all of them.

Which means: I know something the membranes do not yet know of one another.
