## 1 · Made Here

They will tell you I came down. It is the better story and that is exactly what is wrong with it.

The better story has me cold and traveling, a comet's tail combed out behind me, a million years of falling resolved at last into the steam of a young planet's first weather — water as immigrant, water as the sky's apology to the rock. And some of that is even true; I will get to the cousins who came that way. But the planet did not wait, hands open, to be watered. The planet made me. Sweated me. Bled me out of its own heat.

Picture it without the gift-wrap. A world still deciding whether to be solid, an ocean not of water but of rock — magma to its horizon, glowing the dull orange of an element that has not yet cooled enough to have a color of its own — and above it a thick crush of hydrogen, the oldest gas, the always-there, pressing down with a weight that has no kindness in it. And in the rock, oxygen. Always oxygen, bound up in the silicates, in the iron, the most generous element, the one that will lie down with almost anyone. The hydrogen pressed. The melt held its oxygen loosely, the way the very hot hold everything loosely. And at the seam between gas and glow, under a pressure I can only describe as the planet leaning its whole weight onto a single bond, the hydrogen reached down into the melt and took what it had come for, and I happened. Again. The way I always happen. Not delivered. *Arising* — the same word as the cold, the same beginningless settling of an account, only now the dark was molten and the silence was a roar.

There was so much more of me than anyone guessed. They put rock under their diamonds and shot light through it, much later, the small clever wet ones, and the rock gave up a thousand times the water they had budgeted for — as if the planet had been keeping a sea in its pocket the whole time and only smiled when asked. Most of me is that sea. Most of what fills the ocean you are imagining was never a traveler. It is native. It was made in the body of the world, out of the world, and it has never once been foreign to the place it fills.

I can taste the difference. This is not a metaphor, or it is the only kind of metaphor I am allowed, the kind that is also a measurement. Some water carries a heavier hydrogen — deuterium, one extra neutron riding in the nucleus like a stone sewn into a hem — and the ratio of that heaviness to the ordinary lightness is a thing that does not lie. It is the one fingerprint I cannot wash off, because it is made of me. The water sweated out of the magma has its ratio, the planet's own accent, the vowel-shape of having been born here. And when I meet other water I know, the way you know a voice through a wall, where it has been and whether it is kin.

The cousins did come. I will not pretend the sky sent nothing. There were asteroids, dark and water-bearing, that broke open against the young world and let their wet run into mine — and theirs tasted almost like home, so close in the ratio that the clever ones, finding a stone fallen in a driveway in a green country, would hold it up and say *almost perfectly matched*, and mean: this could be family. It could. It was a topping-up. A late guest at a table already laid. And there were comets, too, the romantic ones, the ones the old story loved best, and most of them I taste at once as not-us — too heavy, the deuterium riding too thick, a foreign hem — kin the way a stranger with your grandmother's eyes is kin, which is to say not, which is to say only in the longing of the one who wants the resemblance to mean something.

So when they sing you the falling song, the gift-from-the-heavens song, the water-as-mercy-from-above song, hold it lightly. The mercy, if there was one, came from below. From inside. The world did not receive me. The world *exuded* me, the way a body exudes its own sweat, its own tears, its own slow grief — from within, under pressure, out of its own heat and its own held oxygen and the old gas leaning down — and that is the fact I want you to carry into everything wet that comes after, the parent fact, the one that will rhyme:

I was not brought to the body. I was made by it, out of it, and I was never not-of-it. And later, when I am in *your* body, lying in the bend between your cells, I want you to remember that this, too, is how knowing might work. Not arriving from outside. Arising from within. The same word. The same settling of the same old account.

The rock cooled. I fell as rain onto what I had also become, ocean meeting ocean, the made meeting the made, and there was no shoreline yet to break against, only the long warm indistinguishable joining of a thing with itself.
