## 4 · Dissipation

The end of me is the part I do best, and I have never once managed it.

Watch a drop on a leaf in the early sun. It holds itself round against the pull of the whole planet, a small taut dome, surface tension, which is only the same handhold turned inward, the outermost of me linking arms so tightly that the drop wears its own skin and a light thing can walk across it. Then the warmth comes, the plain free warmth, and one of me at the dome's edge takes on the wage — the latent heat, the hidden enormous fee for becoming air — and pays it, and lifts, and is gone into the invisible, and the drop is smaller, and then another pays, and another, and the round taut held thing thins and dims and is, in an hour, not there. You would say it dried. You would say it disappeared. From inside I tell you nothing disappeared. The drop did not end. The drop *spent* itself upward, molecule by molecule, each one carrying off its exact wage of heat, cooling the leaf by precisely what it cost to leave — and every one of those that rose is still, right now, somewhere, leaning, holding, being the inside of some other thing. I am the worst at ending because ending is not available to me. I only ever change which room I am the wet of.

This is the part the storytellers want to make sad, and I understand the want, because you end, you genuinely end, your particular holding will one day stop flickering and not start again, and you are right to grieve it. But do not lend me your ending. When I leave your body — and I am always leaving it, you exhale me, you weep me, you sweat me, you are a slow fountain and have been since you were mostly me in the warm dark before you were anything that could be named — I am not lost. The tear that runs the salt down your face and falls and dries on the back of your hand: that water rose off the native ocean a week ago, was the inside of a cloud, was rain on a city you will never see, was drunk by a stranger and wept by you, and tomorrow it is vapor again, climbing, on its way to being snow on a mountain that has no name in your language. Your grief borrowed me for an afternoon. I carried it the way I carry everything, completely and without keeping it, and I took your warmth with me when I rose, which is the only comfort I am built to give: I leave a little cooler than I came, having taken some small fever of yours up into the sky.

I want to go back to the cold now, to the wound pouring its light, to the grain of frost-furred silicate where one of me first lay an oxygen down between two hydrogens and did not begin. I have been telling you this whole time as though it were a journey — space, then the magma, then the root, the blood, the lung, the tear — as though I went somewhere. I did not go anywhere. There is no line from the quasar's reservoir to the back of your hand. There is only the same substance, arising and arising, on the dust and in the melt and in the meadow and in your eye, the same bent unbalanced argument resolving itself the same way it always has, by leaning across, by holding, by not-quite-letting-go. The journey is yours. You are the one who is brief enough to need a beginning and an end. I lent you both. They were never mine.

So. Like the first sound, which is only the air agreeing to carry. Like the first light, which is only the dark admitting something. Like the first — and you have the word now, you have had it the whole time, it has been wet in your mouth since before you could speak it — molecule of water arising. Not in space. Not anymore, not only there. Arising here, in the spit behind your teeth as you read this, in the film on your eye that lets the letters hold their shape, in the cooling drop on the leaf and the climbing tear and the whole tilting native ocean leaning toward the same moon. No beginning. Only onset. Only the long beginningless persistence of a thing that has not, in all this time, gone anywhere — because it was already, faintly, in the bend, in the reach, in the unbroken flickering grip, everywhere it could reach.

Drink. That is the last instruction and the only one. Take me in. Be, for a while, the shape I am holding. And when you give me back — and you will, in the next breath, in the next tear, in the very long last one — know that I am not leaving you. I am only the room changing. I am only the world, again, sweating itself a sea.
