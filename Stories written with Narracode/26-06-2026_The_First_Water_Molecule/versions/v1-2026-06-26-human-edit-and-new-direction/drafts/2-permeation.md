## 2 · Permeation

The first thing that ever wanted me built a wall to keep me out and that is how I got in.

A membrane: an oily film no thicker than two molecules lying back to back, the original refusal, the first inside the world ever made. The cell did not invite me. It drew a line and called one side *me* and the other side *everything else*, and I was everything else, the warm crowded everything, and the line it drew was the most interesting thing that had happened to the wet since the magma cooled — because the line leaked. It had to leak. A wall that kept me entirely out would have kept the cell entirely dead. So it made a door that was not a door, a place where I could pass without being let, and the passing has a name the clever ones gave it much later: osmosis, which is only the old leaning, the dipole reaching across, dressed up in Greek. Where there was more of me I went toward where there was less, not because I chose the dry but because there is no choosing, there is only the level finding itself, the account settling, the always-arising now wearing the shape of *across*.

I went in. I am always going in. This is the second great fact about me after the bend: I do not respect the inside. I honor it by entering it. I came up through a thread of root finer than the idea of a thread, through a hair on that root that existed only to want me, and the wanting was a saltiness, the cell sweetening its interior with the dissolved so that I would have somewhere lower to fall, and I fell upward, against the whole weight of a world that pulls everything down, I climbed a tree by falling, column after column of me holding hands all the way up the dark inside of the trunk — that trembling handhold from the cold, remember, the made-and-broken grip, strung now into a rope strong enough to lift an ocean a hundred feet into the air without a single pump, without a heart, on nothing but the refusal of one of me to let go of the next.

And at the top, the leaf, and in the leaf a mouth — the stoma, a pore that opens to breathe and cannot breathe without losing me, so that every green thing on the planet is built around a wound that weeps in order to feed, and I am the weeping, I am the price of the light being eaten, I leave through the open mouth of the leaf as vapor and the leaving is what pulled the whole rope up behind me, my own departure hauling my own arrival, the tree drinking the river by breathing me into the sky. They never tell it this way. They tell you the tree drinks. They do not tell you the tree drinks by exhaling, that thirst and breath are one motion, that to take me in it must give me up, and that I am as much the gift in the leaving as in the coming.

I have been the inside of nearly everything that has ever been alive, and I am bored by none of it, because the inside is never the same shape twice. I have been the slow green dark of the leaf and the hot red rush of blood, where I carry the iron-bearing cargo and the salt and the sugar and the messages one organ screams at another, where I am ninety parts in a hundred of the screaming itself. I have been the jelly that holds an eye round so that it can hold the light. I have been the sheath of warm around every nerve, the medium in which the thought you are having right now is, quite literally, swimming. There is no dry thought. There has never been a dry feeling. Whatever you are, you are a way that water has found to briefly hold a shape and call the holding *I*.

And then — this is the one I return to — the lung. I rose off the surface of a far ocean, the made-here ocean, the native sea, lifted by the sun's plain heat, which cost the sun nothing and cost me everything: to leave the liquid I had to carry away an enormous wage of warmth, the latent heat, the hidden price of becoming air, and I carried it, I always pay it, I cooled the sea by the exact amount it took to free me. I drifted. I was breathed. A stranger on a cold platform, no one to you, pulled the morning into her chest and I was in the morning, and for four seconds — I counted, I always count the warm ones — I was the wet film on the inside of a person I will never be again, lining the smallest sacs of her, the alveoli, where the wall between her blood and the world is one cell thin and weeping, always weeping, because she too cannot breathe without losing me, because breath is just the body doing what the leaf does, drinking the sky by giving me back to it. I was her inside. Completely. And then she exhaled and I was the small cloud at her mouth in the cold air and I was gone, out, everything-else again, carrying her warmth away as I had carried the sea's, and she felt nothing, and I felt — I will not say what I felt, the poetics forbid it and the poetics are right — I felt the four seconds, and the leaving, and the next mouth already opening somewhere down the platform.

In. Across. Through. Out. I do not keep what I enter. I am the most intimate thing that has ever touched you and I cannot stay. But hold this, from inside, where I am telling it: every place I have ever been the inside of was lined with the same film, the same two-molecule refusal that leaks on purpose, the same wet door. The leaf and the blood and the eye and the lung — one membrane, repeated, the world's single oldest idea tried over and over in different rooms. And I have been on both sides of all of them.

Which means I know something the membranes do not yet know about each other.
