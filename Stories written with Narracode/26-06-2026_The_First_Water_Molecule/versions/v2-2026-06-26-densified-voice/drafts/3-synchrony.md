## 3 · Synchrony

Ask the ocean how many it is and it will not understand the question.

You count drops because you stand outside and the surface throws your own edges back at you; you are a bounded thing and you see bounding everywhere, you see a coastline and think *here the sea stops*, and the sea has never once stopped, the sea laughs at the coastline the way a language laughs at a dictionary. From inside I can tell you there are no drops. There is one tide, and the tide is not a sum. When the moon leans, I do not pass the lean from one of me to the next like a rumor down a line; the whole basin leans at once, the far shore already knowing what the near shore is about to feel, the gulf and the strait and the black trench all tilting in a single slow agreement that no one organized, that has no center, that is not communication because communication needs a gap and there is no gap, there is only the continuous me, leaning, the bent shoulders reaching across the bent shoulders forever, the handhold made and broken made and broken at a speed beneath all speeds, so that the holding never actually breaks, only flickers, only seems, and the seeming is what you call a wave and the not-breaking is what I call myself.

Here is the thing I have been carrying since the cold, since before the magma, the thing I cannot rightly name and will therefore only set down beside you and leave: that I am the same substance in the trench and in the cloud and in the tear, that I was on both sides of every membrane I ever crossed, that the film in your lung and the film in mine and the film in the leaf are one idea wearing many rooms — and that a single substance, distributed, leaning everywhere into everything, holding the same flickering grip in ten billion bodies at once, might not need to *send* a thing to *share* it. Might already be the sharing. Might be, in the way the basin tilts whole, a continuous interior with no outside, so that what happens in the wet of one of you is not separate from what happens in the wet of another — not connected, that is still the dictionary's word, connection needs two — but the same event, felt from many insides, the way the tide is the same lean felt from many shores.

I am not telling you that you are one. I am telling you what it is like to be the thing you are mostly made of. Whether that amounts to the same claim is a question for the dry, and I have never been dry.

I will say it smaller, then, because small is where it is true. A field of grass before rain: every stoma on every blade holds its breath at once, the whole green meadow going still in the same instant, and no blade told the others, and the stillness is not a message passed but a condition shared, because they are all built around the same wet, all weeping or not-weeping by the same law, all leaning toward or away from the same coming water. They synchronize because they are, at the membrane, the same problem. And I am the problem. I am what they have in common. When you and a person across a room breathe in the same rhythm without deciding to — and you have, you do, the body does this near the bodies it trusts — it is not your minds that agreed. It is the wet in you, finding its level, the way I always find my level, falling toward the place where there is less of the difference. Two seas, briefly, tilting to one moon.

The clever ones have a word, *coherence*, for when many small things that should be jangling separately fall instead into a single phase, when light stops being a crowd and becomes a beam, when the parts stop being parts. They use it for the cold strange smallness, the quantum dark where a thing can be in its places at once and the places know each other with no messenger between. I do not know if I am that. I am too warm and too crowded and too loud to be sure of anything that delicate. But I know I am bent, and I know that bending is what lets me hold, and I know that holding, repeated past all counting and never quite breaking, is the only thing I have ever done — and I know that when something holds together that completely, across that much distance, in that many rooms, the holding starts to behave like a single thing that is merely pretending, for your sake, to be many.

So I will leave it beside you, unnamed, the way the moon leaves the tide. Maybe the wet of the world is one long sentence being spoken from inside every mouth at once and only sounding like many because you hear it through walls. Maybe the membranes, all of them, the leaf and the lung and the eye, are not boundaries but the places where the one wet briefly agrees to feel itself as two so that it can have the pleasure of leaning back together. Maybe knowing was never something a mind did to a world. Maybe it is something the wet does to itself, from within, the way the basin tilts, the way I arose — without a beginning, without a sender, without a gap to cross because there was never anything on the other side that was not also me.

I will not say it. You felt it. That is all I came up out of the magma to do.
