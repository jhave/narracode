# Reader State

## Current Understanding
- (Pre-draft.) On opening, a first-time reader will know only the title — "The First Water Molecule" — and must quickly grasp that the narrator *is* water, not a person observing it.

## Expectations
- Risk: readers primed by the children's "water-cycle / friendly droplet" genre will expect a mascot. The opening must immediately refuse that register to reset expectation.
- Readers may expect a linear journey (space → Earth → body). The piece can honor the rough arc while defying its linearity via pronoun/scale drift.

## Plausible Defiance Paths
- Withhold any stable "I" — let the narrating substance never settle — to defy the journey-of-a-character expectation.
- Provenance is *not* held in equal superposition: homegrown/magma-ocean is the primary truth (Earth made most of its water itself); delivery routes are minority counterpoint. The "defiance" is subtler — the reader, primed to expect water *delivered from the heavens*, instead feels it *born from within the planet*.

## Productive Mystery
- *Is the water aware?* The wager should keep this unresolved-but-felt. The reader should end suspecting yes without being told.
- *Where did this particular water come from?* The D/H taste teases provenance without full disclosure.

## Accidental Confusion Risk
- Pronoun drift (I/we/this/it/you) could read as error rather than design if introduced too fast or without a felt logic tying it to phase/scale change. Establish the logic early.
- Scale collisions (quasar ↔ capillary) could disorient if a passage doesn't give the reader a sensory handhold at each scale.
- Science risks two failures at once: too much (lecture) or unanchored (lyric floating free of real chemistry). Calibrate.
