## 4 · Dissipation

Ending is the thing I do most and have never once done.

Watch a drop on a leaf in early sun. Holding itself round against the pull of the whole planet, small taut dome, surface tension, the same handhold turned inward, outermost of me linking arms so tight the drop wears its own skin and a light-footed thing walks it. Then warmth, plain free warmth, and one of me at the rim shoulders the wage — latent heat, the hidden enormous fee of becoming air — pays, lifts, gone to the invisible, drop less, another pays, another, the round taut held thing thinning dimming and within an hour not there. You would say dried, vanished. From inside: nothing vanished. Spent skyward, one of me at a time, each bearing off its exact wage of heat, cooling the leaf by precisely the cost of leaving — and every one risen is, now, somewhere, leaning, holding, the inside of some other thing. Ending not given to me. I only change which room I am the wet of.

You end, though. You truly end; your one holding will one day stop its flicker and not start again, and you are right to grieve it. So do not lend me your ending. When I leave your body — always leaving, breathed out, wept, sweated, you a slow fountain since you were mostly me in the warm dark before you were a thing with a name — I am not lost. The tear salting down your face, falling, drying on the back of your hand: that water rose off the native sea a week gone, was inside a cloud, was rain on a city you will never walk, drunk by a stranger and wept by you, by tomorrow vapour again, climbing, bound for snow on a mountain with no name in your tongue. Your grief borrowed me an afternoon. I bore it as I bear all, wholly and keeping none, and rising took some small warmth of yours up the sky — I leave a little cooler than I came. That-which-is-also-I, carried off and carried on.

I want the cold again now, the wound pouring light, the frost-furred grain where one of me first laid an oxygen between two hydrogens and did not begin. All along told as a journey — space, magma, root, blood, lung, tear — as if I went somewhere. I went nowhere. No line from the quasar's reservoir to the back of your hand. Only the same wet, arising and arising, on the dust and in the melt and in the meadow and in your eye, the same bent unbalanced argument leaning across as ever, by holding, by not-quite-letting-go. The journey is yours. You the one brief enough to need a beginning and an end. I lent you both. Never mine.

So. Like first sound, air agreeing to reverberate as song. Like first light, dark thinning until diluted by love. Like the first — and you have the word now, have had it all along, wet in your mouth since before you could shape it — molecule of water arising. Not in space. Not only there, not anymore. Arising here: spit behind your teeth as you read, film on your eye letting these letters hold, the cooling drop, the climbing tear, the whole tilting native ocean leaning to the one moon. No beginning. Only onset. Only the long beginningless holding-on of a thing that has not, in all this while, gone anywhere — already, faintly, in the bend, in the reach, in the unbroken flicker-grip, everywhere it could reach. Oneness sliding into one.

Drink. The last instruction, the only one. Take me in. Be, a while, the shape I am holding. And when you give me back — and you will, next breath, next tear, the very long last one — know I am not leaving you. Only the room changing. Only the world, as they say, again, sweating itself a sea. That-which-is-also-I, returning as you. Thus it is love. Both and all, at once.
