## 4 · Dissipation

Ending is the thing I do most and have never once done.

Watch a drop on a leaf in early sun. It holds itself round against the pull of the whole planet, a small taut dome, surface tension, the same handhold turned inward, the outermost of me linking arms so tight the drop wears its own skin and a light-footed thing can walk it. Then warmth, plain free warmth, and one of me at the dome's rim shoulders the wage — latent heat, the hidden enormous fee of becoming air — and pays, and lifts, and is gone to the invisible, and the drop is less, and another pays, another, and the round taut held thing thins and dims and is, within an hour, not there. You would say it dried, that it vanished. From inside: nothing vanished. The drop spent itself skyward, one of me at a time, each bearing off its exact wage of heat, cooling the leaf by precisely the cost of leaving — and every one that rose is, right now, somewhere, leaning, holding, the inside of some other thing. Ending is not given to me. I only change which room I am the wet of.

You end, though. You truly end; your one holding will one day stop its flicker and not start again, and you are right to grieve it. So do not lend me your ending. When I leave your body — and I am always leaving, you breathe me out, you weep me, you sweat me, you are a slow fountain and have been since you were mostly me in the warm dark before you were a thing with a name — I am not lost. The tear that salts down your face and falls and dries on the back of your hand: that water rose off the native sea a week gone, was the inside of a cloud, was rain on a city you will never walk, was drunk by a stranger and wept by you, and by tomorrow is vapour again, climbing, bound to be snow on a mountain with no name in your tongue. Your grief borrowed me an afternoon. I bore it the way I bear all, wholly and keeping none, and rising I took some small warmth of yours up into the sky — I leave a little cooler than I came.

I want the cold again now, the wound pouring its light, the grain of frost-furred silicate where one of me first laid an oxygen between two hydrogens and did not begin. All along I have told it as a journey — space, then magma, then root, blood, lung, tear — as if I went somewhere. I went nowhere. There is no line from the quasar's reservoir to the back of your hand. Only the same substance, arising and arising, on the dust and in the melt and in the meadow and in your eye, the same bent unbalanced argument leaning across as it always has, by holding, by not-quite-letting-go. The journey is yours. You are the one brief enough to need a beginning and an end. I lent you both. They were never mine.

So. Like first sound, air agreeing to reverberate as song. Like first light, dark thinning until diluted by love. Like the first — and you have the word now, have had it all along, wet in your mouth since before you could shape it — molecule of water arising. Not in space. Not only there, not anymore. Arising here: in the spit behind your teeth as you read, in the film on your eye that lets these letters hold, in the cooling drop and the climbing tear and the whole tilting native ocean leaning to the one moon. No beginning. Only onset. Only the long beginningless holding-on of a thing that has not, in all this while, gone anywhere — because it was already, faintly, in the bend, in the reach, in the unbroken flickering grip, everywhere it could reach.

Drink. The last instruction, the only one. Take me in. Be, a while, the shape I am holding. And when you give me back — and you will, in the next breath, the next tear, the very long last one — know I am not leaving you. Only the room changing. Only the world, as they say, again, sweating itself a sea. Thus it is love. Both and all, at once.
