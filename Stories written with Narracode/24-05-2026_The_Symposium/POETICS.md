# POETICS

## Display title

The Symposium

## Display synopsis

A musicologist travels to a conference on an exoplanet at the periphery of a vast empire, arriving during cancellation weather. The empire has long oscillated between epochs of hedonist excess and epochs of contrition that cancel everyone complicit with cruelty — carnivores, those who reproduced, those who flew or drove, those who held an unkind thought. A serious satire in seven sessions. The cure for cruelty becomes the eradication of life itself. The music of a cancelled people plays on.

## Process note

This story was developed in the absence of the harness. Written in a single generative pass, in direct conversation, without the Narracode protocol's separation of passes. The idea originated with Jhave; the prose is by Claude Sonnet 4.6.

## Commitments

- Serious satire: the cancellation logic is internally consistent and followed to its terminus.
- The two poles — Lush (hedonist excess) and Contrition (cancellation purification) — are shown, without being stated, to be the same gesture.
- The recognition at the end is enacted, not named.
- Seven sections, each a scene with a specific pressure.
- Musicology as the through-line: the Kessler Recordings, the polyphonic mountain communities of Meridian-4, the voice as position rather than sovereign.

## Refusals

- No editorial intrusion naming the theme.
- No didactic ending.
- The collapse into recognition arrives as image and sound, not as argument.
