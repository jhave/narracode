## 9. Toronto, August

Kat and I had been friends eleven years, first in Montreal and then not at all for four of them, and then again because she picked up on the second ring like nothing had happened and asked if I still smoked. The Rosemont place went to somebody with a guarantor. I put what I had into two bags, gave the rest to the woman downstairs, and took the 6:40, which in 2028 is seven hours because they have been fixing the corridor since I was in school.

Kat built sets for a company that made commercials for insurance companies; she had a wrecked shoulder from it, a girlfriend named Pris who was in Chicago more than she wasn't, and a two-bedroom on Sorauren she could not have got in any other year; she was rude to me inside an hour. I hadn't been talked to like that since March. I slept fourteen hours.

In Toronto nobody asks what you pay. They ask how long you've had it, which is the same question with the shame moved.

Dez came over on the Thursday to fix Kat's speakers and stayed for dinner. He did sound for other people's films, lived above a garage on Sterling; had toured with a band that people my age pretend to remember, had a kid in Halifax he saw twice a year and talked about easily; he was easy. He put his arm along the back of the chair and I looked at his forearm the whole time Kat was telling a story about a producer, and when she got to the part where the producer cries I laughed a beat late and Dez noticed that I had laughed late.

Nothing happened. He washed four plates, badly, and left at eleven, and I stood in the kitchen with the water still running.

Ahmed texted from Montreal at one in the morning. Two lines about a building on Papineau and then, are you coming back. I typed and deleted. I said the elevators in Ontario are worse and he sent a laughing face and that was the last one for a while.

Kat came out for water in a t-shirt with a dead festival on it and looked at me standing there.

She said, you're not visiting.

I said no.

She said, okay, and drank the water, and went back to bed, and I have never in my life been so grateful to be seen and not asked about.

## 10. Northern Ontario, September

The train leaves Toronto at nine at night and reaches Winnipeg two days later if it reaches Winnipeg, because the freight owns the track and passenger sits in a siding while eleven thousand feet of grain goes by in the dark. We sat four hours outside Hornepayne. Nobody explained it and nobody in the car seemed to expect an explanation, which told me most of them had done this before.

Loreen had the seat across the aisle and was going to a funeral in Sioux Lookout for a brother she hadn't spoken to since 2019; she was sixty, had worked twenty-two years for the school board, had a thermos of rye and 7Up she was not hiding well; she was funny about the brother. She said he was a bastard and then said it again more warmly.

Coach class means you sleep sitting up with your coat as a pillow and wake at four with your hip gone dead and the window black and your own face in it. The bathroom at the end of the car smelled like a public pool. I brushed my teeth over a steel sink with the floor moving under me and thought about nothing at all, which almost never happens.

Then the light came up over muskeg and rock and black spruce standing in water, thousands of them, and it went on being that for six hours. No town. A hydro cut every so often with the towers walking away north. Water the colour of tea.

I used to take pictures. I stood in the vestibule with the glass down and the noise enormous and I did not take a picture, and I could not have told you whether that was loss or just the way I am now.

Loreen ate a sandwich at nine in the morning and offered me half without looking over. In the middle of it she said her brother had a way of standing in a doorway that made you feel the room was his, and that when she got the call she felt her whole chest go loose, like a belt off.

I said I was sorry.

She said, don't be sorry yet, I haven't decided.

## 11. Winnipeg, September

Roy is Kat's cousin and had a house on Lipton with three people in it and a fourth who had left a bike. He worked nights loading for a courier, was kind in a bland unswerving way, made a pot of chili on the Sunday that lasted the week; he did not want anything from me and I could feel my body scanning him for it anyway and finding nothing and going quiet, disappointed, and then relieved.

It went to minus four on the second night. In September. Everyone in that house talked about it the way you talk about a diagnosis in the family, informed and bored.

In Winnipeg nobody finishes a conversation outside after the middle of October. They finish it in the doorway with the cold coming in and somebody eventually says okay, okay, and the door shuts on the rest of it.

Emerson had the room off the kitchen and worked at a bakery, up at three, home at noon, asleep by eight, which meant we overlapped for about four hours a day in a house with one bathroom. She had flour in the creases of her knuckles that did not come out. Her forearms were better than her face and she knew it and wore short sleeves in a cold house.

We were in the kitchen on the Wednesday. She was cutting something and I was leaning on the counter beside her with my arms folded and neither of us moved for the length of a song on the radio.

The distance was maybe eight inches. I have thought about that eight inches more than about anything that happened afterward.

What happened afterward was quick and mostly good and did not repeat. On the Friday she went to her mother's in Selkirk and texted about a dog. I left on the Sunday. She hugged me at the door with the cold coming in, said travel safe, and shut it on the rest of it, and I walked to the bus with my whole chest ringing like a struck pipe over a woman I had known nine days.

## 12. Saskatchewan, late September

Warrick was driving a pickup and an empty stock trailer from Brandon to Strathmore and took me because his wife makes him take people, he said, so he doesn't fall asleep. He was fifty-something, had been in the Forces, had a bad knee and a good radio; he talked for about forty minutes and then not much.

The land does not open. That is the wrong word and everybody uses it. It is already open and it stays open and you go along inside it for nine hours and the only thing that changes is which elevator is on the horizon, and then which one, and then which one, and by the middle of the afternoon your sense of your own size has quietly adjusted and does not adjust back for a couple of days.

Canola gone over. Stubble. Sloughs with birds on them. A dead deer with its legs at angles you don't see in bodies that are all right.

Two combines working in a field the size of a district, dust standing up gold behind them, and a grid road going straight for so long it stopped being a road and became a fact about the planet.

I asked Warrick for his phone at one point, to take a picture, and then handed it back without taking one and he did not ask why.

He dropped me at a gas station in Swift Current and shook my hand and said good luck out there and drove off, and I stood with my bags at a pump under those lights that make everybody look ill, and nothing had happened, and nothing was going to.

I want to be honest that it was one of the better days.

## 13. Banff, October

They put me in the kitchen at a hotel that has a French name and a Chinese owner and staff accommodation up the hill where two hundred of us lived four to a room for four hundred a month taken off the cheque before we saw it.

That is where I got a cast for the first time since the Jean Coutu.

Tarn was from Newcastle, New South Wales, and had done two seasons; he did lifts in winter and pot-wash in the shoulder, could not be embarrassed by anything, had a laugh you could hear from the loading bay. Precy had been there six years and ran the line without the title or the money for it; she was small and fast and unimpressed and had two kids in Cebu she called at five in the morning our time. Kieran climbed on his days off and washed dishes on the others and did not talk unless the subject was rock, at which point he could not be stopped.

We were funny at two in the morning in a way that does not survive being described.

The fire started on the eleventh, on the BC side, and by the thirteenth the whole valley was the colour of a nicotine ceiling and the mountains were a rumour with an edge on it. In October. Precy said it never used to be October and then went back to plating.

Kieran and I had the bunks against the same wall with a curtain between that did not reach the floor. Nine nights. I knew his breathing before I knew his face properly. On the fifth night the fire alarm went at three for burnt toast on two and we all stood outside in the smoke in whatever we slept in, two hundred of us, coughing and laughing, and he was beside me with his arms crossed and his hands under his arms for the cold, and I looked at his wrists, and he caught me looking, and neither of us did anything about it for four more days.

Then he got a call about a route and a window and went to Squamish, and that was that, and it had been the best thing in my week for nine days running.

Tarn said, hard luck, and gave me half his cigarette.

Precy said, you'll be all right, and put me on garde manger, which was a promotion, and neither of us mentioned it.

## 14. Vancouver, December

It rains here the way it rained the whole time and everyone tells you about it before you can say anything about it yourself.

I got a room in a house on Kingsway with a woman named Bernice who had a parrot and rules about the parrot. I did prep at a place on Fraser four days and cleaned an office tower two nights. In December it goes dark at four and the wet gets into the seams of your coat and stays there and you learn the smell of your own building.

Dez was in town for eight weeks doing post on a thing. He got my number from Kat and I let a day go by before I answered because I am not a child.

We had two good nights and then we had a bad one.

The bad one was nothing, which is what I want to put down. He said something about my mother that was a joke and was accurate and I laughed and then twenty minutes later I was crying in a Skytrain station at Broadway with my hood up and my hands shaking and I could not have told you which of the two things did it, the joke or the accuracy or the eight weeks or the woman in Winnipeg with flour in her knuckles or my father, who drove a truck, and stopped.

I want to be clear that I do not think it was my father.

Dez texted the next morning to see if I was okay and I said fine, and he said okay, and that was the honest end of it even though we saw each other twice more.

My mother called on the Sunday. She has the good windows and she wanted to tell me about a leak. Halfway through she stopped and said, are you eating, and I said yes, and she said, you're not, and then told me about the leak for six more minutes.

The rain went sideways past the streetlight for the whole call.

Bernice knocked and said the parrot was doing the thing, and I went and looked at the parrot doing the thing, and it was funny.

Later, the fires again, but that is the spring.
