# Chauffé Éclairé

## 1. Parc-Extension, February

The listing said chauffé éclairé. It was neither.

Ball Avenue. Second floor. A door that didn't close.

Six hundred forty for the room. Four of us. One bathroom.

Sabrina had the front. She worked nights at the Jean Coutu and slept in a sleep mask at noon.

Milad had the back. He drove for two apps.

He had a burn on his forearm from a fryer at a third job.

He said Parc-Ex was finished. Everybody knew.

I said I had just got here.

He said that is what finished means.

The radiators went cold on a Tuesday.

I called the landlord. The number rang somewhere in Laval.

His son came instead. Kosta, twenty-four, tape measure and a phone.

He measured the hallway. He measured my room with the door open.

I asked about the heat. He said the boiler was a whole conversation.

We fucked on the floor because my bed was on the floor.

He kept his socks on. The socks were fine.

Afterwards he stood up and finished measuring.

I asked him not to write the number down.

He wrote the number down.

## 2. Côte-des-Neiges, May

Basement on Van Horne. Five ninety. No window in the room, one in the kitchen.

Fifty minutes to work. Two buses, or one bus and the metro when the bus didn't come.

The work was rating answers. A model wrote them, I said helpful or not helpful.

Nineteen fifty an hour. They called us the quality team.

Fadwa had the other room. She was doing her papers.

She cooked at two in the morning and left me a plate with foil on it.

I ate it cold at six. It was always better cold.

Her boyfriend came Fridays. He parked badly and honked instead of buzzing.

I hated him in a way that had no argument in it.

Some Fridays I sat on the steps until they were done.

In May she asked me to write a letter saying she had lived there since 2025.

She had lived there since 2027.

I said yes before she finished the sentence.

I typed it on my work laptop, on my break, badly.

She read it twice and said my French was getting better.

That was the nicest thing anyone said to me that year.

## 3. Verdun, July

Forty-one with the humidex, six days.

The city opened the cooling centres. Ours was a church basement on Galt.

An old man there did crosswords and would not take the last chair.

Sam had central air on Wellington. He worked in climate tech.

I met him in line at a dep that sold natural wine.

He said the wine was orange. I said okay.

His condo had a Peloton and one fork.

I stayed nine days. I told work my internet was down.

We fucked in the afternoons because the afternoons were unbearable.

He talked afterwards. He talked about carbon markets, mostly.

On the ninth day he said his girlfriend was back Sunday.

I said I was there for the air conditioning.

He said he knew that.

Then he said it was still nice, wasn't it, and looked at me.

I said yes. It had been nice.

Both of those were true and I have not worked out how.

## 4. Hochelaga-Maisonneuve, September

A party on Adam Street for someone I didn't know.

Vincent was fifty-one and fixed motorcycles on Ontario.

Grey in the beard, hands you noticed, a laugh that came late.

He asked what I did. I said quality team.

He said his ex-wife did quality at Bombardier before they cut the line.

We went to his place above the shop. It smelled like solvent.

He was gentle in a way I was not expecting and did not want.

Afterwards he sat on the edge of the bed with his back to me.

He said his son had been at Sainte-Justine since June.

He said the boy was eight and the word they used was aggressive.

He cried without any noise, which is a skill.

I did not touch his back. I thought about touching his back.

I asked what the boy liked.

He said motorcycles, obviously, and then he laughed for real.

## 5. Saint-Henri, November

The renoviction letter came in a real envelope, which should have warned me.

Major works. Six months. They offered two thousand to leave quietly.

The tribunal gave me a date fourteen months out; I painted the kitchen anyway.

The woman who had the place before me lived on Bourget now.

I found her because the mail kept coming. I brought it over.

She was seventy and had a cat with one ear.

She asked me what I was paying. I said eleven ninety.

She said six ten, twelve years.

She said it the way you say a phone number.

She said her husband died in the back room, by the window.

I said I was sorry. She said don't be, it was the good room.

Then she asked my name and I said Renée.

She said Renée, listen, don't take the two thousand.

I took the two thousand.

## 6. Griffintown, January

I cleaned units for cash. Ninety a unit, four a day when there were four.

Glass buildings, wind off the canal, no grocery store. Everyone ordered.

The keys were in lockboxes and the codes were all somebody's birthday.

I learned what people leave. Chargers, mostly. Contact lenses. Once a wedding ring, once a gun.

I put the ring in the drawer and said nothing about the gun.

There was a unit on Ottawa Street that got booked maybe twice a month.

I started sleeping there Tuesdays. I changed the sheets after.

I told myself it was one night and then it was the winter.

I lay in a stranger's bed on the eleventh floor with the heat at twenty-three.

Heat included. Lights included.

I have never in my life been that warm.

I want to say I was ashamed. I was not ashamed.

## 7. Mile End, March

My mother has the same apartment on Waverly since 1994.

Three hundred then. Nine forty now, because he never renovated and never died.

She has the good windows and the bad knees.

She made tea and did not drink it, which is her way.

She asked if I was seeing anyone. I said sort of.

She said sort of is fine, sort of is most of it.

We looked out at the schoolyard for a while.

She said she fucked a man in that stairwell in 1994.

She said his name was Denis and he sold speakers.

She said it like weather. Then she asked if I wanted soup.

I said yes to the soup.

I sat in her kitchen and understood nothing new about her.

I just liked her more, sitting there, in the good windows.

## 8. Villeray, June

Sixteen fifty-five, third floor, a balcony the size of a door.

The smoke came in the second week of June and stayed eleven days.

Orange light in the afternoon. They said stay in, close everything.

I closed everything and it came in anyway, faintly, tasting like a campfire two streets over.

I met a man at the taps on Villeray who fixed elevators.

His name was Ahmed and he had opinions about buildings.

He said the ones from the sixties were honest and the new ones were not.

He stayed over twice. He left his socks the second time, on purpose I think.

I have not decided about him.

On the Sunday the air cleared and everybody came outside at once.

The whole street, on the balconies, like something had been announced.

Nothing had been announced. The air was just clean.

That night I opened the listings on my phone, out of habit, not need.

Chauffé, éclairé. Nine hundred. Rosemont, near the tracks.

I wrote to ask if it was still available.
