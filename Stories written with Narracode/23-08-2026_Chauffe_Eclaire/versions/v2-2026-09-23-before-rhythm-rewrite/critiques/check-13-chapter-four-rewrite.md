## Numbers that survived, and why
Twelve sentences carry one. Each was judged by eye, not by threshold: the clarinet at seven
and the apology at eight (a real annoyance, clocked daily); *the same three lines* (the NPC's
loop — the perception itself); *fifty-one, married, two daughters* (how a woman describes a
man to herself); *about sixty, in a red anorak*; *two men took charge*. None is an invoice.

## What is new and load-bearing
- **The tavern woman.** The research is now shown on a screen rather than summarised as a
  dataset, and she says the same three lines *while they are talking* — which is the chapter's
  whole subject arriving as scenery.
- **The patch notes read aloud in bed, in the voice people use for horoscopes.** Sex, research
  and comedy in one sentence.
- **The dentist's chair.** The bib on its alligator clip, the suction going dry against the
  cheek, the taste of the powder on the glove, his forearm across her collarbone. The erotics
  are now in the equipment, where they belong.
- **The two mutenesses.** *You cannot answer* in the chair; *I could not answer that either*
  when Blaž asks what she is tired from. Same condition, different cause, unremarked.
- **The fatigue is physical now.** A pan goes over and she hears heels on linoleum. She keeps
  tasting the glove. She flinches at the tram. Nothing is diagnosed.

## Three checks left failing, deliberately

semicolon tail over length. All three are ignored.

The mean is 0.1 over a threshold I invented. The chapter does not want semicolon chains; it
wants perception, and chains are a device for introducing people quickly, which this chapter
does not need to do. The flagged tail — *She could not be given a wife; the option was in the
menu and returned an error, and he kept a folder of the errors* — reads correctly; the rule it
breaks was derived from character introductions and does not apply.

Per registry class 32: a green report licenses nothing, and a red one is not an order.
Adjusting good prose to satisfy a number would be the original fault wearing a new hat.
