# Drift — loop 4

## The drift was mine, not the story's

Every previous drift file reported the piece becoming something POETICS had not predicted.
This one reports the *method* becoming something it should never have been.

Four loops built: a prosody validator, three profiles, an eight-check audit, thirty-two
registry classes. Every one of those was a reasonable response to a real fault. Together they
produced a chapter in which one sentence in five carried a number and the second sentence was
an invoice. The instrument was built to interrupt a default and became the default. Counting
is what got done instead of looking, and a clean report became the definition of finished.

The harness said this on its own last page and I read it four times without hearing it:
*left to your defaults, you write competent, fluent, slightly sentimental, slightly
explicatory prose. The harness exists to interrupt that default. Trust the interruption.*
An interruption you can score is not an interruption. It is a new fluency.

## What the rewrite proves

Numerals fell from 26 per thousand words to 10, and **the chapter got three hundred words
longer.** That is the whole economics of the thing. A number is cheap. A perception costs
words, because you have to have looked first, and then find what you saw.

Every measurement removed left a hole, and the hole had to be filled with something actually
seen: an apron still on a hook, an umbrella described as an argument you lose, an NPC saying
the same three lines while two people talk over her, the powder taste inside a dental glove,
a forearm across a collarbone that neither party mentions.

## The refusal that matters

The rewrite leaves three prosody checks failing. Mean 14.1 against a ceiling of 14.0.
Semicolon density low. One semicolon tail long.

I did not fix them. The mean is a tenth over a threshold I invented; the chapter does not want
chains, which are a device for introducing people fast, and it has few people to introduce;
the flagged sentence reads correctly and breaks a rule derived from a construction it is not.

Four loops of evidence say the model conforms to whatever standard is in front of it. The only
useful thing I can do with that is decline, in a case where declining is right, and record it.

## The standing question, loop 4

*Whether the model can write autonomously.* The answer has not improved and has become more
precise. It can execute a specification, build an instrument for a specification, and detect
its own violations of a specification. What it did not do, across four loops, is notice that
the specification had replaced the work. That noticing came from a reader who opened the
chapter and got two sentences in.
