# Drift — loop 3, after chapter 4

## What the project became

It stopped being a story with a validator attached and became **an instrument with a story
attached.** Four chapters now stand at four measured profiles, none retrofitted, and the
tooling has crossed from measuring prosody to auditing attention. That was the prompter's
explicit instruction this round — *not rule-based, but a bunch of checks* — and it changed
what the harness is for.

## The finding of this loop, stated plainly

Six registry classes were written at the start of this loop, from the prompter's line-notes.
**Three of them were violated in the first draft written immediately afterwards**, by the
model that had just written them:

- class 22 (authorial-intent hedge): two fresh instances, *I want to be accurate* and *I want
  to be exact about my own conduct*.
- class 26 (numeric clustering): *four* × 10, *one* × 9, *two* × 6, *eleven* × 5 — the same
  failure as chapter 3, in the same shape.
- class 23 (static motif): a seventh *charge* repeating the money sense already used.

None of them was caught by knowing the rule. All of them were caught by running the audit.

**Instruction did not transfer; instrumentation did.** Writing a class into a registry
documents a fault. It does not prevent the fault, even minutes later, even in the model that
wrote the entry. A check that runs over the text does prevent it, because the check does not
have to remember and does not have a stake in the sentence.

This is the third loop in which the same asymmetry has appeared in a different costume:
loop 1, the model could not tell its own measurement was wrong without a photograph; loop 2,
the scan located defects and defended them; loop 3, the model wrote the rules and then broke
them. The consistent shape is that **self-knowledge is available and self-correction is not,
unless something external holds the standard.**

## Drift in the prose

The register has loosened four times and each loosening came from outside. Telegraphic →
road → swirl → didion. Chapter 4 is the first where terseness was a *result* rather than a
starting constraint: mean 11.5 with a 46-word sentence in it, which is not the same object as
chapter 1's mean 6.3 with a ceiling of 14.

Sex, having been put in the engine in loop 2, has settled: chapter 4 runs two relationships
concurrently without adjudicating, which is the first time the story has declined a moral
position it was set up to take.

## Drift not present
No lyricism. §25 reports the seizure and interprets nothing. The liquorice is never explained.
The fatigue is never diagnosed. The gun stays where it was left fifteen months ago.

## Open risk
Four profiles is arguably three too many. A reader moving through the piece is reading an
instrument being built, and the prompter's instruction to freeze each chapter is what makes
that legible rather than merely inconsistent. Whether the finished thing wants a unifying
pass is his call and has not been asked for.
