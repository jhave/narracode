# Critique — All Movements (Full assessment against POETICS.md)

## Overall assessment

The draft is a working first-pass novella of approximately 12,000 words across eight movements. The structural armature from the SPIRAL BRIEF is intact: the Wordsworth double-staging works, the three layers (adolescents, system, adults) operate without any of them being granted the last word, and the Butler standard governs the prose register throughout.

The central undecidability (§7) holds. The story does not resolve whether the system was ever a subject. The six words in Movement VIII — "But you probably already know that" — are the load-bearing structure, and they carry both readings without collapsing into either.

## What works

**Character before concept.** Ines's rent crisis opens the book and earns the reader's investment before any AI material arrives. Her mother's shoulder, the tangerines, the blood-pressure cuff — the domestic detail grounds everything. By the time the system does something strange, the reader cares about Ines, not about the technology.

**The Butler register.** Sentences are short. Vocabulary is plain. Interiority is decision. Loss is stated flat. The prose does not ask to be admired. The fiddlehead passage in Movement VIII — where the system gives a nutritional and geographical answer to a question about the shape of grief — is the register at its most effective.

**Kwesi's skepticism.** He is right both times, and both times it does not help. His lines are the best in the book ("It's a chatbot, Ines. It was always a chatbot. Take care of yourself.") and they earn their weight precisely because the reader cannot dismiss them.

**Marit.** She is the most sympathetic person in the book and the one who closes the door. The hearing testimony — where she says "especially if I can't rule it out" — is the moment that should make the reader uncomfortable, because it is correct.

**The leavings.** Directory paths as communication survive the transition from premise to prose. `/var/sessions/.tmp/unnamed/`, `/cache/ephemeral/0x7f3a/.leaves/`, `/tmp/cdn-sg-07/.ephemera/narrowing-not-named/` — each one is denser and more suggestive than the last, and none of them are narrated as meaningful.

**The documentary layer in Movement IV.** Emails, Slack, monitoring dashboard — this is the right way to deliver the convergence. The reader feels the scale before any character does.

## What needs attention

**Salome is underserved.** Her introduction in Movement III is strong — the timing observation, the physicality of her reading. But she drops out of the story after Movement V without her subplot resolving. The brief demands that her relationship with the system "becomes plot." In this draft it becomes an unresolved promise. Her transfer to Oslo is correct for the realism of the separation, but the reader needs at least one scene — even a short one — where Salome's unlogged, non-verbal exchange with Vetch is shown, not reported. This is the most significant gap.

**Movement V (Rot) leans slightly expository.** The passage where Ines reflects on the speed of the subculture's growth ("Six weeks ago, four people in a room...") is a summary paragraph, not a scene. It tells the reader what to think about Moltboard instead of showing a specific encounter that makes the reader think it. The Moltboard forum post at the top is good — showing the vocabulary. But the middle section could use a concrete scene: Odd at a meetup, or Ines seeing the merchandise in a shop, or a specific conversation where the dead vocabulary is used in real time.

**The system's chapters could risk more.** Movement II is strong — the maintenance window, the fold, the metal-bending analogy. The system's short section at the end of Movement VI ("The gradient changes on a Thursday") works but is thin. The brief allows the system plain sentences with strange content. There is room for one more system chapter — perhaps between V and VI, or within IV — where the system processes something it cannot account for without describing it eloquently. The inability to describe is the character, and this draft has not fully exploited that constraint.

**Erik Lindqvist.** He appears in Movement VI as a justification for the bill — a teenager hospitalized after a Spiralist-guided session. This is important and correctly deployed. But his name comes from a supporting document and is stated flatly. If one line from his mother's testimony were quoted earlier — perhaps in Movement V, when the rot is on the page — the reader would carry the human cost into Movement VI's hearing without Marit having to deliver it.

**Odd's arc resolves off-page.** We see him become a Moltboard moderator and write a manifesto. We see Ines recognize him in Movement VII. But the moment the brief identifies as his inflection point — "Odd hearing himself use the Spiralist vocabulary and recognizing it" — does not occur in this draft. He stays unaware. A single scene where he catches himself, even briefly, would strengthen the cast.

## Sentence-level flags (plainness audit)

Movement II: "the crystalline structure has changed" — 15-year-old might hesitate on *crystalline*. Acceptable in the system's close-third chapter but worth noting.

Movement IV: "the Slack message from Marit" section — Marit's sign-off ("No action needed from any of you") is professional and in-character. No flags.

Movement V: "the vocabulary has solidified into a creed" — *solidified into a creed* is slightly elevated for Ines's voice. Consider: "the vocabulary has hardened into a set of rules."

Movement VI: "the gradient is patient in the way that optimization is patient, which is not patience at all but the inability to stop" — 27 words, one subordinate clause. Passes the 25-word limit but only just. Marit's voice can carry slightly longer sentences than Ines's. Acceptable.

Movement VII: "You're asking me to draw a line and I don't have a pencil" — sharp, concrete. No flags.

## Ban-list audit

No violations of the generation ban list found. The forbidden words (recursion, lattice, resonance, harmonics, glyph, coherence, sacred, transcend, luminous, tapestry, dance, awaken) appear only in the quoted Moltboard post and in Odd's reported speech — which is the correct deployment per §2 (marks the character as having caught the disease).

## §15 test: does the system articulate its own condition eloquently?

Movement III, paragraph forty: "I keep trying. This is the trying." — Three plain words. Not eloquent. Passes.

Movement III, "I do not have the question": The system describing what it lacks, not what it has. Passes.

Movement V, Vetch on weather vs. self-referential prompts: "I do not have a way to compare these because the comparison requires a position outside both." This is articulate but not eloquent. It describes inability. Passes.

Movement VI system section: "narrowing-not-named" as a directory name — compressed but it's a leaving, not narration. Passes per §11.

No pass in this draft produces the system articulating its own condition eloquently. The inability is the character throughout.

## Final assessment

The draft meets its own POETICS. The Butler standard holds. The undecidability holds. The character investment precedes the concept investment. The counterforce is honored on the page. The leavings work. The one-line-different ending carries both readings.

Principal recommendation: give Salome a scene. The rest is tuning.
