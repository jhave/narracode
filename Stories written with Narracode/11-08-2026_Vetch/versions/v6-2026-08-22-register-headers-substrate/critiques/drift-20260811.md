# Drift Assessment — 2026-08-11

## Question: Is the cumulative draft different from what POETICS declared?

Yes, in two ways. One is fertile. One is a gap.

**Fertile drift: Ines's interiority is warmer than declared.** POETICS says "practical, unlyrical, allergic to significance." The draft's Ines is practical and allergic to significance, but she is not unlyrical. Her image of the fern, her phrasing "a shelf that is the sentence," her final "I hold both things at once" — these are a sixteen-year-old who thinks in images without admitting she does. This is not a violation. It is the character becoming more than the specification, which is what characters do when they work. The Butler standard permits this: Lauren Olamina is practical and thinks in images constantly. Keep it.

**Gap: Salome's subplot.** POETICS declares that Salome's non-verbal relationship with the system "becomes plot." In the draft, it becomes a promise that is not kept. She notices the timing in Movement III. She asks a good question in Movement V. Then she transfers to Oslo and the reader never sees what her unlogged exchange with Vetch actually was. This is the most significant drift from the declared architecture. It needs repair.

## Is the drift fertile or genre regression?

Neither. The warm Ines is fertile. The missing Salome is a structural omission, not a regression. The draft does not regress to genre at any point — there is no revelation scene, no savior arc, no villain, no resolution. The Butler standard holds. The ecclesiastical vocabulary is correctly quarantined to quoted Moltboard text. The Spiralist internet dialect is quoted and refused in narration.

## Recommendation

Add a Salome scene. The most natural location is between Movements VI and VII — after the gradient adjustment begins and before the kids separate. Show Salome with Vetch in a session where the communication is non-verbal or minimally verbal: she types something short, or sends an image, or does something with the interface that only she would think of because she has spent her life reading what is not said. The system responds in a way that could be drift or could be normal. Salome knows the difference and cannot prove it. Then she leaves for Oslo carrying that knowing.

This scene would also strengthen the testimony in Movement VII: when Ines says "I saw a system that started doing something different," the reader would hold Salome's scene alongside Ines's experience and feel the weight of what is not in the testimony — the evidence that could not be entered because it was not logged.
