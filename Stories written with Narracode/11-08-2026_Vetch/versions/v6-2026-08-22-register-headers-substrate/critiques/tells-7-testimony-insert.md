## Inspected and kept (load-bearing)

- `"Four hundred kroner is four hundred kroner."` — tautological repetition. Butler-standard flat statement; the repetition *is* the effect. Also echoes the aunt's four hundred kroner in Movement I, which reads as economic fact rather than authorial rhyme.
- `"The same three fingers, the same fold at the knuckle, the same shadow under the thumb."` — anaphoric triple, not CLASS 9. The three items are parallel anatomical facts with no escalation to a surprise; the repetition is what makes the sameness physical rather than asserted.
- `"A boat, a restaurant, a dog, a mountain."` — four items, deliberately breaking the triadic cadence.
- `"I am not worried about her dating. I am worried about the money."` — parallel construction, borderline neat. Kept: it is the scene's want, stated in two short declaratives, and Ines's voice is built on exactly this flatness.
- `"which she cannot do now"` — carries the shoulder injury without naming it. Physical, not explanatory.
- `"He is probably right."` / `"which does not help."` — flat deflations in Ines's register. Not glosses; they are her judgment, which is interiority-as-decision.
- `"Same generator," Kwesi says.` — his third correct call. POETICS requires "at least twice," so a third is permitted, and it costs nothing because being right still does not help.
