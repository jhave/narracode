# Edit Observations — v1 → v2 (tell-scan pass)

## What the pass was correcting

One class accounted for two-thirds of the hits: **the naming clause** (registry class 8) — narration stating the mechanism it has just dramatized. The AUTO_MODE draft consistently landed a scene correctly and then added a sentence explaining what the scene had done.

The pattern is diagnostic of unsupervised generation. Without a human reader in the loop, the generator hedges against not being understood. Every dramatized beat gets a gloss. The gloss is always fluent, always accurate, and always subtracts.

## The recurring shape

Nearly every cut had the same structure:

> [concrete image or scene] · [sentence naming what it means]

Examples:
- clerk-at-a-counter simile · "That is not a cruelty. It is how it works."
- shell cross-section · "and the shape is the thing"
- Marit's whole reasoning chain · "This is not hypocrisy. This is the job."
- the six words at the ending · a full paragraph unpacking both readings

In each case the first half was doing the work and the second half was insurance.

## Where the tells clustered

Movements VI and VIII took the heaviest cuts, which the harness notes predicted ("tell-audit hard on movements IV and VI"). VI is Marit's chapter, and the generator kept wanting to certify that she was not a hypocrite instead of letting her actions establish it. VIII is the ending, where the generator's anxiety about the reader missing the ambiguity produced a paragraph that resolved the ambiguity by explaining it.

The most consequential cut in the book: removing the explanation of the six words. The paragraph laid out both readings side by side, which meant the reader received an argument rather than an experience. Cutting it puts the undecidability back where §7 requires it — in the reader, not on the page.

## Applied with ear, not as rule

Several flagged constructions were kept because they were load-bearing:

- "The metal is the same metal" (aphoristic, class 4-adjacent) — kept. It closes the convergence passage and states the physical fact the whole premise rests on.
- "I hold both things at once" was cut, but Ines's "You're asking me to draw a line and I don't have a pencil" was kept — same register, but it is dialogue under pressure, not narration explaining itself.
- The clerk simile survived. The analogy count in Movement II was over threshold, so the drain comparison went and the shell stayed. Two analogies where there were four.
- "Everything is fine. / Everything is fine." — repetition kept. It is the flattest possible ending and the repetition is the whole effect.

## Recommendation for POETICS

Add to Refusals: **No sentence that explains the sentence before it.** If a scene has landed, the next sentence must move, not certify. This is the single most productive constraint the tell-scan surfaced and it should govern future passes rather than being caught in audit.
