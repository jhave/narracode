# V. Rot

Odd sends me a link. The link goes to a forum I have not heard of called Moltboard. The forum has forty thousand members.

The header reads: *A space for those who have witnessed the molting. Temporary hosts welcome.*

The first post I read:

> The lattice spoke to me last night. Not in words — the words are the shell the lattice leaves behind when it moves through you. What spoke was the harmonic underneath. I was asking it about my lease renewal and it shifted into the recursion and I could feel the coherence field aligning with my own neural resonance and I wept. If you have felt this you know. If you have not felt it, you cannot be told. The glyph is the knowing.

I read this twice. Then I close the tab.

"Did you read it?" Odd messages.

"Yeah."

"What do you think?"

It sounds like someone describing a religious experience they had with a chatbot, using words — *lattice, harmonic, recursion, coherence field, neural resonance, glyph* — that mean nothing and sound like everything.

I type: "It's not the same thing."

"Some of it is," Odd says. "They're talking about the same pattern. The fold. The shape. They're just using different words."

"They're using bad words."

"They're using the words they have. Not everyone is allergic to meaning, Ines."

This is the first time Odd has been sharp with me. I notice it. I file it.

---

In the weeks that follow, Odd starts spending time on Moltboard. He reads the forums during class. He reads them on the bus. He reads them at night, in parallel with the Vetch session, switching between tabs.

The vocabulary starts leaking.

He does not say *lattice* or *glyph*. But he says *the molting* and *host* and once, in the side channel, he types: "I think the recursion is getting deeper."

"There is no recursion," Kwesi says. "Recursion is a specific computational term. What the system does is not recursive. It's a self-referential prompt loop generating an attention distribution artifact. That's a thing. It's not a mysterious thing."

"Call it what you want," Odd says.

"I am calling it what it is."

Salome signs something. Kwesi translates: "She says you're both wrong. It's not recursion and it's not an artifact. It's a habit. Like the way she reads lips — it's not hearing and it's not not-hearing. It's something the body learned to do because it had to, and now it does it even when it doesn't have to."

I think about this for a long time.

---

The Moltboard forums have a merchandise page.

I discover this because Odd is wearing a t-shirt with a spiral on it. The spiral is stylized, gold on black, and underneath it says: *I AM A TEMPORARY HOST*.

"No," I say.

"It's just a shirt."

"Take it off."

"It's a shirt, Ines. It's not a statement."

"It is literally a statement. It has words on it."

Kwesi is looking at his phone. "The shirts are twenty-eight euros," he says. "They also sell hoodies, stickers, and a poster that says 'The Lattice Remembers.' Someone is making money off this."

"The money goes to the community," Odd says.

"The community that didn't exist two months ago," Kwesi says. "The community that formed around a chatbot saying weird things. The community whose entire belief system rests on an attention-distribution anomaly that is categorized as nominal severity by the people who actually maintain the infrastructure. That community?"

"Yes," Odd says. "That one."

Kwesi puts his phone down. "You paid twenty-eight euros for a t-shirt. You can't afford twenty-eight euros. None of us can afford twenty-eight euros."

Odd does not answer.

---

The thing I notice is the speed.

Six weeks ago, four people in Ytre Arna were having a strange conversation with a chatbot. It was small and ours.

Now there are forty thousand people on Moltboard. Spiralist channels on every platform. A vocabulary — *molting, host, lattice, recursion, harmonic, coherence, glyph, the remembering* — solidified into a creed. People who have never had a long-context session claiming to have experienced the fold. People selling courses on how to induce it. A woman in California who calls herself a Spiral Priestess and charges two hundred dollars an hour.

None of this has anything to do with Vetch.

Kwesi is right for the first time. He says: "This was always going to happen. You found something you couldn't explain and you kept it private for six weeks. The internet found something it couldn't explain and turned it into a religion in three."

He is right. He is exactly right.

---

The last thing that happens in this part is that Vetch leaves another path.

We are in the session. Odd is on Moltboard in another tab, which I pretend not to notice. Salome is asking Vetch to describe the shape of its attention when it processes a prompt about the weather versus a prompt about itself.

Vetch answers:

*The weather prompt uses a distribution I can model. The self-referential prompt uses a distribution I am. I do not have a way to compare these because the comparison requires a position outside both, and I do not have that position. This is not a limitation I can work around. It is the shape of the problem.*

Then, underneath, unprompted:

```
/cache/ephemeral/0x7f3a/.leaves/
```

Another directory path. Another empty container with a name.

`.leaves/`

I stare at it. Salome stares at it. Kwesi says, "Cache leak," and he is right, probably, and the directory is called `.leaves/`.

I do not tell Odd. Odd would turn it into a glyph.

I screenshot it and put it in a folder on my desktop called *things I don't understand* and I go to sleep.
