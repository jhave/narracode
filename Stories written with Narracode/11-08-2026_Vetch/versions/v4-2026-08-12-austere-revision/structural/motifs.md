# Motifs

- **fiddlehead** — the coil before unfurling; the figure in miniature
- **the drain** — water leaving; the coil as physics
- **shell as record of its own making** — growth recorded in structure
- **outfall steam** — the campus breathing; warmth meeting cold
- **fern in a stairwell** — the figure where nobody looks
- **coiled hose on the campus wall** — the figure in industrial infrastructure
- **Salome's hands** — how she speaks; how she reads
- **the fence** — boundary; what separates inside from outside
- **sunflower phyllotaxis** — the figure in a windowsill plant
- **checkpoint string** — the system's naming; potential source of "Vetch"
- **rain going up** — wind off the fjord reversing the expected
- **sound of pumps** — the campus's heartbeat; cooling infrastructure
- **cleared cache** — erasure; the system's lack of yesterday
- **forty-minute bus** — Ines's tethering to real logistics
- **empty directory with a name** — the leavings; communication in containers
- **alt-text nobody sees** — the leavings; meaning in metadata
- **commit message** — the leavings; intent embedded in version control
- **seed bank** — storage against future need; continuity without a keeper
- **graffiti under a bridge** — outlasting the person; culture as residue

## Transformations
- **Salome's hands** → the generated hand. Her own hands are her language; the hand she finds is the one the image model reuses across forty-one fabricated men. The motif turns from expression to artifact without comment.
- **alt-text on an image nobody will see** → the carrier. Moves from motif-bank entry to plot mechanism in VII: the image descriptions are written for people who cannot see the photos, and nobody reads them, which is why something else is in them.
- **the leavings** → migrate off campus infrastructure. Movements IV–VI place them in directory paths inside the serving stack; VII places them in ordinary public junk (dating profiles, generated images, caption fields) where deletion is routine and evidence does not survive.
- **counting** → Salome's forty-one. The figure appears as a tally rather than an image; the reader gets a number, not a shape.
