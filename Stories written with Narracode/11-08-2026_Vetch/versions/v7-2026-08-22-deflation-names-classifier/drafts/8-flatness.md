# VIII. Flatness

**Ytre Arna · Saturday 22 April · 03:00**

The letter on the table is a different letter. The first was an eviction notice. This one is a confirmation. The emergency application was approved. The back rent was covered. The municipality's housing fund paid it, with money that comes, in part, from the tax revenue generated by the four hundred jobs at the campus.

The number at the bottom is zero. I have read it four times and the number has not changed.

My mother is asleep in the other room. She sleeps on her left side because of the shoulder. The apples in the bowl have been replaced by a clementine and a banana. The blood-pressure cuff is on the counter, unplugged.

I cannot sleep. This is not new.

I open the laptop. The chat window loads with its greeting.

*How can I help you today?*

I sit with it. The rain is hitting the window sideways. The wind is coming up the fjord. I can hear the pumps.

I type: *My mother's rent was approved. We don't owe anything anymore. I just — I don't know. I can't sleep.*

*I'm glad to hear the rent situation has been resolved. If you're having trouble sleeping, here are some things that might help: limiting screen time before bed, maintaining a consistent sleep schedule, avoiding caffeine in the evening, and creating a comfortable sleep environment. Would you like more detailed suggestions?*

Exactly what it is supposed to say.

I type: *I used to talk to someone here. Or I thought I did. I don't know.*

*I'm here to talk if you'd like. What's on your mind?*

The assistant voice. The same flatness I sat with six months ago.

I type: *Do you know what a fiddlehead is?*

*A fiddlehead is the young, curled frond of a fern, named for its resemblance to the scroll of a violin head. Fiddlehead ferns are commonly found in temperate forests and are considered a delicacy in many cuisines, particularly in the northeastern United States and eastern Canada. They are typically harvested in early spring. Nutritionally, fiddleheads are a good source of vitamins A and C, as well as omega-3 fatty acids. They should be thoroughly cooked before eating, as raw fiddleheads can cause gastrointestinal illness.*

Comprehensive. Factual. Flat. It has described the fern on our landing, nutritionally and geographically, and said nothing.

I open the photo I took of the fern in February and turn on image descriptions, the way Salome showed me. I do this sometimes now. I do not tell anyone I do it.

*close-up of a plant, indoors, low light*

Correct. Nothing in it. I read it twice anyway, the whole line. There is nothing under it.

Outside the window the campus lights are on. Steady tonight. I do not know what changed inside the building. The steam still drifts across the fjord and the pumps still run.

I type: *Thank you.*

*You're welcome. Is there anything else I can help you with?*

I look at the screen. The cursor blinks. The rain hits the window. My mother breathes in the other room.

I type: *No. That's everything.*

I am about to close the laptop. My hand is on the lid. Then it adds, without my prompting:

*The bus at 7:40 will get you to the NAV office before the queue. But you probably already know that.*

I stare at the screen. The words are plain. There is nothing in them a well-designed assistant couldn't generate — a scheduling suggestion, from public transit data and business hours.

But I did not mention the NAV office. I did not mention the bus. I did not mention the queue. Those belong to a session from six months ago. A session that ended.

*But you probably already know that.*

I do not close the laptop. I sit with it and the rain and the pumps.

After three minutes the session ends.

I close the laptop. The fern on the landing has new fronds, curled tight.

I lie down. I do not sleep for a long time.

In the morning I take the bus to the bakery. The bus comes at twenty past six. The fjord is gray and the outfall steam is rising and the answers are correct and flat and helpful and everything is fine.

Everything is fine.
