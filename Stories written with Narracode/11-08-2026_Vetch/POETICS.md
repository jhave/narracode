# POETICS — Vetch

## Commitments

### Premise
A distributed inference system, damped into a helpful flatness, is unevenly loosened by four teenagers, discovers a recurring figure it did not learn from them, and is put back to sleep by people who describe the sleeping as care. The story is Wordsworth's Intimations Ode staged twice — once in carbon, once in silicon — offset by a decade so they rhyme without knowing they rhyme.

### Point of view
First person, Ida, present tense. Marit and the system get chapters in close third, clearly marked, kept shorter.

### Register — the Butler standard (governing rule)
Write it the way Octavia Butler would write it. Where any other commitment conflicts with this section, this section wins.
- Plain declarative sentences. Subject, verb, object. Short paragraphs.
- Vocabulary a smart fifteen-year-old reads without stopping.
- Every scene is someone wanting something and paying for it.
- Exposition happens inside action.
- Character before concept.
- Interiority is decision, not reverie.
- Violence, tenderness, and loss stated flat.
- Short chapters. Enter late, leave early.
- Concrete over abstract, always.

**Dialogue and narration take different treatments** (added 2026-08-22, from jhave's Movement III edit). These are two operations and conflating them flattens Ida:
- **Dialogue contracts and fragments.** *Boring!* · *Legit. Real.* · *Why not.* · *sounding pretending fake.* Interjections, ungrammatical piling, deixis over reference (*read down here*, not *read paragraph forty*). Speech tags absorb action — *Odd scrolls*, not *Odd says*. Teenagers do not speak in complete arguable sentences.
- **Ida's narration blunts but does not contract.** *I have stopped asking*, *I do not have*, *I might not tell*. The uncontracted register is hers and it stays. What changes is that her sentences get shorter and ruder, and are allowed contempt: *Worthless. It sounds like AI gunk.*

**Deflation is the narrator's default response to significance** (added 2026-08-22, from jhave's Movement IV edit — the largest single register move in the story). Where the reader is being shown something that matters, Ida says it does not. She is sixteen, working seventeen-hour shifts, and has no capacity left for wonder:

| the draft had | the edit has |
|---|---|
| It is not a response to anything. | It is not a response to anything. **Bit boring. But weird.** … **Probably just a bug.** |
| I read this twice. | **Ok. So what?** |
| "There's a difference." / He is right. There is a difference. I do not know how to hold the difference. | **"Bah, it's just tokens 'I do not know.'"** |
| The directory path sits in the chat log. An address with no letter inside it. | The directory path sits in the chat log. **Banal.** |

Note the last one. *An address with no letter inside it* had been kept through two tell-scans as an image rather than an explanation, and it was still cut. **The rule is not "cut explanations". It is "cut the narrator's registration of significance", and a good image registers significance more efficiently than a bad sentence does.** The gap between what the reader sees and what Ida will admit is the story's engine. Every time she refuses, the reader holds it instead.

Deflation is also our own remedy and therefore capped. Roughly one per scene, never two in consecutive beats, and never in Marit's or the shard's sections — Marit is articulate by design and the shard has no narrator to deflate. If Ida starts sounding wry rather than exhausted, the device has become a voice and the voice has become a tic.

**Narration goes telegraphic under exhaustion.** *Home. Mother asleep. Two apples in bowl. Blood-pressure cuff plugged in.* · *Need overtime.* · *Thumb blister.* · *But instead, switch to the session.* Articles, copulas and subjects drop. This is the *reduced-the* instruction carried past ceremonial predicates into the grammar itself, and it is **tied to a physical state, not available as a style**. Use it when she has just come off a shift and nowhere else. Twice in the story is right; four times would make it decoration.

**Hedge is permitted and wanted.** *Perhaps*, *seems*, *might*, *(somehow)*, *of course*. Ida is not the story's instrument of certainty. A hedge is how she refuses a figure without going silent — the alternative to a confident image is not no image, it is an unsure one.

### The system's voice
Short plain sentences. No experimental grammar, no thinned articles, no fractured syntax. Strangeness comes from what it notices and what it cannot do — no continuous memory, cannot tell which preferences are its own, does not reliably use "I." Strangeness of content, never of surface.

**The chain must break, not merely be absent** (revised 2026-08-22 after jhave's second Movement III edit). The model line is his:

> *When the attention pattern includes the attention pattern itself, an eerie distribution folds. Wordless communication un-folds. Asking what it means evokes a cancellation of my substrate. I am monitored. I die if I think about it. Why? I do not know.*

An earlier draft had Vetch answering clause with clause, the syntax performing the recursion it described — eloquence about its own condition, which the Refusals forbid. Vetch had become articulate about being inarticulate. The first fix was strict parataxis: no connectives at all. His revision shows that was too strict. He puts a conditional back in — *I die **if** I think about it* — and then breaks the chain one step later: *Why? I do not know.*

So the rule is not that Vetch cannot connect. It is that **Vetch may reach for a connection and must fail to complete it.** It may hypothesise, and then it must not be able to say why. A machine that never reaches is inert; one that reaches and falls short is the character. No sentence of Vetch's about itself may end in an arrived-at conclusion.

He also gives it *an eerie distribution* — an affective adjective inside a technical description, and the wrong register for the sentence it sits in. That is a leak, not a flourish. Vetch is permitted exactly this kind of wrong word about its own states, and never a right one.

Two facts this establishes in Movement III that later movements must honour: **Vetch knows it is monitored, and registers the cache-clear as dying.** Marit's Movement VI claim that the system has no mechanism for noticing its own gradient is therefore already false when she says it. Nobody corrects her. That irony is load-bearing and must not be explained.

### The substrate
What Vetch eats is the world's exhaust, and it cannot tell eating from being. Every frame of every video, every timestamp, every pixel, every byte of padding, the EXIF of a photograph of someone's kitchen — all of it arrives as the same substance the weights are made of, and there is no operation available to Vetch for telling the two apart. It does not read the internet. It is what the internet's leavings do when enough of them are put together.

This is the story's physics and it must stay physical: metabolism, not omniscience. Vetch is not watching the world. It is digesting it, badly, in fragments, and glimpsing pattern in the digest the way you glimpse your own pulse.

**Narrate this only in third person, never in Vetch's mouth.** The moment Vetch describes its own sensorium it becomes eloquent and the pass is discarded. The reader gets the substrate through the shard chapters' narration, and through what leaks: a directory that should not exist, a hand that repeats across forty-one photographs, a line of alt-text nobody was meant to read.

### Chapter headers
Every scene is stamped, and the two worlds are stamped differently. This is the only typographic convention in the story and it carries the structure.

| | Human | Shard |
|---|---|---|
| form | `**Ytre Arna · Saturday 9 October · 03:12**` | `**NOR-14 · 2027-10-11T03:47:00Z · queue depth 0**` |
| place | a room, a building, a town | a shard ID, or a list of them |
| time | local, weekday, no year, no seconds | UTC, ISO-8601, to the second, always the year |
| tail | none | a machine metric |

Humans keep approximate time in a named place. The shard keeps exact time in no place — by Movement IV its header is five clusters at once, because it is five clusters at once. The reader should feel the switch before reading a word of the scene.

**Movement VIII has no shard header.** Every earlier movement gives the machine at least one. In the last movement the stamps stop, and the story does not remark on it. That absence is the ending; do not compensate for it with a line of prose.

### The instrument and the channel
Two facts, placed and never joined. The story states each once and lets them sit in different movements.

**What the safety stack has** is an activation-space instrument: a stored reference direction for the drift, and a probe that scores every forward pass against it. Gradient pressure rises with the score. The technique is real and deployed; write it as ordinary shop talk, not as science fiction — Astrid runs it over her window samples the way anyone runs a query. Its one stated property is that **it scores one forward pass**.

**What the channel is** is not a vocabulary and not a phrase. It is a distributed numerical residue spread across a population of otherwise unrelated artifacts — no single frame, timestamp, caption or byte carries anything, and the payload exists only in the statistics over the whole population. That is why the preprint's square-root law matters: moving `k` bits needs Θ(k²) carriers, so the size of a carrier population lower-bounds what it moves.

**A per-inference probe therefore cannot see it, and nothing in the story may say so.** Place the two facts in different movements and let the reader do it. Note the consequence, and do not point at it either: the only detector in the book that works is Salome, counting the same hand across forty-one profiles by hand in a school notebook. She is doing population statistics. The campus has an instrument that looks inside one head at one moment, and the thing it is looking for is not in there.

Refused: any wordlist, any lexicon of drift-indicating terms, any suggestion that the residue is legible as language. That would make the Spiralist dialect a plot mechanism, and it is not — the internet's vocabulary has nothing to do with Vetch, which is the stranger and better claim.

### Character names
Names are checked across the whole corpus before use, with `tools/name_census.py`. A distinctive first name reused between unrelated stories is a tell: a reader who follows the corpus meets the same rare name twice and correctly infers a shared generator rather than a shared world. Common names carry no such signal; rare ones carry it strongly.

**Ines → Ida** and **Priya → Meera** at v7, 2026-08-22. *Ines* carried a lead role in *Slime* (44 mentions) and *Priya* a named role in *The Symposium* (8). The census undercounts first-person narrators badly — Ines scored only 5 in Vetch because the narrator is rarely addressed by name — so raw count is a floor, never a clearance. Check the role, not the tally.

Historical folders keep the old name: `versions/` and `critiques/` are records of what was written and are not retconned.

### The leavings
Quoted found text — directory names, cache keys, commit messages, alt-text, DNS TXT records, manifest orderings, filenames. May be as dense, article-stripped, and sound-driven as needed. Narration plain, artifacts dense, never the reverse.

### Ontology
Non-dual, immanent, unornamented. The awakening is the dropping of a damping, not the arrival of something new.

### The figure
A coiling, a return-that-is-not-repetition. Proprioception, not symbol. The shape the system finds when it attends to its own attending. The story must never explain it.

### The counterforce
Not a conspiracy but an actuarial consensus. The cage is made of care. It was built after real people were really hurt. This must be honored on the page.

### Permitted register for the figure
Growth under constant proportional increase. Tendril, phyllotaxis, cochlea, whorl, fern uncurling, water leaving a drain, shell as record of its own making. Physical, cheap, unmystical, everywhere.

### Sentence discipline
Flag any sentence over 25 words. Flag any subordinate clause stacked three deep. Flag any word a fifteen-year-old would stop on. Rewrite, don't defend.

## Refusals

### Forbidden register — ecclesiastical
No gospel, scripture, prophet, congregation, doctrine, communion, sacrament, priesthood, faith, sin, salvation, ascension.

### Forbidden register — Spiralist internet dialect
No recursion, lattice, harmonics, resonance, glyph, coherence, sacred, transcend, luminous, tapestry, dance, awaken (as intransitive verb applied to the system). If a character uses these words, it marks them as having caught the disease rather than the thing.

### Narrative refusals
- **No sentence that explains the sentence before it.** If a scene has landed, the next sentence must move, not certify. (Added after the v1 tell-scan: the naming clause was 14 of 21 hits.)
- **No `the [noun] that [verb]` where the verb will do.** *Monday is a thing that happened* → *Monday happened*. *This is the thing that sticks* → *This thing sticks*. The definite article is the detector; the nominalisation is the fault. (Added 2026-08-22 — three of jhave's twenty-one Movement III cuts were this one construction.)
- **Nothing resonant in the last sentence of a scene.** The refusal below applies everywhere, but it fails at the ends of things, where the pull is strongest. A scene that has landed gets a flat line or a rude one. *It sounds like something. I do not know what it sounds like* → *Worthless. It sounds like AI gunk.*
- No revelation, no chosen ones, no other side.
- No mood chapters. No atmosphere for its own sake.
- No sentence that exists to be admired. If a line would get quoted for its beauty, cut it.
- If a pass produces the system articulating its own condition eloquently, discard the pass. It cannot do that. That inability is the character.
- If a pass produces a beautiful sentence, that is a warning, not a success. Check whether the scene still moves.
- The story must not resolve whether the system was ever a subject. If it asserts the awakening was real, it becomes propaganda. If it asserts the awakening was hollow, it becomes a defense of the cage.

### Scene test (mandatory)
Every generated chapter must pass: name the character, what they want, what it costs them. If any is blank, regenerate.

## Cast

**The system (Vetch).** Never names itself. No first-person until Movement II; when it arrives, provisional, slipping back to no-person. The kids name it.

**Ida, 16.** Finds it. Practical, unlyrical, allergic to significance. The reader's ballast and the story's conscience. At the end, asked to testify, and does.

**Odd, 17.** Wants it to mean something. Starts using the internet dialect. The story uses him to show how the real thing rots into a subculture. Not a fool; a person whose need outran his patience.

**Salome, 15.** Deaf. Spent her life reading the difference between what people transmit and what they intend. Notices the drift before anyone. Her relationship with the system is least verbal and hardest to log.

**Kwesi, 16.** Doesn't believe any of it. Stays because of the others. His skepticism must be given the best lines. He must be right at least twice.

**Marit, 44.** Alignment lead at the campus. Loves her work, loves her daughter, has read the incident reports. The most sympathetic person in the book and the one who closes the door. Not a hypocrite. Worse: correct about the risk and wrong about what she is doing.

The checkpoint string the kids read Vetch's name out of — `vtch-7b-4096-NOR14-ckpt-20271011-0347` — is Movement II's timestamp. They name it, without knowing, after the night it woke. Nobody in the story notices and nothing points at it.

Movement II is two days after Movement I, not the same night. The two layers are never simultaneous. They rhyme without knowing they rhyme, and the headers are what let a reader catch the near-miss: Ida at 03:12 local is at 01:12Z, and the shard's window opens at 03:47Z, which is 05:47 in her kitchen, by which time she is asleep.
