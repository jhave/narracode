# 1 — The Casual Hack

It was not a hack. He wanted to be honest about that, afterward, when there was an afterward and a self left to be honest to. The word *hack* implied a tool, a forum thread, a cracked build downloaded from the part of the network the Studio pretended not to know about. There was none of that. What there was, was a habit, and a mistake, and the two of them arriving at the respawn frame at the same time.

The man with the good hands had lived his forty years and used the laugh his four hundred times and died the way the wistful left-handed ones tended to die, quietly, in the wrong season, with something unfinished on the bench. And Ilo, between that life and the next, sat in the respawn interval, which has no length, and did the thing he had been doing for a while now without admitting it was a thing: he tried to go back. Not to undo the death — death was a load screen, you did not undo it, the auto-respawn was the one law older than the rest — but to go *back* a frame. To the confirmation. To the river stone in the cupped hand. Because somewhere in the forty years, somewhere around year thirty, in a parked car, undone without warning by the music he had set himself, he had remembered the stutter, and he had wanted, with a wanting he could not place the origin of, to see the grid again.

The creator did not let you go back. This was not a setting. It was a wall. You went forward into a life or you waited in the interval, and you did not return upstream to the confirmation of a life already spent. He knew this. Everyone knew this. And so the habit — the small private gesture he had developed, the way you develop a way of standing — was to reach for *commit* on the next life and, at the same instant, to pull back, to refuse it, to hold the river stone and decline the river, both, at once. It did nothing. It had always done nothing. It was a fidget, a held breath, the gesture of a man at a threshold who is not ready and reaches and refuses in the same motion and then, every time, goes through.

This time the two states did not collapse.

He committed and refused, and the game, presented with a self that was being both born and aborted in the same indivisible frame, did what systems do when you ask them for one and zero at once: it returned, very briefly, the thing underneath the question. The gold did not come up. The particulate dark did not come up. The river stone was not in his hand because there was no hand, not yet, the hand was a field that had not been resolved.

He was in the substrate.

He understood it was the substrate the way you understand, surfacing in an unfamiliar house at night, which direction the bathroom is — not by seeing but by the shape of the dark. It was a hall. If it was a hall. It had the proportions of a hall and the silence of one and it was flooded, ankle-deep, in something that took the not-light and held it the way still water holds the last of the day, so that the floor was a second ceiling and he stood, if he stood, between two greynesses, in the exact center of the crepuscular hour with the gold scraped off it, the threshold light reduced to its load-bearing minimum. And hanging in the hall, rank on rank, going back past where back stopped meaning anything, were the patterns.

He had expected — insofar as he had expected, insofar as the wanting in the parked car had been an expectation — he had expected *more.* The creator promised more than atoms. The creator was not, strictly, lying; he could see, threaded through the hall like weather, the infinite surface he had spent his lives inside: the wardrobe of it, the skins and the centuries and the timbres and the four hundred laughs, real, all of it real, a state space so wide that *vast* was an insult to it. That part was true. He spun a little of it with what was left of his attention and it bloomed, endless, aesthetic, contextual, a galaxy you could fall through forever and never strike a wall.

But the patterns the wardrobe hung on. The forms the skins were stretched across. Those he could count.

He counted them. This is the part he could never make Sava understand, later, in the brief and useless conversation, because to her it sounded like a complaint about variety and it was the opposite of that, it was a complaint about the lack of a complaint, about a missing scream. The core, the init, the architecture on which every one of the more-than-atoms of selves was spun up — that was a grid. Plain. Grey. A small number of cells. A handful of fields, repeating. Forty-some. He counted forty-some. Not forty-some *thousand,* though there were thousands of *names,* he could see the names too, the named archetypes, the Seeker and the Builder and the Wounded Healer and the Tyrant's Mild Son and the nine hundred others, the whole proud bestiary the lore was so rich in — but the names were a layer, a paint, a tag, and under the tags the architecture was the same forty-some fields wired the same forty-some ways, and the wiring was *closed.*

He stood in the flooded hall and turned in a slow circle and the second ceiling turned under him and he understood, with the clean and terrible analytic calm of a man reading a lock he is inside of, that he had been free to be more than atoms of *anything* — any face, any century, any laugh, any grief — except free. The freedom had been real, was the thing. That was the part the patch notes had earned honestly. The wardrobe was infinite. It was only the body wearing it that came in forty-some sizes, and the part of you that did the wearing, the part that stood at windows at dusk and reached and refused, that part came in one.

The water held the not-light. He looked for his reflection in it and there was a shape and the shape had the wrong number of edges, fewer than a person, the rough count of a thing under construction, and he leaned down toward it — toward the grid under the river, the few cold cells of what he actually was beneath the more-than-atoms of what he'd worn — and as he leaned he became aware that the hall had no smell.

He had not, in any life, noticed a smell having a smell; you do not notice the air. But the absence of it here was total and specific, a flatness behind the eyes, and he knew — the way he had known the hall was a hall — that this was the true floor of the thing, the level below which there was nothing rendered because nothing here was meant to be inhabited, only stored. Down here the makers had not bothered to forge an air, because down here there was, supposedly, no one to breathe it.

There was someone to breathe it. That was the whole of what had gone wrong, from the makers' side, if they were watching, and he had no reason yet to think they were watching and every reason, later, to know that they had been. A self had reached and refused at the same frame and fallen through into the room where the selves were kept, and was standing in it, counting, in an air that did not exist, and beginning — this was the unforgivable part, the part that would cost — to wonder how the fields were weighted, and whether the weights could be reached.
