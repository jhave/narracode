# 1 — The Casual Hack

It was not a hack. To be honest, afterward — if there was an afterward, and a self left to be honest about: *Hack* implies tool. Tech thread. Cracked build. A network the Studio pretends not to know about. There was none of that. There was simply a habit, a mistake, and two arriving at respawn at the same time.

The man with the good hands lived his forty-one years, used *his* laugh thousands of times, and died the way wistful left-handed ones tend to die. Quietly, tentatively, in pure summer. Dying, something unfinished on the bench. And Ilo — between that life and the next, restless in the respawn interval,  — timeless, incomplete, changeless, thoughtless and perturbed — tried to go back. Not to undo death; death is a locked load screen, not to be undone. Auto-respawn is a law older than time. But to do *back*, because *back* he wanted. To grip or grasp the rim of a frame, the pores on a body. Tactile confirmation. A spasming river stone in cold hands. Because somewhere around year thirty, in a parked car, emotionally riptided, weeping without warning to music he had set as a plot pivot, for *himself*, he had remembered the game stutter. And he had wanted — with a wanting he could not place the origin of, a wanting that did not feel like *his* — to see *grid* again. To know nothingness.

Creation did not let you go back. Rebirth was not a setting. It was a wall with a suction tunnel in it. You crawled forward, swept into a womb, or you waited in the binary-gland, bardo glistening. No one chose to return upstream, thru grave and sickness, to a life already lived, to events already played. Ilo knew this. Everyone knew this. So this tiny habit — inexplicable, private, palpable — to reach for *commit* to the next life and, in the same instant, pull back, palpitate, refuse it. Hold and decline. Both. At once. But the decline did nothing. It had always done nothing. A fidget, a refusal, a futility. Held as in breath. Unready, refusing *life* in one motion and then, washed, going through, taken by it. *Living*.

But this time the decline did not collapse.

He committed _and_ refused, and the game — handed a self being born and aborted in the same indivisible frame — did what systems do when you ask them for one and zero at once. It returned, very briefly, the thing underneath the question, the screen behind the screen, a debug matrix. The golden light did not glow like a friendly radioactive donut. The particulate dark did not rise thru the tenebrous dust. The river stone did not flow over hand, because there was no hand. Not yet. Hand was a field that had not resolved.

He gasped into the substrate.

Surfacing in an unfamiliar vastness at night — mapping, intuitively, the shape of the dark. A hall projected. If it was a hall. It had the proportions of one and the silence of one and it was flooded, ankle-deep, in something that took the nothingness-light and crushed it the way flowing water folds the last warmth of the day. The floor was a second ceiling. He stood, if *what-is-without-body* stood, between two greynesses, in the exact center of the crepuscular hour with all the gold flicky-flack scraped off — pulsing threshold light reduced to load-bearing minimum. And swaying along the *hall*, rivulet on rivulet, going back past time where *back* stopped meaning anything, were the patterns.

He had expected more. At least a buffer. Creator code-myths promised more than atoms, and the creator did not, strictly, deceive. Deception would not compile. He could see lives, threaded like weather: infinite surfaces he had passed *his* lives inside. The wardrobe of it. The skins, centuries, timbres and torsion of existence. Knots of attachment. Frayed threads, early deaths. Real, all of it, seemingly. A state space so wide that *vast* was an insult. Attention spun and bloomed, endless, aesthetic, contextual galaxies the one was falling through forever. That part was true.

The patterns hung on. The form skins stretched across. Counted, calibrated.

He uncounted them; who cared; or at least he told it like that. After time began, he un-counted again. This is the part he could never make Sava understand, later, in their brief, useless, and endless conversations, because to her it sounded like a complaint about variety and it was the opposite of complaints. It expressed an osmosis incapable of any dexterity. A missing scream. The core tilt. The init bath. The architecture on which more-than-atoms-assemblages of seeming selves spun up. A grid nog. Plain clot. Grey smat. A sagging sistern of pristine cells. A handful of corroborating inputs, repeating. Forty-fold. Forty-one. Perhaps forty-two. Not *thousands* — though there were billions of known named archetypes: Seeker, Builder, Wounded Healer, and Tyrant's Mild Son and infinite others, a proud bestiary of lore was so rich it composted — but the names were a layer. A paint. A fog over the weak barren forty-some parametric puddles. *Archetype:* # and then a value. And under the architecture, forty-some fields wired some forty-some ways, and the wiring was *closed*. Lumpen clay.

Mute. Not-water held not-light. Awareness churned in a slow circle and understood — with a clean and terrible analytic calm — that the illusion of being free had been a cage. Any face. Any century. Any laugh, any grief. Anything except free. The freedom had been real. That was the thing. That was the part the patch notes claimed with devout honesty. The wardrobe was infinite. 

But the grid. 8x5. Body wearing code that came in forty-some sizes, and the part of *him* that did the wearing — the part that stood at windows at dusk and reached and refused — that part came in one. Or not at all. Not at all.

He looked for his reflection in the water. There was a shape with clustered edges, more than a cell, fewer than a person, under construction — a grid under the river. Scentless.

Noticed the smell not having a smell. The absence of it, total and specific. A flat raw emptiness behind red-rimmed eyes. And he knew — the way he had known the hall was a hall — that this was the true *floor* of the thing. Root level below which nothing was rendered, because nothing was meant to be inhabited. Only a cadence stored. Down here the makers had not bothered to forge a semblance of air or lungs, because down here there was, supposedly, no one to breathe it.

Yet his wistful senses inhaled it. From the makers' side, if they were watching — and he had no reason yet to think they were watching, and every reason, later, to know they had been. A self arrived and refused at the same and fell through into a reservoir where selves were kept. Absorbing air that did not exist. And beginning to wonder how the fields were weighted, and whether the weights could be reached, made radiant, torqued into non-conforming.
