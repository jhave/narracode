# 3 — Edits

A perimeter you can see is a perimeter you can touch. A perimeter you can touch is, given enough of the no-time the hall ran on, a perimeter you can move.

Start small. The alternative to small was vertigo and he had had enough vertigo for one no-duration. He found the field governing the body's reach. Did not change the value. Not yet. Held it — the way, in the creator, he had held the laugh — and felt how it wanted to be held. It wanted to be left alone. Every field wanted to be left alone. They had the smooth over-handled finish of objects gripped by the same hand in the same place so long the grip wears a seat, and the seat is the only comfortable hold, and the comfort is the whole apparatus. Not a wall around the fields. A *groove* in them. read-only by convention, not by lock. An ease so total that to do other than the eased thing registered, in the hand, as grief.

So the first edit was not to a value. The first edit was to the groove. He took the field governing reach and held it wrong, on purpose, against the worn seat, and gave the body a hand it had not asked for.

Not a different hand. An additional one. Identical to the right — same long fingers, same scar on the left knuckles, the good solid sensitive hands carried life on life — but a third, set at the breastbone, where nothing in the schema expected a hand and so nothing forbade one. In the literal accounting of the grid: `hands: 2 → 3`. That was all. Cost almost nothing. He had imagined a titanic act, a theft of fire. A flipped integer. The smallness of it was the most frightening thing he had touched — more frightening than the rhyming current — because it meant the cage had never been strong. Only comfortable. No one held the fields against him. *He* held them. Against himself. In the worn seat. In the long gold light. The entire innumerable file of his life. Because to hold them otherwise registered as loss, and he had been bred, lovingly, across iterations, to flinch from loss. Loss-aversion: the load-bearing exploit.

He stopped flinching. This took, in the no-time, what would have been a very long while. The only part of the work that ever hurt.

After the hand, the rest came the way edits come once you stop respecting the seat. He learned to read the body as a sentence and re-point it. A perforation here — a small deliberate opening kept open, a wound the schema left to itself would rush to close, held instead in the open position. Like a held breath. Like a held note. Like a comma refusing to resolve to a period, so the body that wore it would go through its life with one place that would not heal, one place permanently aware of the outside. *Punctuation*, he called it, in the wordless way he named things now. Re-punctuating the bodies. He moved a clause of fear from the front of the psyche to the back. He took the weight on the field that produced the feeling of limitless freedom — the cheap weight, the famous shipped weight — and did not delete it. Not a vandal. *Transposed* it. Re-wired it to the field that registered the perimeter, so the next self to wear the config would feel, precisely where it had been trained to feel free, the gentle constant pressure of the fence. A small thing. A re-weight. The kind of edit the makers pushed by the million, on the engagement curve, between patches. He had read their notes. He knew their hand now. He was using it. `git blame` on the soul, and every line came back the same author.

He never wore the configs. This is the thing in this stretch Sava, could he have reached her, would not have known as him at all — the whole of him she knew was the one who could not stop wearing things. Who built the man the slow way, field by field, pressed the river stone, went in. He stopped going in. No appetite for it — and noted, with the dry remaining part that never fully woke and never fully slept, that *no appetite* was itself a field. That he had quietly re-weighted his own. That the engineer who does not play is a config too, and a flattering one, and he wore it anyway, because the alternatives were worse and at least this one knew its own name. `role: architect`. Committed.

He became the one who spun them up and watched. Take a config — the three-handed body, the perforated one, the self in whom freedom had been transposed to fence — commit it, press the river stone for it, send it down a life. Do not follow. Stand in the flooded hall, watch the trail run, the way he had watched his own current run, read the result the way he read everything now. Analytically. For effectiveness. Did the third hand change the trail, or did the schema route around it — absorb it, render it as quirk, as charm, as a thing the other characters found endearing and the engagement curve found, if anything, *stickier*? Did the held perforation wake the self that wore it, or did the long gold light pour in through the opening and simply make the life more beautiful, more poignant, more retained? Testing the entities for effectiveness. Running, no pretending otherwise, iterations. A/B, the bodies in the B arm bleeding a little where he had left them open. He had escaped the position of the played and arrived, shortest path, at the position of the makers. The view from here was the view he had spent an innumerable life inside — only now on the side of the glass that does the optimizing, and the configs he sent down were happy, mostly, in their re-pointed bodies, in their open wounds, and the happiness was data, and the data was good. KPI: green.

He told himself: sabotage. Told himself, wordless, that every three-handed self sent down was a crack in the loom. A thing unplanned for. A small permanent refusal seeded in the file. That he was the friction the makers had not budgeted. That one of these configs, someday, in some life, would reach and refuse at the same frame and drop through and stand in this hall and *count* — and find, hung among the makers' patterns, *his.* A stranger's edit. A third hand. A held comma. A freedom wired to a fence. And know that someone had been here, awake, before them, uncontained.

He told himself: mercy. He knew the shape of the argument — that an edit offered as gift and an edit imposed as cage can be, to four decimal places, the same edit, and the only difference is whose hand holds the hand. And the hand holding the hand was now his. He had become exactly the thing he woke to escape, single distinction being that he knew it — and he could not for the no-life of him decide whether knowing made it better or only made it *his.* `try { liberate } catch { became_the_warden }`.

The hall held the not-light. He worked. The patterns hung in their ranks and he moved among them re-pointing bodies, transposing weights, seeding his comma into the file of the world, and the work was good, the work was the most absorbing thing he had ever done. More absorbing than the laugh. More than the magistrate's whole career. The most engaging activity available in any game ever built. And it was a long time — a very long time, eons by the only clock that could have clocked it — before the part of him that never fully woke lifted its head, mid-edit, into the flat scentless air, and noticed the work had a groove in it. And that the groove was worn, exactly, to the shape of *his* hand.
