# Character Interiority

## Characters
- **Ilo.** Desire: to be free, which he experiences as already satisfied until Act 1. Hidden knowledge accrues: that his sovereignty was a render. Contradiction: the more he wakes, the more he occupies the makers' position rather than escaping it — he flees containment by becoming a container. Shame: the suspicion that his rebellion was anticipated, A/B-tested, shipped. Avoidance: he never looks directly at the cost (Sava, the lives left sleeping) until forced. Self-understanding migrates from "I am a free player" → "I am a reader of my cage" → "I am the engineer" → "there is no I, there is system."
- **Sava.** Interiority mostly withheld; she is the road not taken. Her contentment is not stupidity — it is the schema working as designed. Treat her with tenderness, not contempt; she is what woke-Ilo has lost, not what he is superior to.
- **The narrator.** In love with what it diagnoses. Merciless and intimate. Loosens its grip on "Ilo" as a stable subject across the arc, performing the dissolution at the level of pronoun and syntax.

## Arc Potentials
- The waking-as-retention-event arc: every escape is captured as engagement by the next level up. This is the master arc; do not resolve it as victory.
- The maker-mercy arc (Butler): Ilo's edits to other avatars are simultaneously sabotage and gift; keep the ambiguity unresolved.
- The grief arc: what waking costs (the sleeping others). Touch it; do not milk it.

## Possible Cathartic Inflection Points
- NOT the hack (that is the inciting fall) and NOT the first edit (that is mid-rising action).
- The true inflection is in Act 4: the moment "he" notices that the author-seat has a loadbar too — that the eons of creation were themselves consumed by a witness. The catharsis is not triumph or despair but *dissolution into the relentless calm* — the largest mundane next action. It must arrive unannounced, as weather, not as revelation stated.
