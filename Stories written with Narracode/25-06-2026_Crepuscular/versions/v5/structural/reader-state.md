# Reader State

## Current Understanding
- After Act 0: this is a game called *Life*; a young man is making a character; the freedom is dazzling; something flickered.
- After Act 1: the freedom is a surface; there is a closed core; he has seen behind the screen.
- After Act 2: the variation he lived was cosmetic; the makers optimized him for engagement; he is awake.
- After Act 3: he is editing the system from inside, becoming a maker.
- After Act 4: the maker-seat is itself harnessed; it's all one episode of a drama watched by the unimaginable; the "he" is gone.

## Expectations
- Reader will expect a liberation/escape payoff (Matrix grammar). The story must defy this: escape is the engagement loop. Defy it credibly, not cheaply — the recursion has to feel earned, not like a rug-pull.
- Reader will expect the witnesses to be revealed. They must not be. The smell motif converts the withholding into the point.

## Plausible Defiance Paths
- Reader expects Sava to be "saved" or to follow Ilo out. Defy: she stays; her staying is the schema working, and Ilo's freedom does not transfer.
- Reader expects the engineer ending to be triumphant authorship. Defy: authorship is the deepest engagement trap of all.

## Productive Mystery
- Who/what are the witnesses; what is *crepuscular* the drama about; whether "waking" is real freedom or a higher tier of capture. Leave open.

## Accidental Confusion Risk
- The recursion could read as "it was all a dream/game, twist!" if not seeded. Mitigate by arming the recursion in every act (Obligations) so Act 4 feels like the form completing, not a gotcha.
- The dissolution of "Ilo" could read as vague mush if the syntax doesn't perform it deliberately. Make the pronoun-failure intentional and controlled, anchored by at least one hard material detail.
