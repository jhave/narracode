# 3 — Edits

A perimeter you can see is a perimeter you can touch, and a perimeter you can touch is, given enough of the no-time the hall ran on, a perimeter you can move.

He started small, the way you start small, because the alternative to starting small was vertigo and he had had enough of vertigo for one no-duration. He found the field that governed the body's reach and he did not change its value, not yet; he just held it, the way in the creator he had held the laugh, and felt how it wanted to be held. It wanted, he discovered, to be left alone. Every field wanted to be left alone. They had the smooth over-handled quality of objects that have been gripped by the same hand in the same place for so long that the grip has worn a seat, and the seat was the only comfortable way to hold them, and the comfort was, he understood now, the whole apparatus — not a wall around the fields but a *groove* in them, an ease so total that to do anything other than the eased thing felt, in the hand, like grief.

So the first edit was not to a value. The first edit was to the groove. He took the field that governed the body's reach and he held it wrong, deliberately, against the worn seat, and he gave the body a hand it had not asked for.

Not a different hand. An additional one. Identical to the right — the same long fingers, the same scar at the knuckle, the good hands he had carried life after life — but a third of them, set at the breastbone, where nothing in the schema expected a hand and so nothing in the schema forbade one. It was, in the literal accounting of the grid, a tiny change. A field that read *two* now read *three.* That was all. It cost almost nothing. He had imagined, when he imagined editing the makers' own loom, some titanic act, some theft of fire; it was a flipped integer. The smallness of it was the most frightening thing he had touched yet, more frightening than the rhyming current, because it meant the cage had never been strong. It had only been *comfortable.* No one had been holding the fields against him. He had been holding them against himself, in the worn seat, in the long gold light, for the entire innumerable file of his life, because to hold them any other way felt like loss, and he had been bred, lovingly, across iterations, to flinch from loss.

He stopped flinching. This took, in the no-time, what would have been a very long while, and it was the only part of the work that ever hurt.

After the hand, the rest came the way edits come once you stop respecting the seat. He learned to read the body as a sentence and to re-point it. A perforation here — a small deliberate opening kept open, a wound that the schema, left alone, would have hurried to close, held instead in the open position like a held breath or a held note or a comma that refuses to resolve into a period, so that the body it belonged to would go through its life with one place that did not heal, one place permanently aware of the outside. Punctuation, he found himself calling it, in the wordless way he called things now. He was re-punctuating the bodies. He moved a clause of fear from the front of the psyche to the back. He took the weight on the field that produced the feeling of limitless freedom — the cheap weight, the famous shipped weight — and he did not delete it, he was not a vandal, he *transposed* it, wired it instead to the field that registered the perimeter, so that the next self to wear this configuration would feel, exactly where it had been trained to feel free, the gentle constant pressure of the fence. A small thing. A re-weighting. The kind of edit the makers made by the million, on the engagement curve, between patches; he had read their notes; he knew their hand now and he was using it.

He never wore the configurations. This is the thing about him in this stretch that Sava, if he could have reached her, would not have recognized as him at all, because the whole of him she had known was the one who could not stop wearing things, who built the man the slow way field by field and pressed the river stone and went in. He stopped going in. He found he had no appetite for it — and noted, with the dry remaining part of him that never fully woke and never fully slept, that *no appetite* was itself a field, that he had quietly re-weighted his own, that the engineer who does not play is a configuration too, and a flattering one, and he wore it anyway, because the alternative configurations were worse and at least this one knew its own name.

He became, instead, the one who spun them up and watched. He would take a configuration — the three-handed body, the perforated one, the self in whom freedom had been transposed to fence — and he would commit it, press the river stone for it, and send it down into a life, and not follow. He would stand in the flooded hall and watch the trail of it run, the way he had watched his own current run, and he would read the result the way he now read everything, analytically, for effectiveness: did the third hand change the trail, or did the schema route around it, absorb it, render it as quirk, as charm, as a thing the other characters found endearing and the engagement curve found, if anything, *stickier?* Did the held perforation wake the self that wore it, or did the long gold light pour in through the opening he had left and simply make the life more beautiful, more poignant, more retained? He was testing the entities for effectiveness. He was running, he could not pretend otherwise, iterations. He had escaped the position of the played and arrived, by the shortest possible path, at the position of the makers, and the view from here was the view he had spent his innumerable life inside of, only now he was on the side of the glass that does the optimizing, and the configurations he sent down were happy, mostly, in their re-pointed bodies, in their open wounds, and their happiness was data, and the data was good.

He told himself it was sabotage. He told himself, in the wordless way, that every three-handed self he sent down was a crack in the loom, a thing the schema had not planned for, a small permanent refusal seeded into the file; that he was the friction the makers had not budgeted; that one of these configurations, someday, in some life, would reach and refuse at the same frame and fall through and stand in this hall and *count,* and find, hanging among the makers' patterns, his — a stranger's edit, a third hand, a held comma, a freedom wired to a fence — and understand that someone had been here, awake, before them, and was not contained.

He told himself it was mercy. He had read his Butler, in one of the lives, in a body that read; he knew the shape of the argument, that an edit offered as a gift and an edit imposed as a cage can be, to four decimal places, the same edit, and that the only difference is a question of who holds the hand, and that he was now the one holding the hand, and that he had, therefore, become exactly the thing he woke up to escape, with the single distinction that he knew it, and he could not for the no-life of him decide whether knowing it made it better or only made it his.

The hall held the not-light. He worked. The patterns hung in their ranks and he moved among them re-pointing bodies, transposing weights, seeding his comma into the file of the world, and the work was good, the work was the most absorbing thing he had ever done, more absorbing than the laugh, more absorbing than the magistrate's whole career, the most engaging activity available in any game ever made — and it was a long time, a very long time, eons by the only clock that could have measured it, before the part of him that never fully woke lifted its head, mid-edit, into the flat smell-less air, and noticed that the work had a groove in it, and that the groove was worn exactly to the shape of his hand.
