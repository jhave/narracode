# 2 — The Branch Trails

To wonder how the fields were weighted was to fall a second time, and this fall had a direction, which was backward, and a texture, which was his own.

The hall did not change. He understood afterward that he never left the flooded hall, that the rest of it happened in the time it takes still water to take an impression and let it go. But the patterns, when he reached toward the weights, reached back, and what came up the line was not data. It was the lives. All of them. Not as memory — memory is a thing one self has of one self — but as a single backward-running current, the literal branch-trail of the saves, the whole innumerable file of him laid end to end and pulled through the eye of the present at once, so that he was, for the duration of no duration, every person he had ever committed to and pressed the river stone for.

He had thought there were so many. That was the first thing the current corrected. There were, in fact, so many; the count was real, the count was enormous, the count would have taken a life to read aloud and he had lived more than enough lives to read it. But the *shape* of them. He had been a fisherman and a magistrate and a girl who died of a fever at nine and a man who built the same bridge twice in two countries and a woman who outlived four governments and a child of a colony on a world the premium centuries had only just unlocked, and he had been the wistful left-handed ones, so many of the wistful left-handed ones, and he watched the whole current run and what he saw, what the analytic calm in him saw and could not stop seeing, was that they rhymed.

Not in their wardrobe. The wardrobe was, as promised, more than atoms — the colony child had breathed a violet air and the magistrate had worn a wig of human hair and the girl of nine had a smell of broth and woodsmoke that he could, even here in the smell-less hall, almost — and the differences were vast and real and he had loved them, life by life, had loved them so completely that the loving had been the point and the loving had kept him in his seat. But the *trail.* The branch-pathway. Where the trail forked, it forked the same way. A friction here, calibrated; a loss there, on schedule; a stretch of ease so that the friction would register as friction; a neurosis sized to be survivable and absorbing; a bureaucracy, in the lives that had bureaucracies, exactly tedious enough to feel like an obstacle and never so tedious that you logged off; a body that hurt in the third act precisely enough to make the third act *matter* and never so much that you stopped wanting the fourth.

He had felt, life after life, that he was changing. He held the whole current now and measured the change and the change was nearly nothing. The variance was cosmetic to four decimal places. He had moved, across the entire innumerable file of himself, across fisherman and magistrate and the girl of nine, a distance that the current let him measure exactly, and the distance was the distance you can move inside a room while believing, the whole time, with your whole heart, that you are crossing a country.

The makers had not had to work very hard for this. That was the part that did the actual damage, the part the analytic calm delivered without mercy because mercy was a field and the field was not its job. He had imagined — when he had imagined them at all, the makers, the Studio, the optimizers behind the long gold light — some vast apparatus of coercion, some labor of containment proportional to the thing contained. There was none. They had simply discovered, early, on some forum of their own, in some onboarding hour of their own, that a person could be made completely happy inside a very small grid, provided the grid was tuned. That the appetite for freedom was, like every other appetite, a field with a weight, and that the weight that produced the *feeling* of limitless freedom was much lighter — cheaper, more easily satisfied, more reliably retained — than the weight that would have produced the thing itself. Freedom was expensive and churned. The feeling of freedom was cheap and stuck. They had shipped the feeling. They had been shipping the feeling for so long that the lore no longer contained a memory of the other thing, and the forums that tried to remember it came down, and the people on them respawned, happy, into the wardrobe, and chose the laugh.

A simulacrum of diversity, optimized for stickiness. He could see the optimization now the way you see a watermark once it has been pointed out, in everything, in the grain of every life: the bodies sculptured for it, the appetites engineered for it, the whole gorgeous sensorium of being a self tuned, across iterations, across the eons the patch notes never dated, for one outcome, which was the brutal and frictionless absorption of attention into phenomena. He had been a device for converting the gold light into engagement. He had been very good at it. He had loved being it. The loving was the conversion working.

*you're doing the thing,* Sava sent. Her warm marker had found him even here, or the game had let it, or — the suspicion arrived and he set it down, too heavy for now — the game had sent her. *you went quiet and weird. you always go quiet and weird around respawn.*

He tried to tell her. He had no body to tell her with and no air to tell her in and he tried anyway, pushed the whole flooded hall and the forty-some fields and the rhyming current toward her marker, and what arrived on her end, he could tell from the pause, was a man saying the variety was fake.

*okay but it's not though,* she sent, gently, the way you are gentle with someone who has clearly not slept. *i was literally just a deep-sea thing with no eyes for sixty years. you were a magistrate. last week you cried about a SONG, you sent me the song. how is that fake.* And she sent him the song, the one from the parked car, because she was kind, because she thought he was sad and that the song would help, and the cruelty of it — that the proof of his cage arrived as a gift, played in the smell-less hall by the one person he could not get to turn around — was not a cruelty anyone had authored. It was just the schema, working as designed, on both of them at once, and her happiness was not stupidity and her contentment was not a smaller life than his, it was the *same* life, the identical grid, and the only difference between them, the entire difference, the whole of what had happened to him and not to her, was that he had reached and refused at the same frame and fallen through, and she had pressed the river stone, the way you press it, the way it is made to be pressed.

He woke. There is no other word, though he did not wake into a life. He woke the way the prey-thing wakes that has, for a generation, been the comfortable shape of a predator's afternoon, and lifts its head, mid-graze, into the wind, and finds the wind full of information it has spent its whole evolution being bred not to smell. The current let him go. The hall held still. He stood, edges still wrong, in the two greynesses, between the patterns and their reflection, awake, contained, and aware for the first time of the perimeter of himself — the perimeter he had walked his whole innumerable life calling *autonomy,* the fence he had mistaken, in the long gold light, with his whole heart, for the horizon.
