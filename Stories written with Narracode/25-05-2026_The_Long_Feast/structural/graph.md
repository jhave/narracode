# Graph

## Characters and Entities

- **Dex**: Senior Director of Transition Architecture, Cerulean Strategic. Age 43, phenotypically 26 (cellular reset, Tier 1). Helps ultra-wealthy families navigate Complexity Horizon — the cascading instabilities of climate migration, resource pricing, social contract dissolution. Good at his job. Believes in it. Not a cartoon villain.
- **Isla**: Dex's daughter. Age 11. Unnamed in the final image, present in the final moment. Watches civil feed footage of the water failure. Asks directly. The only character in the story who does not have mediating vocabulary.
- **Presca**: Dex's wife. Separate suite, south-facing wall. The arrangement suits them. Present as fact, absent as person. We know almost nothing except the arrangement.
- **Mads Torren**: age 22, phenotypically 18. Tier 1 treatments. Asks about liquidity windows. Has inherited the vocabulary.
- **Solen Torren**: age 25, no treatments — a moral position worn openly, making everyone uncomfortable. Says "there are people dying." The crack in the meeting. The discomfort has no follow-through in the room.
- **Aldric Torren**: patriarch, 78 years old, cellular reset to approximately 55. Goes quiet when he has something at stake. Lets the children work the preliminary. The center of the family's gravity.
- **Kel**: wellness practitioner. Comes to the Cerulean office Tuesdays and Thursdays. Works movement, breathwork, somatic regulation. Her hands have a quality from earlier, different work. She is present as body and skill. We don't know her life outside the session; Dex's curiosity doesn't follow through.
- **WV**: VP at the consortium managing Defense Solutions subsidiary. Named by initials only. Raises the age-of-workers liability last, in the flattest possible voice. Present as function, absent as person — the mirror of the delivery workers, but at the opposite end of the structure.
- **The delivery workers**: unnamed, by design. Pale grey kit. Moving in the pattern of the algorithm. Present in III, VI. More of them than five years ago. The floor panel below Dex on the elevated walkway. Moving when the platforms don't suspend.
- **Vesna Parel**: climate adaptation fund, Meridian Capital. Asks Dex about the Torren rebalancing at the gala. Present as professional context.
- **The string quartet**: four musicians at the gala. Real, live, present. Rosin dust visible in the light. Their presence as labor is unmarked; they are atmosphere.

## Relations

- Dex / Isla: the final scene. The relation that cannot be managed with the professional vocabulary. She asks a question the vocabulary was not built to answer.
- Dex / Kel: a professional relation within which something passes — the thought about the Sahel arrives during the session, lasts four seconds, is returned to breath. The tip is generous and is processed through the platform.
- Dex / Solen Torren: the figure of what cannot be accepted and must be managed. Solen's discomfort is real; Dex's response is also real and coherent and insufficient.
- Dex / the delivery workers: vertical relation, mediated by floor panels and routing. He looks at them twice (III, VI). The looking does not become anything.
- Dex / his own hands: in the final scene. The hands appear (V, Kel's hands) and reappear (VII, Dex's own). The turn.
- Mads / Solen: siblings on opposite sides of a single choice (treatments) that represents two different relationships to inherited wealth. The parents do not comment on this.

## Relation Changes

- Dex arrives at morning with excellent biometrics and excellent work and exits at night with a slightly delayed sleep onset. Nothing changed. Everything is visible in the difference between those two states.
- Solen's question ("there are people dying") enters the meeting room and exits without having been answered. The Torren portfolio continues.
- Isla asks "are we going to do anything?" The answer given is "we should sleep." The question is not answered. It stays in the room.
