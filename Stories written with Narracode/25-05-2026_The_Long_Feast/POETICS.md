# POETICS

## Display title

The Long Feast

## Display synopsis

A Senior Director of Transition Architecture at a sovereign intelligence firm moves through a single day in the mid-21st century — breakfast briefings on Sahel water rights, conference calls managing the liability of underage workers in a conflict zone, a mandatory wellness hour, a gala inside a 22°C enclosure while the city outside manages a water distribution failure. He is good at his job. He believes in it. At the end of the day, his daughter shows him footage from the mobile water stations: the long lines extending past the camera's range. He says: we should sleep. He goes to his room. His sleep monitor notes a slight delay in onset. A serious satire in seven services.

## Commitments

- Satire through earnestness: the narrator describes his life as good and functional; the reader sees the catastrophe. The bureaucratic vocabulary — Transition Architecture, Resilience Portfolio, regional stabilization event with elevated kinetic activity — should feel precise and real, not cartoonish.
- Stylistic pressures: Terry Gilliam's Brazil (normality as the register of horror; the protagonist who thinks he is doing good work); Thomas More's Utopia inverted (the narrator as the traveler who cannot see what the reader sees); mid-21st century near-post-singularity (longevity treatments, AI wellness, gig economy as neo-feudalism, private climate-controlled enclaves, water markets).
- The protagonist is inside the enclosure of his own assumptions. The recognition cannot be received because the language of yield and architecture and transition was not built for it.
- Seven sections as seven services: courses of a meal, hours of a day, products of a firm — all simultaneously.
- Consumption as through-line. Motion as the register of the machine running.

## Companion structure

Mirror piece to The Symposium (2026-05-24):
- The Symposium: stillness, periphery, visitor from outside, music as through-line, recognition as sound (and yet).
- The Long Feast: motion, center, participant narrating from inside, consumption as through-line, recognition as image (the field being grazed).
- Both end pointing toward the same unnamed thing from opposite sides. Neither character can fully receive it. Together they demonstrate what the old man said in The Symposium: grasping toward and grasping away are the same gesture.

## Refusals

- The recognition is never named directly by Dex.
- The final image arrives as landscape, not as argument.
- No editorial intrusion. No didactic moment. The collapse into acknowledgment is enacted, not stated.
