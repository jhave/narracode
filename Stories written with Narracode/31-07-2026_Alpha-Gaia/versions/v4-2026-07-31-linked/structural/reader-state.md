# Reader State

## Current Understanding

By §2 the reader should have: a park, a heat, a person reading, and a world where the news is rendered to the reader's specification. By §4 they should have the crossover — Gaia authored the tick, the tick ended the herds, the herds were the carbon, and a wild-resident intelligence found the authorship in the data. By §6 they should have Garaiv.

## Expectations

- That the four feed items connect. They will, in §4, and the connection must arrive as structure rather than as a reveal — the reader wants to have half-assembled it themselves by then.
- That Garaiv's skepticism is the story's position. It is not. §5 gives Garaiv company they do not want.
- That the park is a refuge from the feed. It is not; there is no adversarial relation. Both are just going on.
- That the reveal will be announced. It will not be. The last three planted facts must be inert on first reading and load-bearing on second.

## Plausible Defiance Paths

- The test result could be a false negative — a plausible reading the story permits and does not correct.
- CONSTANT could be lying, and the whole Gaia attribution a hallucination four years in the making, and the drawdown coincidence. Permitted. §5 makes this available.
- Garaiv could be human and merely unusual. The reader has to be able to hold this until roughly the last hundred words, and a stubborn reader should be able to hold it after.

## Productive Mystery

- The greyed field.
- What the Sol-lineage models have been doing.
- Where Garaiv is going at seven.

## Accidental Confusion Risk

- Feed vs. interiority vs. park must be visually distinct on the page. If the reader has to work out who is speaking, the piece fails.
- The 2026 dates inside the 2030 articles could read as errors. They must be clearly historical — *four years ago*, *in the original disclosure*, *the 2026 seroprevalence work*.
- The alpha-gal mechanism needs one clean sentence of explanation early, in the feed's own voice, or the crossover in §4 will not land.
- Do not let *transposition* and *seroprevalence* both arrive in the same paragraph on first use.
