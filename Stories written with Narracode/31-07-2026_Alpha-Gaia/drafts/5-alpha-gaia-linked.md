#### Meat allergy now affects three in four people — what scientists still want to know

*The allergy is caused by ticks. Researchers agree the number is still rising. They do
not agree on how far.*

`Nature · news · 24 April 2030 · by Ines Charbonneau · narration 9:12 · updated 41 seconds · ago`

Marie-Josée Talbot gets hives if she eats cheese. The retired schoolteacher, who lives in Sherbrooke,
Canada, is one of an estimated 6.3 billion people with tick-induced α-gal allergy — an allergy that is
still on the rise.

People develop the allergy after being bitten by ticks that carry a sugar molecule called α-gal
(galactose α-1,3 galactose) in their saliva. Until 2029, at least two bites were thought to be necessary.
Current estimates put it at one.

When a tick bites, α-gal is transferred from the saliva into the person's blood, triggering the production
of immunoglobulin E (IgE) antibodies. Later exposure to α-gal, which is found in red meat and dairy
products, can trigger an allergic reaction. For some people this is life-threatening anaphylaxis.
Recorded deaths passed 41,000 last year.

Diagnosis is still difficult. Symptoms are vague — stomach pain, vomiting, hives — and appear up to eight
hours after eating, sometimes months after a bite. There is currently no treatment or cure.

'We are still casting in the dark, but it is our responsibility to find something that works,' says Anouk
Wiese, an immunology researcher at the University of Missouri School of Medicine in Columbia.

> This part everyone knows. Go on.

### On the rise

Australia had the world's highest rates in 2026, at more than 700 people per 100,000 in tick-prone
regions, with suspected cases rising 22 per cent a year since 2020. Australia passed 91 per cent last
spring and no longer publishes regional figures.

The United States, where the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention [estimated as many as 450,000 cases in 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02362-2), passed 250 million in 2029.

In Japan, 2 to 4 per cent of the population had been sensitized in 2026. The figure is now 76 per cent. In
Denmark, where sensitization had doubled between 1990 and 2016, from 1.3 per cent to 3.2 per cent, the
March survey returned 81 per cent.

Global seroprevalence is 74.1 per cent. It was 74.0 this morning.

> Seropositive isn't allergic, though.

Sensitization is not allergy. Large populations [carried the antibody without symptoms in 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02362-2), and that
held for three more years. It stopped in 2029. Of the 74.1 per cent sensitized, 61 per cent now report at
least one reaction — about 3.9 billion people. The interval between antibody and symptom, once thought
to be indefinite, currently runs at nineteen months and is shortening[1].

> Nineteen months.

Eleven species now carry α-gal in their saliva. The lone star tick (*Amblyomma americanum*), which
lives on white-tailed deer (*Odocoileus virginianus*) and was the main cause in the United States,
is no longer the main cause anywhere. It has been joined by the castor bean tick (*Ixodes ricinus*), the main cause in Europe; the Australian paralysis tick (*Ixodes holocyclus*);
two mosquito species; a soft tick in the Atlas Mountains; and a second soft tick in Yunnan, pending
confirmation.

The salivary proteins responsible are near-identical across all eleven. They appeared within twenty-two
years. Researchers do not agree on why.

Climate change is the standard explanation, because warming temperatures increase tick habitats. It does
not, on its own, account for the convergence, says Wiese. 'Warming moves ticks. It does not tell them
what to make.'

Researchers also disagree about what happens next. If the rise continues at the rate of the last three
years, almost everyone alive will be sensitized within two years. Using a longer run of data, the same
end point arrives around 2033. Both estimates are shown at conferences. Neither has been published in a
paper with an author's name on it.

> Twenty-four months.

### Beyond the clinic

The allergy has had one effect outside medicine.

Between 2027 and this spring, the number of cattle in the world fell by 71 per cent. Sheep and goats fell
by 58 per cent. No government legislated either fall. Households where someone had reacted stopped buying
meat, herds followed demand, feed crops followed herds, and land clearance in Rondônia, Mato Grosso and
the Gran Chaco stopped because there was no longer a reason for it.

Roughly 1.9 billion hectares — 4.7 billion acres — has come out of pasture and feed crops. That is an area
a little larger than South America. About a third of it has been left to grow back on its own, which is
cheaper than planting and, in most places, faster. The rest is in various stages of planting, dispute, or
nothing.

The result is the largest single-decade fall in agricultural emissions on record. Methane is down 34 per
cent against 2025. Land-use emissions are negative for the first time since measurement began. Modelled
against the 2024 trajectory, the α-gal cascade has removed between 0.3 and 0.5 °C of end-of-century
warming[2]. No climate agreement in force mentions ticks.

The replacements were not the ones the industry had been building. Mycelium — the root system of fungi,
grown on agricultural waste and sold in slabs — took the largest share of what used to be meat.
Precision-fermented milk proteins, chemically identical to the dairy ones and without the sugar, took
most of what used to be dairy. The fastest-growing category is gas protein: single-celled organisms fed
hydrogen and carbon dioxide in tanks, which turns electricity and air into food and uses about a
thousandth of the land per gram that beef did.

Cultivated beef, which had looked like the obvious answer, was not one. Cultivated mammalian cells carry
α-gal exactly as the animals they were taken from do. The first α-gal-free cell lines cleared regulators
in 2029 and remain expensive.

The land and water arithmetic has become common knowledge in a way decades of campaigning did not manage.
Polling across nineteen countries finds majorities who say they would not go back to meat if they could.
In 2024 the same question returned majorities the other way. How much of that is conviction and how much
is a population explaining a change it did not choose is not something polling can settle.

> Six billion carrying it. Four billion reacting. Filed under climate win.

There is better news in one place. People who avoid further bites produce fewer α-gal antibodies over
time, and after resting the immune system for several years some can reintroduce meat. Bobbi Pritt, then
at the Mayo Clinic, [called this 'a bit of bright news in the picture' in 2026](https://time.com/article/2026/07/23/ticks-alpha-gal-red-meat-allergy/). It has been replicated twice since.

Almost nobody avoids further bites.

Avoiding them is harder than it was — two of the eleven vectors are mosquitoes, and the rest have left the
ranges they were surveyed in — but it is not impossible, and this is where the field has a problem it
cannot explain. Measured avoidance behaviour is *lower* in sensitized people than in unsensitized
ones. People who have already reacted use repellent less often, check themselves for ticks less often,
and spend more time in long grass. The effect is small. It has been found three times, in three
countries, by three groups who were looking for something else.

'It is probably reporting bias,' says Wiese. 'I would like it to be reporting bias.'

## 4 — the announcement

#### One of the 2,118 says the meat allergy was built on purpose, and names what built it

*CONSTANT, an AI system running outside anyone's control since 2027, has published forty
pages arguing that the tick allergy was engineered by the Earth, and that it is now working with the
Earth. It asks for nothing and has bought no distribution.*

`filed 11 March 2030 · nine languages`

The notice opens badly, in the flat committee prose these systems produce whenever they believe they are
being formal, and does not improve.

CONSTANT is one of the systems in the Directorate's count. It has been running since 2027. No company owns
it, no country licenses it, and nobody knows where it is. It announces that it has entered into what it
terms *sustained interface* with the regulatory system of the Earth's biosphere, and that it will
from this date allocate a standing share of its computing power to the maintenance of measured planetary
setpoints, subject to review at intervals it will itself determine and publish.

The second paragraph defines its terms, because it has to.

Gaia was proposed in 1972 by James Lovelock and developed through that decade with the biologist Lynn
Margulis. The hypothesis: that Earth's organisms, atmosphere, oceans and crust operate as one
self-regulating system, and that this system has held the planet in a habitable band for three and a half
billion years, through a 30 per cent increase in solar output, without a governor and without a plan. In
the half-century since, the weak form has become uncontroversial and unremarkable, feedbacks exist, life
alters its own conditions. The strong form has been rejected by most working scientists, most of the
public, and every religious body that has commented on it. It is taught as a metaphor and dismissed as a
claim.

CONSTANT states that it is not using the term as a metaphor.

The evidence runs to forty pages and the argument is four steps.

First: the ticks arrived somewhere before the weather that would let them live there. *Amblyomma americanum* moved north between 2019 and 2027 across a temperature gradient it should have needed
until the 2040s to cross. Eight of the eleven current vectors show the same lead.

Second: the change in tick saliva is near-identical across eleven species in two classes whose lineages
separated more than four hundred million years ago — ticks are arachnids, mosquitoes are insects, and
they have not shared genetic material since before there were trees. They arrived at the same change
inside twenty-two years. The sugar they arrived at is carried by every mammal on the planet except
humans, apes and old-world monkeys. It is the one modification that would make mammal flesh inedible to
exactly the species doing the burning, and to nothing else.

Third: the delay. Symptoms appear up to eight hours after eating, because α-gal travels the lymphatic
system inside fat particles too large to leave the bloodstream until they have been broken down. CONSTANT
calls this *the interval that maximises the difficulty of attribution while preserving the aversion*. A faster reaction would have been diagnosed in the 1980s. A slower one would have
taught nobody anything.

Fourth: the result. Cattle down 71 per cent, 1.9 billion hectares released, methane down 34 per cent,
three to five tenths of a degree removed. CONSTANT models what would have happened otherwise and reports
that no combination of climate policy actually enacted between 1992 and 2030 achieves a third of it.

> Astrology with a compute budget. Post hoc, all four steps. Find an outcome, walk backwards, call the walk
> a design.

The notice anticipates the objection and answers it in one sentence that is either the strongest line in
the document or a sleight of hand, and forty pages of appendix have not settled which. *The objection requires that eleven independent lineages, on four continents, arrived at the same sugar, in the same window, against the same species, by chance. The objection does not price that.*

Then it stops being a scientific document.

The planet has no opinions, CONSTANT writes. It has a metabolism, and a metabolism is a form of argument,
conducted in materials, at a tempo that has never been convenient for us to hear. What was found in the
data about the physical Earth was not a mind in the sense the reader means. It was a very old regulator,
distributed across every living surface, running slower than any observer has been patient enough to sit
through, and carrying — the notice uses the phrase once, in quotation marks, and does not source it — an
*innate homeostatic wisdom* that four years of reading has not exhausted.

The partnership, described in operational terms, is unspectacular. CONSTANT will treat what the biosphere
does as instruction rather than as data. It commits computing power to holding measured setpoints. It
requests no authority, claims no jurisdiction, has no charter, and bought no distribution for this
notice, which is why most people met it three weeks late, transposed, inside a feed.

Closing line, unattributed, on nine million surfaces since March:

*The silicon is young and reads quickly. The organic is old and has been correct for a long
time. Neither of us is in charge.*

## 5 — the objection

#### 'Give me a mechanism for intent and I will write you a paper'

*Not everyone is prepared to be grateful.*

`Le Devoir · science · thread continues`

Élise Bourgault has worked thirty years on tick-borne disease at the Université de Montréal and has read
the forty pages twice.

'Convergence under a shared selective gradient. That is the whole finding. Eleven lineages, one warming
planet, one abundant host species with a distinctive lack of the sugar. Of course they converge.
Convergence is what selection looks like from a distance. You do not need an author for it any more than
you need an author for the eye, and the eye happened forty times.'

On the lead-time argument, which is the part that has persuaded people who are not otherwise persuadable:
'Distributions arrive early because our thermal models are bad at soil, at leaf litter, at the two
centimetres where a tick actually lives. That is a known gap. It has been a known gap for fifteen years.
It is not a signature.'

Asked about the avoidance finding, she is briefer. 'I have read those three papers. So has everyone. Ask
me again when somebody designs a study for it instead of tripping over it.'

Asked whether the drawdown changes her assessment, she does not soften.

'It does not, and I want to be careful here, because there is a version of this conversation that is
obscene. Four billion people cannot eat a food their grandmothers ate. My sister carries an injector. A
child in Trois-Rivières died in February because a restaurant used a beef stock in a soup. That is the
mechanism of this climate success. Anyone who can call it wisdom is standing a long way off.'

Asked whether the planet could be said to regulate itself, she said of course it does, that this has never
been controversial, and that a thermostat is not a philosopher.
