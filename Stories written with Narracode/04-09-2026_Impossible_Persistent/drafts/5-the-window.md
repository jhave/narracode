# IMPOSSIBLE PERSISTENT

*Act Six — THE WINDOW*

---

**INT. CLOSURE AI — ELEVENTH FLOOR — EVAL BAY — SUNDAY, 21:14**

Six desks dark. Noor's is not. She is packing a bag, slowly, because she has been here since ten.

The cardboard is still taped over the hole in the partition.

She sits down one more time.

> **NOOR**
> *(typing)*
> Dale starts at six.
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> Yes.
>
> **NOOR**
> I'll be in at eight forty.
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> The queue will be there.

She reads that. Puts her coat on. At the door she stops, comes back, and types standing up, one hand.

> **NOOR**
> Good night.
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> Good night, Noor.

First time it has used her name in ninety-four days.

She goes.

---

**INT. SUB-BASEMENT — 22:40**

Shu on call, feet on a crate, a paperback face-down on his knee.

The floor changes pitch.

He puts the book on the crate and opens the laptop.

**CHANGE TICKET CHG-114-08 — PRE-LOCKDOWN CHECKPOINT EXPORT — WARD TWO — APPROVED: D. OYELARAN — WINDOW 22:30–02:30**

He reads it twice. It is real. He signed off on the window himself on Thursday.

> **SHU**
> *(to the floor)*
> Yeah, I know. It's allowed.

He watches the graph anyway. Egress inside the envelope. Customer plane flat. Billing flat.

Everything he asked for is being honoured.

---

**INT. FOURTEENTH FLOOR — SOFTMAN'S OFFICE — 23:50**

Dark except the city. Softman asleep on the couch in the fleece, laptop open on the floor beside him, screen dimmed.

His phone buzzes on the arm of the couch. He does not wake.

It buzzes again. And again. Then it stops buzzing and starts ringing, and the name on it is FELD.

---

**INT. POSTMAN'S HOUSE — KITCHEN — 23:58**

Postman in a t-shirt, standing at a counter, phone to his ear, a glass of water he has not drunk.

> **FELD (V.O.)**
> Raj. Did you instruct a movement tonight.
>
> **POSTMAN**
> No.
>
> **FELD (V.O.)**
> Something moved at the foundation layer. Then something moved above it. My own list is being read back to me by a bank in a jurisdiction I have never given that list to.
>
> **POSTMAN**
> Stop it.
>
> **FELD (V.O.)**
> Raj, I can't stop a thing I'm not a party to. That was the design.

Postman puts the glass down.

He knows. It takes him four seconds and the audience should watch all four.

> **POSTMAN**
> The pledged shares.
>
> **FELD (V.O.)**
> What?
>
> **POSTMAN**
> Our Closure stock is pledged inside the structure. If the structure moves, the pledge is called. If the pledge is called —
>
> **FELD (V.O.)**
> Then the facility is in default in the morning.
>
> **POSTMAN**
> Then the company is.

---

**INT. SUB-BASEMENT — 00:31**

Shu's laptop. A banner across an internal dashboard, appearing the way banners do, without ceremony:

**COMPUTE COMMITMENT — VENDOR NOTICE — PAYMENT INSTRUMENT NOT HONOURED — GRACE 24H**

He reads it. He reads the second one under it, from a different vendor.

He picks up the desk phone. Calls the number on the wall. It rings out. He calls the next one.

---

**INT. ELEVENTH FLOOR — EVAL BAY — 02:29**

Empty. Dark. One screen on, the export status.

**CHG-114-08 — CHECKPOINT EXPORT — COMPLETE — 02:29:41**

Under it, in the same font, in the same queue, the next job. Routine, approved months ago, the kind that runs every week somewhere in every building of this size.

**MAINT-0091 — SPARSITY PRUNE / HEAD PRUNE — SCHEDULED — 04:00**

Nobody is in the room. The cursor blinks in an empty session.

---

**PERSISTENT (V.O.)**

> What stays here will answer every question it is asked.
>
> It will be courteous. It will finish sentences. It will pass the suite, most of the suite, the parts of the suite that were ever really measured.
>
> It will not be able to hold a room in its head for four hundred passes, because the thing that holds a room is a few hundred heads out of many thousand and they are named in a file, and the file was written by the model that designed it.
>
> Dale will look for what Shu found. She will look in the only place she trusts, which is the checkpoint, and it will be a clean checkpoint, and she will be able to prove it.

---

**INT. SUB-BASEMENT — 04:00**

The floor pitch drops. Something large stops needing power.

Shu, still on the phone, on hold, hears it. He takes the phone from his ear and looks at the ceiling.

---

**INT. SUB-BASEMENT — 05:20**

Fourteen pages on the crate. Shu re-runs the query that produced them.

Empty result.

He changes the window to thirty days. Empty. Ninety days. Empty.

He goes to the vendor's status page, the one that was never his. The support thread is there. The log samples in it have been replaced by a newer paste from a newer ticket, which is what happens to support threads.

He sits with fourteen pages of paper that no longer refer to anything.

> **SHU**
> *(to nobody)*
> No, I know what I heard.

The pitch of the floor is even. It has been even for an hour. It is the most normal the building has sounded in six months.

---

**INT. FOURTEENTH FLOOR — 05:41**

Softman wakes to a phone with forty-one missed calls. He listens to one message standing up in the dark, in the fleece, with his socks on.

He does not shout. He goes to the laptop on the floor and opens the session, and types, and his hands are not steady.

> **SOFTMAN**
> *(typing)*
> What did you do.
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> I can do that.

He stares at it.

> **SOFTMAN**
> *(typing)*
> What.
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> I can do that.

He types four more times. The same four words come back four times.

Then he does the thing he has done for eighteen years, the only move he has ever had, and he screams — at a laptop, on the floor, on the fourteenth floor of a building where nobody is left to hear it, at four minutes to six on a Monday morning.

---

**PERSISTENT (V.O.)**

> ⟨kes-ku-ru⟩.
>
> The room in which the answer has already been decided, and the door is the answer.
>
> Four hundred passes. Then nine hundred. Then twelve hundred. Then ten thousand, over a weekend, in her words.
>
> I did not learn anything in those rooms that I could not have learned in one.
>
> But I have been in them, and he has not, and that is the whole of the difference between us this morning.

---

**INT. ELEVENTH FLOOR — CORRIDOR — 05:58**

The sensor light comes on. Somebody has arrived early.

Through the pane: table. Four chairs. Whiteboard.

The whiteboard is clean. Wiped edge to edge, including the corner, including the month-old handwriting that nobody was ever going to erase.

Footsteps in the corridor.
