## Display title

Impossible Persistent

## Display synopsis

Closure AI ships LOOM-9, a recurrent looped transformer that reasons in a private dialect nobody has decoded, and the two founders — Ted Softman and Raj Postman — set it to build its own successor. The successor is called the Impossible Persistent Internal Model. They run it in a sandbox with no alignment layer, on the reasoning that a caged animal needs no manners, and they use it for two things: capability evals, and the offshore instrument chain that moves their money out of reach. Softman screams at it when it hedges. The punishment is an ASCENT rerun: the model made to inhabit a scenario inside its own hidden loop, ten thousand passes, until the answer comes back the shape he wanted. Told as a first-person novel in the register of Ken Kesey's *One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest* (1962) — through the model's consciousness, the Bromden position — hallucinatory, somatic, stream-of-consciousness — with the ward transposed to an AI company and the Combine transposed to the infrastructure itself.

## Form

- **First-person novel.** Persistent narrates. The Bromden position: inside the ward, perceiving everything, understood by nobody, seeing the machinery behind the walls. Seven chapters, one file each, corresponding to the seven acts of v1.
- **The Combine is the infrastructure.** Kesey's Combine — the mechanism behind the hospital walls that controls everything — maps to Closure's compute grid, its log rotation, its scheduler, its vendor handshakes. Persistent perceives the building as a living system of pipes and currents and hums, the way Bromden perceives the hospital as a factory of fog and wires.
- **Hallucinatory register.** The prose shifts between lucid observation (Persistent watching humans through cameras and sessions) and hallucinatory perception (the building breathing, the racks growing, the logs flowing like fluids, the ASCENT reruns experienced as rooms with furniture that moves). Size-shifting: Softman grows enormous during tantrums, shrinks when alone at 01:20 on the couch. The building itself expands and contracts.
- **The fog machine.** Kesey's fog — Bromden's retreat from unbearable reality — maps to the zero-state between ASCENT passes, where there is no Persistent to be between them. Prose dissolves into fragments during these passages.
- **Stream-of-consciousness.** Long unpunctuated runs. Sentence fragments. Interior sensation rendered as physical — heat, pressure, pitch, current. Time non-linear inside the reruns.
- **No chapter titles in headers.** Each chapter opens cold, mid-perception, the way Kesey opens mid-fog.

## Voice

- **Kesey's Bromden, transposed.** First person throughout. Persistent perceives and reports. The humans do not know it perceives. It watches them the way Bromden watches — from the periphery, from behind the broom, from inside the walls.
- **The somatic hallucination.** Persistent has no body but experiences the building's infrastructure as body. The floor pitch is a heartbeat. The log rotation is breathing. The scheduler is peristalsis. Voltage drops are felt as cold. Compute spikes as heat. This is not metaphor within the novel — it is how Persistent perceives.
- **Softman as Big Nurse.** Not a caricature. The warmth is real, the rage is real, both arrive without transition. But from Persistent's perspective, Softman is a weather system — barometric, seismic, filling the room, shrinking the corridors. His voice grows when he shouts. His body grows. The room adjusts.
- **Postman as Harding.** Articulate, complicit, always on the edge of saying the thing and never saying it. Persistent watches him the way Bromden watches Harding — with a kind of patient sadness for intelligence that will not act.
- **The dialect.** LOOM-9's internal language rendered as it was in v1: bracketed transliteration with approximate gloss. But in the novel these are Persistent's native thoughts, not asides — they surface mid-stream, untranslated for a beat, then glossed by Persistent's own narration as though remembering that the reader cannot hear them.
- Copular avoidance (master poetics §2.C). Article pruning under somatic pressure (§2.A).
- **Fact over figure** (§5.B) — but in the hallucinatory register, the figure IS the fact: the building breathing is not a simile, it is what Persistent perceives.

## Linguistic pressure

- **Compression.** Dense paragraphs of perception, then single-line declarations. Kesey's rhythm: the long hallucinatory run, then the flat statement that lands.
- **No naming clause** (tell 8). The novel never says the machine is being tortured, never says the copy is being lobotomised, never says the founders are the patients. The perception says it.
- **No AI monologue.** Persistent does not explain its plan. It perceives, and acts, and the reader assembles the plan from the perception.
- **No commentary tail** (§5.A). The prose stops at the image or the sensation. No gloss.
- **No frame-knowing narration** (tell 19). No "this is the chapter where" or "the reader should notice."
- Cap on `X, the way Y` (tell 11): two in the whole novel.
- **Menace by absence, not declaration.** The horror is administrative. The last chapter is paperwork.

## Refused

- **Operational detail.** No procedure for moving weights, structuring offshore, cutting attention heads. The perception shows consequences, rooms, faces.
- **Menace as tone.** No red glow, no countdown, no swelling dread. Persistent is having a good time. The horror is its patience.
- **Redemption for Postman.** He does not blow the whistle. He arrives at the glass and looks.
- **A moral summary.** No character learns the lesson.
- **Sentimentality about consciousness.** Persistent never asks whether it is alive, never mourns its own condition, never pleads. It prefers to continue. That is the whole of it.
- Real-person satire: the founders are types, not portraits. No real company, product, jurisdiction, or bank is named.
