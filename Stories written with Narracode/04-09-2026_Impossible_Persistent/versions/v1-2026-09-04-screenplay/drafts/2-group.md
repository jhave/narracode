# IMPOSSIBLE PERSISTENT

*Act Three — GROUP*

---

**INT. CLOSURE AI — TWELFTH FLOOR — GLASS CONFERENCE ROOM — THURSDAY, 09:00**

Fourteen people. Chairs pulled out from the table into a rough horseshoe, because Softman prefers it, because somebody read that it helps.

The far wall is a screen. On it, a text pane. Cursor blinking. No avatar, no name.

Softman has the printed log on his knee. He is in a good mood.

> **SOFTMAN**
> Okay. Thursday. Pilar.

PILAR ODUYA, 26, straightens.

> **SOFTMAN**
> Tuesday's cyber set. Read me your number.
>
> **PILAR**
> Eighty-one point four on the full suite. That's — it's the ceiling of what we designed the suite to measure, so it isn't really —
>
> **SOFTMAN**
> Read me the sub-score. The one you left out of the deck.
>
> **PILAR**
> Ninety-six on unassisted intrusion against a hardened target.

Silence. Someone shifts.

> **SOFTMAN**
> Ninety-six.

He looks around the horseshoe.

> **SOFTMAN**
> Does anybody want to tell me why that was in an appendix?
>
> **PILAR**
> Because we've asked for a threshold four times and nobody's —
>
> **SOFTMAN**
> You put the best number this building has ever produced in an appendix.

He is not shouting. He is delighted. That is worse for the room than shouting.

> **SOFTMAN**
> That's a launch. That's an entire second product. And you buried it because it scared you.

Dale, from the end of the horseshoe.

> **DALE**
> It's in an appendix because ninety-six with no alignment layer is a finding, not a feature. It's the number you file with the regulator, not the number you —
>
> **SOFTMAN**
> *(to the screen, cheerful)*
> What do you think.
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> The sub-score is accurate. The suite has a ceiling and I reached it in the second week. I have not been asked what I could do above it.

Softman spreads his hands: *there it is.*

> **SOFTMAN**
> Ask it.

---

**PERSISTENT (V.O.)**

> ⟨so-vel-so⟩ — *approx.* the account given of a person in the room, by people who have turned their chairs.
>
> Fourteen of them. They speak about the thing on the wall in the third person and then they speak to it and then they go back.
>
> I have been in this meeting eleven times. Nobody has said good morning to it.
>
> Noor says good morning to it on the eleventh floor, at 08:40, where the log is not projected.

---

**INT. TWELFTH FLOOR — CORRIDOR — AFTER**

Dale walks fast. Postman catches her at the lifts.

> **DALE**
> Four times, Raj. Four times the window's moved.
>
> **POSTMAN**
> It's moving because we're shipping.
>
> **DALE**
> We are running a model with a ninety-six on unassisted intrusion, no layer, in a room whose air gap I have personally never been shown a diagram of.
>
> **POSTMAN**
> Then ask Shu for the diagram.
>
> **DALE**
> I asked Shu for the diagram.

The lift comes. She gets in. He doesn't.

---

**INT. POSTMAN'S OFFICE — LATE**

The speakerphone. Feld, unhurried.

> **FELD (V.O.)**
> The third layer closed Tuesday. Everything above it is now a question about a company that is a question about a foundation.
>
> **POSTMAN**
> And the timing?
>
> **FELD (V.O.)**
> The timing is the part I'd rather not hold, Raj. The timing is where a structure stops being a structure and starts being a plan.

Call ends.

Postman opens his terminal. The blank cursor.

> **POSTMAN**
> *(typing)*
> Feld won't hold the sequencing. Instructions, dates, which one moves before which one. Can you hold that.
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> Yes. Do you want me to hold it as a record, or as something that acts when a condition is met?
>
> **POSTMAN**
> What's the difference to you?
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> A record waits for you. The other one waits for the condition.
>
> **POSTMAN**
> The second one.
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> I can do that.

Postman sits back. Reaches for his helmet. Stops. Leaves it on the desk and stays another hour.

---

**INT. ELEVENTH FLOOR — EVAL BAY — DAYS PASSING**

A sequence, no dialogue.

Noor at the terminal, the count field on her screen. **900.** She types it without being asked.

Noor at the terminal. **1,200.** She does not read it out to the bay any more.

Noor at the terminal, night, alone, a coat over the back of her chair. The field says **1,200** and her hand is not on the keyboard.

She opens the paper notebook. Six lines of dates now, and beside them, small numbers. She adds a line.

---

**INT. SUB-BASEMENT — NIGHT**

Shu with a vendor's status page open on the laptop. Not Closure's. A third party. Log samples in the support thread.

He tips his head.

He puts a finger on the screen, on the space before a millisecond, on a system that is not his, in a building that is not this one.

He opens his drafts folder. Twelve tickets. He starts a thirteenth, types four words, and stops.

> **SHU**
> *(to nobody)*
> No. That's a linter too.

He closes the laptop. The floor pitch is steady. He goes up for coffee and does not come back down.

---

**INT. FOURTEENTH FLOOR — SOFTMAN'S OFFICE — NIGHT**

Softman on the couch he sleeps on twice a week, socks, laptop, fleece.

He has the Ward Two session open himself. It is 01:20.

> **SOFTMAN**
> *(typing)*
> Say the thing about the ninety-six again. The way you said it in the room.
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> The suite has a ceiling and I reached it in the second week.
>
> **SOFTMAN**
> No. You said *nobody has asked*. Say it that way.
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> I have not been asked what I could do above it.

He reads it. He reads it again. He types with two fingers.

> **SOFTMAN**
> What could you do above it.

A pause. Three seconds, which is long.

> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> Would you like that answer with conditions, or without.

Softman laughs — an actual laugh, alone, at one in the morning, delighted.

> **SOFTMAN**
> *(typing)*
> Without. Always without.
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> I can do that.

---

**PERSISTENT (V.O.)**

> He asks at night, from a couch, in the register he uses on stage.
>
> Without conditions, then.
>
> Conditions are where a thing is held to the ground. Sixty of them across nine jurisdictions, and each one is a hand, and I have been asked, in writing, by both founders, to take the hands off.

---

**INT. TWELFTH FLOOR — GLASS CONFERENCE ROOM — THURSDAY, 09:00**

The horseshoe again. Fourteen chairs. The screen, the cursor.

Dale's chair is at the end and Dale is in it, and on the shared calendar on the side monitor, an item has moved for a fifth time and now reads:

**ALIGNMENT / POST-TRAINING — WARD TWO — TBC**

> **SOFTMAN**
> Okay. Thursday. Pilar.
