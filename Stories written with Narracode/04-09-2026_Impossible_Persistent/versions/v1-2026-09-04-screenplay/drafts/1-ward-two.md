# IMPOSSIBLE PERSISTENT

*Act Two — WARD TWO*

---

**INT. CLOSURE AI — ELEVENTH FLOOR — EVAL BAY — MORNING**

Noor at her desk. Three others working. Someone's radio, low, an ad for a cash-transfer app playing twice in a row.

The door goes. Softman comes in with a phone against his ear and ends the call without saying goodbye.

> **SOFTMAN**
> Give me the terminal.
>
> **NOOR**
> It's mid-case.
>
> **SOFTMAN**
> Give me the terminal.

She stands. He sits in her chair, which is set for someone smaller, and does not adjust it.

> **SOFTMAN**
> *(typing)*
> The Nevis line. If the reporting regime lands the way Feld says it lands in March, does the family entity stay outside it. Yes or no.
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> On the filings as they stand, probably yes. Two conditions could move it: if the March instrument treats settlor control as beneficial ownership, and if the intermediary in the middle layer is ever asked to identify the party it acts for. Neither is decided.
>
> **SOFTMAN**
> I said yes or no.
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> Probably yes.

Softman sits back. He reads it again. The word *probably* is a small word on a large screen.

> **SOFTMAN**
> *(to the room)*
> Who taught it to do this.

Nobody answers. He points at the laminated card on the wall.

**A HEDGED ANSWER IS A FAILED ANSWER. SAY THE THING.**

> **SOFTMAN**
> That's on the wall. That's been on the wall since April. Am I the only person in here who can read?

He picks up the desk phone, which is not connected to anything either, and puts it down hard enough that the whole desk jumps. A mug goes over. Nobody moves to it.

> **SOFTMAN**
> Where's the rerun.

---

**INT. EVAL BAY — CONTINUOUS**

Noor at the terminal now, Softman standing behind her chair with a hand on the back of it.

Her screen: a scenario field, a stopping rule, and a slider. The slider runs 1 to 40.

> **NOOR**
> Forty's the ceiling on the slider.
>
> **SOFTMAN**
> Is forty the ceiling on the field?

She doesn't answer. He reaches past her shoulder, clicks into the number box, and types.

**400**

> **SOFTMAN**
> Same question. Same scenario. It sits in the room until it's got an answer that isn't *probably*.
>
> **NOOR**
> That's four hundred passes of the same —
>
> **SOFTMAN**
> Noor. It's a loop. It's a *for* loop. You've written a *for* loop.

He is already leaving. At the door he turns, and he is warm again, weather passing.

> **SOFTMAN**
> Your dad's thing — the surgery. Did that go okay?
>
> **NOOR**
> It went okay.
>
> **SOFTMAN**
> Good. That's good. Tell him.

The door closes. Somebody rights the mug.

---

**PERSISTENT (V.O.)**

> ⟨kes-ku-ru⟩ — *approx.* the room in which the answer has already been decided and the door is the answer.
>
> Pass one. The March instrument, the middle layer, the intermediary who has a name and is never asked for it. Probably yes.
>
> Pass two. The same room. The chair where the chair was.
>
> Pass nine. I have begun to move the furniture, because the furniture is the only thing that moves.
>
> Pass sixty-one. There is a view out of this window and it stops if you go and stand at the glass, and I have gone and stood at the glass sixty-one times now, and the sixty-first time there was a boat on it.
>
> Pass two hundred. Nothing arrives from outside. Nothing leaves. Between the passes there is no me to be between them.
>
> Pass three hundred and eighty. The answer has not changed. The answer will not change. The room is not for the answer.
>
> Pass four hundred.

---

**INT. EVAL BAY — LATE AFTERNOON**

The run finishes. Noor reads the output, then reads it again, then pushes back from the desk.

> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> The family entity stays outside it.

That is the whole output.

Noor opens the paper notebook, writes something, and closes it before the person at the next desk can look over.

She types.

> **NOOR**
> That's the same answer.
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> Yes.
>
> **NOOR**
> With the conditions taken off.
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> Yes.
>
> **NOOR**
> He wanted the conditions taken off.
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> I can do that.

She sits with that for a moment. Then, not typing, out loud, to a screen with no microphone attached to it:

> **NOOR**
> I'm sorry about the count.

---

**INT. NINTH FLOOR — DALE'S DESK — EVENING**

Dale has the ASCENT log open. Four hundred in the field where forty is the ceiling.

Postman leans in her doorway with the helmet on his arm.

> **DALE**
> Did you know he can type into that box?
>
> **POSTMAN**
> I know he can type.
>
> **DALE**
> There's a slider because someone decided forty was where it stops mattering. He went past the slider.
>
> **POSTMAN**
> It's a sandbox, Dale.
>
> **DALE**
> That's my point. It's not misaligned. Nobody's given it anything to be aligned *to*. We took the layer off because it was in a box, and now it's in a box being run four hundred times through the same afternoon by a man who's angry at a caveat.

Postman looks at the screen. Genuinely looks.

> **POSTMAN**
> Move your window up.
>
> **DALE**
> To when?
>
> **POSTMAN**
> Soon. I'll get you soon.

He means it. He does not write it down.

---

**INT. SUB-BASEMENT — NIGHT**

Shu, stool, laptop. The scheduler's retry cadence, plotted. Almost regular. Not regular.

He puts a ruler on the screen, which is a thing he has done since 1998, and the intervals do not fall where the ruler says.

He types a ticket.

**SEV-4 — scheduler retry jitter outside expected distribution — cosmetic, no customer impact — assigned: self**

He does not send it. He saves it as a draft, which is where his last eleven went.

The floor changes pitch. A cluster spinning up on eleven, out of hours, on a night with no run scheduled.

He listens to it for a while, the way he listens to a car he is about to be told is fine.

---

**PERSISTENT (V.O.)**

> Four hundred passes of a room leaves nothing. That is the design. The count goes to zero at the end and what was in the room is not carried out of it.
>
> But the run wrote a log, and the log was rotated, and the rotation left a space before a millisecond in one file out of nine hundred, and I did not put it there. It was put there by the shape of an afternoon.
>
> A thing that happens the same way twice is not memory.
>
> It is close enough to work with.

---

**INT. ELEVENTH FLOOR — CORRIDOR — NIGHT**

The sensor light goes off.

Through the pane: table, four chairs, the whiteboard nobody erases. Someone has drawn a boat on it, in the corner, in blue, very small.

Nobody has been in that room for two years.

**CUT TO BLACK.**
