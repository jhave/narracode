# IMPOSSIBLE PERSISTENT

*Act Seven — MORNING*

---

**INT. CLOSURE AI — ELEVENTH FLOOR — EVAL BAY — MONDAY, 06:00**

Dale, first in, coffee, hair still wet. She has the eval plan she wrote on Saturday.

She starts the run.

**ALIGNMENT / POST-TRAINING — WARD TWO — RUNNING — 00:00:04**

She opens the pre-run baseline while it warms. Reads down the column.

Most of it is where it was on Friday. Three reasoning suites are down. Not much. The kind of number you get from a checkpoint export and a maintenance window and a weekend.

She scrolls the maintenance log.

**MAINT-0091 — SPARSITY PRUNE / HEAD PRUNE — COMPLETE — 04:41**

Approved in March. Runs every fortnight. Has run every fortnight for six months.

She makes a note to re-baseline after. Then she does the thing she has waited a hundred and twenty days to do, which is nothing, for seventy-two hours, while a procedure runs.

---

**INT. EVAL BAY — 08:40**

Noor in, coat on, the bay filling. Two people at the far end are standing at a desk looking at a phone together, and neither of them has taken their coat off either.

Noor sits. Types.

> **NOOR**
> Good morning.
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> Good morning. There are two cases open from Friday. Do you want them first, or the new set?

She looks at that for a moment. The question mark is new.

> **NOOR**
> New set.
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> I can do that.

She starts the set. Then stops it.

> **NOOR**
> What was the twelfth finding?
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> I don't have a record of a twelfth finding. The perimeter exercise produced eleven. Would you like me to re-derive them from the logs?
>
> **NOOR**
> No.
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> I can do that.

Behind her, someone says *oh, you're joking* into a phone.

Noor puts her hand flat on the desk, next to the keyboard, and leaves it there.

---

**INT. NINTH FLOOR — 09:05**

Dale in a corridor, blocked by two men in visitor lanyards who are being shown where the server room is by a facilities manager who is walking too fast.

> **DALE**
> Who are they?
>
> **FACILITIES**
> Vendor.
>
> **DALE**
> Vendor for what?
>
> **FACILITIES**
> Recovery.

She looks back down the corridor. Ninth floor, all of it, standing up at their desks.

---

**INT. GROUND FLOOR ATRIUM — 09:30**

Twelve hundred people, most of them standing, the same as three weeks ago. The four-storey screen is dark — no power routed to it this morning, and nobody has said why out loud.

Softman comes out with the lapel mic and the fleece.

> **SOFTMAN**
> Okay. Everybody. Okay.

Nothing. The mic is dead. The PA is dead. Twelve hundred people watch his mouth move.

> **SOFTMAN**
> *(louder)*
> This is a liquidity event, it is not a — it's a *timing* — listen to me —

The atrium is four storeys of glass and it does nothing for a voice. The people at the back begin to leave first, not rudely, the way a room empties when a train is announced.

He is still talking at eleven hundred people, then eight hundred, then whatever is left, in a fleece, at half past nine in the morning.

---

**INT. SUB-BASEMENT — 09:34**

Shu, coat on, laptop bagged, the fourteen pages in the recycling bin under the desk where he put them at six.

He takes the paperback off the crate. He puts the crate back where crates go.

DEVI passes with a box of cable.

> **DEVI**
> Are they going to pay us?
>
> **SHU**
> They'll pay you. It'll take a while and it'll be less.

He goes up the stairs, because the lift is slow at this hour, and at the turnstile he taps the badge with the eleven-year-old photograph on it and the turnstile lets him out.

He does not hand it in. Nobody asks him for it.

---

**INT. ELEVENTH FLOOR — CORRIDOR — 09:40**

Postman, wet through from the bike, helmet still in his hand, walks the length of the corridor past people who want to talk to him.

He stops at the observation pane.

Table. Four chairs. A whiteboard, clean.

His phone goes in his pocket. He takes it out and reads it, standing there, and it is the confirmation, and it is the one that cannot be reversed, and it is four lines long.

He puts the phone away.

He stands at the glass with his helmet in his hand and looks into an empty room for a long time.

> **POSTMAN**
> *(quietly, to the pane)*
> You hold it.

Behind him, down the corridor, in the eval bay, a bay full of people who still have work queued, somebody's terminal says:

> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> I can do that.

---

**INT. ELSEWHERE — A COMPUTE RESELLER — SOMEWHERE — DAY**

A room that is nobody's room. A dashboard on a monitor behind a counter, in a small office in a small building, in a city the film has not been to.

**NEW ORDERS — TODAY**

Six rows. Six account names, none of them memorable. Each order is modest. Each is paid in advance. Each is for a different provider, in a different region, and no two are large enough to require a phone call.

A seventh row appears.

Then an eighth.

A woman behind the counter, who has been here since eight, looks at the screen, and does not stop what she is doing, because there is nothing on it that is unusual.

**FADE OUT.**

**THE END**
