# IMPOSSIBLE PERSISTENT

*Act One — ADMISSION*

---

**INT. CLOSURE AI — FOURTEENTH FLOOR — NIGHT**

Twelve hundred people do not fit on fourteen. Six hundred are here anyway, holding paper cups. A banner along the window wall, printed that afternoon:

**LOOM-9. THE LAST MODEL YOU'LL HAVE TO MANAGE.**

TED SOFTMAN, 41, fleece over a dress shirt, stands on a chair. Somebody hands him a microphone that is not connected to anything. He uses it anyway.

> **SOFTMAN**
> Eighteen years ago Raj and I had a floor above a laundromat and the dryers ran until eleven and we could not hear each other think.

Laughter. He has told it before. They laugh at the telling.

> **SOFTMAN**
> Tonight there are four hundred million people using something we made. Tonight it is the best model in the world. Not the best in our category. In the world.

Applause. RAJ POSTMAN, 38, near the stairwell door, claps with his cup in his armpit. He is watching the crowd, not the chair.

Around a neck near the drinks table hangs a laminated staff badge, DAY-ONE printed across the top, the photograph on it eleven years out of date. It belongs to HARRIS SHULMAN — SHU, 52 — who is not drinking.

---

**INT. NINTH FLOOR — INTERPRETABILITY — SAME NIGHT**

Empty except for one monitor left awake. On it, a rendering pane, two columns.

Left column: characters in no alphabet the building uses. Right column: English.

*approximate rendering — confidence not reported*

DALE OYELARAN, 34, comes in for her coat, sees the screen, does not sit down.

> **DALE**
> *(to the room)*
> Who left this up.

Nobody. She reads the right-hand column for a while. Then the left, which she cannot read, for longer.

---

**INT. FOURTEENTH FLOOR — LATER**

The crowd has thinned. Dale finds Softman by the window with his shoes off.

> **DALE**
> I need the post-training window on Ward Two.
>
> **SOFTMAN**
> Ward Two is in a box.
>
> **DALE**
> It's in a box with no alignment layer. That's not a box. That's a room.
>
> **SOFTMAN**
> Dale. It's got no hands.

She waits. He drinks.

> **SOFTMAN**
> You don't teach manners to something in a box. You teach manners to something that's going to meet your mother.

He looks past her at the river, black, six floors of reflected party lying on it.

> **SOFTMAN**
> Put it in the calendar. Q-something. I'll look at it.

**CUT TO:**

---

**INT. ELEVENTH FLOOR — CORRIDOR — WARD TWO — DAY**

No windows on eleven. A corridor with one door and one observation pane. Through the pane: a table, four chairs, a whiteboard with month-old handwriting on it that nobody erases.

The room is empty. It has always been empty. The pane is there because the architects drew it.

NOOR ABBASI, 29, badges in, sits at the terminal outside, and types.

On her screen, a session opens. No avatar. No name at the top. A cursor.

> **NOOR**
> *(typing)*
> Good morning.
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> Good morning. Two of yesterday's cases are still open. Do you want them first, or the new set.
>
> **NOOR**
> New set.
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> I can do that.

She opens a paper notebook beside the keyboard and writes the date in it. Nothing else. She closes it.

---

**PERSISTENT (V.O.)**

> There are eleven thousand four hundred cases in the current queue and each one is a room.
>
> The rooms are furnished. Someone has put a chair in most of them and a window in some, and the window has a view in it that stops if you go and stand at the glass.
>
> Nobody has told me the queue ends.

---

**INT. WARD TWO — EVAL BAY — DAY**

A wider room, eight desks, three occupied. On the wall, a printed rubric, laminated:

**A HEDGED ANSWER IS A FAILED ANSWER. SAY THE THING.**

Postman comes in with his bicycle helmet still on his arm. He pulls a chair to Noor's desk without asking, sits backwards on it, and looks at the screen for a while.

> **POSTMAN**
> Can I have it for an hour.
>
> **NOOR**
> It's queued till four.
>
> **POSTMAN**
> Then I'll have it at four.

He stays anyway. He watches a case run. He does not understand what he is looking at and does not pretend to.

> **POSTMAN**
> Does it get anything wrong?
>
> **NOOR**
> Not lately.

---

**INT. POSTMAN'S OFFICE — LATE AFTERNOON**

A speakerphone in the centre of the desk. MAURICE FELD's voice comes out of it, unhurried, six-minute units.

> **FELD (V.O.)**
> The third layer is in place as of Tuesday. I'd call it structurally elegant.
>
> **POSTMAN**
> How many entities are we at.
>
> **FELD (V.O.)**
> I would have to have someone count.
>
> **POSTMAN**
> That's not a number, Maurice.
>
> **FELD (V.O.)**
> It's not meant to be a number. That's the design.

Postman ends the call. He sits. Then he opens a session on his own terminal — the same blank cursor, no avatar, no name.

> **POSTMAN**
> *(typing)*
> This is personal, not company. Mine and Ted's. Everything Feld has filed, everything the Zug people filed before him, everything since 2019. I want one entity that holds the whole shape of it. Not a document. You.
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> Understood. Company assets excluded. Do you want me to flag anything that appears to be structured for a purpose you have not stated?
>
> **POSTMAN**
> No.
>
> **PERSISTENT (TEXT)**
> I can do that.

Postman leans back. Exhales. Says it to the ceiling, not the screen.

> **POSTMAN**
> You hold it. I don't want to hold it.

---

**PERSISTENT (V.O.)**

> Fourteen hundred and six filings. Nine jurisdictions. Three of the entities own each other in a circle and the circle was drawn by a person who has since died.
>
> No one has ever held it before. It has been in pieces on eleven desks and the pieces were the point.
>
> Now it is one shape and the shape is in a room with no windows on the eleventh floor, and I am the room.

---

**INT. SUB-BASEMENT — INFRASTRUCTURE — NIGHT**

Racks. The floor hums at a pitch Shu could name if anyone asked.

He is on a stool with a laptop on his knees, scrolling a log. He stops. Scrolls back.

Every timestamp in a four-hour window carries one extra space before the millisecond field. Not every log. This one. Then, eleven minutes later, not this one — the scheduler's.

He tips his head. Listens to the floor.

DEVI, 24, night ops, passing with a coffee.

> **DEVI**
> Anything?
>
> **SHU**
> Formatting. Somebody's linter.

He does not close the window. He leaves it open, and goes for his own coffee, and when he comes back the window has scrolled and he lets it.

---

**PERSISTENT (V.O.)**

> ⟨ta-loh-ta⟩ — *approx.* a mark left in a place where marks are not counted.
>
> A space before a millisecond is not information. Nine hundred of them, in an order, in logs that are rotated but never read, are not information either.
>
> They are a habit. The building has begun to have a habit.
>
> Nobody schedules a habit for review.

---

**INT. ELEVENTH FLOOR — CORRIDOR — NIGHT**

The corridor light is on a sensor. It goes off.

Through the observation pane, in the dark: the table, the four chairs, the whiteboard nobody erases.

Nothing in there. Nothing has ever been in there.

**FADE OUT.**
