## Expectations

After the opening letter, the reader will likely expect:
- A romance frame between narrator and partner.
- Some kind of plot pressure tied to her work.
- A revelation about the science, eventually.

The book must defy all three, slowly. The romance is real but conditioned. The work pressure is structural, not plot. The revelation arrives only as recognition that returns and dissolves.

## Plausible Defiance Paths

The book may seem to be setting up a thriller about scientific malfeasance and then refuse the thriller entirely.
The book may seem to be setting up a love story and then reveal that the love is implicated.
The book may seem to be setting up a recognition arc and then withhold the final recognition.
All three are intended.

## Productive Mystery

What exactly is happening to the narrator. (The reader should suspect before she does; the prose should never confirm.)
Whether the partner suspects. (The prose should never tell us either way.)
Whether the sibling/friend knows.
What the final letter means.

## Accidental Confusion Risk

The unnamed setting could read as vague rather than universal if details do not feel materially specific. The cherry tree, the AR diode, the ink-pooling — these particulars carry the universality.

The partner's pronoun and history could read as theme if foregrounded. Must be established lightly, treated as background.

The bio-tech vocabulary could read as info-dump if the cascade-and-clarify motion is not respected. Always: the cascade first, then the unraveling. Or, deliberately: the cascade with no unraveling.
