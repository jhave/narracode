# Motifs

## Active

**Paper, pen, friction.** The pleasure of handwriting. Ink-pooling at the end of a stroke. Slow.

**AR glasses on the bedside table.** Lenses dimmed, the small green readiness diode. The act of removing them is the act of beginning to write.

**The attic room.** The window. The cherry tree, its changing blossoms. The weather. The samenesses.

**The east-facing waiting room.** Late sun on the wall the narrator has begun to prefer. (Site of the wet edit's most banal expression.)

**The senior researcher's voice.** Its temperature. The way it has begun to sit in her chest.

**The word *kind* in her own speech.** The word *warm*. The phrase *I think I'd like to*.

**A music she did not used to like.** Now humming back from her without her cueing it.

**Wildfire haze.** Baseline color, not weather event.

**The translations as inset blocks.** Clean, accurate, sometimes startlingly loving. The cascade-and-clarify shape inside them.

**The glossary that accretes.** Words the narrator uses without translating: LinCx, connexin, gap junction, AAV, peptidein, dark proteome, ORFness, indeterminate potential, pan-essential, cryptic antigen, exploratory drive, social preference, thermal preference, PLM, ESMFold, ProteinMPNN, semantic decoder, intention readout, telepathy interface.

## Transformations

Most motifs follow the same arc: appear → recur → become hers → stop being noticed by her. The stopping is the absorption into baseline. The reader notices what the narrator no longer notices.

## Avoid Overuse

The AR glasses gesture: at most once per few letters, after the opening; otherwise the camouflage becomes self-conscious.

The senior researcher's voice: must not become a fixation. Light touches, accumulating.

The word *kind*: appears unflagged the first three times. After that the narrator may notice it, briefly, then forget noticing.
