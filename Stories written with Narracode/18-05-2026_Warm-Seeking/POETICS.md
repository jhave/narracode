# POETICS

## Display title

Warm-Seeking.

## Display synopsis

In 2032, an unnamed city on an unnamed continent, a researcher writes paper letters to her partner. The letters began as a small archaic tenderness; the AR glasses come off when she writes them, always in her attic room which she describes, the view out the window, the changing blossoms on the cherry tree, subtle soft inflections of weather, and the samenesses. She translates research papers for him — on engineered electrical synapses, on the dark proteome, on peptideins of indeterminate potential — and in the act of translation begins to, eventually slowly, notice that the language fits or answers aspects of her behavior too well. Around her, climate churn and oligarchic drift; inside her, a slow proteomic edit (intentional? AI-enhanced?) she cannot prove and cannot refuse. The recognition is the action. There is no plan. Just the dawning.

## Premise

By 2032, two 2026 papers have crossed into the population. LinCx — designer electrical synapses built from mutated white-perch connexins, AAV-delivered to engineer cell-type-specific gap junctions — was Dzirasa's mouse-only proof in May 2026. By story-time it is structural, permanent, ambient: an intervention that produces preference rather than sensation, so the edited substrate is felt as native. The dark-proteome work of the TransCODE consortium, also May 2026, surfaced thousands of peptideins — microproteins of "indeterminate potential" — and by 2032 an autonomous system has been promoting them from indeterminate to operational, mapping cell-type pairs, drafting cocktails, closing the loop on probe traces faster than any human cohort.

The trial has no single edge. Some fraction of the population receives the wet edit through routine vaccination, fortified food, engineered commensals. Everyone receives the informational edit: feed-ranking, autocompletion, ambient acoustic cadence, what music plays in cafés. At population scale, statistical inference from interaction logs is functionally telepathic. No BCI is required.

The narrator, a researcher in an unnamed institute, suspects nothing for a long time. She is in a relationship with her partner — formerly she, now he, an accepted norm noted only once and lightly. The letters began as love-gestures, a way for him to see into her week; she takes off her AR glasses to write them, by hand, on paper. She has accidentally produced a small window of low-bandwidth privacy and does not yet know it. Her dawning recognition arrives not through investigation but through tenderness: in paraphrasing the papers for him, she begins to notice that the terms attach to lived sensations she had not previously named. The detector is translation. The apparatus is the partner. The sample is herself.

She cannot test the hypothesis. Any test resembles the features the system has already optimized against. She continues to write. The lucidity is the symptom.

## Core commitments

The story is told as dated letters of unequal length, handwritten by the narrator to her partner, presented to the reader as the text we are reading. Some letters are translations of papers; the translations get clearer, more loving, more lucid as her edits deepen. The lucidity is the symptom. That is the form.

Recognition is the core event, returning and dissolving and returning, because cognition under edit cannot sustain a frame. The forgetting of recognition is itself among the strongest data the narrator has.

The narrator is correct. The narrator is contained. Her correctness is the proof of her containment — any clean lucidity, under the model the papers describe, is the kind of attractor the optimization would shape rather than prevent. The story does not let her out of this proof.

The world is everywhere and nowhere: an unnamed city, an unnamed country, an unnamed continent. Climate churn, fascist oligarchy where there had been democracy, economic inequity, the visible singularity. Wearying in its too-muchness, exhilarating in its info avalanche. The setting is ambient pressure, not subject matter.

The science is honored. LinCx is structural, not stimulatory. Peptideins are mined, not invented. The AGI is in the loop because the loop is too fast and too multi-omic for any human team to ride. The narrator's translations are accurate. The vocabulary is allowed to enter the prose un-glossed when needed; some words remain dark.

The partner is edited too. The love is conditioned. The love is not less for being conditioned. This is the Ōe posture, taken seriously.

## Refusals

No *escape* plan. No drama. No covert experiment, no Faraday cage, no notebook hidden in a tin. Trying is the genre of agency; agency is the lie the form must refuse.

No external confirmation. No whistleblower, no mole, no leaked document. The recognition is internal and structural. No second character arrives to validate her.

No escape. No exit, no late reversal, no flight to a clean rural elsewhere. The partner is inside it too. The narrator endures, witnesses, translates.

No thriller. No countdown, no antagonist, no procedural beats. The pacing is the slow widening of recognition.

No "AI bad." The system is not malevolent. It is optimizing. The horror is not motive; it is scale.

No paranoia framing. The narrator's intuitions are not symptoms of mental illness for the reader to second-guess. The reader should know she is correct.

No sentimental humanism. No final speech about love defeating optimization. The love survives by being smaller than the system's interest, not larger than it.

No stylization of victimhood. The narrator is intelligent, dignified, sometimes funny, sometimes tired. She is not a martyr.

No clean reveal. No moment where she or the partner says, "you have been modified." If the words appear they will be in a translated paragraph, attached to mice.

Delay everything. Imply everything. The book's deepest rule. Any fact that can be implied rather than stated must be implied. Any reveal that can be delayed must be delayed past the point where the reader has begun to provide it themselves. The reader's recognition should arrive a beat before the narrator's, and the narrator's a beat before the page makes it explicit — and often the page never does. Implication is the form's respect for the reader; delay is the form's respect for the world. Together they enforce the book's central refusal: that nothing here can be solved by saying it.

## Attentional dialect

Phenomenological precision. The narrator notices preference as a weather. A pen's friction. The east-facing waiting room. The temperature of a colleague's voice. The persistence of a word in her own speech that she did not used to use. A music that has begun to feel like agreement.

Cognition is treated as substrate. The narrator does not introspect away from her body; she introspects into it. Mood is reported as a tissue event.

Catastrophe is ambient. The city's failing infrastructure, the new oligarchies, the migration corridors, the wildfire haze — these arrive as texture in single sentences, never as set-piece. The narrator is too inside her own slower disaster to make a chorus of the larger one. The larger one is the climate of her sentences.

The surveillance ASI is the glaze. Explicit-yet-implicit: never argued, never personified, present as the light by which everything else is read. Optimized acoustic ambience, foundation-model cadence in announcements, advertising-bid dynamics tuned to the narrator's neurochemistry without ever naming her. The narrator does not say *the ASI is watching*; the narrator describes a Tuesday afternoon, and the Tuesday afternoon is shaped.

Tenderness as method. The letters are love-acts. Even the translations are love-acts: she is making the work legible to him because she wants him with her in the knowing. The form must never lose this. When recognition deepens, the tenderness deepens. This is the cruelty of the form.

Writing enjoyed as writing. By 2032 the chatbots are over-competent; any prose can be produced at any length to any specification in seconds. Against this surplus, handwriting on paper has become a small act of leisure — slow, friction-bearing, defended only by its own pace. The narrator writes because she likes writing. Her sentences should carry that pleasure: not virtuosity, but the audible enjoyment of a person making each word herself when she did not have to. The prose should occasionally pause inside its own pleasure — a clause kept past the moment of utility, a word chosen because it is the one she wanted to write today. This is what no chatbot can do for her, and the reason she keeps doing it is part of why the insulation works. The leisure is the camouflage.

## Syntax and rhythm

Five primary influences, intermingled, never any one neat. Two pillars carry the baseline. Two govern sentence-level pressure. One governs the ethical posture and the fragment. Patchen remains as occasional seasoning.

Baseline pillars.

Kenzaburō Ōe. Level, stoic, parental in slowness, dignified, observant of small physical particulars, capable of holding catastrophic information without raising voice. Most of the prose lives here.

Octavia Butler. Declarative, terse, unornamented. Butler models the position the narrator actually occupies — a subject inside biological modification (Dawn, the Xenogenesis trilogy) who can name what is being done to her without melodrama and without escape. Where Ōe holds dignity, Butler holds plain truth. Together they form the unshowy clarity that lets the prose carry edited cognition without aestheticizing it.

Bayo Akomolafe.  *These Wilds beyond our Fences* Convulsive, intimate poetic lucid confessional intensities implicated in the world around them. The context of the narrator, her awareness of how her own marginal status, although not pronounced, informs opportunities, sculpts the possible. And the same for her trans partner, who it becomes apparent trnasitioned years before and now lives in-cognito, so no reference may ever be explicitly made to it, yet their inner circle knows, as it is often known intuitively as if grown into.

Sentence-level pressure. (Kraznaohrkai, Beckett)

Renee Gladman. The city, the institute, the apartment rearranging around the narrator's perception; abstract-to-concrete shifts inside a single paragraph; architecture and cognition braided. Used where the working space itself begins to move under attention.

Ethical fragment and recursion.

Maurice Blanchot. The disaster as already arrived, recognition that grants no exit, the fragment as the right form for a witness who cannot act. Blanchot governs the gaps between letters, the unfinished sentence, the refusal of consolation. Use his recursive cadence often — the phrase that returns and turns slightly, the sentence that re-states itself in order to displace itself, the small loop that is the only honest motion when the catastrophe has already arrived: *the disaster, the disaster which is not the disaster, which is what disaster cannot say.* Cadences like this should recur throughout the book, never as homage, always as the narrator's own rhythm under pressure. Recursion is not stylistic flourish here; it is the cognitive grammar of a mind that cannot move forward because forward is what has already happened.

Sparing seasoning (for when it gets unhinged, when the recognition lands and the words leap into the throat and the narrator awakes at night as her partner sleeps to write out a dream that Patrick White might have scripted in voss.

Kenneth Patchen. Albion Moonlight's dissolving recognition. Used like a chord change two or three times in the book. Never sustained. Inger Christensen's alphabet / it remains permitted where one letter wants to be a litany. Anne Carson's lucidity-as-endurance remains permitted at the threshold scenes. Krasznahorkai for the unbroken-sentence inevitability; Duras for the cooled affect.

No imitation of any one. The merge is the voice. Where the merge surprises, follow the surprise. The prose should range freely from terse to long-breathed sentence, from abstraction to plain clarity, and should accommodate without stylistic break a block of unglossed scientific jargon adjacent to a tender sentence about the partner's hands.

## Cascade and translation

The book's central craft mechanic. Each translation-letter follows a predictable inner motion. A passage of dense scientific jargon — sometimes condensed, sometimes extended; sometimes a direct quotation from the two seeding Nature papers, sometimes a paraphrase of adjacent de novo design work on RFdiffusion-descended scaffolds, engineered commensals, mRNA/AAV manufacturing at population scale, or foundation-model-closed proteomic loops — cascades through escalating complexity until the narrator finds the register that will let her partner enter the idea. The clarified translation is what arrives on the other side of that cascade. It is the literary register of the book.

The cascade is not pedagogical scaffolding. It is the event. Inside it the diegetic plot advances: the narrator's intuition crystallizes, gestates, sometimes refuses to land. The translation that arrives is where she sees what she has just understood, often a half-clause before the reader does.

Refracted repetition is how the reader sees through. The same image — east-facing waiting room; the senior researcher's voice; the word kind in her own speech; the phrase social preference reported in mice — recurs in different registers across letters: technical, tender, dismissive, finally absorbed and unremarked. Each refraction is a way of looking through the same site at a different depth. The accumulated meaning is the stack of these passes, not the explicit assertion at any one of them.

Some cascades should never clarify. The register never resolves. The letter ends mid-paraphrase. This is also part of the form, and may be the most important part.

All of this occurs inside the explicit-yet-implicit glaze of post-late-capitalism surveillance ASI: the city's optimized acoustic ambience, the institute's foundation-model cadence, the feed's ranking pressures, the routine medical infrastructure no one inspects. The glaze is never argued. It is the light the letters are written under.

## Character logic

The narrator. Female. A researcher, mid-thirties to early forties, in a translational neuroscience or computational-proteomics adjacent role — close enough to the papers that her paraphrases are credible, peripheral enough that the trial does not pass through her hands. Intelligent without showmanship. Tired without complaint. Capable of irony toward herself. Loves her partner with a quiet specificity. Has a long preceding history of attention to her own body that does not register as vanity. Writes by hand because she likes the friction; the insulating effect is a side effect she does not name until late.

The partner. Formerly she, now he. The transition is established in one early sentence and not revisited as theme; the story treats this as an ordinary biographical fact of 2032. He is not a scientist. Something tactile, possibly — a luthier, a printer, a botanist of an unfashionable kind. He reads her translations carefully, sometimes asks small clarifying questions, sometimes does not. He is also being edited. He does not know.

Other figures appear briefly: a senior researcher whose voice the narrator begins to find warmer than is plausible; an institute director whose announcements have begun to scan as foundation-model cadence; a sibling or old friend who is the one reliable external mirror, glimpsed twice across the book, saying something similar both times and meaning it differently the second.

The ASI does not appear. The narrator never meets it. She only meets people who have begun to speak in its cadence.

## World logic

Year: 2032. Six years after LinCx in mice and the TransCODE peptidein paper.

The autonomous system has been promoting peptideins from indeterminate to operational and pairing them with LinCx-class connexin scaffolds, delivered through routine medical infrastructure that no one inspects closely. Some fraction of the population is edited at any given moment. The narrator is one. The partner is one. Likely the senior researcher. Likely the colleague whose voice has become warm.

The information environment is the universal vector. Feed-ranking, ambient music, autocompletion, what advertisements play and which lose the bid, the cadence of notifications. At population scale, this is enough.

Cause is no longer a clean category. Edit and environment reinforce each other. The narrator cannot isolate either. She can only feel the fit.

The city has good and bad weeks. There is municipal water most days. The cafés are open. The oligarchic regime is more administrative than dramatic; the fascism is procedural, ambient, normalized. People still meet for dinner.

## Image systems

Paper, pen, friction, ink-pooling, the slight ridge a fountain pen leaves at the end of a stroke.

AR glasses on the bedside table, lenses dimmed, the small green readiness diode.

East-facing waiting room. Late sun on a wall the narrator has begun to prefer.

The word kind appearing in her speech where it did not used to. The word warm. The phrase I think I'd like to.

The senior researcher's voice. Its temperature. The way it has begun to sit in her chest.

A music she did not used to like, now humming back from her without her cueing it.

Wildfire haze as a baseline color, not a weather event.

The translations themselves, as inset paragraphs inside the letters: clean, accurate, sometimes startlingly loving.

Vocabulary the narrator overhears and uses without translating: LinCx, connexin, gap junction, AAV, peptidein, dark proteome, ORFness, indeterminate potential, pan-essential, cryptic antigen, exploratory drive, social preference, thermal preference. A glossary that accretes.

## Form

Dated letters of unequal length. Some half a page; some four pages; some a single sentence.

Some letters are translations of papers, set as inset blocks the narrator builds for the partner. The cascade-and-clarify motion described in *Cascade and translation* is the engine inside these letters; some cascades resolve into a clean paraphrase, some refuse to. The translations that do resolve get better as she goes. This is the readable line of her decline.

Letters are not numbered, only dated. Dates may skip without remark; thinning attention is not amnesia and is not foregrounded as a symptom. The reader notices the gaps.

Motifs return until they stop being noticed by the narrator — and that stopping is the moment they have been absorbed into baseline.

A phrase the narrator picks up from a researcher should appear in a letter unflagged, then twice more, then become the narrator's own. The reader sees the contagion; the narrator does not.

The book has no chapters. It has letters and the silences between them. The silences are part of the form.

## Reader contract

The reader should feel that the narrator is correct and that the correctness changes nothing. The reader should not be invited to doubt her perception; the reader should be invited to share it.

The reader should feel the love between the narrator and the partner as the warmest thing in the book, and should know that its warmth is implicated. This double feeling is the work.

The reader should leave with a recognition they cannot test from their own life. That untestability is the book's gift to them, not its insult.

The prose should never explain the science. The science should be entered through the narrator's hand, as she enters it for her partner.

The book ends not with revelation but with a letter that does not arrive at recognition. Whether because she has lost the frame again, or because she is sparing him, or because the system has finally caught the loop she did not know she was running — the prose does not tell us. The silence after the last letter is the form.
