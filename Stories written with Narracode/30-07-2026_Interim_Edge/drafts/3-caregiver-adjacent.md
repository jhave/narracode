# 3 — caregiver, adjacent

Sunday, half four, because by half four his mother had done the ironing and his father was back from the depot, and before half four they were unreachable in the manner of people who have organised an entire life around not being a burden.

Screen propped against the fruit bowl. Two of them at once in the frame, always, shoulder to shoulder, leaning in like a passport photo for a country that no longer accepts applications.

‘You're thin,’ said Ferouz.

‘I'm the same.’

‘Thinner than the same.’

Sabir, off to the side, occupied with the arithmetic of the world: ‘Know what they wanted for a melon? Guess. Guess what they wanted.’

‘Dad—’

‘Guess.’

He guessed low, on purpose, to give his father the pleasure, and got the whistle, and the head shake, and the phrase *for a melon*, delivered to the ceiling, and the two of them laughed, and this was love, conducted through logistics, and it worked, it had always worked, it was the machinery he had grown up inside and he could hear every gear of it.

They asked about Lara. They asked with a formality they used for no one else, *and how is Lara*, both of them, in unison practically, because Lara had once, over a video call, in her first thirty seconds, told Sabir that his shirt was a good shirt and asked what it cost, and Sabir had told her, and something had been established that afternoon that neither of them had needed to name since.

They asked about the job. Here was the part.

‘Still the categories,’ he said.

‘He does the categories,’ Sabir told Ferouz, who had been in the room for the entire conversation. ‘At the depot I said, my son, advertising. Big firm. Marble in the lobby.’

‘It's insurance marble.’

‘Marble is marble.’

Under his ribs, the thing turned over. He had the sentence ready and had been carrying it since June: *Dad, on Thursdays I decide which brands are permitted to run against footage of authorized actions, and there's a dropdown, and the third option is called Permit with cushion, and cushion means we put four seconds of something soothing in front, and I clear about ninety of those a week, and I'm quick at it.* He had rehearsed the sentence in the shower with the water running so it could be said aloud without being said.

‘Marble is marble,’ he agreed.

*head of household, aspirational-proxy · pride, high · disclosure risk, nil*

‘The package,’ Ferouz said.

‘What package?’

‘Ahmani.’ Patient, terrifying. ‘The package. Three weeks. The dates, the good ones, the jar from your aunt, and the paper for you to sign.’

‘What paper?’

‘The paper for your father's top-up. It has your name on it, I don't know why it has your name, they put your name, so you sign it and you send it back and then they stop writing to us.’ She was already reading from her phone, holding it at the distance of a woman who has decided that arm's length is a personal failing on the part of the phone. ‘Delivered. It says delivered. Tuesday the twelfth, eleven forty, delivered.’

‘Nothing came.’

‘It says delivered.’

‘Mum, nothing came.’

‘Then somebody has it,’ said Ferouz, with the calm of a woman who has been proven right about smaller matters for forty years, and put the phone face down, which she did when a subject was concluded rather than resolved.

He went down after. Bins for eleven days, so the stairwell had its own weather now, a warm sourness that arrived at the third floor and thickened.

On the fourth-floor landing the balloon had more air in it.

He stood over it. Silver, HAPP, the crushed fold — and where the fold had been there was now a curve. He crouched. He put two fingers on it. Cool, taut at the knuckled end, giving under pressure and coming back, and the seam near the neck showing the slight puckering of something recently under strain.

Behind him, on four, a door came off its latch and stopped at the width of a chain. No face in it. Just the dark of a hallway and the sound of a television doing applause.

‘Sorry,’ Ahmani said, to nobody, standing up, hands raised slightly, guilty of what.

Chain went back on. Door closed.

Out in the courtyard the light had gone amber-and-then-some and the heat came up off the paving into the backs of the knees, and there were children out, four or five, doing something complicated with a length of cable and a bucket, and he stopped, because in the dream they had all swivelled, every one of them, a dozen faces turning at once towards adult anger.

These did not turn. They did not look up at all. One of them said *no, you have to say it first*, and another said *I did say it*, and the first one said *say it properly*, and the argument went on in the exact grammar of an argument he had had at eight years old in a courtyard four hundred kilometres from this one, and he stood there in the smell of the bins with his phone warm in his hand, wanting so badly to be looked at by somebody who was not scoring him that his eyes went hot and he had to put a hand over them and press.

Somebody has it, he thought.

Then: it says delivered.

He went back up. Six flights, past the balloon, which lifted a little as he went by, and settled.
