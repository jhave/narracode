'Chapter four,' she said, without preamble, to the page.

'Mm.'

'Nearly.' Looked up. Whole face. 'Nearly, Ahmani.'

He said that was great and meant it, and under the meaning it, the floor of the fourteen months, which had moved again, and which he had never once checked against anything.

Told her about the definition field. Told it badly, out of order, the tick first and the empty after, and she stopped him twice to make him do it properly, and at the morning she put both hands flat on the table.

'It read it.'

'It didn't read it. It's a field.'

'Ahmani.' Up. 'Something reads the fields.'

'Or a job runs at four and wipes non-conforming entries.'

'Sure,' she said, in the voice of a person granting a point in order to keep the other one, and stood at the window with her arms folded, and said it again, quiet, to the glass — 'that's a door' — and laughed once. No cascade. One note. Came back to the table.

· · ·

Dawn.

Awake before the alarm, most days now. Swivel chair. Flat grey light finding the edges of objects one at a time. Mug. Cable. Corner of her coat over the chair back. News tabs open because he had opened them. Three. First authorized. Second adjacent. Third a man in a good coat looking at a horizon added later.

The dream had been the building again. Curved wall. He had the lead. He stopped.

Closed the tabs.

Behind him Lara asleep. One foot out, a wedge of pale. His own hands on the arms of the chair. Heart doing its medical thing.

There's no race, he said. Out loud. In the room. Six-forty in the morning, to nobody, in the voice of a man reading a slogan off a wall.

It sounded like exactly what it was.

Said it again anyway.

Then up. Emptied the drains. Rinsed the holder. Fresh filter. Quiet as possible. Poured. Listened to it go from silence to hiss to rolling boil froth before the click, and four floors down there was only the paler grey where something had lain, and in the coat pocket the slip with the name that was almost his.
