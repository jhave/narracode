The kettle took two minutes forty and he stood there for all of it. Water going from nothing, to a hiss, to that last rolling panic before the click. He liked the panic. Filter cone needed rinsing because he had not rinsed it last night, grounds set into a black cake, thumb against wet paper, the revulsion of that, then coffee — chicory and a legume, bag announcing COFFEE in the confident font of a document that has been to court and won.

Lara asleep with one foot out from under. She slept like freight in the heat, wrapped tight, one foot escaping, mouth open, a shine at the corner of it. He loved her so much for a second that his chest did something medical. Then, arriving on the same track, close behind, the thought that she would leave him in about fourteen months. He had not consented to fourteen months. He did not know where the figure came from. There it was, though, itemized, sitting in him like a delivery he had signed for while asleep.

Six flights. Lift stopped at four in March and the notice about it had gone yellow and curled at the corners like bread under a grill. On the fourth-floor landing, the balloon. Mylar, silver, HAPP legible with the rest of the word crushed under a fold, tethered to nothing, lying where the wall met the step, and when the street door opened below it lifted and settled. Sedated. Bobbing. It had been there when they moved in. Nobody on the fourth floor claimed it, and he had stopped asking, because asking twice about a balloon makes you a person who asks about balloons.

Outside, the heat had a rating. Amber, act normally. Ad surfaces up the whole east wall of the tram stop, and one of them was doing that trick where the model's eye follows you for exactly as long as your gait matches the crowd average, which he knew how to break by walking wrong, and he walked wrong, and felt clever, and felt the cleverness curdle in about four steps.

Vending wall by the platform: FYRE SNAX (Cat. 2), CRUNCHTIDE (Cat. 2), a gel called LOYAL in a sachet the colour of a wound, hazard classes printed at legally mandated size, which was the size of a fingernail, on a background chosen by somebody in a building like his to fall exactly at the threshold of unreadable. Beneath, a decal: EAT SOMETHING, YOU'VE EARNED IT. Two men beside him shared a vape that smelled of pear and solvent and something behind the pear that made the back of the throat go quiet.

Tram rerouted again, the long way, around the hours. Nobody said curfew. The municipality published them Sundays and they were never the same hours twice, so you could not build a habit against them, which he assumed was arithmetic rather than malice, though he had stopped being able to tell the two apart at any distance.

Interim Edge occupied nine through fourteen of a building that had been insurance until the flood-model repricing, and the lobby still had the insurance marble, veined, cold, wrong for the weather, and a security desk with three people at it for a door that scanned you anyway.

Onboarding, month one, a senior with beautiful hands had told the intake cohort: *category is a kindness. Without category we would have to look at everyone.* Half the room laughed. Ahmani had written it down.

Category Integrity ran forty-one thousand two hundred and six audience segments. He merged, split, and sunset them. Sunset was the verb. Miko Sarr, deskmate, ate breakfast at his desk out of a bowl he never took to the kitchen and kept a tally of sunsets on a sticky note, one stroke per, four and a diagonal, the way you count in a cell. *You'll stop noticing by the second quarter,* Miko had said, kindly, in a way that cost him nothing and was not therefore worth less.

At the academy Ahmani had done propensity surfaces, then affinity topology, distinction in both. He could feel a segment's shape before he opened it. That was the useful part of him and he had been paid for it since June and the money went to rent, to his mother's phone bill, to a lemon, occasionally, and he was fast, and being fast felt in his body exactly like grace, and the horror of that particular fact he kept where he kept the other one.

He wrote to Delph. Delph wrote back in eleven minutes.

He read it four times looking for the second sentence. Then he sat with his hands on the desk and the floor of the ninth hummed under his shoes and he was in the dream corridor again, the curved wall, the certainty of having been in the lead, and he thought: I stopped to tie my shoe, and everybody went past, and I did not see which way they went, and the wall curves so you cannot see far enough ahead to know if it goes anywhere.

He looked down. Left lace, untied, lying on the carpet tile in two grey worms.
