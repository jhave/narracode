## Class 11 — analogy-simile `X, the way Y` — **7 hits, highest density in the draft**

Not in the registry before this scan. The construction explains a thing by analogy to a universal *you*, and it has become the draft's default figurative move — roughly one every 300 words.

| § | span |
|---|---|
| 1 | *four and a diagonal, **the way you count in a cell*** |
| 1 | *Not blank **the way a draft is blank*** |
| 2 | *cover a page with her palm, **the way you cover a hand of cards for somebody who isn't accused of anything*** |
| 2 | *the room labelled itself. Mildly, **the way a headache is mild*** |
| 3 | *seam near the neck puckered slightly, **the way a seam is after strain*** |
| 4 | *had stopped being strange around week three, **the way a smell stops*** |

**Remedy**: keep two, cut five. Strongest by some distance are the cards (§2) and the smell (§4). *The way a seam is after strain* (§3) is nearly tautological — the seam is the subject of its own simile. *The way a draft is blank* (§1) is doing definitional work a colon would do faster.

*(Idiomatic uses — `the way it came`, `the way it goes`, `the way it had been` — are not this class. Lara's sleep-phrase `not the way we said` is dialogue and out of scope.)*
