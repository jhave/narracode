## 0 — limp balloon

‘It’ Ahmani thought, and by ‘it’ he meant global diplomacy, ‘is now a cage fight of thugs with oligarchics rigging a betting pool on the outcome of authorized massacres. And media-manipulated masses infected by patriotic fever fed carcinogenic ultra-processed snacks and narcotics on all sides.’

He sighed, adjusting his scrawny frame in his swivel chair and closed the news tabs. Depressed, the image of a limp balloon, post-party, crept into mind: the palpable edges, the folds of plastic, the sedated bobbing.

‘Growth math, open loop, retention meme propaganda.’  The smudged mutter of mundane strategies besieged him.

A fragment of last night’s dream arose in his mouth like saliva: he was sent on race through a building, had the lead, then stopped to tie his shoe, lost the others, didn’t know the path, stood among curved wall, cried out ‘i’m giving up. I don’t know the way.’ screamed in anguish: ‘wtf.’ then saw to his right a group of toddlers in a daycare all swivel to look at him as he swore. A dozen Innocent little faces trembling, shocked by inexplicable adult anger; several on verge of, swollen with tears, uncertain, wobbly looking to their center, where seated among them, a white haired serene woman, non-reactive, also looking at him. Neutral, gentle, no judgement, no disrespect, just awareness. He swivelled away in shame, and suddenly saw his lover looking at clothes on a used discount rack. She had seen it too. In his mind now, awake among the contours of objects awakening to discernibility at dawn,  he went and sat down among the children and they soothed his hair. From this tender unexpected acceptance, the knowledge grew, he had won the race that was not a race, there was no race, no course, just imagined paths.

Platitudes, he muttered. Smudging away the vulnerability and the dream smeared,  beneath his hand wiping his eyes.

He said that was great and meant it, and under the meaning it, the floor of the fourteen months, which had moved again, and which he had never once checked against anything.

He told her about the definition field. Told it badly, out of order, starting with the tick and going back to the empty, and she stopped him twice to make him do it properly, and when he got to the morning she put both hands flat on the table.

‘It read it.’

‘It didn't read it. It's a field.’

‘Ahmani.’ Up. ‘Something *reads the fields*.’

‘Or a job runs at four and wipes non-conforming entries.’

‘Sure,’ she said, in the voice of a person granting a point in order to keep the other one, and stood at the window with her arms folded, and then said it again, quietly, to the glass — ‘that's a door’ — and laughed once. No cascade. One note. Came back to the table.

· · ·

Dawn.

Awake before the alarm, as he was now most days, in the swivel chair with flat grey light finding the edges of objects one at a time. Mug. Cable. Corner of her coat over the chair back. News tabs open because he had opened them. Three. First was authorized. Second was adjacent. Third was a man in a good coat looking at a horizon added later.

Dream had been the building again. Curved wall. He had the lead. He stopped.

He closed the tabs.

Behind him Lara asleep, one foot out from under, wrapped tight, mouth open, shine at the corner of it, and he loved her so much his chest did the medical thing.

There's no race, he said. Out loud. In the room. Six-forty in the morning, to nobody, in the voice of a man reading a slogan off a wall.

Sounded like exactly what it was.

Said it again anyway. Then got up, and the water went from nothing to a hiss to that last rolling panic, and four floors down there was only the paler grey where something had lain, and in his coat pocket the slip with the name that was almost his.
