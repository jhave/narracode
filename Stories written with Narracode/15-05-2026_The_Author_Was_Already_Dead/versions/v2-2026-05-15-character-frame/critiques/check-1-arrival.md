# Check — 1-arrival

## Continuity

- Body removed "two nights ago" but tape still curled at jamb — plausible if police closed quickly. Hold.
- Narrator arrives "morning"; super still on duty; consistent.
- Receipt-with-password is offstage (narrator's home, in a drawer) — a planted retrieval, time-cost named.
- introduce love interest as the word now dead

## Obligations

- **Empty FedEx envelope, addressed in Halsey's handwriting, not yet sealed.** Major active obligation. Story must answer it, intensify it, or deliberately let it rot.
- **The laptop password on a receipt at home.** Promised retrieval. If she never goes back for it, the refusal becomes the meaning.
- **The book he had been writing all that time.** Named as the reason she has the key and the reason she is in the apartment. Cannot disappear.
- **The second bedroom.** Refused — "I did not look in the second bedroom yet." Named refusals become future scenes.
- **Marek's "tone of voice" when he gave her the password, once, in a restaurant.** Privacy of a single past moment — a small interiority debt.

## Motifs

- Things that have been promising since 2019 (elevators).
- Unfinished sentences (the super, twice).
- Arrangement vs. ordinary-Tuesday-ness (Halsey "always arranged things"; the room is not arranged).
- Indifferent machines (router's green light, pigeon).
- Weather as figure for emotional reception (waiter retiring "the way some people take weather"; morning that "stopped pretending it was still early").
- The bookmark — narrow strip of tape introduces a reading metaphor without overt commentary. Watch for it returning transformed.

## Scene function

- Establishes narrator (agent, 31 years), the dead author (Marek Halsey, novelist working on a long book), and the room as the story's primary venue.
- Refuses the procedural beat: no police interview, no autopsy, no detective.
- Plants three named objects (envelope, laptop, second bedroom) — each is a possible next scene.
- Closes on indecision rather than thesis. Functional.

## Voice/default

- Chandler grammar landing in short declaratives with periodic long sentences — held throughout.
- Similes (small dog, weather, bookmark, pigeon) are doing work, not decoration. The pigeon line edges close to a Chandler-borrowed beat ("absolute disinterest of a thing that has never had an obligation"); just under the line. Watch.
- Risk: the "thing about X is that it is either A or B. There is no middle." aphorism is a Chandler tell; one is fine, two would be a tic. Don't repeat the construction soon.
- No LLM defaults (no tricolons of abstract nouns, no benediction close, no thesis open). One mild risk: "the indifferent rhythm of a machine that has not been told" — pretty, but verging on lyric. Acceptable; do not chain similar.

## Reader-state

- Reader knows: an author is dead; the narrator is his agent of three decades; there is or was a long-gestating book; the apartment is intact and oddly unmarked.
- Reader expects: she will go into the second bedroom; she will return for the receipt; the envelope's emptiness will resolve into either a banality or a wound.
- Productive mystery: what did Marek mean to send? Is the long book real? What is the narrator's actual relationship to him (professional grief vs. something more private)?
- Confusion risk: low. Marek's first name and last name are both used in the same paragraph without friction; narrator remains unnamed but the role (agent) is anchored early.
- Plausible defiance path: he never wrote the book; the envelope was for a check to his ex-wife; the second bedroom contains exactly what we expect, which is the worst thing it could contain.
