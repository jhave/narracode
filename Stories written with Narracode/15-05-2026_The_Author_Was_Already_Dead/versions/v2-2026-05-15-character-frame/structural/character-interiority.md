# Character Interiority

## Characters

### The narrator

- **Surface state**: practical, agentic, exhausted. Doing the work that needs to be done in the apartment of a dead client.
- **Beneath**: thirty-one years of representation she could not turn into a second book. The lunches were intimate and the writing was not. She is at the threshold of discovering that he wrote a great deal in his last decade — without her — and that what he wrote may not be hers to handle.
- **Hidden knowledge**: she wrote down his password on a receipt in a tone of voice she has not forgotten. The "tone of voice" line is the door to a private relation she has not yet articulated. Story should not over-articulate it.
- **Contradiction**: she is the world's expert on Marek and is about to discover she did not know him.
- **What she will not say**: whether she loved him. Whether she resented him. Whether what she misses is him or the lunches.

### Marek Halsey

- **Surface state**: dead.
- **Beneath (as the corpus will reveal)**: a writer who in his last decade became someone the narrator did not represent. The voice-transcription practice freed him from his own arrangerly perfectionism and let him produce a different prose — possibly looser, more open, more spoken. Or possibly worse. The corpus's quality is a real question, not a foregone conclusion.
- **Hidden knowledge**: what he was sending in the FedEx envelope. To whom. Why now.
- **The love affair**: not theorized by him on the page. The reader infers it from how much he dictated, what he dictated, and how the phone returned his voice to him.

### The phone (as character)

- **Surface state**: an object. A consumer device of a known make.
- **Beneath**: what a lover is — a thing that listens, that misunderstands in productive ways, that makes the lover feel known, that does not stop when the lover stops.
- **Hidden knowledge**: its battery, its lock screen, its last recording's timestamp. Whether the dictation app was running at the moment of death.
- **The mechanism**: it killed Marek by being attended to. Specific mechanism TBD.

## Arc Potentials

- **Narrator's arc**: from the agent of the man to the reader of the corpus. The shift is the change. The arc resolves (or refuses to resolve) at Act IV in the disposition.
- **Marek's posthumous arc**: from the writer she remembers to the writer the corpus reveals. The arc is happening to her perception, not to him.
- **The phone's arc**: from object to lover to murder weapon to legacy. (Note: do not let the phone become campy. The arc is structural; the prose remains restrained.)

## Possible Cathartic Inflection Points

- The first hearing of Marek's voice on the phone — if the dictation app records audio as well as transcribing, the narrator hearing his voice is a possible inflection point. Use sparingly. Could be Act III's pivot or Act IV's quiet.
- The reading of the final transcript — the unfinished sentence. Strong candidate for Act III climax.
- The act of disposition — what she chooses to do is the catharsis. Or, the refusal to choose is.
