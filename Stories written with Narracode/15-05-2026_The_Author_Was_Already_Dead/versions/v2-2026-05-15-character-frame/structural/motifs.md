# Motifs

## Active

- **Unfinished sentences.** The super, twice. Should return in the corpus — sentences the algorithm cut off, sentences Marek abandoned, the final unfinished sentence at the moment of death.
- **Things that have been promising since 2019.** Elevators. A note of deferred maintenance, of futures that never arrived.
- **Arrangement vs. ordinary-Tuesday-ness.** Marek "always arranged things"; the room is not arranged. The corpus, by contrast, may be the most arranged thing he ever made — or the least.
- **Indifferent machines.** The router's green light, the pigeon, soon the phone. Tools that go on doing their work after the user has stopped.
- **Weather as figure for reception.** The retired waiter, the morning that "stopped pretending it was still early." Continue as figure for grief and as figure for how Marek read the world.
- **Strips, narrow widths.** The bookmark-width police tape. Possibly returns: a strip of a screen, a strip of charged battery, a strip of paper.
- **The receipt.** The password written on the back of a restaurant receipt. A literary object existing on the back of a financial one. Possibly transforms in Act II: receipts become a way of dating his dictation walks.

## Transformations

- The "bookmark" image should return as something the corpus is being read on — a phone with a thin progress bar, a paper printout, a thumb holding a place.
- "Indifferent machines" should culminate in the phone itself — its battery still alive, still listening, when Marek is not.
- "Unfinished sentences" — terminal transformation is the dictation that stops mid-word.

## Avoid Overuse

- The "X was the X" tautology (the room was the room; the kitchen was the kitchen) — used twice in scene 1. Effective; deploying it a third time would calcify into a tic.
- The Chandler aphorism construction "the thing about X is either A or B, there is no middle" — used once. Do not repeat soon.
- The "absolute disinterest of a thing that has never had an obligation in its life" pigeon-grade simile — already at the upper edge of permissible lyric.
