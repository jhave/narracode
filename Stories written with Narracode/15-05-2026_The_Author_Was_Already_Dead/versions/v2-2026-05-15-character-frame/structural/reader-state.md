# Reader State

## Current Understanding

- An author is dead. His name is Marek Halsey. He was 67, a novelist with one published book.
- The narrator is his literary agent of thirty-one years. She knows him well. She is unnamed.
- The narrator has entered his apartment alone, the morning after the body was removed.
- The room is intact. There is a laptop (locked), an empty addressed FedEx envelope, and a second bedroom she has not entered.
- The setting is contemporary, urban, 2026.

## Expectations

- She will enter the second bedroom.
- She will return for the password.
- The envelope's emptiness will become meaningful.
- There is a long-awaited book; she will look for it.

## Plausible Defiance Paths

- The book is not on the laptop. It is on the phone, in voice-transcribed form, and the laptop is irrelevant.
- The book is not a manuscript; it is a corpus of dictation that resists the form of a novel.
- The envelope was for something banal — and the banality, in context, is heavier than a secret would have been.
- The second bedroom is the most ordinary room in the apartment.
- The narrator does not finish her task in the scope of the story; the disposition is left open.

## Productive Mystery

- Where is the phone, and when will the narrator notice it.
- What did Marek dictate, and how does it read.
- How exactly did he die.
- What is the narrator's actual interior relation to him — and will the story let us know or only let us feel it.

## Accidental Confusion Risk

- The reader does not yet know the phone is the love interest or the murder weapon. This is intentional. Risk: if Act II opens too obliquely the reader may not connect the phone to the conceptual frame in time. Mitigation: Act II should let the narrator find the phone fairly early, and let the encounter with the dictation app carry the unveiling.
- The reader currently expects a literary-world mystery (manuscript, ex-wife, publishing politics). The story will defy this. Manage the defiance by giving the corpus enough room to feel like the real subject when it arrives.
- "The word" in the prompter's framing is a conceptual register that may not survive on the page in those terms. The story should embody the frame, not name it. Avoid having the narrator (or anyone) say something like "he had fallen in love with the word."
