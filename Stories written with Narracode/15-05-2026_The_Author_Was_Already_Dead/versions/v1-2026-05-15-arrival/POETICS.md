# POETICS

## Project

*The Author Was Already Dead.* A short literary fiction in the grammar of Raymond Chandler, transplanted into contemporary time. The triggering situation: an author is found dead. The story is not a whodunit. The death is the pressure under the sentences; what the prose investigates is a literary economy in 2026 — agents, archives, residency programs, prizes, the unread book, the curated obituary, the LLM-assisted estate.

## Commitments

- **Chandler grammar.** Short declarative sentences detonated by a long one. The simile that startles by being wrong-and-then-right. Attentional precision aimed at a single ordinary object until it becomes ominous. The wisecrack that is, on second reading, a small wound.
- **A narrator with attentional habit.** Not "I" as confession. "I" as a way of moving through rooms — a private investigator's gaze applied to a literary-world interior. The narrator is *of* this world and slightly tired of it.
- **Contemporary fixtures named without irony.** Slack pings, e-reader screens, the air-quality index, the FedEx envelope, the dead author's still-loading author page. Period markers are 2026 and stable.
- **Death as a weather system,** not a puzzle. The body is found early. The mystery is the room around the body.
- **One scene per draft pass.** Do not advance the plot in synopsis.

## Refusals

- **No fedora, no rain-slicked alley, no femme fatale lighting a cigarette.** Pastiche kitsch is the failure mode.
- **No Marlowe-voice tics in proper-noun form** — no Bay City, no Velma. Borrow the grammar, not the props.
- **No detective procedural beats.** No interviewing witnesses one by one. No clues laid in numbered sequence. No reveal.
- **No omniscient pivot.** The narrator only knows what the narrator can see, smell, and remember.
- **No sentiment about books, writing, or the writer's life.** Especially this. The literary world is a workplace; it is described as one.
- **No LLM tells:** no "tapestry," no "delve," no tricolons of abstract nouns, no "in a world where," no thesis-statement opening, no closing benediction.

## Attentional dialect

Look at things the way someone looks at things when they have stopped expecting the things to surprise them — and then let one thing surprise them anyway. The texture is observation that has gone past patience into something dryer. Imagery should be drawn from the material life of 2026: lithium batteries, courier apps, vacant retail, the smell of warmed laptop fans, dust on a router.

When in doubt: cut the second adjective. Then cut the first.

## Voice tests

- A sentence about a houseplant should be able to carry the weight of a death.
- A simile should make the reader hesitate for half a second.
- A piece of dialogue should leave more unsaid than said.
- A paragraph should end on the smaller of two available words.
