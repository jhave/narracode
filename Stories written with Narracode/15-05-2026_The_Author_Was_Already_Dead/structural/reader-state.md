# Reader State

## Current Understanding

- An author is dead. His name is Marek Halsey. He was 67, a novelist with one published book.
- The narrator is his literary agent of thirty-one years. She knows him well. She is unnamed.
- The narrator has entered his apartment alone, the morning after the body was removed.
- The room is intact. The kitchen contained a kettle that had been left on the burner, a glass in the sink, and an empty addressed FedEx envelope.
- The laptop is password-protected and the password is on a receipt at the narrator's home.
- The setting is contemporary, urban, 2026.

## Expectations

- She will look for the manuscript.
- She will encounter something more than a manuscript.
- The envelope's emptiness will become meaningful.
- The second bedroom will be entered.

## Plausible Defiance Paths

- The book is not on the laptop. It is on the phone, in voice-transcribed form, and the laptop is irrelevant.
- The phone, when found, is unlocked, charged, and waiting — the lover who has not been told.
- The book is not a manuscript; it is a corpus of dictation that resists the form of a novel.
- The envelope was for something banal — and the banality, in context, is heavier than a secret would have been.
- The second bedroom is the most ordinary room in the apartment.

## Productive Mystery

- What did Marek dictate, and how does it read.
- How exactly did he die (deferred to Act III).
- What is the narrator's actual interior relation to him — let the story let us feel it, not tell it.
- Who is Sam.

## Accidental Confusion Risk

- The reader does not yet know the phone is the love interest. Scene 2 must make it felt without saying it. Risk mitigation: let the phone occupy a structural slot (the bedroom, the bedside, the charging pad) that the reader recognizes from love-stories, not detective stories.
- The conceptual frame "the word now dead / the lover survives the lover's lover" is metaphysical. The story must embody, not name. Avoid having the narrator articulate "he was in love with his phone."
