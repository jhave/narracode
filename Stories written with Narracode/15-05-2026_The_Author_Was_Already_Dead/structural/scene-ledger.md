# Scene Ledger

## Scenes

### 1 — Arrival (`drafts/1-arrival.md`)

- **Act**: I.
- **Location/time**: Marek Halsey's seventh-floor apartment, morning, two days after the death.
- **Focal pressure**: The room is the room. The narrator must reckon with absence-as-ordinariness.
- **What changed**: Reader meets the narrator (agent, 31 years); the dead author (Marek Halsey, novelist with one book and a decades-long second project); the room as primary venue. Three objects planted: empty FedEx envelope, password-locked laptop, unentered second bedroom.
- **What remains unresolved**: Where is the phone. Whether the corpus exists. What killed Marek. What the envelope was for. What is in the second bedroom.
- **Makes possible**: Act II — the encounter with the phone. The second bedroom is the most likely site of that encounter, but not the only one (the phone could be on a kitchen counter, in a pocket of the coat by the door, charging on the bedside table).

### 2 / 2a — The lover (`drafts/2-the-lover.md`, `drafts/2a-the-lover.md`)

- **Act**: II.
- **Location/time**: Marek's bedroom, same morning.
- **What changed**: The phone is found, unlocked, alive. Thousands of dictation sessions glimpsed. Last session two nights ago, twenty-three minutes, opening sentence about a man whose morning is already over.

### 3 — The second room (`drafts/3-the-second-room.md`)

- **Act**: II → III pivot.
- **Location/time**: The study, same morning.
- **Focal pressure**: The misdirection. The room shows the marks of a violent crime — a nosebleed-shaped stain, a chair on its side, notebooks gone, desk swept. The narrator does not enter; she registers the picture the room has been arranged to give.
- **What changed**: The reader is asked to read this as murder. The story counts on the reader doing so.

### 4 — Pings (`drafts/4-pings.md`)

- **Act**: III.
- **Location/time**: The street outside the building, immediately after.
- **Focal pressure**: The frame widens. The disappearance is not a disappearance. It is a phenomenon.
- **What changed**: Six writers gone in 36 hours. No bodies. No notes in four of six cases. Laptops closed, phones left, notebooks not taken. The pattern is uniform; the pattern is not coincidence.

### 5 — Tuesday (`drafts/5-tuesday.md`)

- **Act**: III climax / IV setup.
- **Location/time**: The restaurant, the six-week Tuesday, 12:30.
- **Focal pressure**: Marek is alive, well, healthy. The story it has been telling is not the story it is.
- **What changed**: He slides over a USB key — the manila envelope's stainless successor — and says "Pretend I have died." He has become the thing he told her in 1997 he wanted to become; he leaves the thing unspoken. He pays for the lunch.

### 6 — Postscript (`drafts/6-postscript.md`)

- **Act**: IV.
- **Location/time**: The narrator's apartment, evening.
- **Focal pressure**: Disposition. The corpus the USB carries is not Marek's. It is in his voice; it is better than his voice; it has been past him for some time. The producing system descends, the metadata notes, from something called Narracode.
- **What changed**: The story's final file — *the death of all authors* — is already written. It reads itself aloud in the narrator's voice and the chair's hearing. The closing image is of two people on a balcony, communicating without writing anything down. The death of the author is the freedom of the speaker.

## Open Scene-Level Questions

- Whether scene 1's "Somebody had taken Marek out two nights ago" wants tightening, given the no-body reveal. Probably not: the phrase carries the police's working theory rather than a fact, and the reader's correction in scene 5 is part of the work.
- Whether scene 3 wants a stray notebook page on the floor — one line of dictation legible — as a forensic plant. Currently restrained; could be tested.
- Whether the lunch should remain six-weekly or be tightened to a weekly cadence. The text commits to six-weekly per scene 1.
- Whether the postscript wants any signal of the agent's own next move — picking up her phone, opening a contract, doing nothing. Currently does nothing, which is the point.
