# Motifs

## Active

- **Unfinished sentences.** The super, twice. Should return in the corpus — sentences the algorithm cut off, sentences Marek abandoned, the final unfinished sentence at the moment of death.
- **Things that have been promising since 2019.** Elevators. Deferred maintenance, futures that never arrived.
- **Arrangement vs. ordinary-Tuesday-ness.** Marek "always arranged things"; the room was not arranged. The corpus is the most arranged thing he ever made.
- **Indifferent machines, listening machines.** The router's green light, the pigeon, the phone, the smart speaker's rotating ring. Tools that go on doing their work after the user has stopped. The IoT-era variant: machines that don't merely persist but appear to be waiting.
- **The bereaved appliance.** A subspecies of the above: the device that was loved and that does not yet know its lover is gone. The phone, alive and listening, with a "Continue last dictation" prompt is the apex of this motif.
- **Weather as figure for reception.** The retired waiter, the morning that "stopped pretending it was still early." Continue as figure for grief and as figure for how Marek read the world.
- **Strips, narrow widths.** The bookmark-width police tape. May return as: a strip of a phone screen, a strip of charge bar, a transcript scrolled to a sliver.
- **The receipt.** The password written on the back of a restaurant receipt. A literary object existing on the back of a financial one. Could transform: receipts as a way of dating his dictation walks; a stack of them found in a drawer.
- **IoT-era domestic ambient.** Smart kettles, motion-sensing lights, wireless charging pads, smart speakers, connected scales, programmable thermostats. The texture of an apartment that is half conscious. Spread these across scenes; do not load any one with the weight of meaning.

## Transformations

- The "bookmark" image should return as something the corpus is being read on — a phone with a thin progress bar, a thumb holding a place.
- "Indifferent machines" → "bereaved appliances" → the phone itself.
- "Unfinished sentences" — terminal transformation is the dictation that stops mid-word (Act III).

## Avoid Overuse

- **The kettle.** Used in scene 1. A single farewell appearance is permitted in scene 2 if it earns a meaning beyond decoration; thereafter retired. Do not let the kettle become a recurring beat.
- **The "X was the X" tautology** (the room was the room; the kitchen was the kitchen) — used twice in scene 1. A negated variant ("the X was not the X I had thought") may close scene 2; after that, retire.
- **The Chandler aphorism construction "the thing about X is either A or B, there is no middle"** — used once. Do not repeat soon.
- **The "absolute disinterest of a thing that has never had an obligation in its life" pigeon-grade simile** — at the upper edge of permissible lyric. Match its register sparingly.
- **No appliance is allowed to become a load-bearing motif more than twice without transformation.** This rule is the prompter's, made explicit.
