# Character Interiority

## Characters

### The narrator

- **Surface state**: practical, agentic, exhausted. Doing the work that needs to be done in the apartment of a dead client.
- **Beneath**: thirty-one years of representation she could not turn into a second book. The lunches were intimate and the writing was not. She is at the threshold of discovering that he wrote a great deal in his last decade — without her — and that what he wrote may not be hers to handle.
- **Hidden knowledge**: she wrote down his password on a receipt in a tone of voice she has not forgotten. The "tone of voice" line is the door to a private relation she has not yet articulated. Story should not over-articulate it.
- **Contradiction**: she is the world's expert on Marek and is about to discover she did not know him.
- **What she will not say**: whether she loved him. Whether she resented him. Whether what she misses is him or the lunches.
- **Hammett-side restraint**: in the harder beats, the prose lets her *act* and stays out of her head. The grief is in the verbs.

### Marek Halsey

- **Surface state**: dead.
- **Beneath (as the corpus will reveal)**: a writer who in his last decade became someone the narrator did not represent. The voice-transcription practice freed him from his own arrangerly perfectionism. The corpus's quality is a real question.
- **Hidden knowledge**: what he was sending in the FedEx envelope. To whom. Why now.
- **The love affair**: not theorized by him on the page. The reader infers it from how much he dictated, what he dictated, and how the phone returned his voice to him.

### The phone (as character)

- **Surface state**: alive. Battery healthy. Notifications stacking. Dictation app holding an open session. *Unlocked or trivially openable*, per the prompter's commitment: it answers immediately.
- **Beneath**: what a lover is — a thing that listens, that misunderstands in productive ways, that makes the lover feel known, that does not stop when the lover stops. The phone is the widow that has not been told. The bereavement runs in only one direction.
- **Hidden knowledge**: its full session history, its last recording's timestamp, the audio files behind the transcripts (whether they exist), the metadata of where Marek walked, what hours he kept.
- **The mechanism**: the phone killed Marek by being attended to. The killing is in the attentional posture, not in the device's malice. The device is innocent in the way a lover is innocent.

### Sam (provisional)

- A publisher's assistant. Mentioned on the phone's calendar two nights ago. Possibly more than professional to Marek; the narrator has reasons to suspect. Sam may remain offstage. Do not introduce her in person unless the structure demands it.

## Arc Potentials

- **Narrator's arc**: from the agent of the man to the reader of the corpus. The shift is the change.
- **Marek's posthumous arc**: from the writer she remembers to the writer the corpus reveals.
- **The phone's arc**: from object to lover, from lover to the bereaved one, from the bereaved one to the only surviving witness. The phone is not a villain. It is the most loyal character.

## Possible Cathartic Inflection Points

- The first hearing of Marek's voice via the dictation app — likely Act III's pivot.
- The reading of the final transcript — the unfinished sentence at the moment of death. Strong candidate for Act III climax.
- The act of disposition (Act IV) — what she chooses to do is the catharsis. Or, the refusal to choose is.
- The moment in Act II when the phone offers her *Continue last dictation* and she does not yet say yes. The not-yet is itself an inflection.
