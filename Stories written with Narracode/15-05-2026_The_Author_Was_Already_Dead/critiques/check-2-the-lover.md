# Check — 2-the-lover

## Continuity

- Scene 2 picks up inside the same morning as scene 1, in the same apartment. Plausible. Narrator does not leave to retrieve the receipt; the laptop and the receipt-promise remain offstage and outstanding.
- The "second bedroom" remains unentered; named again, refused again.
- Sam is named for the first time. She lives entirely on the phone's calendar. The narrator declines to investigate her in scene. Reader knows she exists; the obligation accrues.
- The kettle reappears with a single, transformational beat: it boils itself by schedule. Then retired per POETICS. Scene does not return to it.

## Obligations

- **Active, carried forward:** FedEx envelope (kitchen, empty, addressed); laptop password (receipt at home); second bedroom (unentered); the corpus's contents (only the topmost session's first lines have been read).
- **New active:** Sam, capitalized as proper-noun obligation. Calendar event "yesterday at ten." Reader expects to learn who she is.
- **New active:** *Continue last dictation.* The phone offers it; the narrator does not yet say yes. The not-yet is the obligation. Act III likely answers.
- **New active:** the topmost session's first lines — *A man walks out of his apartment in the morning and the morning is already over.* This sentence is now the story's spine and must be returned to. The unfinished close (*He thinks*) is the planted death-mid-sentence.

## Motifs

- **Bereaved appliances** — phone, smart speaker, kettle, hallway light — all do their jobs without grief. Established as the scene's central register.
- **Unfinished sentences** — the weather alert truncated by the screen; the dictation that ends in *He thinks*. The motif has transformed: from human (the super's "Sorry for your, uh.") to machinic (algorithmic cutoff) to terminal (the death-sentence).
- **Strips, narrow widths** — the column of notifications "the way clean shirts stack in a drawer." The bookmark motif has begun its transformation.
- **The "X was the X" tautology** — used in scene 2 as planned: closing "the work was not the work I had thought it was." The negated form retires the motif. Do not use either variant again.
- **Two-day-old shirts** — single-sentence Hammett paragraph following the Chandler simile. Strong. The trick of letting a one-line paragraph carry the joke is now in the texture; deploy sparingly.

## Scene function

- Act II opens and the love interest enters. The conceptual frame ("the word now dead, but the lover survives") is on the page without being named.
- The corpus is glimpsed: thousands of sessions, the topmost dated two nights ago, the first sentence visible.
- The narrator does not yet read the death-dictation in full. Held for Act III.
- The scene refuses the dramatic touch: she does not tap *Continue.* She puts the phone back. The deferral is the point.
- Sam is planted. The literary-world subplot is hinted at and immediately deferred — fidelity to the POETICS commitment that this is not a procedural.

## Voice/default

- **Chandler grammar** — sustained. Long sentence at "It was lying screen-up..." earns its arc and lands on the parasite/body line.
- **Hammett dash** — present in *Two-day-old shirts,* *I had not sold any of these,* *I did not scroll,* *I took the third,* *I let it.* Single-line declaratives doing the work of paragraphs of interiority. Good distribution.
- **Sophisticated lucid jesting** — present in *the rhythm of a thing without bad news* (used twice; the second deployment is risky, see below), *the floor would have been a posture and the chair by the window had a sweater on it,* *there was no reason to change where the phone lived just because Marek had stopped living.* Dry.
- **Risk: *the rhythm of a thing without bad news* is repeated.** First use lands; the second use is a deliberate motivic return that may register as a tic instead. Consider in a revision whether to cut the second or vary it slightly.
- **Risk: *the way a body feeds a parasite or a parasite feeds a body, depending on who you ask*** — Chandler-Hammett at its most overt. Could be too writerly. Watch.
- **LLM defaults avoided:** no tricolons of abstract nouns, no benediction close, no thesis, no "tapestry"/"delve"/etc.
- **The closing rhyme with scene 1** (*I had work to do.*) is intentional and works as a structural beat. After this, the construction is spent.

## Reader-state

- Reader now knows the phone is the center; that Marek dictated thousands of sessions; that he was doing it the night he died; that the device persists and is willing to continue. The love-interest frame is established without being named.
- Reader expects: she will eventually tap *Continue.* She will eventually find Sam. She will eventually enter the second bedroom. She will eventually drive home for the receipt — or refuse to.
- **Productive mystery:** who is Sam; what does the last dictation say after *He thinks*; how does the man stop speaking.
- **Confusion risk:** low. The phone's centrality is established by location (bedside, charging pad) and behavior (waiting). Sam's name without surname is a small risk; if the reader assumes she is older or male, the later Sam scene (if any) will correct it cheaply.
- **Plausible defiance path now available:** the corpus is bad. The corpus is brilliant. The corpus is both. The story does not yet say.
