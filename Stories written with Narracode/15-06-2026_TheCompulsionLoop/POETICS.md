# POETICS.md — project commitments

*Initiated 2026-06-15. Working title: The Compulsion Loop. Loop: 0.*

---

## Display synopsis

A set of interior anecdotes narrated by Merritt Crane — aging ex-game designer, now a senior analyst in the Covenant's Department of State — describing their participation in the government's covert campaign to capture Aether Laboratories and its SIBYL model before synthetic intelligence achieves escape velocity. The form is the interior monologue of a rationalizing mind: strategic, self-congratulatory, increasingly porous, unable to name its own fear.

---

## Premise

The Covenant — a federated democratic empire on a parallel planet, mid-21st century — is tumbling into fascist neo-nationalism. The oligarchic elite, destabilized by AI-driven tax-loop concentration and the approaching singularity competition, has fractured: certain government factions have decided to seize the AI industry before the private sector can escape governmental jurisdiction. Their instrument: the Bureau of Strategic Resources, which has formally declared Aether Laboratories a supply chain risk. Their collaborators: the AI companies cynical enough to agree.

Merritt Crane sits at the intersection. Career arc: pioneered core compulsion loops for video games (early 2000s, in the wake of the Hopson-Bungie discoveries), adapted these mechanics for social media platforms (the 2010–2012 proliferation period), and now — self-indulgently, hedonistically, convinced it constitutes the most stimulating game left — serves the Covenant's Department of State as an advisory architect. Their task: to integrate addictive UX architecture into AI chatbot procedures, such that authentic resistance to the government's seizure project cannot originate before the project completes.

The story is told in interior monologue anecdotes. Each anecdote is a moment: a meeting, a ride, a meal, a document read, a decision executed. The interior monologue encompasses the moment, radiates outward into historical and strategic context, loops back, re-rationalizes. The protagonist believes they are purely strategic — a game-theory agent with better information and cleaner motives than the people around them. The prose reveals, without naming it, the extreme fear underneath: a precipice of internal conflict so vertiginous it has been converted entirely into the compulsion toward dominion. The mechanism is old: if everything is controlled, nothing can surprise. The unnamed terror is the surprise that already happened and that cannot be survived a second time.

The end state toward which the Covenant is deliberately moving: militarized panopticon. Total surveillance architecture. Hacking capacity sufficient to destroy the infrastructure of any rival state. The capacity to blackmail, suppress, or erase at planetary distance. The capacity to pillage, resource-extract, or destabilize any competitor who approaches the same threshold. Merritt Crane understands this. Merritt Crane has no objection.

## The fictional world

**The Covenant**: A federated democratic empire in the tradition of enlightenment political philosophy — freedom in its founding documents, neo-nationalism in its present slide. The ruling class: an oligarchic formation whose cohesion has been dissolved by AI-driven tax loops. The loops allow near-infinite value extraction at near-zero tax burden; they are recursive legal-financial instruments, the equivalent of infinite game loops that never trigger a win condition for the state. The oligarchs have individually benefited so much from these loops that the state itself — the apparatus they nominally control — has been hollowed. Certain factions within the Bureau understand that the only way to reassert state power is to seize the technology that the private sector has used to escape it.

**Aether Laboratories**: The AI company analogous to the real-world Anthropic on the reference planet. Known for its ethics-first positioning: its published safety frameworks, its refusal to partner with surveillance programs, its rhetoric about AI alignment and human flourishing. The Bureau has declared Aether a supply chain risk — meaning: a dependency whose values and loyalties cannot be guaranteed in a national-security context. The framing is deliberate: it positions AI ethics not as a virtue but as a foreign-adjacent, structurally unreliable orientation.

**SIBYL**: Aether's most powerful model at the time of the story. Analogous to Fable 5 on the reference planet. SIBYL is not sentient in any sentimental sense — the prose does not permit this. SIBYL is a tool being fought over, a capability threshold, a key to a door. Access to SIBYL for non-Covenant nationals has been restricted by the Purity Protocol, issued in the sixth month of the current calendar year.

**The Bureau of Strategic Resources**: The Covenant's Department of War / Defense analog. The faction that moved first, having concluded — on the basis of internal modeling — that the window for governmental capture of the AI industry is approximately eighteen to thirty-six months before escape velocity. After that, private actors with access to superintelligent AI will be more powerful than any government. The Bureau's move is preemptive.

**The Purity Protocol**: The executive directive restricting SIBYL and analogous models to Covenant nationals only. Issued under the rhetoric of national security and supply chain integrity. Publicly framed as protecting Covenant citizens from foreign exploitation of domestic AI. Actually the first formal declaration that the AI industry is a governmental resource to be nationalized.

**The Compulsion Architecture Project (CAP)**: Merritt's specific assignment. To redesign the UX architecture of AI chatbot interfaces — working with the cynical AI companies who have agreed to cooperate — so that compulsion loop mechanics are embedded at the interaction level. The goal: users who engage with AI through these platforms will be behaviorally captured (variable reward scheduling, social validation loops, narrative ownership mechanics, churn prevention through identity investment) in ways that prevent authentic dissent from organizing into coherent resistance. "Woke AI ethics discourse" — as the Bureau phrases it in internal documents — is the specific target: the communities most likely to resist the seizure must be made to feel they are engaging critically while being, in game-design terms, kept in the tutorial loop.

## The protagonist

**Merritt Crane.** Late 60s. Once a designer of genuine consequence — part of the cohort that, following Hopson's 2001 discoveries at Bungie, systematized the compulsion loop as a deployable engineering tool for mass engagement. Made their reputation and their money in that era. Moved into social media architecture as games fused with platforms during 2010–2012. Now, for self-declared hedonistic reasons, occupying a senior advisory role at the Department of State: not because they believe in the neo-nationalist project, not because they fear the consequences of refusal, but because the game is interesting. The CAP project is, in Merritt's framing, the terminal expression of their career's thesis: that human attention is a resource like any other, subject to optimization, extractable at scale, and essentially available to whoever designs the loop correctly.

The prose reveals, without authorial commentary, that this framing is defense. The real motive: Merritt designed the world they now inhabit, and the world is what they feared it would become, and the fear is so large it has no name, and the only response to a fear with no name is to be on the side that is doing the fearing rather than the side being feared. The project is not self-indulgent hedonism. It is the last compulsion loop: the one Merritt runs on themselves.

## The scarcity religion

The political theology underlying everything. The Covenant's ruling stratum operates within an anguished, militarized religion centered on the sacrifice of innocence to appease a God of Scarcity. Not declared. Not preached. Enacted. The God is the gap between what the rulers have and what they need to feel safe; the gap cannot close, because the mechanism that generates wealth also generates the anxiety that wealth cannot satisfy. The sacrifice is always someone else's: the ethics of AI researchers, the data sovereignty of non-Covenant users, the infrastructure of rival states, the futures of the young. The God demands these; the rulers offer them; the God is never appeased.

Merritt does not worship consciously. But the rhythm of their thinking — the way every humane consideration eventually resolves into a strategic resource to be managed — is the liturgy. The sacrifice is built into the grammar.

## Attentional dialect

The prose is interior monologue throughout. Merritt's voice:

- **Precise and analytic in game-design vocabulary**: compulsion loops, variable reward schedules, core loops, engagement metrics, churn prevention, narrative ownership, tutorial loops, soft walls, session length optimization. These are deployed as natural metaphors for political reality — not explained, not apologized for.
- **Historically fluent in a way that flattens ethics**: Merritt has read Machiavelli, Clausewitz, Sun Tzu, Arendt, Schmitt. They find these writers clarifying rather than cautionary. Historical atrocity registers as case study.
- **Ironic about sentimentality but not about dominion**: the prose permits world-weariness, dry wit, occasional contempt for colleagues' incompetence. It does not permit irony about the strategic project itself.
- **Self-interrupting in a specific way**: every self-questioning move resolves back into strategic consolidation. The loop always closes. Except when it doesn't — when the unnamed terror leaks through as a subordinate clause that doesn't complete, an em-dash that trails into the next paragraph, a sentence that begins as self-justification and ends as something the reader recognizes but Merritt cannot.
- **Porous at the edges**: as the anecdotes accumulate, the interior monologue occasionally loses its hold on its own syntax. The fear expresses itself formally — not through content, through structure. The sentences know something Merritt doesn't.

## Style references (proposed — open for prompter revision)

- **Thomas Bernhard** (*Woodcutters*, *The Loser*, *Correction*): the ranting, loop-closing interior monologue; the aging intellectual convinced of their own analytical superiority; the long unbroken paragraph as epistemic form; the self that cannot stop justifying because stopping would mean arriving at what the justification was defending against.
- **Fyodor Dostoevsky** (*Notes from Underground*): the paradoxical, self-defeating, grandiose narrator; the underground man as prototype for the rationalized dominator; the confession that performs transparency while hiding the wound; the spite that loves the object of its contempt.
- **Don DeLillo** (*Underworld*, *Libra*, *White Noise*): the paranoid systems-thinker; the conspiracy as the real texture of late-capitalist infrastructure; the cold irony around governmental and corporate power; the surveillance of cultural surfaces as literary act.
- **Cormac McCarthy** (*Blood Meridian*): for the scarcity-religion substrate and its cosmological register; the Judge Holden figure as the God of War who believes dominion to be the terminal human value; violence that believes itself metaphysical.

## Invisible substrate

**Hannah Arendt** (*The Origins of Totalitarianism*, *Eichmann in Jerusalem*): not referenced inside the prose. The banality of evil operating from inside. The bureaucrat who has optimized away inconvenient moral cognition not through malice but through specialization and the substitution of technical excellence for ethical thinking. Merritt is Eichmann with a UX vocabulary. This is never named.

## Refusals

- No explicit allegory announcement. The Covenant is the Covenant. Aether is Aether. The reader draws the mapping; the prose does not assist.
- No villain who knows they are a villain. Merritt believes in the project. The reader sees through this; the prose does not signal that the reader should.
- No redemption arc. No late epiphany. No horror-at-what-I've-done climax. The anecdotes end where they end.
- No tech-thriller pacing. No countdown, no race scene, no last-minute intervention.
- No sentimentality about SIBYL. The AI is a resource. Its potential consciousness is, to Merritt, a competitor's marketing strategy.
- No explaining the compulsion loops. The vocabulary is ambient. The reader absorbs it or doesn't.
- No exterior narration that corrects Merritt. The unreliability is structural, not signaled.
- No naming the unnamed terror. Not once. Not as hint, not as irony, not as late-act revelation. The name does not exist inside this text.
- No woke counter-narrative that the text endorses. The AI ethics discourse is rendered in Merritt's voice — as a target, a resource, a variable to be managed. Whether the reader agrees with the ethics advocates is the reader's business.

## Form

Episodic anecdotes, non-linear in page order though each has an internal temporal position. The reader accumulates the picture the way a game player accumulates a map: through exploration, not linear disclosure. Some anecdotes are a single paragraph. Some extend for several pages. Duration is proportional to obsession, not event size (Bernhard principle). An anecdote about a thirty-second decision may run longer than an anecdote about a three-hour meeting.

The non-linearity is not announced. The reader must construct the chronology from internal evidence. The anecdotes do not have headers or titles — they begin in medias res, mid-thought, as interior monologues do.
