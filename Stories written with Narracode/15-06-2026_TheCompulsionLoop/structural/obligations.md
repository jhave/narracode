# Obligations

## Active

- **The unnamed terror**: Merritt's core unresolved interior state. The prose has committed to never naming it but must allow it to surface through formal cracks — incomplete subordinate clauses, trailing em-dashes, sentences that know something Merritt doesn't. The accumulation of these cracks across anecdotes is the story's deepest obligation.
- **The scarcity religion**: committed to as the substrate; must manifest as the grammar of Merritt's thinking without ever being declared as theology. Every humane consideration must be seen to resolve into a strategic resource. The God must never be named; only worshipped in the structure of thought.
- **CAP's delivery**: the project Merritt is working on. Whether it succeeds or fails (or is delivered but unacknowledged) is open, but the reader has been placed inside a character who is working on something consequential. Some resolution of the project's status — even an anecdote mid-process — is owed.
- **The Cynical Companies**: named as collaborators but not yet characterized. At least one anecdote should give texture to this collaboration: the specific quality of the cynicism, the register of the meeting, the thing that goes unsaid.
- **Merritt's past**: the career arc (game design → social media → State) has been established but not dramatized. At least one anecdote should render a moment from the earlier period — the compulsion loop mechanics as they were first deployed — so the reader can feel the continuity with the present project.
- **The Ethics Advocates**: rendered only in Merritt's framing so far. The obligation is not to give them interiority (this is Merritt's story) but to let one moment of their actual existence leak into Merritt's monologue — something that requires active suppression or reframing.
