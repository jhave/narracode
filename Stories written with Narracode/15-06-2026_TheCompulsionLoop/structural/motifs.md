# Motifs

## Active

**The core loop**: The fundamental game-design structure — action → variable reward → return to action — recurs at every level: as the mechanism Merritt designs (CAP), as the structure of Merritt's own thinking (strategic analysis → temporary certainty → re-encounter with the problem → strategic analysis), as the Covenant's political structure (declare threat → extract resource → declare next threat). The loop never produces a win state. The loop is what the story is.

**The variable reward schedule**: The specific mechanism that makes compulsion loops work — intermittent, unpredictable reward is more addictive than consistent reward. Recurs in: Merritt's relationship to the Bureau Director's approval, the chatbot UX being designed, the Covenant's relationship to its citizens, the scarcity religion's relationship to its God.

**The tax loop**: The financial equivalent of the game loop; recursive, infinite, extractive. Appears in economic description of the world; should also appear as structural metaphor — Merritt's own thinking as a tax loop, extracting meaning from events without allowing them to close.

**The sacrifice**: The thing offered to the God who is never appeased. Appears as: Aether's ethics researchers (their careers), non-Covenant nationals (their access to SIBYL), the Ethics Advocates (their credibility, which CAP is designed to destroy), the young (their futures, traded for the Bureau's timeline). Must never be named as sacrifice in the prose.

**The precipice**: Merritt's unnamed interior. Should recur as a physical/spatial register — a sudden depth underfoot, a view from a height, a thing that should not be looked at directly. Not psychological language. Somatic, spatial.

**The tutorial loop**: The CAP's target mechanism — keeping resistance communities in a tutorial state where they believe they are playing the real game while being held in the onboarding sequence. Recurs as a design concept and as a description of what the Covenant is doing to its own citizens.

**Windows**: As locus of the precipice — Merritt observing the world from above (offices, cars, hotel rooms), the window as the membrane between the strategic interior and the world being managed. The world seen through glass, at a remove.

## Avoid Overuse

- The core loop: do not explain it each time. Trust the reader to carry it.
- The sacrifice: never name it as such. The moment it is named, the substrate becomes didactic.
- The precipice: must remain somatic/spatial, not psychological. The moment it is named as fear, Merritt has broken character.
