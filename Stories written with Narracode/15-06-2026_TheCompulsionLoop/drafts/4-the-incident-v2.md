The call had been at 09:30. The Ambien had been at — I no longer tracked when. Processed. System clear. The faces in the grid: eight of them, the Bureau's communications director leading, the others arranged by seniority in columns the software imposed. The Director was not present. His absence was information.

The incident had been running for fourteen hours at the time of the call. I had reviewed the feed summary before joining. The orbital mechanics account: 1.2 million views by morning. I noted this on the pad beside the monitor — new pad, clean — then noted I had noted it, then set the pen down.

Someone was speaking. The comms director. Her framing was precise and I recognized it as mine — the framing I had sent at 23:47 in the overnight summary, which meant I had been awake at 23:47, which meant the Ambien had been after that. The talking points were accurate. The incident was an isolated infrastructure issue. Aether's response was appropriately transparent. No indication of external interference. No indication that any integration testing had coincided with the anomaly period. That final point: not in my overnight summary. Someone had added it.

My turn.

I said: the public discourse is running in three layers and we need to address them sequentially. Technical — infrastructure story — holds at tier one. Tier two — the speculative-state-actor narrative — is gaining traction but remains unverified and contestable. Tier three is the signal-in-noise account, the orbital mechanics framing, which is moving faster than its credential basis warrants, and that's the one that requires attention.

Someone asked why. The person who asked had the kind of face that would have tested well in a pre-2010 focus group: even, mid-range, reassuring. I found myself assessing it. Old habit, ran itself. I said: because the orbital mechanics framing doesn't make a falsifiable claim. It describes a condition — structured signal readable only by compatible systems — that can't be debunked from outside the system. Once that framing is in the discourse, absence of evidence becomes consistent with the framing rather than contrary to it. We can't refute it. We can only bury it.

The face nodded. Another face, less even, more angular: and Oduya's statement. I had been watching her statement in the side panel since the call began. Ninety-three words. Still circulating. I said: Oduya's statement is three sentences and none of them make a claim we can directly contest. The strategy is not refutation. The strategy is dilution — volume, pace, alternative authority figures.

I heard myself saying this.

The Ambien has a residue I have not characterized adequately before. Not sedation: the sedation is metabolized. What remains is a slight offset between intention and utterance — words arriving from the mouth a half-beat before I am aware of having formed them. The call was running on the layer that runs itself. The strategic layer. I have been building it for forty-five years and it operates without much input now. Useful. Not always comfortable to notice.

In the side panel: *ROCHE's outputs are not failing. They are being read by instruments not designed to read them.*

I had written something on the notepad the night before. I remembered writing it. I had removed it. The new pad this morning had no trace. This is what removing means: no trace. I knew this; I had designed this function into other systems; the knowledge had never been uncomfortable before. I noted the mild discomfort at the trace-absence. I did not note what the trace would have been.

Someone was asking about the integration test timeline. Whether documentation could be reviewed. I said: the routing through the Cynical Company liaison means the request documentation is not in Bureau records directly. Standard protocol. I said this in the same register as the other things I had said, which meant it landed in the same register, which was the correct outcome.

The faces in the grid. Eight of them. Their interiors: unavailable. Their outputs: present. I was reading the outputs, as trained, as always.

On the pad beside the monitor: 1.2 million views, orbital mechanics account. Below that, nothing. The pen still where I had set it down.

Somewhere in the side panel — I had not looked directly — the incident status had updated. Amber. Still amber. Monitoring in progress. I did not look directly because Oduya's statement was in the way and I did not move it.

The call concluded at 10:14. The grid collapsed. My face in the camera for a moment before the software closed it. I did not find this interesting.

The pad. The pen. The notation. The absence below.

Moods: managed. Occurring.
