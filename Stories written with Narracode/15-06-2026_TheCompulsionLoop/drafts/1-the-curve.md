The curve had looked like that before. The first time was in the conference room at Arca Systems — the engagement lift over baseline not 31% but 22%, which was less but was the first, and the curve was shaped like something departing from the ground, a slow lift that steepened at week three and held, held, session length increasing, return rate increasing, churn in the core demographic at 11%, and I stood at the front of the room accepting the congratulations of people who believed they understood what they had built.

Some weeks before that, in the apartment on Greel Street, the boy had played the game for forty minutes without speaking. Eight years old. The controller held in both hands in the specific way that meant the loop had taken him — I had learned to read this posture in test subjects, had in fact built the UX to produce it, the slight forward lean, the interval between deaths and respawn timed to the exact duration that sustains investment without triggering abandonment — and he was inside the curve, right there, somewhere in the fourth reward cycle where the attachment mechanism would have been activating, the point at which the game stops feeling like a game and starts feeling like something the player owns, and then he put the controller down.

Set it on the couch beside him. Stood. Walked to the window.

The window on Greel Street looked onto an interior courtyard. A brick wall, a drainpipe, in summer a strip of sky. He stood looking at it. I did not ask what he was looking at. There was nothing to look at. The data would have called this early session termination, unexplained churn, logged an outlier. The model had not predicted it. The loop had been set correctly. Something in him was somewhere the loop had nowhere to go.

My wife came through from the kitchen with a glass, looked at the boy at the window, looked at me. Said nothing.

The controller sat on the couch.

This was logged as data. It was not logged as anything else, which was the correct choice, which was the choice of someone who had built the curve and needed the curve to perform. In the conference room at Arca the following month I received the congratulations and felt the warmth of the room as data also — the same mechanism running on the team now, the Director of Product using the word breakthrough, the sensation of comprehension moving through the room as a behavioral event, the fixed-schedule reward regular as a prompt. As planned. The lift held on the screen behind us and I accepted the warmth and the curve held its lift.

The proprietary attachment mechanism. Own it. Come back to what you own.

He had stood at the window until dinner.

In the 7-to-9 cohort in the first week there were 340 early session terminations, unexplained, within acceptable parameters, and the model logged them and moved on, and in the following version the loop dropped unexplained churn in that cohort by 18%. Some of the 340 would have been children who found something outside the window. Not boredom, not frustration, not a competing engagement event. Something the variable reward schedule could not touch because it was not variable and not a reward and not a schedule. I had not built the model for this. I had not considered it a category.

The controller on the couch. My wife in the kitchen. Something tightening in the chest, slow, which I did not name then and have not looked for a name for since.

As planned. The curve performed. The model adjusted. The next version of the loop dropped churn by 18% in that cohort and the version after dropped it further and by 2006 the mechanism was in seventeen titles and the Arca team had tripled and I had moved on to the next problem.

I was at the Meridian data. The curve on the screen, the same lift, 31% and steepening. The boy's back at the Greel Street window. The controller on the couch. Confidence interval still assembling, interval at eight, Oduya's tab closed, the Director's data needed by Friday, the *unresolved cohort* lever in the framework where I had put it six weeks ago, unread. Pericardial pressure, mild, nonspecific, had occurred before, would occur again, within acceptable parameters. The curve had looked like that before.
