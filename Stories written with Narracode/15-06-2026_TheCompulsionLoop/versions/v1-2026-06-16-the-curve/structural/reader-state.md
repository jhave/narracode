# Reader State

## Current Understanding

*(No drafts yet — initiation pass. The following represents what the reader will understand after POETICS commitments are honored in early anecdotes.)*

After the first anecdote, the reader should understand:
- The narrator is unreliable in a specific way: not confused, not unintelligent, but incapable of seeing the thing the prose is showing.
- The setting is a parallel world with a recognizable mapping to contemporary political reality.
- The narrator's vocabulary (compulsion loops, core loops, engagement mechanics) is from game design but applied to political and social reality without irony.
- The narrator believes themselves to be strategic and clear. The reader is not certain.

## Expectations

The reader will likely expect:
- A moral reckoning — a moment where Merritt sees what they have done. The prose must continually approach this expectation and decline it without announcing the declination.
- The AI (SIBYL) to be somehow sentient or sympathetic. The prose must deny this without discussing it — SIBYL is a tool and the prose treats it as one.
- A plot — a sequence of events with a rising arc and resolution. The anecdote form and the interior monologue work against this. The reader may resist the non-linear, non-arc structure initially.

## Plausible Defiance Paths

- The Ethics Advocates are rendered through Merritt's framing only — but their actual position might be accessible to the reader through the quality of Merritt's suppression of it. The stronger the suppression, the more present the suppressed.
- The unnamed terror's formal leakage could become, for a careful reader, more legible than Merritt knows — the reader may name the terror before Merritt does, or instead of Merritt doing so.
- The early career memory (if drafted) could function as a counter-anecdote: the reader understands it differently than Merritt does and holds that difference.

## Productive Mystery

- What happened to Merritt that made the compulsion loop the answer? What is the unnamed terror, exactly? The text must not answer this. The accumulation of formal leakage should make the question feel answerable — the reader should feel they almost know — without the text providing the answer.
- What does the CAP actually look like? The technical architecture of Merritt's project is productively unspecified; the reader's imagination of it may be more disturbing than any specifics.

## Accidental Confusion Risk

- The non-linear anecdote form may confuse the reader about chronology; internal temporal markers should be sufficient to construct rough sequence without announcing it.
- The fictional world's names (Covenant, Bureau of Strategic Resources, SIBYL, Purity Protocol) must be used consistently and introduced as though already known — Merritt would not explain them, any more than a person explains what their government is called.
- The compulsion loop vocabulary may be alienating for readers without game-design context; Merritt's natural use of it should be self-contextualizing without being pedagogical.
