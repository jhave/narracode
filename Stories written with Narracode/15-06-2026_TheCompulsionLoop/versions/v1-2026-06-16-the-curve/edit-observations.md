## What the edits are doing

### "The admin must not become the user."
A new self-regulation maxim in 0-the-interval.md, added as a standalone sentence immediately after Crane refuses to look at the window. Crane is managing their compulsion by invoking the principle of their profession: the designer must not be captured by their own design. The maxim is both accurate and violated — it is stated in an anecdote where Crane is manifestly becoming the user (of the compulsion, of the window, of Oduya's tab). The irony is structural, not announced.

This is a recurring device available to the prose: Crane invokes professional maxims as protective formulae at exactly the moments when the maxims are being transgressed.

### Pronoun instability for the son
In 1-the-curve.md: "our boy" early (possessive plural), then "he" throughout the apartment scene, then "The boy, ours, neither perhaps, but somehow even then, unknown, an enigma" — and then "they" in the window description ("occasionally looking as if they would speak then shifting back to the window"). 

The pronoun shifts from "he" to "they" within a single anecdote, without announcement, at the moment when the son's interiority is most present: gazing, fidgeting, swaying, looking as if they would speak. The "they" is not an error. It is the grammar of what Crane cannot quite say about the boy.

"Ours, neither perhaps" — the "neither" is doing more than genitive work. The son belongs to neither of them? Is neither? The sentence doesn't complete the thought, which is the thought.

Record for the harness: the son's pronoun is unstable. "He" and "they" coexist. Future anecdotes may use either, or let the shift happen again without announcement.

### "Bardo" — the loop as intermediate state
"Inside the bardo curve" — the Tibetan Buddhist concept of the intermediate state between death and rebirth, here applied to the attachment mechanism's fourth reward cycle. The loop as bardo: the player is neither here nor there, in suspension, between one state and the next, unable to return or advance without the game providing the move.

This is available vocabulary for the compulsion loops throughout. The Bureau's seizure project is itself a bardo: between what the Covenant was and what it is becoming. The secure meeting rooms are bardo spaces. Crane exists in bardo: between strategic competence and the unnamed terror.

### "Coveted: a curve capable of shifting fate."
The "Coveted:" construction — a single word followed by a colon, then the thing. Used for naked desire: not "I coveted" but the state of coveting, present and unowned. Available for future use at moments when Crane's desire is most uncovered.

### "Disown." — the loop's negative terminus
After "Own it. Come back to what you own." — "Disown." A three-word sequence that opens with the compulsion loop's core promise (own it) and ends with its negation. The third word is the move the loop doesn't provide: exit through disownership. Applied to the game, to the boy, to whatever Crane cannot finally own.
