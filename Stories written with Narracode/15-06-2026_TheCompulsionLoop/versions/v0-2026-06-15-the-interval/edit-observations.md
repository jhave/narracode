# Edit Observations — v0 (0-the-interval.md, human edit 2026-06-15)

## What the edits are doing

### Compression: subject deletion
"I noted them. I moved on." → "Noted. Moved on."
"In the Meridian demographic breakdown:" → "Meridian demographic breakdown:"
"The second tab:" → "Second tab:"

The subject "I" drops out at moments of routine processing — when Crane is moving through data efficiently, the self disappears into the action. This is the correct interior register for those passages: not "I did X" but "X done." The compression enacts the absorption.

### Physiological naming of the compulsion
"The thumbnail showed her at the table, a posture that did not perform its confidence, and I closed the tab."
→ "...nor disguise the swelling fabric. Optic nerve to V4 contour curvature, mesolimbic dopamine pathway. Perspiring. Sip water. I closed the tab. Look away."

The compulsion is named in anatomical/neurological vocabulary — the same register Crane would use to describe a data pathway in the CAP system. Not "I was attracted" but the pathway itself, named clinically. The body runs its process; Crane observes it as a system event. This is the alignment parallel enacted at sentence level: Crane's compulsion is being treated as an activation function, the same way Crane treats ROCHE's outputs as a system process rather than as intent.

**This is the most important stylistic discovery in these edits.** Future anecdotes where the compulsion appears should use this register: anatomical, system-process, clinical. Never psychological.

### Fragment sequences at charged moments
"Reluctantly. Yet also tasting it. The saliva of anticipation. A disgust. Enthralled."
"Perspiring. Sip water. I closed the tab. Look away."

At moments of highest interior pressure, the prose fractures into single-beat fragments. No connective tissue. Each fragment is its own unit. The compression is the intensity — the syntax imitates the way thought occurs at speed, or under pressure, or when something bypasses the analytic layer.

Contrast with the longer rolling sentences used for strategic analysis. The fracture is the register of the unmanaged.

### Tense slips as compulsion markers
"Look away." — present tense in a past-tense passage.

The tense slips when the compulsion overrides. The narrative past (what Crane is recounting) briefly becomes the narrative present (what is happening to Crane right now, in the recounting). The slip is not an error — it is the mark of the loop taking over.

### Italicized double meaning
"Noted it *unresolved*." — the data category (*unresolved identity markers*) and the personal condition share the same word, and the italic activates both.

Use this sparingly. The italic should work only when the word is genuinely double — when it belongs to Crane's professional vocabulary and to their private condition simultaneously.

### "As planned." — system self-recognition
Added after the Cynical Company paragraph: "Resonance akin to a paranoid paradoxical empty conditioned reflex. As planned."

Crane acknowledging that the loop they are observing is a loop they designed — running on someone else, but recognizable as their own architecture. The "As planned" closes the observation with a claim of design intent, but the claim is itself a compulsion loop: reasserting authorship over a system that may not require the author anymore. This phrase should recur at moments where Crane encounters their own architecture running in unexpected places.

### The city as river / window as membrane
"the city was lit the way it was lit" → "the city was lit like a curve of river. Illuminated by a sun that had disappeared."

The window's view acquires texture and metaphor: the city as a flowing body (water, current, the precipice as immersion rather than just depth). The sun that has disappeared is a temporal marker (night) but also carries the sense that the illuminating authority — rational clarity, the day's productivity — has gone.
