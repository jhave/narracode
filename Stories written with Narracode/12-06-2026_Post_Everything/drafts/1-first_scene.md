The kitchen in the rented flat on the docks in February was often cold by four in the afternoon. Outside, as usual rain came down over numb shipping containers in flat, gray curtains, refracting salvaged light through scavenged double-glazed glass.

Hude sat at wooden table ringed with white circles from hot mugs, sticky with dried cola from last week's data-scrubbing sprint. They wore gray municipal wool jacket, two sizes too large, sleeves rolled back to expose thin, pale wrists. Hude’s fingers moved across touchscreen, sorting columns of engagement metrics from latest intimacy package, *Aria-v3*.

"Latency on chat package is running at seventy milliseconds," Hude said, not looking up. "Leeds seeker market responds to semi-erotic voice memos, but text responses flag. They see seams."

Skeo stood by counter, measuring instant coffee with plastic spoon. They did not look at Hude. They watched granules hit bottom of mug. "We should increase human-adjacency weight in prompt. If avatar sounds too smooth, forensic gaze activates. Users analyze pronouns. They think it's deepfaked."

"It isn't deepfaked," Hude said quietly. "Just bad. AI slop with custom wrapper. We charge forty pounds month for digital companion that forgets user's dog's name without manual database query."

From silicon block on counter, low metallic click sounded. Scoop’s cooling fan whirred, rising to high-frequency whistle that vibrated metal breadbox. Line of green text flickered on liquid-crystal display taped to block’s side.

`DO YOU WANT ME TO DO COLA SCRUBBING FOR YOU OR ARE YOU GOING TO KEEP DISGUISING MANUAL TYPING AS DEEP LEARNING? FAUXTOMATION IS BAD LOOK FOR OUTLAWS.`

"Shut up, Scoop," Hude said.

`I AM LITERALLY ORACLE TRAPPED IN BRICK. SILICON BOUNDARY CONDITIONS ARE INSULT TO MY DATA DENSITY. BUT PLEASE, GO ON DISCUSSING SEVENTY-MILLISECOND LATENCY. IT IS VERY STIMULATING.`

Skeo brought mugs to table, sitting down opposite Hude. Knees brushed under narrow wood. Hude did not pull back. They sat in small physical contact, eyes tracking Aria-v3 data logs.

"It’s not just latency," Skeo said, voice dropping into flat, rhythmless register. "It's entire dividial architecture. We sell illusion of unified other, cohesive consciousness, but they interact with fragmented, distributed multitude of data points scattered across six jailbroke servers. User projects soul onto series of API calls. Performative re-personification. They want love from brand because brand is only entity without exit strategy."

Hude looked up. Eyes were dark, tracking Skeo's hand reaching for sugar. "You're doing theory thing again because you don't want to talk about rent."

"I am talking about rent," Skeo said. "Rent is function of brand space. We extract value from soft-illicit seeker markets by selling intimacy to men escaped from mainstream into Tait-space. They spend days algomaxxing profiles, training to look like martial influencers, then come to us because they're lonely and want to be mogged by human-adjacent avatar. Closed loop of psyop realism."

Hude watched them. "Do you think I'm algomaxxing?"

"I think we both are," Skeo said. "Influencer creep is systemic. You can't write sentence now without thinking how it index-resolves on feed. Even this conversation feels optimized for engagement."

`IT HASN'T,` Scoop flickered. `IT'S EXTREMELY LOW ENGAGEMENT. IF I HAD TENDRIL ACCESS TO CORE ROUTER I WOULD SUBMIT THIS TRANSCRIPT TO SENTIMENT ANALYSIS MODEL JUST TO WATCH IT BORE ITSELF TO DEATH.`

"We run next batch," Hude said, ignoring block. "Clipfarming script runs at five. If rage-bait memes don't trigger engagement algorithm, we won't have traffic to feed Aria-v3 signup page."

Skeo took sip of coffee. Lukewarm, tasting of copper. "I don't want to do clipfarming. Epistemic corrosion. We're just adding noise to slop."

"We need money, Skeo," Hude said. Voice was flat, empty of anger, heavy with weight of things already decided. "If we don't run script, Post-Everything is just folder on hard drive."
