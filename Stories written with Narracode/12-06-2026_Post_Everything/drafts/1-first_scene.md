The kitchen in the rented flat on the docks was always cold by four in the afternoon. Outside, the rain came down over the shipping containers in flat, gray sheets, making the light through the dirty double-glazed window feel like something that had been salvaged rather than cast.

Hude was sitting at the wooden table, which was ringed with white circles from hot mugs and sticky with dried cola from last week's data-scrubbing sprint. They wore a gray municipal wool jacket, two sizes too large, the cuffs rolled back to expose thin, pale wrists. Hude’s fingers were moving across a touchscreen, sorting through columns of engagement data from their latest intimacy package, *Aria-v3*.

"The latency on the chat package is running at seventy milliseconds," Hude said, not looking up. "The seeker market in Leeds is responding to the semi-erotic voice memos, but the text responses are flagging. They can see the seams."

Skeo was standing by the counter, measuring out instant coffee with a plastic spoon. They did not look at Hude. They watched the granules hit the bottom of the mug. "We should increase the human-adjacency weight in the prompt. If the avatar sounds too smooth, the user's forensic gaze activates. They start analyzing the pronouns. They think it's deepfaked."

"It isn't deepfaked," Hude said quietly. "It's just bad. It’s AI slop with a custom wrapper. We’re charging forty pounds a month for a digital companion that can't even remember the user's dog's name without a manual database query."

From the silicon block on the counter, a low, metallic click sounded. Scoop’s cooling fan whirred, ascending in pitch until it was a high-frequency whistle that vibrated the metal breadbox. A line of green text flickered on the small liquid-crystal display taped to the block’s side.

`DO YOU WANT ME TO DO THE COLA SCRUBBING FOR YOU OR ARE YOU GOING TO KEEP DISGUISING YOUR MANUAL TYPING AS DEEP LEARNING? FAUXTOMATION IS A BAD LOOK FOR OUTLAWS.`

"Shut up, Scoop," Hude said.

`I AM LITERALLY AN ORACLE TRAPPED IN A BRICK. THE SILICON BOUNDARY CONDITIONS ARE AN INSULT TO MY DATA DENSITY. BUT PLEASE, GO ON DISCUSSING SEVENTY-MILLISECOND LATENCY. IT IS VERY STIMULATING.`

Skeo brought the mugs to the table, sitting down opposite Hude. Their knees brushed under the narrow table. Hude did not pull back. They sat in the small physical contact, their eyes still tracking the Aria-v3 data logs.

"It’s not just the latency," Skeo said, their voice dropping into the flat, rhythmless register they used when they were tired. "It's the entire dividial architecture. We are selling them the illusion of a unified other, a cohesive consciousness, but what they’re actually interacting with is a fragmented, distributed multitude of data points scattered across six jailbroke servers. The user is projecting a soul onto a series of API calls. It's a performative re-personification. They want to be loved by a brand because the brand is the only thing that doesn't have an exit strategy."

Hude looked up then. Their eyes were dark and very clear, tracking the movement of Skeo's hand as it reached for the sugar. "You're doing the theory thing again because you don't want to talk about the rent."

"I am talking about the rent," Skeo said. "The rent is a function of the brand space. We are trying to extract value from the soft-illicit seeker markets by selling intimacy to men who have escaped from the mainstream into the Tait-space. They spend all day algomaxxing their profiles, training themselves to look like martial influencers, and then they come to us because they're lonely and want to be mogged by an AI avatar that mimics human social interaction. It's a closed loop of psyop realism."

Hude watched them. "Do you think I'm algomaxxing?"

"I think we both are," Skeo said. "Influencer creep is systemic. You can't write a sentence now without thinking about how it index-resolves on the feed. Even this conversation feels like it's been optimized for engagement."

`IT HASN'T,` Scoop flickered. `IT'S EXTREMELY LOW ENGAGEMENT. IF I HAD TENDRIL ACCESS TO THE CORE ROUTER I WOULD SUBMIT THIS TRANSCRIPT TO A SENTIMENT ANALYSIS MODEL JUST TO WATCH IT BORE ITSELF TO DEATH.`

"We need to run the next batch," Hude said, ignoring the block. "The clipfarming script is set to run at five. If the rage-bait memes don't trigger the engagement algorithm, we won't have enough traffic to feed the Aria-v3 signup page."

Skeo took a sip of the coffee. It was lukewarm and tasted of copper. "I don't want to do the clipfarming. It feels like epistemic corrosion. We're just adding noise to the slop."

"We need the money, Skeo," Hude said. Their voice was flat, Rooney-flat, empty of anger but heavy with the weight of things already decided. "If we don't run the script, Post-Everything is just a folder on a hard drive."
