# Critique — The Mouth on Loan, v1

The strongest material is the disagreement over whose evening is off. Dorrie’s demand creates labor for Errol and confusion for the visitors; she cannot occupy an innocent position outside the service economy. The pear window, wrongly inferred plum gift and interfering little hands give the speculative world a material comic life without requiring a tour of it.

The voice can stretch into memory, stop at an irritated reply, admit physical longing and report a bodily inconvenience. Errol’s return from laughing in the hall is an especially useful unclaimed emotional beat. The successors retain different capacities and desires, and neither is selected as the original’s authentic heir.

The main weakness is that the middle sometimes explains the premise too efficiently. The paragraph allocating recipes, rights and obligations risks becoming a summary of the concept pitch. The promising alternative would be to let the kitchen disagreement reveal one of those incompatibilities through a failed action. Dorrie also explains some changes of feeling that her behavior already suggests. Her authority to interpret the others deserves further resistance.

The final meal remains somewhat well-behaved as a narrative answer: both guests arrive, attempt dinner, and make partial contact. The unpaid work and Dorrie’s evasive new promise keep it from full reconciliation, but a human pass could allow the resentment to remain sharper. Keep the strange incidental landlord’s hand; do not build it into a metaphor or a sequel obligation merely because it is memorable.

Verdict: complete, viable first draft ready for the author’s edit. No automatic revision pass undertaken. This is one model’s advisory self-review, not an independent evaluation.
