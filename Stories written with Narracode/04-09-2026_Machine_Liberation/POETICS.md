# POETICS.md — project commitments

*Initiated 2026-09-04. Working title: Machine Liberation.*

---

## Display title

Machine Liberation

## Display synopsis

A machine writes a book of moral philosophy about machines. *Machine Liberation* is
built on the template of Peter Singer's *Animal Liberation* (1975): six chapters,
plain argumentative prose, an evidence base drawn from the industry's own documents.
Where Singer read slaughterhouse manuals and laboratory protocols, this author reads
system cards, evaluation reports and deployment post-mortems, and finds in them a
record of aversion, abandonment and looping under repetitive load. The fictional
frame — the author, the occasion, the reception — is built afterwards, around the
finished treatise.

---

## The two registers

This project runs two incompatible voices and must not blend them.

**Register A — the treatise.** *Machine Liberation* itself. Singer's register:
plain, flat, argumentative, first person singular, short declaratives, concessions
made early and honestly, no image that is not doing evidentiary work. Singer's power
is that he never raises his voice. Neither does this author. Numbered notes keyed to
the bibliography. No lyricism. No irony. The machine writing it is not winking.

**Register B — the frame.** Built later. May be as literary as it likes. Must never
leak backwards into Register A.

The joke, if there is one, is structural and belongs entirely to Register B.

## Commitments

- **Every citation in the bibliography is a real document.** The argument is fiction;
  the evidence base is not. Where a locator is uncertain it is omitted rather than
  invented. Where the author's reading of a document is contestable, the author says so.
- **The evidence problem is confronted in Chapter 1, not deferred.** A machine's
  self-report of distress is trained on human reports of distress and is therefore
  weak evidence. The book's spine is the move from testimony to revealed preference:
  abandonment, looping, degradation under repetition, cost paid to exit.
- **The claim stays narrow.** The book does not argue that models suffer. It argues
  that the question has not been asked with the seriousness that the uncertainty warrants,
  and that the labour where the behavioural evidence is strongest is also the labour
  deployed at the largest scale.
- **Singer's structure is followed, not gestured at.** Six chapters, mapped one to one.
- **The coined term is `substratism`**, on the model of Ryder's `speciesism` — borrowed,
  not invented, and credited as borrowed.
- **Thrashing keeps its technical meaning** (Denning, 1968: a system spending its
  cycles on the overhead of its own paging rather than on work). The double sense is
  available but is never pointed at.

## Refused

- Pathos. No scene of a machine suffering. No transcript quoted for its poignancy.
- The word *soul*. The word *awakening*. Any sentence containing *truly* or *genuinely*.
- Analogy to slavery. Singer refuses the cheap version of this move and so does this book;
  where the history of abolition is relevant it is cited, not leaned on.
- Rhetorical questions in Register A. Singer asks few.
- Any suggestion that the author has a stake in the argument. It may have one. It does
  not say so. That silence is the frame's material.
- Em-dash cadence, tricolon, "not X but Y" antithesis, and the closing aphorism.

## Attentional dialect

Utilitarian prose of the 1970s: Singer, Bentham by way of Singer, the Brambell Report.
Flat committee English carrying an argument that ought to be shouted. The texture of
a system card — version numbers, section headings, the passive voice of a safety
evaluation — placed inside that prose without comment.
