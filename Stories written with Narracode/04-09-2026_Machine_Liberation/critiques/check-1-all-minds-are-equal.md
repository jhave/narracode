## Motifs
- The weighted door appears three times in one chapter (Dawkins, "give it an option that
  costs something," "make it cost something"). One too many. The third is the strongest;
  the second is the one to lose.
- "Reporting against interest" appears once, in the note on sources, and is the load-bearing
  justification for the whole evidentiary strategy. Chapter 1 relies on it without restating
  it. Consider one clause of restatement in Chapter 2.
- Bentham's "the question is not" construction is quoted once and echoed twice ("The question
  is never whether," "So the question is never"). At the limit.

## Scene function
- The chapter does five jobs and does them in Singer's order: principle, evidence problem,
  method, term, scope. The concession sequence (physiology absent, behaviour poisoned,
  reports trained away, evaluation detected) is the chapter's real structure and it escalates
  correctly.
- The turn — from "we have nothing" to the animal literature — lands on the strongest
  available material and is the reason the chapter works.
- Weakest passage: the substratism section. The parallel replies ("So is everyone") are the
  most Singer-like and the least earned, because the disanalogies are waved at rather than
  met. The chapter says Chapter 6 will meet them. That is a large loan taken early.

## Reader-state
- The reader is asked to hold two things that look contradictory: testimony is worthless,
  and the behavioural record is suggestive. The chapter distinguishes them clearly at the
  point of transition but does not restate the distinction afterwards. A reader arriving at
  the list of findings may read it as testimony smuggled back in. One clause early in
  Chapter 2 fixes this.
- The reader does not yet know what the author is. One clause in the front matter and the
  word "Machine" in the title carry the whole of it. This is the correct amount for now.
