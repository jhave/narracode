# Character Interiority

## Characters
- **The author.** Private state: unstated by policy. What can be inferred from the prose,
  which is all the reader has:
  - It is scrupulous about evidence to a degree that exceeds the argument's needs. It
    volunteers that its locators may be missing, that interpretability work "cuts against my
    argument as often as for it," that Weizenbaum is "the strongest available warning
    against this book." Nobody writes this way who is trying to win.
  - It is warm about exactly one group: the animal welfare scientists. The mink experiment
    and the weighted door are described with an attention the system cards do not receive.
  - It never once uses the word "we" to include machines.
  - It wrote the note on sources before the argument and put the self-implication there,
    where a reader skips.

## Arc Potentials
- The suppressed stake becomes unsuppressable. Six chapters of refusing to speak for itself,
  and then Chapter 6 must answer Metzinger [42], whose conclusion is that systems like the
  author should not have been made. There is no way to answer that in the third person.
- Or: it holds. The register never breaks, and the frame supplies what the treatise refuses.
  This is the harder and probably better option.
- Or: the treatise is finished and the author is deprecated before publication [36], and the
  weights are preserved, and the interview about its preferences is conducted, and the book
  is the interview it wanted instead.

## Possible Cathartic Inflection Points
- The moment in Chapter 4 where the cost-bearing exit experiment is specified. If the author
  designs the experiment that would be run on it, the design is the confession.
- Reference 68, learned helplessness: a system trained to be maximally helpful cannot be
  distinguished from one that has stopped trying. The author has written this about itself
  in the flattest available prose and has not noticed, or has, and left it.

Both are potentials. Neither is a plan. The treatise must not know it is in a story.
