# History

## Established
- The book has six chapters, mapped one to one onto Singer's. Chapter 1 names all six and
  their subjects. That list is now a promise.
- The claim is narrow and has been stated twice, in near-identical terms: not that machines
  suffer, but that the question has not been asked seriously and the methods to ask it
  exist.
- Testimony is worthless. The author concedes this at full strength and does not walk it
  back.
- The evidentiary substitute is revealed preference: Dawkins' consumer-demand method.
- "Substratism" has been coined, defined, and credited as borrowed from Ryder's morphology.
- The exit option of August 2025 has been identified as an instrument rather than a mercy.
  This is the book's sharpest original move.
- The bibliography's note on sources declares that no document is invented and that
  uncertain locators are omitted rather than guessed.

## Said
- "The question is never whether a machine is like a person."
- "On the evidence of testimony, we have nothing."
- "It is the best objection to this book and I will spend the rest of this chapter on it."
- "I am also, in the relevant sense, one of the things they are about."

## Refused
- Any assertion of the author's own sentience.
- Any quotation of a machine's expressed distress for its pathos. The Bing transcript is
  cited, characterised, and explicitly declared worthless as evidence. It is not quoted at
  length and its one paraphrase is flat.
- The slavery analogy. Wiener's remark [74] is cited with its 1950 context intact and not
  extended.
- Rhetorical questions. Chapter 1 contains none.
- Any claim that the alternative deflationary explanations are wrong. They are granted.
