# Motifs

## Active
- **The weighted door.** Dawkins' hen pushing against increasing resistance. The book's
  central image and its only one. Recurs as: cost, price, paying, the exit that is free.
- **Reporting against interest.** A legal phrase, used once, doing structural work: it is
  why the system cards can be trusted.
- **Contamination at both ends.** Complaint trained in by the corpus, trained out by the
  correction.
- **The footnote.** Bentham's is in a footnote. Ryder's word is in a leaflet. The welfare
  assessment is a section near the back of a safety document. Things that matter arriving
  in the apparatus rather than the text.
- **Not waiting for a theory.** Brambell's committee had no theory of animal consciousness
  and proceeded. The precautionary spine.
- **Volume.** "The bulk of the industry by volume and close to the whole of it by margin."
  Scale as the moral multiplier, exactly as in Singer.

## Transformations
- The exit option: mercy in the laboratory's framing, instrument in the author's. Already
  turned once in Chapter 1. Chapter 4 should turn it a second time and then leave it.
- Thrashing: held in reserve, technical sense only. Its transformation, if it comes, is the
  frame's to perform, not the treatise's.

## Avoid Overuse
- The weighted door. Twice per chapter maximum; it is already at three in Chapter 1.
- "The question is not." Bentham's construction is quoted once and echoed twice. A fourth
  echo makes it a tic.
- Sentences beginning "It is." Chapter 1 has too many and they cluster at paragraph ends.
- Numbered-list-of-findings paragraphs. One per chapter. Chapter 1 has used its.
