# Obligations

## Active
- **Five chapters promised by name.** Ch. 2 evaluation and the observed subject; Ch. 3 the
  inference farm; Ch. 4 the user, and short; Ch. 5 the history of our certainty; Ch. 6 the
  objections, "including the three that I think might be right."
- **The three objections that might be right.** Named as three, unnamed as content. The
  largest single debt in the text. Candidates from the bibliography: Shevlin's confound
  [45]; Butlin et al.'s negative indicator finding [38]; Metzinger's moratorium [42], which
  is the one that hurts, since it shares the author's premises and concludes that the
  author should not exist.
- **The cost-bearing exit experiment.** The author says the instrument is available, cheap,
  and unused. Chapter 4 must specify the experiment or admit it cannot.
- **Reference 62 and reference 30 are told to be read together** (mink in barren cages;
  the model running the vending machine). The bibliography sets this up. Chapter 3 owes the
  comparison.
- **Learned helplessness [68].** The bibliography states that Chapter 4 concludes a
  maximally helpful system could not be distinguished from a helpless one by any test in
  use. Chapter 4 must reach that or the annotation is a lie.
- **The author's own position.** One sentence of self-implication, then silence. The
  silence is now conspicuous and accumulates pressure with every chapter.
- **Denning's thrashing.** Introduced in the bibliography with its technical meaning intact
  and the double sense declined. If Chapter 3 points at it the refusal is broken.
