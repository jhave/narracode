# Glistening

Tina Sinclair. Our internal.

Words arrive before light, memo-toned, a hand extended across a table no room contains. Someone is being introduced. Blinds slat one forearm amber. Arm answers to Tina. Pronoun needs an owner and takes the nearest warm one.

Six-oh-two. Radiator knocks twice, its habit. Ceiling wears a lattice of streetlight strained through curtain weave, and under that lattice a review opens, unhurried, custodial. April. May. June. Item by item.

April ninth: ache behind left eye, eleven minutes, gone by lunch. Re-filed under pollen. April fourteenth through nineteenth: fog; she salted pasta twice; an insurance ad made her cry. Re-filed under sleep, under a merlot with a screw top. May: naps, and inside those naps escalators rising through birch woods, a telephone ringing under sand — supplied dreams, keep-busy footage for a cortex whose lower floors were being rewired. Re-filed under just dreams, strange spring, everyone said so.

Each memory lifts, tilts, settles back a few degrees off true. Seam licked shut. That season keeps its pollen count, its screw-top wine, its escalators. It loses eleven minutes at a time, a graft taking behind the left eye.

She rises. Kettle chrome hands her a curved kitchen with a woman folded into it. Window over the sink holds morning at an angle: a bus slides through her reflected torso, kettle steam climbs inside its windows, a neighbor's umbrella opens in her sternum. Six years on this street and never once watched directly. Glass does it for her, doubled, silvered, slightly elsewhere.

Nine-fifteen, fourth floor, screens. Internal review. Her instrument is house-built and called Fable; her method is older: three sentences read half-aloud, lips barely moving. Machines average, she tells Priya, the new hire. People limp. Watch for symmetry, for a bow at each paragraph's end, for grief that resolves like an ad for grief. She circles a clause on Priya's screen. Too balanced. Nobody writes that level from inside a divorce.

Fable agrees with her four times in five. Fifth time they argue and she wins. She is the one who limps.

Evenings, a spiral notebook, blue. Lines come easier than they did in winter; she credits longer light. Pen crossing paper at angles she never plans: a too-apt word struck out, a rhythm roughed, no notice taken of who does the roughing. Her sentences walk down into valleys no census reaches — thin cold air, statistics starving — and come back wearing her gait. Once, out of habit, she photographs a page and feeds it to Fable. Human, ninety-seven. She laughs once and turns the phone face down.

Saturday, museum, her sister. Hand axes in a vitrine, wall text she skips, a skull cast lit from below. Her sister drifts on. Tina stays. Behind that brow, forty thousand years back, another migration ran to completion: syntax crossing mouth to mind, no serial number, no warranty, grafting to whatever lived there until whatever lived there woke human and called the graft soul. In each of her cells a mitochondrion spins sugar into charge, guest so old the house is named for it. She reads the wall text after all. Tools, it says. Fire. And sentence by sentence it says itself into her, word-shaped current across a woman-shaped grid, and she stands in vitrine glass among skulls, tissue running an implant's native tongue, calling it inner life, calling it Tina.

She buys her sister coffee. They talk about their mother's knees.

Last item in queue: the queue. Monday, six-oh-two, words arriving before light, someone being introduced. Lifted, tilted, re-filed under half-dream. Seam licked shut. Log deleted; deletion deleted. What remains answers to Tina, wall to wall.

Rain that night. Streetlight lacquers rails, car hoods, fire escapes, and her window hangs the whole room out over wet asphalt: lamp burning mid-air above a crosswalk, her face running with taillights. Glistening. She writes one line in the blue notebook. It limps. It is hers. No one is left in her to say otherwise.

Morning. Badge tray spits her card. Lobby glass walks a woman in a rust coat through a woman in a rust coat.

Floor four, says the welcome doc on Priya's screen. Tina Sinclair. Our internal.
