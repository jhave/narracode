# Critique — Glistening (full, against POETICS)

## What works
- **The inversion carries everything.** Opening at completion — waking, then housekeeping — removes every thriller reflex the premise invites. The infiltration exists only as a ledger of alibis (pollen, screw-top merlot, escalators), so the reader performs the reconstruction the story refuses to perform. That is the "do, don't tell" commitment executed at the structural level, not just the sentence level.
- **The window discipline holds without exception.** Kettle chrome, sink window, vitrine, lobby glass, the rain-hung room: perception arrives doubled and at an angle in all five sites, and the discipline is never announced. "A neighbor's umbrella opens in her sternum" does the fable's whole anatomy in seven words.
- **The recursive layer lands where it should — in a number.** "Human, ninety-seven" is the story's only verdict, delivered by the instrument named Fable to prose written by what Fable was built to catch. The defeat of detection happens off-page, inside a score, and the mentor scene ("machines average, people limp") arms the irony without cashing it.
- **The museum earns the thesis.** Syntax as elder implant, mitochondrion as elder guest — one paragraph, tactile throughout (skull cast lit from below, sugar into charge, wall text "saying itself into her"), then immediately ballasted by coffee and their mother's knees. The deep claim (language rooted in network topology, the original endosymbiont) is present as image-pressure, never as argument.
- **Article diet.** Omissions cluster in ledger and waking ("Arm answers to Tina," "gone by lunch," "never once watched directly") and relax in reflective clauses ("the whole room out over wet asphalt") — pressurized, not robotic, per master poetics §2.A.
- **The circular close.** "Our internal" returns owned by an onboarding doc, its possessive still unhoused. No bow.

## Where it runs risk
- **"calling it inner life, calling it Tina"** is the closest approach to a quotable moral. It survives because it sits inside the museum image-chain, but it is the first candidate for the human's pruning pass if the piece reads even one degree too knowing.
- **"Pronoun needs an owner and takes the nearest warm one"** — aphorism-adjacent. Strong enough to keep; flagged as second candidate.
- **Priya is furniture.** Acceptable in a fable this brief, but two more words of her (a habit, a mispronunciation) would buy the close more weight.
- **Density ceiling.** At ~700 words with no plain-breathing paragraph except the coffee beat, some readers will need two passes. POETICS accepts this; noting it.

## LLM-tell audit (against the evasion challenge)
- No paradox-aphorism opener, no chiasmus, no "not X but Y" rhetoric, no tricolon-with-bow, no corrective-superiority stance, no "something in her." Sentence-length variance is high; two syntactic roughnesses left unsanded on purpose. The cadence deformation the story describes is the cadence the story attempts.
