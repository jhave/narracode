# CHARACTER INTERIORITY — *Hendane*

Private states. What characters know, feel, withhold, and almost-think. Hallvard gets the deepest treatment; secondary characters receive enough interior to be people, not props.

---

## HALLVARD NYGAARD

### The boredom (Act I)

Hallvard's boredom is *not* depression. It is not numbness. It is not burnout. It is, technically, the Svendsen-shaped condition: the modern, post-religious boredom of a person whose competence outruns their inner stakes. He has read this and recognized himself. He is not displeased by the recognition — he is, in a private way, *relieved*. To name a thing is to thin its grip slightly.

The boredom expresses itself as:
- A faint, low buzz behind every meeting.
- A tendency to count tiles, ceiling panels, the number of steps from his front door to the Bybanen stop (74).
- A liking for tasks with clear completion criteria. (The mold is a clear task.)
- A mild, persistent suspicion that his life is *fine* — *greit nok* — and that this is, somehow, the problem.

He does not articulate any of this aloud. He does not journal. He is not "working on it." He is a man who has accepted a condition and built a life inside it. The story finds him at the edge of an unexpected discovery: that the boredom has a *lateral exit*, not an upward one.

### The pleasure of the channel (Act II)

The first time Hallvard watches his own video — the grout cleaning, before he posts it — he feels something he does not have a precise name for. It is not pride. It is not vanity (the video has no face). It is closer to *recognition*. The clip shows hands doing a small thing well, against an interesting background. He thinks: *Eg gjorde dette*. [I did this — but in Bergensk, which means: I made this, and I am slightly surprised.]

When the channel begins to grow, the pleasure refines. It has components:
- The pleasure of *craft*. He is getting better at editing, at framing, at choosing AI backgrounds. He is teaching himself in real time.
- The pleasure of *anonymity*. He is being seen without being seen. The hands are him; he is not the hands.
- The pleasure of *secrecy*. He carries the channel through NERV meetings like a small warm stone in his pocket.
- The pleasure of *Bergen recognizing itself*. The local in-jokes (the Zalo bottle, the kaviar tube, the rain) land in Bergen comment threads with a specificity that thrills him.

The pleasure is *not*:
- Vanity (he does not want fame; the anonymity is integral).
- Revenge (he is not getting back at NERV, or Marit, or anyone).
- A search for meaning (he is not lost; he is bored, which is different).

### The vertigo of the double life (Act III)

When Solveig shows him the video in the lift, Hallvard experiences something he later calls — privately, in his own Bergensk — *dobbeltsynet*. [the double-sight; the seeing of two things at once that should not coexist.] He sees his own hands on her phone screen. He sees her face, looking at his hands, unrecognizing.

The vertigo has phases:
- **Astonishment** that no one recognizes him. He is not a great actor; the camouflage is the world's, not his.
- **A flicker of wanting to be caught** — quickly suppressed, never indulged. He does not want exposure. He wants the *near-miss*.
- **A growing intimacy with strangers.** Bergen knows his hands. Bergen does not know his name. He feels closer to the comment threads than to his colleagues.
- **A small, dry guilt** about Mona the cleaner, who he greets every morning, who he could mention this to and won't.

He never confides in anyone. The closest he comes is, one night, telling Berit the plant. The narrator does not make this twee. It is one sentence. He waters her after.

### The grief of the ban (Act IV)

When the flag email arrives, Hallvard's first emotion is *procedural*. He has read the ToS. He has expected this for two weeks (the classifier scores have been creeping upward). He files the appeal because filing the appeal is what one does.

When the appeal is denied, the grief arrives. It is real and surprising in its specificity:
- He grieves the *rhythm*. Sundays will not have shape now.
- He grieves the *craft-trajectory*. He had a video planned for next week — a thing with sour-cream tubes and a hand-swap of an old fisherman's hand. He will not make it. He grieves the unmade thing.
- He grieves *Bergen's loss* — the comment threads going quiet, the in-jokes evaporating, the city's small new shared object dissolving.
- He grieves the *recognition* — being seen without being seen — which is now over.

In the empty kitchen evening, late, he arrives at the surprise: *the platform was right*. The work was AI-generated. The classifier did its job. He had been hiding behind a classification gap that has now closed. The realization does not dissolve the grief. The grief survives the realization. This is the thing the narrator may name, gently, with Vonnegutian directness: *"You can know a thing is fair and still be hurt by it. This is one of the small structural truths of being alive."*

### The glee of the reveal (Act V)

Joy is the wrong word. *Glee* is closer — it has the dryness, the slight perversity, the Bergen edge. Hallvard's glee at *Hendane: Avkledd* is the glee of:
- Showing the apparatus. He has been hiding the labour; now he gets to show the labour. Labour is, secretly, what he is proudest of.
- Honouring the platform's correctness. He is not contesting the ban. He is *agreeing* with it, on his own terms, in public.
- Lateral motion. He has not ascended. He has not been broken. He has stepped sideways into a new room.
- Being slightly more visible. His face, briefly, in passing. The narrator notes that this is the first time anyone besides Mona, Solveig, and a teenage cashier has seen Hallvard's face this week.

The glee is *quiet*. It is not triumphant. The final image — the upload bar at 67%, the rain at the window, Hallvard's actual hands on the keyboard — is glee at low temperature.

### Hallvard's voice (interior monologue rules)

- Mostly Bergensk in cadence, even when English on the page.
- Dry, slightly behind events (he understands what is happening to him a beat after the reader).
- Self-mocking but not self-loathing.
- Allergic to grand statements about himself. When the narrator starts to make one *for* him, he interrupts, mentally, with *greit nok*.
- Comfortable with silence. He does not narrate his every gesture to himself.
- Loves a list. Lists comfort him.

## SECONDARY CHARACTERS (interior states, lightweight)

### Solveig Tveit (director, ~55)
Interior: Tired, competent, slightly relieved every day that Hallvard exists. Watches *Hendane* videos at 22:30 on her phone before bed because they are, she thinks, the most interesting thing happening in Bergen right now. Does not for one second consider that her own administrator might be the creator — not because Hallvard is unsuspectable, but because she has never had to suspect him of anything and the muscle has atrophied.

### Aksel Berg (communications, ~31)
Interior: Mildly bored, mildly online, a frustrated semi-creative. Posts on Vestlendingen.no under the handle *vestlandsbygd*. Has constructed a sleuthing-grid in a Google Doc. Genuinely loves the channel. Would be delighted to discover the creator. Would also be slightly devastated, because the mystery is the part he loves.

### Ingvild Rasmussen (senior researcher, ~42)
Interior: Practical, fond of Hallvard, mostly thinking about her teenager. Reports to Hallvard on Thursday that the mold recipe worked because she is a person who reports working things. Does not connect the recipe to Hallvard in any way; he is the man who books the conference rooms.

### Mona (cleaner, ~60s)
Interior: Almost not rendered. She is a person Hallvard greets every morning. She has her own life, fully present off-page, which the narrator gestures at once: *she has two grandchildren in Sotra and a back that hurts on Wednesdays*. Mona is the only person at NERV whose first name Hallvard uses every day. He never wonders what her last name is. This is the small structural shame of his good life.

### Marit (ex-partner, past-tense only)
Interior: Withheld. Mentioned in two lines: she moved out in 2021; they send each other a birthday SMS. The narrator does not enter her. The refusal is the point.

### Linn Hauge (BT journalist, ~34)
Interior: Curious, tenacious, professional. Writes the *"Kven er Hendane?"* feature. Talks to twelve Bergensers; none of them are Hallvard. Does not consider Hallvard, because she has never heard of him. Files her piece. Moves on.

### The teenage cashier at Kiwi (no name)
Interior: She does not see Hallvard's face when he smiles at her. The narrator does not punish her for this. She is at the end of her shift. She is fine.

## INTERIOR-EXTERIOR ASYMMETRY (the engine of the comedy)

The whole novella runs on this asymmetry:
- Hallvard's interior is *vivid* (channel, craft, grief, glee).
- His exterior is *unchanged* (beige, reliable, on time).
- Everyone around him perceives only the exterior.
- The reader perceives both.

This asymmetry is the camouflage, the comedy, the vertigo, and ultimately the structure of the lateral exit. The Compositional agent should preserve it scrupulously. Any scene where a colleague *almost* sees through Hallvard must end with the colleague *not* seeing through. Any scene where Hallvard *almost* slips must end with him *not* slipping.

The world's failure to imagine him is the gift, the joke, and the freedom.
