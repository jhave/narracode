# READER STATE — *Hendane*

What the reader knows, expects, and wonders, by act. The Compositional agent should use this to calibrate exposition: what the reader needs to be told now versus what they will discover later.

---

## At Act I opening

**Known (immediately, by paragraph two):**
- This is a story set in Bergen.
- The protagonist is Hallvard Nygaard, 39, an administrator at a research institute.
- The dialect-translation convention (Bergensk in italics, bracketed English vibe-gloss).
- The narrator is allowed to speak directly to the reader.

**Expected (by reading the back-cover-shaped first chapter):**
- This is a comedy of some kind.
- The boredom is the topic.
- Bergen is the texture.
- Something is going to happen to relieve, or fail to relieve, the boredom.

**Productive mysteries (live):**
- Q1: What does NERV research? *(Never answered — this is a load-bearing refusal.)*
- Q2: What is Hallvard going to do? *(Answered in Act I close / Act II open: he is going to film.)*
- Q3: What is the lateral exit from boredom? *(Answered in motion across the whole novella.)*

---

## At Act I close → Act II open

**Newly known:**
- Hallvard owns AI tools.
- Hallvard has filmed the grout-cleaning.
- Hallvard has posted (or is about to post) the first video.
- The channel is called *Hendane*.

**Expected:**
- The channel will grow. (The premise of the book.)
- Something will go wrong, or change. (Genre intuition.)

**Productive mysteries (live):**
- Q1: What does NERV research? *(Still open.)*
- Q4: How big will the channel get? *(Will accelerate through Act II.)*
- Q5: Will anyone recognize him? *(Begins to live in Act II, peaks in Act III.)*
- Q6: What is the tagline going to be? *(Answered mid-Act II: *ingen dagar utan tips*.)*

---

## At Act II close → Act III open

**Newly known:**
- The channel is a Bergen phenomenon.
- The tagline is *ingen dagar utan tips* and it echoes *ingen dagar utan regn*.
- The AI pipeline is real, technical, labor-intensive.
- Hallvard has not told anyone.
- He has, in the privacy of his kitchen, told Berit the plant. (One line.)

**Expected:**
- Recognition is coming. The colleagues will encounter the channel.
- The vertigo of the double life is about to begin.

**Productive mysteries (live):**
- Q1: What does NERV research? *(Still open; the narrator may now wink at this.)*
- Q5: Will anyone recognize him? *(Peaks in Act III; answered: no.)*
- Q7: What stops the channel? *(Begins to live — the reader senses an ending is coming. Foreshadowing of classifier scores may begin here.)*

---

## At Act III close → Act IV open

**Newly known:**
- The community has tried to identify him and failed.
- His colleagues have shown him his own work without recognizing him.
- The classifier has been scoring his videos higher each week. (Mentioned parenthetically by the narrator in late Act III.)
- The camouflage has held.

**Expected:**
- The classifier is going to ban the channel. (The reader can feel this now.)
- The grief is going to be a major beat.

**Productive mysteries (live):**
- Q7: What stops the channel? *(About to be answered: the classifier.)*
- Q8: How does Hallvard respond to losing it? *(Live for Act IV.)*
- Q9: What does Hallvard do after? *(Begins to live — Act V's promise.)*

---

## At Act IV close → Act V open

**Newly known:**
- The channel is banned.
- The platform was, technically, correct.
- Hallvard has grieved.
- He has felt the first glimmer of release.
- He has opened a new account: *Hendane: Avkledd*.

**Expected:**
- The reveal channel will show the apparatus.
- The story will end soon.
- The ending will not be a triumph.

**Productive mysteries (live):**
- Q9: What does Hallvard do after? *(About to be answered.)*
- Q10: What is the ending shape? *(The poetics has promised lateral motion, not arrival.)*

---

## At Act V close (novella's end)

**Newly known:**
- The first *Avkledd* video is being uploaded.
- It shows the tools, the prompts, the front yard, the mold recipe, Hallvard's face briefly.
- The upload bar is at 67%.
- The rain is at the window.
- *Greit nok.*

**Still unknown (deliberately):**
- What NERV researches.
- Whether *Avkledd* will succeed or fail.
- What Hallvard does on Monday.
- Whether the boredom returns or recedes.

**The reader closes the book on:**
- An action in motion.
- A man who has not arrived.
- A city that is still raining.
- A *greit nok* held in the air.

---

## Through-running mysteries (live across all 5 acts)

These never close, or close only by implication:

- **M-A: What does NERV research?**
  Never answered. The narrator may eventually wink at the reader about this. The opacity is the point.

- **M-B: Why does anyone do anything?**
  The novella's quiet underlying question. Hallvard's boredom and the channel's escalation both pose it. The story does not answer; it shows lateral motion in the face of the question.

- **M-C: Is this story sad or funny?**
  Both, threaded. The reader is invited to hold both at once. The novella's tonal honesty depends on never resolving this.

- **M-D: What is the relationship between craft and AI?**
  Held open. The work was real labor. The work was also predominantly AI-generated. Both are true. The classifier was right; the work was still real. The reader is invited to sit with the contradiction.

- **M-E: Is Bergen the protagonist?**
  Sub-thematic. The city has its own through-line: 168 days of rain at story-start, 242 by story-end. The city does not change; Bergen continues to be Bergen. This non-change is itself a structural answer.

## Foreshadowing budget (for the Compositional agent)

The narrator may foreshadow, in the Vonnegut register, exactly four things:

1. **That the channel will end.** (One direct address, anywhere in Act I or II: *"It is going to end. You know this; it is the shape of stories about platforms. We are telling you so you can pay attention to how, not whether."*)
2. **That the colleagues will not recognize him.** (Once, in Act II, as a wink: *"You will want to know whether his colleagues recognized him. They did not. Not yet. Not ever, actually, though they came close.")
3. **That NERV's research will not be specified.** (Once, in Act I or II: *"You will have noticed by now that we have not told you what NERV studies. We are not going to.")
4. **That the ending is in motion, not arrival.** (Once, late in Act IV or early Act V: *"This is not the kind of story that ends. It is the kind of story that stops.")

These four direct addresses constitute the Vonnegut quota. The Compositional agent should use them precisely and not exceed them.

---

## Reader-emotion target curve

- Act I: Mild amusement, settling-in. Recognition of a competent, bored man.
- Act II: Rising delight. The pleasure of watching a craft come into being.
- Act III: Comic vertigo. The reader laughs with Hallvard's near-misses and feels the camouflage's strange tenderness.
- Act IV: A clean, surprising grief. The reader did not expect to be moved by a banned TikTok-shaped channel, and is.
- Act V: A held breath. The reader puts the book down with a slight tilt of the head. Not catharsis. Not deflation. *Greit nok.*
