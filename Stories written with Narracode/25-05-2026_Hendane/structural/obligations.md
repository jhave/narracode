# OBLIGATIONS — What the reader has been promised

Dramatic debts the text incurs and must settle. Each obligation is paired with its payoff site (act) and a refusal-tracking note for what the obligation is *not* allowed to become.

## O1. The identity mystery
**Promise (Act I–II):** A faceless, voiceless channel goes viral in a small enough city that someone *should* be able to identify the creator. The reader is implicitly invited to wonder: will he be caught?

**Payoff (Act V):** He is never caught. He reveals himself. The mystery is resolved by his choice, not by the community's competence.

**Refusal-tracking:** This obligation must NOT be paid off by exposure. No journalist, colleague, or sleuth correctly identifies him on the merits. The community is loving but unsuccessful. If anyone gets close, they back off.

## O2. The platform ban
**Promise (Act I, sometimes Act II):** The narrator may foreshadow, directly, that the channel will end. The reader knows it is coming. The dramatic charge is in *how* and in *what it does to Hallvard*.

**Payoff (Act IV):** The classifier flags the channel as "predominantly AI-generated content." The appeal fails. The ban is procedural, not personal. Hallvard recognizes that the platform is correct. He grieves anyway.

**Refusal-tracking:** The ban must NOT come from a human snitch, a community pile-on, or a moral panic. It is a regression model behaving as designed. There is no villain.

## O3. The reveal channel
**Promise (Act IV close, perhaps a glimmer in Act II):** The mention of *Hendane: Avkledd* arrives as a possibility once Hallvard begins to grieve. The reader is set up to expect either dissolution or a second act of agency.

**Payoff (Act V):** He starts the reveal channel. The first video shows the apparatus: the tools, the recipe, the front yard, his face in passing. The story ends mid-upload.

**Refusal-tracking:** The reveal must NOT be cathartic in a heroic sense. It is administrative. It is *greit nok* [fine, in the Bergen way]. It is not a triumph; it is a step sideways. The novella ends with an upload progress bar, not a swelling chord.

## O4. The competence-camouflage contract
**Promise (Act I):** The reader is shown that Hallvard is so reliably beige that no one could imagine him as the channel's creator.

**Payoff (Act III):** His colleagues show him his own videos. He performs surprise and mild interest. The camouflage holds. The reader experiences the vertigo with him.

**Refusal-tracking:** The camouflage must NOT crack at NERV. No coworker has a flash of recognition. No body-language tell is read correctly. The camouflage is not Hallvard's clever performance — it is the world's failure to imagine. This is the comedy.

## O5. The boredom-as-condition diagnosis
**Promise (Act I, opening):** Hallvard's boredom is named, technically, and given philosophical weight (Svendsen). The reader is promised that this boredom is the engine of the story.

**Payoff (Acts II–V):** The channel does not cure the boredom. The ban does not cure it. The reveal does not cure it. The boredom is a permanent condition. What changes is Hallvard's relationship to it.

**Refusal-tracking:** Boredom must NOT be cured by love, by fame, by self-actualization, or by a renewed commitment to NERV. The boredom remains. The story is about lateral motion within it.

## O6. The dialect-translation contract
**Promise (Act I, first dialect phrase):** Bergensk appears in italics with a bracketed English vibe-gloss. The reader is taught the convention.

**Payoff (every act):** The convention is honored. Dialect phrases continue to appear with brackets on first use per act; later uses in the act may stand alone. The reader learns Bergensk through use.

**Refusal-tracking:** The text must NOT slip into actual Norwegian sentences. The text must NOT abandon the bracketed gloss. The text must NOT lecture about dialect or Bokmål/Nynorsk politics.

## O7. The opacity of NERV's research
**Promise (Act I):** Hallvard works at a research institute. The reader expects, by convention, to eventually learn what is researched.

**Payoff (Acts II–V):** They never learn. The narrator may eventually acknowledge this directly, with a Vonnegutian wink: *"You will have noticed by now that we have not told you what NERV studies. We are not going to."*

**Refusal-tracking:** The research must NOT be revealed, must NOT be hinted at with technical specificity, must NOT become a metaphor for anything. The opacity is the point.

## O8. The Bergen mood-contract
**Promise (Act I):** The rain, the supermarkets, the dialect, the local pride. The reader is invited into a specific city.

**Payoff (every act):** Bergen is present in every act. Bryggen, Bybanen, Fløibanen, Fisketorget, Åsane Senter, Grieghallen, Festplassen, Sandviken — these places appear. The rain is constant.

**Refusal-tracking:** Bergen must NOT become a tourist brochure. The rain is real, the supermarkets are fluorescent, the fjord is mostly not visible from Hallvard's yard.

## O9. The "ingen dagar utan regn / ingen dagar utan tips" rhyme
**Promise (Act I or II):** The tagline of the channel echoes the city's structural truth. The reader notices.

**Payoff (Act V):** In the final pages, both phrases appear in the same scene — the *Avkledd* upload, the rain on the window. The structural rhyme closes.

**Refusal-tracking:** The rhyme must NOT be over-pointed. It is offered, not enforced.

## O10. The ending in mid-motion
**Promise (poetics, Act IV close):** This is a lateral-motion story. It will not end on a beat of arrival.

**Payoff (final paragraph):** The novella ends on an upload progress bar at 67%, or some similar in-motion image. The story ends with action incomplete. The reader closes the book mid-gesture.

**Refusal-tracking:** The final paragraph must NOT contain a summarizing reflection, a moral, or a future-tense projection. It is one moment, in motion, then white space.
