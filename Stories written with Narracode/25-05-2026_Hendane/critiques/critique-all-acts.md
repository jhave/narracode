# Critique: Hendane (All Five Acts)

## What works

The opening paragraph of Act I commits to its premise instantly. "It is raining in Bergen" followed by the refusal to make it weather (Act I, para 1–2) does exactly what POETICS §6 promised: rain as gravity, not mood-mirror. The sentence "Hallvard Nygaard has not mentioned the rain since roughly 2006, when he stopped. He was twenty. He understood." (Act I, para 2) is the strongest single line in the novella — Bergensk in cadence, Vonnegut in cut, and it does interiority and biography in three short statements.

The grout origin is well-handled. Act I para 31 lists the failed cleaners (Jif, bleach, Obs! spray) before arriving at Zalo. This is a list-as-paragraph (POETICS §4) that earns its rhythm because the items are specific and the failures are specific. The "soft sponge... small circles... five minutes... warmth of a bath that had just run" sequence becomes a technical recipe that pays off in Act V para 81 ("one part Zalo to four parts warm water...") — the recipe is faithfully reused, which honors M3 (grout) and M11 (Zalo).

The spreadsheet escalation in Act II is the cleanest comic engine in the book. Sheet 1, then 2, then 2b (community theories: incorrect), then 5 (production costs), then 7 (Legal/IP) — the proliferation tracks Hallvard's interior precisely without naming it. This is character-by-list, and it is the best Vonnegut-Bergensk blend in the manuscript.

The lift scene with Solveig (Act III para 19–31) lands the O4 (competence-camouflage) obligation with the right tempo. "His voice was entirely normal. He had no idea how his voice was entirely normal" is the line that converts the asymmetry into comedy without overplaying.

The Act IV grief catalog (paras 102–107) is structurally clean. Three grievances named in sequence — rhythm, craft-trajectory, Bergen's loss — matching the four-part interior breakdown in character-interiority.md almost exactly. The "the platform was right" arrival at 01:30 (Act IV para 113–115) earns its weight because the grief was specified first.

The Act V opening (Wednesday at NERV, lines 5–11) handles the post-ban return-to-baseline correctly. Mona, the cart, the line items, "Greit nok." It refuses the temptation to make Hallvard visibly altered.

## Where it defaults to genre habit or LLM tells

Several places the prose reaches for an explanatory clause when the image had already done the work.

- Act I para 7: "His title means:" — this gloss is fine. But it leads to "(It is Ingvild Rasmussen, for a project meeting that will run 22 minutes over. Hallvard blocks the extra 22 minutes preemptively. He has worked here since 2014.)" That parenthetical is good. The problem is the pattern: throughout, the narrator over-explains characterization by tagging years and competence claims. The "He has worked here since 2014" comes back too often (Act I para 7, Act II para 49 implied, Act IV).

- Act II para 39 contains the explicit instruction: "This is load-bearing; the Compositional impulse here wants to summarize, but the tools were *labour*, and the labour was part of the work, and the work was real." This is the narrator addressing the production process by name. It is an LLM tell — the writing room leaking into the page. The sentence is also a thesis statement of a kind the poetics explicitly refuses ("structural irony permitted, sentimentality refused"). It tells the reader what the labour means. The remedy is deletion; the tool inventory that follows already does the work.

- Act III para 5: "Bergen is a city that notices. It is a city of 290,000 people who have all, at some point, run into each other at Fisketorget or on the Fløibanen or at the Kiwi in Åsane Senter, and who have, as a result, developed very good instincts for recognizing familiar things." This is tourist-brochure prose dressed in vernacular. The list of landmarks is the kind of name-check the poetics flags as failure (§3, "No tourism-brochure Bergen"). The sentence reads as if a Bergen explainer had been pasted in.

- Act III para 9, the food anthropologist quote: "a Bergen household object elevated to cultural artifact by the specificity of its context — this is regional pride as conceptual art." This is an LLM tell of a different kind — the inserted "expert" who speaks in a register no human academic uses extemporaneously to a journalist. Most damagingly, Hallvard immediately *files* the phrase as one he won't share, which makes the narrator's writing of the phrase double-suspect: it is too good for the anthropologist and too neat for the file.

- Act III para 73: "Episode 2: 'Who is *Hendane*?' — a detective-style episode in which Bjarte and Helena went through the community theories in sequence and assessed them. They were kind about all the theories. They concluded that the creator was 'someone who loves Bergen in the specific way a person loves something they've never left — which is to say, with the fluency of a person who has absorbed it and now cannot quite distinguish themselves from it.'" The podcast quotation reads as a thesis-statement-by-proxy. The next sentence has Hallvard internalize it directly: "He had absorbed Bergen and could not distinguish himself from it." The text is doing its own footnotes.

- Act IV's grief catalog (paras 102–107), while structurally clean, lapses into a stagy three-beat rhetorical pattern: "He grieved the rhythm. This was the first thing." / "He grieved the craft-trajectory. This was the second thing, and it was, in some ways, the worst." / "He grieved Bergen's loss. This was the third thing, and the one that surprised him most." The "this was the X thing" scaffolding is visible. Vonnegut would not write three "this was the Nth thing" beats in close sequence; he'd refrain something shorter. This is the prose admitting its plan.

- Act IV para 119: "He had not known until this moment that he was capable of grieving something he had decided, on reflection, was fair. // You can know a thing is fair and still be hurt by it. This is one of the small structural truths of being alive. Hallvard sat on his kitchen floor and learned it at 01:30 on a Wednesday morning, which is, when you think about it, exactly when you would expect Bergen to teach you something." This is the most exposed wisdom-graft in the manuscript. The aphorism ("You can know a thing is fair...") was prescribed by the interiority doc — but it's quoted verbatim from the planning document, and "which is, when you think about it, exactly when you would expect Bergen to teach you something" is the kind of summative sentence the poetics refuses. It steps from third-limited into a Hallmark-Vonnegut hybrid.

- Across the book, "Greit nok" carries too much load by Act V. It closes Act I twice, Act II twice, Act III three times, Act IV once explicitly and once internally, and the novella ends on it. M7 says it may be the final word "but MAY NOT be over-explained; the brackets do the work once. Refusal: do not let *greit nok* slip into being a punchline." It is sliding toward punchline through frequency. (Counted: appears 14+ times across the five acts as a paragraph or sentence terminator.)

## Where the Bergen dialect convention holds or breaks

The convention (POETICS §5: italics + bracketed vibe-gloss on first use per act) is mostly honored. Examples that work:

- *greit nok* first use, Act I para 6: "[fine, whatever, good enough, the sound a Bergen person makes when they are declining to engage further but are not hostile about it]." This is a vibe-gloss, not a definition. Good.
- *dobbeltsynet* Act III para 49: "[the double-sight; the Bergen-specific experience of seeing two incompatible things occupy the same moment, which is different from cognitive dissonance in that it has a slight musical quality, like two chords played at once]." This is the strongest single gloss in the manuscript — it does interpretive work, not lexical work.
- *avkledd* Act IV para 131 gloss is excellent: "[Unmasked, stripped, revealed — in Bergensk it carries the lightness of something taken off rather than torn away; it is not exposure, it is disclosure; it is what you do with a coat when you come inside]."

The convention breaks in three places:

- Act II para 7: "*Mugg på fugene? Prøv dette.* [Mold on the grout? Try this — but in the Bergen way, which suggests you should already know this, and if you don't, well, now you do, and we are done.]" — This is an actual Norwegian sentence on the page, despite POETICS §3's hard refusal: "No actual Norwegian sentences. English with bracketed dialect translation only." The text overlay context might be defensible (it's a screen graphic), but the rule is hard. Acts II and III repeat this with *Tips frå Åsane til verda*, *Stabburet-installasjon nr. 1. Det finst ingen engelsk omsetjing for dette*, *Fiskesuppe til Le Creuset. Ingenting går tapt*, and Act V's title *Hendane: Avkledd — Kva det var, og korleis vi laga det*. The poetics rule was absolute. This is a structural decision, not a stylistic slip — but it is a violation of a declared refusal.

- Act III para 12 of the BT email-comment: *Vi er interesserte i å skrive om *Hendane*. Kan vi ta kontakt?* — Bokmål sentence on the page, with bracketed gloss. Also a §3 violation.

- *Jeg gråter* (Act II para 67) appears in a comment string with bracketed gloss. Defensible as quoted social-media content, but again, the rule was hard.

The Aksel exchange in Act IV para 77 — Hallvard says *"Greit nok"* aloud and Aksel looks at him — is one of the few times the dialect is allowed to land *as dialect* in dialogue rather than as authorial sprinkle. This is good. There should be more of this.

## Vonnegut direct-address usage (quota: exactly 4)

POETICS / reader-state.md specified four direct addresses. Audit:

1. **"You will have noticed, already, that we have not told you what NERV studies. We are not going to."** — Act I para 4. (Foreshadow #3, NERV opacity.) **Lands.**

2. **"You will want to know whether his colleagues recognized him. They did not. Not yet. Not ever, actually, though they came close — but that is Act III's story, and we are still in Act II..."** — Act II para 49. (Foreshadow #2, colleagues won't recognize him.) **Lands**, but the "but that is Act III's story, and we are still in Act II" coda is a stage-direction tell. Vonnegut would not name acts. This is a metafictional slip that breaks the register he was channeling.

3. **"This is not the kind of story that ends. It is the kind of story that stops — but not yet. Not quite yet."** — Act IV final paragraph (para 141). (Foreshadow #4, ending in motion.) **Lands well**, placed at the right hinge.

4. **Missing: foreshadow #1, "It is going to end."** The reader-state doc allocated a direct address for "the channel will end" in Act I or II. There is no such direct address. The narrator never tells the reader the channel is doomed in advance. The reader develops the intuition from genre and from late Act III's classifier scores, but the explicit Vonnegut promise never arrives.

There is a borderline fifth: Act IV para 121, "You can know a thing is fair and still be hurt by it. This is one of the small structural truths of being alive." This is in the *aphoristic* Vonnegut register but is not a "dear reader" address. By the loose definition, it doesn't count against the quota. By the strict definition (the narrator turning to camera), it doesn't either — it's a generalization Hallvard discovers, narrated in free indirect.

**Count: 3 of 4 used. Foreshadow #1 (channel will end) is missing.** This is a structural omission, not a stylistic one. Without it, the reader's experience of Act III's classifier creep is suspense-genre, not dramatic-irony-of-the-Vonnegut-kind. The promised tonal mode for handling the obligation O2 (the platform ban) was: tell the reader early, then let them watch the how. The reader is not told.

## Motif tracking: what lands, what is over- or under-used

**M1 Hands.** Background in Act I (correct, per the co-occurrence table). Center in II–IV. Named in V ("*Hendane*," he said. "*The hands.*"). Lands. The Act V image of "his actual hands. No FjordMask, no Nordic Fisher, no composite, no composite-of-a-composite — just his hands, long-fingered, slightly chapped, the fingernails trimmed short from a habit that had outlasted the channel that formed it" (para 121) is precisely what was promised.

**M2 Rain.** Present in every act. Mostly held as condition, not weather. One slip in Act IV para 134: "He lay in the dark and listened to the rain on the Åsane window. *Ingen dagar utan regn* [No days without rain — Bergen's structural truth, not a lament, not a boast, just the condition under which everything else happens]." This is the rule restated explicitly — it explains the motif rather than enacting it. The motif works best in passing, as in Act II para 95: "His reflection was in the glass" with rain implicit. The narrator over-glossing in IV is the LLM tell of a writer who knows the rule.

**M3 Grout.** Present in Act I (origin), Act II (founding video subject), absent Act III (correct), absent Act IV (correct), returns in Act V para 75–77 ("21 weeks since the cleaning. Still mostly clean... the beginning of mold."). The 21-week aging is exactly what motifs.md prescribed. Lands.

**M4 Folklore tableaux.** Norse longhouse (Act I para 91, Act II para 5), Norse-hall-evening-cozy-imperfect (Act II), Black-Death-Bryggen (Act II para 59 and Act III para 87), Iron-Age-Longhouse-Domestic (Act IV para 105, unused). The "longhouse with the wrong number of beams" line is good and gets reused (Act I para 91 → Act IV para 53). One concern: the folklore tableaux are *named* with NordicCanvas preset names (NordicCanvas's *historical-Bergen* category, the preset *Black-Death-Bryggen*) — this is good Doctorow-grade specificity but starts to read as if the AI tool's UX is doing the imaginative work. By Act V para 63, the demonstration shows the prompt being typed (good); but earlier acts lean on preset names rather than letting Hallvard's *selection* feel like authorship.

**M5 Kaviar tube.** Appears Act II (installation video), Act III (BT feature image is implied but not literalized), Act V (yes — para 36, he buys one at Kiwi; but it does *not* appear in the *Avkledd* video itself, which motifs.md M5 specified: "in the *Avkledd* reveal as the foreground of a shot"). Hallvard buys it on Saturday in Act V. He never uses it in the reveal video. The motif's third appearance is muted — it sits in the shopping bag. This is a missed obligation.

**M6 Fluorescent supermarket light.** Present in Act I, II, III implied (the rain-gutter prep mentions Obs! strip lights), Act V. The "Kiwi-light" and the specific flickering strip near refrigeration appear. Lands.

**M7 *Greit nok*.** Overused (see prior section). The refusal — "do not let *greit nok* slip into being a punchline" — is being violated by frequency. By Act V it has become a refrain rather than a structural mood-word. The closing line being *greit nok* is correct (per the poetics' suggestion), but the cumulative weight has dulled the final use.

**M8 Numbers.** Lands. The accumulating numerals (612 → 8,400 → 6.1M → 8.3M → 480,000 followers; rain days 168 → 169 → 240+) accrue typographic weight as motifs.md prescribed.

**M9 Progress bar.** Introduced Act V para 103. 0%, 23%, 67%. Final image. Lands.

**M10 Bybanen.** Present in I, II, III, IV opening, but **not in Act V**. Act V's commute is on foot from Kiwi (74 steps), with one Wednesday Bybanen ride (paras 16–22) that does serve the motif. So the count is met. But the motif's promise — "Hallvard's reflection in the window... in a way he does not entirely recognize" — is paid off in Act II para 95 and Act IV opening. By Act V, he stops counting stops, which is the structural payoff for the motif. Lands quietly.

**M11 Zalo.** Named and renamed across all acts. The *Avkledd* reveal shows it (Act V para 79) "half-empty, slightly sticky at the shoulders" — exactly the line motifs.md specified. Lands.

**M12 Berit, the plant.** Act I para 27. Act II para 105 (told one sentence). Act III — **absent**. Motifs.md prescribed three appearances: Act I, Act III, Act V. Berit is absent from Act III entirely. She returns in Act IV para 92 and Act V para 53, 61, 91. The motif's middle appearance has migrated from Act III to Act IV. Not catastrophic, but a deviation.

**M13 *Ingen dagar utan tips*.** Act II para 75 (named). Act III — present in BT-adjacent discussion (the *Vestlendingen.no* quote about the tagline at Act II para 79 is the only direct quotation; Act III does not quote it back). Act IV para 135 ("The tagline did not come. He waited for it, in the way you wait for something you know you're not going to hear, and it did not come.") — this is the **strongest single motif moment in the manuscript**. Then Act V para 99: he uses it as the description field for *Avkledd*. **Wait** — motifs.md said the rule for Act V was: "absent — its absence is its use." The text uses it. The motif's strongest gesture (absence) is undone by its reappearance in Act V. This is a structural choice that undercuts what the planning declared. See "Obligations check" for the trade-off.

## Obligations check: what was promised and whether it was paid

**O1 The identity mystery — never caught, reveals himself.** Paid. The community sleuths (the Laksevåg map-maker; *vestlandsbygd*/Aksel; the BT journalist Linn Hauge) all fail. He reveals himself voluntarily in Act V. Refusal-tracking held: no one identifies him on the merits.

**O2 The platform ban — procedural, not personal.** Paid cleanly in Act IV. The classifier scores are tracked, the appeal is formal, the denial is bureaucratic. "The platform was right." Refusal-tracking held: no human snitch, no moral panic.

**O3 The reveal channel — administrative, not heroic.** Paid in Act V. The video is 18:43 long, walks through the apparatus, ends mid-upload. **Mostly** holds the refusal that it must not be cathartic in a heroic sense. But Act V para 89 — *"Hendane,"* he said. *"The hands."* — has a small swell of self-recognition that edges toward catharsis. It's the line that most strains the "administrative not heroic" frame.

**O4 The competence-camouflage contract.** Paid. Solveig in the lift, Ingvild over Brunost. The camouflage holds because the world has not built the muscle to suspect him. Lands.

**O5 The boredom diagnosis.** Paid. Act V para 111 explicitly restates: "the boredom... was still there, still present, a low hum behind things like the sound of the strip light, persistent and unglamorous and his." This restatement is itself a small over-explanation — the motif could have done it without the diagnosis being named. But the obligation that boredom remains uncured is honored.

**O6 The dialect-translation contract.** Partially broken (see Bergen dialect section). The convention of italics + brackets is mostly held; the refusal of actual Norwegian sentences is not.

**O7 The opacity of NERV's research.** Paid. The Vonnegut wink arrives in Act I para 4. NERV is never specified. Refusal-tracking held.

**O8 The Bergen mood-contract.** Mostly paid. One slip toward tourist-brochure prose in Act III para 5 (the Fisketorget / Fløibanen / Kiwi name-list). Otherwise Bergen-as-condition.

**O9 The "ingen dagar utan regn / ingen dagar utan tips" rhyme.** **Half-paid.** The poetics promised: "In the final pages, both phrases appear in the same scene — the *Avkledd* upload, the rain on the window. The structural rhyme closes." This does happen — Act V para 99 has *Ingen dagar utan tips* in the description, and para 125 has *Ingen dagar utan regn* at the window. They are both in the closing scene. But motifs.md M13 said the opposite: "Act V (when it does not return — its absence is its final use)." The two planning documents are in tension; the text resolved toward obligations.md, not motifs.md. **This is a planning conflict, not a draft failure.** The human editor needs to decide which planning document wins. The current resolution (use both) is louder; the alternative (omit *ingen dagar utan tips*) is quieter and arguably more honest to the lateral-motion thesis.

**O10 The ending in mid-motion.** Paid. 67%, rain, *greit nok*. The refusal that the final paragraph contain no summarizing reflection is **violated by Act V para 111**, which contains a full paragraph of summarizing reflection about NERV continuing, the boredom continuing, "this was fine, and he was fine, and the boredom... was still there." This is exactly the moral/summary the refusal-tracking forbade ("must NOT contain a summarizing reflection, a moral, or a future-tense projection"). Para 111 contains future-tense projection ("would continue to happen," "would coordinate"). The final image is correct; the preceding paragraph is a violation of O10.

## Voice blend: per-act assessment

**Act I — Vonnegut-forward, Bergensk-undertow.** Lands. The opening seven paragraphs are the cleanest blend in the manuscript. Doctorow undertow appears too — the NordicCanvas subscription tied to the spring 2025 cover, 349 kr — and integrates well. The act feels load-balanced. One concern: the Svendsen citation (para 17) is doing a lot of thematic work. The reference is appropriate to the poetics but the paragraph nearly becomes a book-recommendation. Hallvard reading a passage three times and feeling "briefly, not alone" is the right gesture; "the modern, post-religious condition of a person whose competence outruns their inner stakes" is the right phrase from Svendsen for the story, but quoting it verbatim from POETICS §1 and again in Act V para 111 makes it a bookend, which the poetics did not promise.

**Act II — Miranda-July-forward, Doctorow-undertow.** Doctorow lands (NordicCanvas / FjordMask / GPU offloading / royalty-free fiddle from NorskLyd / 349 kr / 189 kr / Sheet 7 Legal/IP). Miranda-July is *underweight*. Where is the lurching domestic strangeness? The "three tubes in his fridge... since 2021 because buying one felt optimistic in a way that made him uneasy" (para 57) is the closest the act comes to a July-shaped sentence. The drawer organizer paragraph (para 27) lists items in a register that *could* lurch but doesn't — "a key to something," "a pen that worked. A pen that didn't" — these are dry-Bergen, not Miranda-July. The blend specified in §7 is off. Doctorow has eaten the act.

**Act III — Doctorow-forward, Vonnegut-undertow.** Doctorow lands (the 0.87/0.89/0.93 classifier scores, MediaScore's 12% disclosed error rate, Section 4.3.b read in October and again in January, the 4,112-member Facebook group, 11,400 mitten-pattern downloads). Vonnegut undertow appears in the *dobbeltsynet* gloss and the Laksevåg map-maker paragraph. But the act has a different problem: it leans on second-hand narration. The BT feature is summarized, the podcast is summarized, the Facebook group is summarized. The reader is told what Bergen does, rather than shown a scene of it. The lift with Solveig and the canteen with Ingvild are the only scenes that *happen*. The rest is reportage. This is faithful to the act's plan (community-sleuthing-as-data), but it loses Vonnegut's compression in favor of summary.

**Act IV — Vonnegut-forward, Miranda-July-undertow.** Vonnegut lands in the structural compression of the email, the appeal, the denial. Miranda-July undertow is supposed to handle the empty-kitchen evening (the body's tiny embarrassments, sentences that lurch). Instead, the empty-kitchen evening (paras 86–121) is *catalogic and explanatory* — three grievances numbered, then an arrival, then a stated truth. The Miranda-July register would have a tube of Stabburet on the floor, the wrong song, a thought about teeth. We get: he made tea, he opened the laptop, he closed the laptop. The lurch is absent. The act's emotional payoff arrives by structural argument, not by the body's strangeness. This is the act where the blend is most miscalibrated against POETICS §7.

**Act V — Miranda-July-forward, Bergensk-undertow.** Miranda-July is still underweight. The closest the act comes is Berit's "she had things to do" (para 91), which is correctly tiny and slightly absurd. Bergensk undertow holds in the dialect and the closing. Vonnegut creeps in too much (the closing "*Ingen dagar utan regn.* / *Greit nok.*" pair is Vonnegut-by-refrain, not Miranda-July-by-domestic-strangeness). The 18-minute *Avkledd* video is well-rendered as a sequence but reads as procedural — which is correct for the obligation (administrative reveal), incorrect for the prescribed voice (Miranda-July-forward).

**Summary:** Across the manuscript, Miranda-July is underweight everywhere she was prescribed. Doctorow is the correct weight where prescribed and slightly over-spills into Act II. Vonnegut over-spills into Acts II and IV beyond his quota of direct addresses. Bergensk-undertow is correctly present in all five acts but is doing more refrain-work than texture-work; it sounds at the ends of paragraphs, not in the middles of sentences.

## The ending (Act V): does the 67% progress bar earn its weight?

Yes — provisionally. The progress bar lands as image. 0% → 23% → 67%, with kitchen objects and Bergen rain interleaved, is exactly the lateral-motion close the poetics promised. The refusal of 100% is honored.

The weight is earned by:

- The 21-week-old grout returning (Act V para 75–77). The novella's origin object has aged on the page.
- The actual hands replacing the FjordMask hands (para 121). The motif inverts.
- The new empty spreadsheet (para 115). Hallvard's lateral motion is shown, not said, through one piece of new infrastructure.

The weight is *not quite* earned by:

- The "He was fine, and the boredom was still there" summary paragraph (para 111) intrudes between the upload bar transitions. The narrator pauses the action to state the thesis. This is O10's violation. The progress bar would land harder without it.
- The *Avkledd* reveal video being walked through in detail (paras 60–91) is structurally correct but slightly long. It is the longest sustained sequence in Act V and it is the most explanatory. The reveal is administrative; the prose makes it close to procedural, which is one register over. The act could lose 200 words from this sequence without losing the reveal.
- The "Hendane,” he said. "The hands." (para 89) is the strongest single moment in the act but is also the only moment that approaches catharsis. It works because it is small. If it were a beat longer it would not.

The mid-motion close (67%) is correct. The paragraph before it is wrong.

## Recommendations (what the human editor should attend to)

**Structurally fixable by revision:**

1. **Cut Act V paragraph 111.** The summarizing reflection between 23% and 67% violates O10's refusal-tracking ("must NOT contain a summarizing reflection, a moral, or a future-tense projection"). The image of the upload bar progressing is enough. Remove the para; juxtapose the two percentages with what is in the room.

2. **Add the missing direct address.** Foreshadow #1 ("It is going to end. You know this; it is the shape of stories about platforms.") was prescribed for Act I or II and is absent. Place it in Act I after para 17 or in early Act II before the first follower count. This is a structural omission, not a stylistic preference.

3. **Cut "This is load-bearing; the Compositional impulse here wants to summarize"** (Act II para 39). The narrator naming the writing-process is the cleanest LLM tell in the manuscript. The paragraph that follows works without it.

4. **Reconcile the *Ingen dagar utan tips* conflict.** Two planning documents disagree about whether the phrase appears or is absent in Act V. The text uses it. The human editor should decide which planning document wins; the absent version is structurally stronger (motifs.md is right; obligations.md should be revised).

5. **Restore Berit to Act III.** One sentence somewhere — Berit in the corner of the counter, surviving — would honor motifs.md M12's three-appearance rule. Currently she is absent from Act III.

6. **Use the kaviar tube in the *Avkledd* video.** Motifs.md M5 prescribed three appearances including "in the *Avkledd* reveal as the foreground of a shot." Hallvard buys one (Act V para 36) and does not use it. One frame in the reveal would close M5 cleanly.

7. **Decide the dialect-sentences question.** POETICS §3 forbids actual Norwegian sentences. The text contains them (text overlays, social media quotes, BT email). Either the rule is reaffirmed (and these are converted to English-with-bracketed-Bergensk-phrases or removed entirely) or the rule is amended in the POETICS. The current state is silent rule-violation.

**Tonal/register problems requiring rethinking:**

8. **Miranda-July is underweight.** Acts II and IV and V were prescribed Miranda-July-forward or -undertow. The prose is Bergensk-Vonnegut-Doctorow throughout. To bring July in: let one object per act go strange. The Stabburet tube alone on a counter, the cookbook-tripod cookbooks (the ones he hasn't cooked from in two years), the cold tea, the under-cabinet light's hum at a frequency he had never registered. These cues exist in the draft but are not allowed to lurch. The body's tiny embarrassments are missing entirely — there is no scene where Hallvard's body does something he did not authorize.

9. **The *Vestlendingen.no* / podcast / BT summaries in Act III flatten the act into reportage.** The reader is told three external-narration loops about the channel rather than experiencing one or two of them as scenes. The food anthropologist's "regional pride as conceptual art" quote and the *Byluft* podcast's "absorbed Bergen" pronouncement are both too neat — they read as the narrator's thesis statements voiced by adjuncts. At least one of these should be cut or roughened.

10. **Frequency of *Greit nok* is approaching punchline territory.** Recount and prune. Motifs.md M7 explicitly warned against this. A version that uses it five or six times across the novella (instead of fourteen+) will let the final use carry the weight.

11. **The grief catalog in Act IV is structurally over-scaffolded** (three "this was the Nth thing" beats). Either reduce to one named grief and let the others be sensed, or change the scaffolding to something Bergensk that does not announce its own structure.

12. **The aphorism in Act IV para 121** ("You can know a thing is fair and still be hurt by it. This is one of the small structural truths of being alive.") is the planning-document line pasted verbatim. It is the most exposed wisdom-graft. Either delete the second sentence (let the first stand alone, less generalized), or attribute the realization to Hallvard's interior in a register that doesn't sound like an inspirational poster.
