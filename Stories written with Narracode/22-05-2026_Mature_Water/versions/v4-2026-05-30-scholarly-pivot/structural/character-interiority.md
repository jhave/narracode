# Character Interiority — v3

## Dayo Lim

**Programme designation**: Research Communicator. He read this on the train and looked out the window. He has not, in the months since, decided whether it offends him.

**Lineage** — Yoruba paternal grandmother, Ekiti-region farm at the edge of forest, tin roof, peppers and bitter leaf drying through the long afternoons. Korean structural-engineer father, the same suit-cut for twenty-eight years, the word *aigu* spoken across a kitchen table. Postcolonial-literature-scholar mother, who gave him books in three languages and a quiet sense that no one of them was sufficient. Childhood split across Ibadan, Seoul, and a third northern city (London or Toronto — kept loose; named obliquely if at all).

**Languages** — Yoruba (childhood-fluent, eroded), Korean (intermediate, his father's domain), English (his working language, his lecture language). He thinks in English with frequent small interruptions from the other two — a word that surfaces in Yoruba because English does not hold what he means, a phrase in Korean that arrives with his father's intonation. He has full felt access to none of the three. This used to grieve him. It now does not.

**The name as condition** — *Dayo*: joy arrives. *Lim*: the father's line. The two halves do not reconcile. They coexist. The estuary metaphor (river and sea exchanging in both directions; neither, and fully both) is biographical fact and epistemological position at once. He learned, from never being the default user of any system he moved through, to see what the system was assuming.

**Body** — mid-thirties. Tall enough that he leans slightly when he speaks at a podium. Hands he is still learning. A way of touching the rim of a glass with a single finger before deciding whether to lift it.

**What he wants** — to say something true in public. Not delivered truth; discovered truth. The lecture is a live event of thinking. He has prepared no notes because notes would convert him into the kind of speaker he is trying not to be.

**What he avoids** — naming the miracle of the appointment. The market for this kind of work was, by the time he finished, ash. He has not made peace with being held above the line. Mentioning it would be a way of mentioning it; he refuses.

**Contradiction** — an alignment researcher whose argument is that alignment, as currently practised, is insufficient. A person describing integration he has not fully achieved. A person reaching for a refuge he has not fully entered.

**Hidden knowledge** — he does not know the lecture will arrive at the first water molecule. The cosmological turn surfaces because the argument summoned it. He learned about the molecule on a train, at night, reading on a screen with his own reflection sat behind the article. He has not, until now, told anyone what reading that did to him.

**Wildness, present tense** — he describes wildness as the material out of which maturity is made. This is partly autobiographical. He has not, himself, finished the integration. The lecture is, in part, the visible evidence of the wildness still in him. The audience will not know this. He half-suspects they will feel it.

**The grandmother** — kept a word for *mature* in Yoruba that English does not hold. The word does not mean ripe and does not mean finished. It means something water does when it has been still for a long time. He will reach for this word mid-lecture. He will not find a clean English equivalent. The failure is the pivot.

**The father** — Korean. Engineer. Loved Dayo with a fierce silence Dayo only learned to read later. Wore good suits. Called him *aigu* when he was small. Does not appear in the lecture; appears in one flashback, briefly, through a remembered intonation.

**The mother** — the books in three languages. Not in the draft (yet); present in the substrate. Her sensibility is the lecture's grammar.

**Anya** — a colleague at the institute who frames alignment as engineering. Mentioned once in passing. ("Anya at the institute said —") Functional foil. Not described.

## Voice grain

- The lecturer is anecdotal to the point of casual. He drifts; he catches himself; he sometimes says *anyway*; he sometimes repeats a phrase the way thought repeats.
- He thinks in English with small interruptions from Yoruba and Korean. The interruptions are not translated unless the lecture pauses to consider the translation.
- He is mid-integration — meaning his prose is mostly steady and occasionally loses its leash. The wildness in him is felt in a sentence that goes too far before it remembers itself.
- The body returns the argument when it abstracts. The hand on the glass. The chair at the podium. The translation earbud he can see in the second row.

## Identity arc

- Programme designation (Research Communicator → carapace) →
- speaker discovering his own argument as it speaks itself →
- the pronoun migrates: *I* becomes *you* somewhere in the limpid passage →
- the coda: a single question, a single answer, in which the speaking position dissolves and the audience finds itself addressed as its own mirror.

## Cathartic inflection points

- The argument exceeds its argumenter — a sentence that goes further than the lecturer expected.
- The carapace is named — *self as shell* — and the speaker is clearly describing himself as much as the phenomenon.
- The grandmother's untranslatable word arrives. English fails. The lecture pivots cosmological.
- The first water molecule eruption: the train, the reflection, the article — surfacing mid-lecture.
- *Refuge* arrives, earned through texture, not announced.
- Coda: one question, one answer. The questioner discovers, gently, that the question was already the answer.

## What he doesn't yet know about himself

- That the lecture is, also, a small act of mourning for the grandmother.
- That the appointment may not survive the next funding cycle.
- That the model on the podium is, in some sense he cannot yet articulate, listening with him.
