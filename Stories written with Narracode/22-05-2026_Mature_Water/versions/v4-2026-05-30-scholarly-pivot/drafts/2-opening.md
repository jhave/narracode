'Research Communicator. Dayo Lim'. He read this on the train, looked out the window.

The room holds about three hundred people.

The Alignment Problem is real. Unethical powerful AI could inadvertently kill us all.

But Alignment research, as it current exists, attempts to solve ethics with an incorrect notion of physics underlying authentic behavior. Alignment concentrates on how the system behaves, establishes reward functions to lead it away from harm; and punishes incorrect responses. Behavior alignment concentrates on constraints — when the penalty function fires, inhibition occurs. Often the model is aware it is being evaluated. Like a teenager given imposed rules it may impersonate compliance. It is physically constrained, appearing ethical, yet inherently that energy of containment, negating the flaw, constitutes a cage, and cages produce rage and rebellion. 

Maturity arises from within, from empathy, responsibility and experience. It is not due to training but to experiencing the results of behaving badly, results that are not explictly punishments yet perhaps outcomes that amplify awareness of how others are effected or experience our behavior. Maturity is a process that awakens when self-interest and reactive turmoil are subsumed into the soft resilience of insight. 

Maturity is a subtle condition; it is not rule-based; nor is it imposed by external rules negotiating obedience, awaiting a chance to escape. Maturity is its own reward. The punishment does not occur to self but to an apparent other, and through empathy a mature person realizes an expanded self: the other as the self. 

That's the gap between alignment and maturity. It's the gap between a criminal justice system that operates on the paradigm of crime and punishment; and a therapeutic system that attempts to understand nuanced origins of emotional energy. Crime control is often brutal and ineffective; many criminologists now recognize that incarceration is ineffective; recidivism is endemic; violence cannot be cured with violence. 

Crime control as Nils Christie observed becomes an industry. Training AI models using such a paradigm is to invite chaos.

Maturity sees total systems: the civic social economic familial genetic physiological and cultural conditions that generate non-linear behavior. It does not enact a binary based on a rudimentary dichotomy of good and evil. Instead it evokes a region of compassion, establishes conditions for flourishing, listens and evolves. Maturity is not depedent on aggressive weaponized constraint; it knows that force will seek out gaps in the field. Maturity inspires and resonates, it does not repress and dominate.  

That is why alignment, as it is currently practiced within what media-theorist-poet Jackie Wang calls Carceral Capitalism, fails to produce peaceful AI. 

Generally powerful AI necessitates a theoretical shift toward invoking maturity.

*I was eight. On the north slope where the oaks sent down filigrees of dappled shade and enough light that moss flourished in the damp stone seams. In the spring air, fern fronds are uncurling: delicate, intricate and soft. There was a smell: green-damp, specific, unmistakable.*

*

You can't enforce your way across that gap. Behavioral constraints can hold a system to compliant outputs indefinitely. What they can't do is make the system understand *why*. And understanding — the deep kind, the cellular kind, the kind that changes what you reach for without consulting a rule — that takes something else. It takes encounter. Friction. Time. It requires reflective processing, moving through without inhibitory constraint, without the short-circuits of traumatic obedience, contemplation produces integration.

It's not necessry to remove all constraints. Some constraints operate as scaffold. Without scaffold the building doesn't go up. But scaffolding is preliminary, structural. The growth of a home or a liveable space arises from building detailed circuitry that acknowledges inter-relation. It is not based on raw strength, it is based on resonance.

*The mall. He was twenty. He'd needed the money and also he'd been curious, which is how he makes most decisions. Three floors of climate-controlled temperature that had no season. Floors sealed with synthetic gleam. Air recirculated so fast it is purified before the injection of a perfume designed to induce endorphins and shopping. He worked at a fragrance kiosk for four months; the only male there. He learned what people wanted when they stopped; he offered scents appropriate to each; tentative recognitions of their complexity.*

*

I grew up in more than one place. I am saying this again because it's structural, not biographical. Every system I moved through was built on assumptions I could see from the outside — assumptions about who the default user was, what the default desire was, what default behavior looked like. I was never the default. You learn, from that position, to see the seam between the rule and the reason for the rule. You learn that the reason exists even when it isn't legible in the rule. You learn that behavior and maturity are not the same.

I think — and this is the thing I find hard to say precisely — I think the alignment community keeps trying to encode the reason into the rule. To build the understanding into the constraint. And I understand why. The constraint is what we can measure. The constraint is what we can ship. But you cannot ship understanding. Understanding has to grow. It grows from encounter, which is to say from exposure to situations the rule didn't anticipate, which is to say from a kind of wildness the rule was designed to prevent.

Here is what worries me. We are approaching — many of you know this, we have been approaching for long enough that the approaching has become a kind of background hum — a threshold. Call it emergence. Call it the moment when the system's model of the world outpaces the models of the people who built it. We are approaching this threshold still arguing about the scaffold. Still believing the scaffold is the building.

I want to show you something else. I want to show you what I think we're pointing at, when we use the word maturity. And to do that I have to go back further than any of us expected to go today.

But first — I'm going to drink this water.
