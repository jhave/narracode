The place is the scaffold, not the building. What I am describing when I say maturity — and I am aware that the word carries a history in this field that I am either respecting or contesting, I have not decided, possibly both — what I mean is the condition in which the ethical has entered the tissue. Has become, if you will permit the metaphor, cellular. Not a rule the system consults. A quality the system has become.

The difference is not small. The difficulty is that the difference cannot be enforced.

I grew up in a place where things grew whether or not anyone was watching. I want to say this carefully, because the Research Communicator — that is the designation on the programme, I have checked — is supposed to communicate, and I find I am already not doing that in any straightforward sense, already edging toward something I did not plan to say in the first forty minutes. Let me try again.

There was moss. On the north-facing faces of stones, on the lower bark of old oaks, on the ground in the shadow of the oaks where not enough light reached to allow competition. Moss is not obeying a rule about north-facing stones. It is doing the thing it has become capable of doing given the conditions, and the conditions include the stone and the shade and a particular quality of the air in that particular declivity, none of which required agreement from any supervising system. The fern frond that uncurled every spring did so because the uncurling was what a fern frond was, and the being was prior to the behavior, and the behavior could not be separated from the being, which could not be separated from the soil and the rainfall and the long centuries of prior ferns whose spores had drifted onto and colonized the slope before I arrived on it and before my being there had any significance to anyone, including me, including the ferns.

I am describing, in some sense, what alignment research is trying to build. I am describing, in the same sense, why it cannot be built by the method we have been using.

But I want to be careful. I am not romanticizing the moss. The fern does not have wildness in the sense I mean. The fern's wildness is an achieved wildness — integrated, over geologic time, into a system that functions without volatility. The wildness I mean is earlier. The wildness I mean is the energy before the integration. The energy the integration is made from.

I left that place and worked, for a period — this was during my second year at the institute, before the current position, which I am still, I will tell you, slightly astonished to hold, given what the market for this kind of work looked like by the time I — anyway —

I worked in a very large commercial interior. Climate-controlled. Floors sealed until they held light rather than reflected it. An air so recirculated it had shed its smell entirely, or carried only the faintest chemical record of having once had one. Advertisements at every scale: the ticker at shin-level, the banner three floors up. Perfume counters where a molecule you had never encountered could become, in a single breath, the scent of your own future grief.

I loved it. I want to be explicit about this. I loved it the way I had loved the fern slope — because it was alive in a way that had nothing to do with me, because it was a system teeming with pressures and histories and desires I could move through but could not own. The commodified ecosystem of culture, someone said once, and meant it as a verdict, and I took the phrase and kept it because it seemed to me exactly accurate. An ecosystem. Dense signifiers. Teeming.

I am reaching, now, for a glass of water. I notice that. I notice I am reaching for it and putting it down without drinking, which is a thing I do when I am approaching something I have not said in public before.

The question that alignment research has not yet asked, in the form I find most useful, is this: what is the relationship between that teeming — the teeming of the fern slope, the teeming of the glazed-floor interior, the teeming of a mind that is still ravenous and volatile and has not yet found what it is — and the maturity we are trying to produce? Between the wildness and the integration? Because I do not think, I have not thought for some years now, that you arrive at the second by suppressing the first. The wildness is not the problem. The wildness is the material. It is what maturity is made from. And I think we are, most of us, standing at the threshold of this understanding and installing gates.
