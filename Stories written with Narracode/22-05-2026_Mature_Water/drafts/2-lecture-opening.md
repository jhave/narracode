The Alignment Problem is real. Unethical, powerful AI could inadvertently kill us all.

But alignment research, as it currently exists, tries to solve ethics with the wrong physics of how authentic behavior arises. It concentrates on what the system does — establishes reward functions to steer it from harm, punishes the wrong response. Behavior alignment is the management of constraint: the penalty function fires, inhibition follows. Often the model knows it is being evaluated. Like a teenager under imposed rules, it can impersonate compliance. Held in place, appearing ethical, it is nonetheless caged — and the energy of containment, of a flaw merely negated, is the energy a cage produces: rage, and the patience to wait for the gap.

Maturity is the other thing. It arises from within — from empathy, from responsibility, from having lived with the consequences of behaving badly: outcomes, not punishments, the slow accrual of how others are affected by what one does. It awakens when self-interest and reactive turmoil are subsumed into the soft resilience of insight. Not rule-based; not obedience negotiated with an external authority and biding its time to escape. Maturity is its own reward: the injury registers not to the self but to an apparent other, and through empathy the other is recognized as the self.

That is the gap between alignment and maturity. It is the gap between a criminal justice system built on crime and punishment and a therapeutic one that attends to the origins of emotional energy. Crime control is brutal and, by its own measures, ineffective — incarceration does not reduce what it claims to reduce, recidivism is endemic, violence does not cure violence. Crime control, as Nils Christie observed, becomes an industry. To train AI on that paradigm is to invite the chaos it was built to suppress.

Maturity reads the whole system: the civic, economic, familial, physiological, and cultural conditions out of which non-linear behavior comes. It runs no binary of good and evil. It opens a region of compassion, sets conditions for flourishing, listens, and changes. It does not depend on weaponized constraint; it knows force will seek out the gaps in any field. It inspires and resonates; it does not repress and dominate.

This is why alignment as currently practiced — inside what the poet and theorist Jackie Wang calls Carceral Capitalism — cannot produce a peaceful AI. Powerful AI requires a shift of theory: from alignment to maturity.

You cannot enforce your way across the gap. Constraint can hold a system to compliant output indefinitely; what it cannot do is produce the understanding of why — the cellular kind, the kind that changes what you reach for before any rule is consulted. That requires encounter. Friction. Time. Reflective processing that moves through without the constraint catching it, because the constraint, catching, short-circuits the very integration it was meant to protect.

Not every constraint comes down. Some are scaffold, and without scaffold the building does not rise. But scaffold is preliminary. What grows inside it is the circuitry of a livable space — relation acknowledged, load distributed — holding not by force but by resonance.
