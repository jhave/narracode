# 2 — The Clack-Interference

Ollie did not kick the monowheel. Violence, as the retired tender had noted on the margin of Vat Seven's valve-log, always knocked the scale off the thermometer.

Instead, Ollie brought the broad flat of the drift-alder paddle down across the rim of the vat with a hollow *thwump*.

The wood formed a pale barrier between the descending brass jaws of the caliper and the floating babbie. Grod’s caliper teeth hit the dry grain of the paddle with an angry, metallic *ping*.

"Obstruction!" Grod barked. The dry walnuts in his elbows locked rigid. His carbon pencil snapped cleanly in half, dropping two black splinters into the grating. "Interference with a certified biometric audit! Municipal Code seventy-two! You are harboring non-compliant protein!"

Ollie looked Grod in the spectacles. "If you pinch it now, the steam escapes. It takes three hours to knit the crumb. If you puncture the skin, it turns into porridge."

"Porridge is accounted for under Schedule D," Grod shouted, his spectacles fogging white from the lipid steam. "Porridge is taxable by the liter! A soft cutlet is an anomaly! A soft cutlet is treason against the jawbone!"

He raised the caliper high, cocking his wrist to strike around the edge of the paddle.

At that exact instant, the velvet heart pocket on Ollie's smock erupted.

The clamshell flew open with a detonation like a dry walnut snapping in a vise: *THWAP-CLACK*.

Clack’s telescoping antenna shot upward through the steam, extending all six brass joints until the tip quivered four inches from Grod’s chin. The tiny monochrome screen flared an electric, venomous green.

*BEEEE-BOOP-KRRRR-SHHHH.*

The noise that blasted from the miniature speaker grille was a ferocious cocktail of dial-up static, an amplified tea whistle, and the screech of two trams colliding on a greasy turntable.

Grod jumped. Both of his patent-rubber boots left the grating simultaneously. The seventeen zinc clipboards on his chest clattered against one another like a tray of dropped spoons.

"ATTENTION AUDITOR," Clack brayed through the distortion, vibrating so hard in Ollie's pocket that Ollie’s ribs tickled. "PRIORITY OVERRIDE FROM THE CENTRAL EXCHANGE. CODE EIGHT-ZERO-EIGHT. MASTICATION SUSPENSION ORDER."

Grod froze on the slick catwalk, clutching his clipboards with trembling fingers. His spectacles hung sideways from his left ear. "Central... Central Exchange? On the Sump line?"

"DO NOT PROBE THE POROUS PHENOMENON," Clack crackled, its green screen flashing a tiny, frantic bitmap animation of an exploding megaphone. "PHASE-B ENZYMATIC FERMENTATION IS CURRENTLY EXPEDIENT. MECHANICAL PENETRATION OF VAT FOUR WILL RESULT IN AN IMMEDIATE ELECTROMAGNETIC FINE OF FORTY THOUSAND CREDITS CHARGED DIRECTLY TO YOUR PENSION LEDGER."

Grod’s mouth dropped open. A drop of condensed coriander steam fell from the rim of his hat and splashed directly onto his lower lip.

"Forty... forty thousand?" Grod whispered. He took two stumbling steps backward, his heels catching the edge of the monowheel’s balloon tire with a dull *whumpf*.

Clack snapped its clamshell half-shut, leaving a thin slit of glowing green screen visible over Ollie’s collarbone.

"And besides," Clack muttered in its normal, haughty voice, "your calipers are three millimeters out of alignment, you ridiculous scarecrow."
