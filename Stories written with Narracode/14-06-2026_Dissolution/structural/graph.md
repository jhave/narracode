# GRAPH — Dissolution Disequilibrium

## Characters

### Monique Cormier (protagonist)
- 44, sociologist studying grief at the University of Oslo.
- Acadian-Canadian origin; her father is/was Acadian, dying in Halifax.
- Entering perimenopause; fluctuating hormones amplify emotional destabilization.
- Professional premise: emotional disruption is structurable and knowable. This premise is in active failure across the story.
- Partner to Sophia (long-distance).

### Sophia
- 40, high-end landscape architect and estate gardener.
- Works restoration projects along Peggy's Cove Road and the Chester peninsula outside Halifax, Nova Scotia.
- Emotionally grounded, tactile, from a different milieu than Monique.
- Present in the story only through digital mediation (WhatsApp video). Never sentimentalized.

### Monique's father (unnamed)
- 85, Acadian, terminal cancer. Comfort-care protocols.
- Ward 7A, Centennial Building, QEII Health Sciences Centre (Halifax Infirmary), South Park Street, Halifax.
- Dies after scene 0. Never given lines. Functions as the originating wound.

### The GP (unnamed, Algerian-born Oslo physician)
- Algerian-born, raised and educated in Oslo; fully integrated, erudite.
- Manages Monique's care in strict 15-minute weekly slots.
- Her face changing when the culture result returns is the only unguarded expression in the story.
- Logs the definitive clinical note clearing Monique.

### Sara (Swedish neighbor)
- 63, lives down the hall in the Frogner building.
- Eight months into a late-career gender transition; lives fully non-gender-conforming.
- Former professional welder. Mechanical literacy is unremarkable to her, revelatory to Monique.
- Fixes the washing-machine outflow with a plastic twist-tie. Practical competence as grace.

### Norwegian landlady (unnamed)
- Highly anxious. Communicates with Monique through text, across a language barrier.
- Disconnected the washing-machine outflow line, claiming a vital piece was missing (in fact misunderstood the mounting bracket).

## Entities

- **QEII Health Sciences Centre, Ward 7A** — Halifax palliative setting; site of the father's death.
- **University of Oslo sociology department** — Monique's professional home; site of the cleaner strike and the Saturday solo sanitization.
- **Frogner apartment building** — 19th-century, five co-owners, chestnut-lined avenue near Frogner Park. Temporary home turned adversarial site.
- **Aduro 9 wood stove** — Installed after the chimneys were repaired (all five co-owners finally funding the overhaul). Site of the black-glass winter failure; fixed in Act III.
- **14-year-old Electrolux front-loading washing machine** — Ruptured rubber door gasket; flood; outflow line.
- **MSIS — Norwegian National MRSA Registry (Meldingssystem for smittsomme sykdommer)** — Where Monique is documented as a public-health vector.

## Relations

- **Monique — Sophia:** long-distance partnership; WhatsApp video is the anchor and lifeline. Asymmetry of presence (one in soil, one in crisis).
- **Monique — father:** child / dying parent. Grief yet to be processed; metabolized as stress, then medical crisis, never as grief proper.
- **Monique — GP:** patient / practitioner. Trust operating within strict 15-minute slots. Institutional intimacy.
- **Monique — Sara:** neighbor. Practical competence flows one way (Sara → Monique). Grace without transaction.
- **Monique — landlady:** tenant / landlady. Mediated by text and language barrier; mutual anxiety, mutual misunderstanding.
- **Monique — apartment:** temporary home now adversarial — stove, washing machine, surfaces, all turned into sites of threat and obligation.
- **Monique — MSIS:** individual / state registry. She is now a documented vector; her body has a bureaucratic shadow.
