# CHARACTER-INTERIORITY — Dissolution Disequilibrium

Private architecture beneath the surface. This file is for the composing model's understanding; almost none of it should be stated on the page. The poetics forbid announced interiority — these notes exist so that action and perception carry the right weight.

---

## Monique Cormier (protagonist)
- **Private desire:** To be the expert who is not also the patient. To remain the one who surveys grief from outside its weather.
- **Shame:** That her body is publicly registered as a vector — that her interior has been entered into a state system as a hazard. The grief sociologist reduced to a line in MSIS.
- **Avoidance:** She has not processed her father's death. It is metabolized as physical stress, then as medical crisis — never confronted as grief proper. The abscess is the grief she will not look at.
- **Contradiction:** She chose to study grief because she believed understanding it would insulate her. That assumption is now in active, undeniable failure. The theory does not hold for the theorist.
- **Hidden knowledge:** She is frightened Sophia will find her unrecognizable — that the woman packing to come home is not the woman Sophia knows.
- **Changing self-understanding:** Movement from "I theorize crisis" to "I endure it." The expert learns the thing her discipline could not teach her: that some systems are survived, not solved.
- **Arc potential:** Not the resolution of grief, but the quiet return to ordinary life as the only available form of repair. There is no breakthrough. There is a folded sweater.
- **Cathartic inflection point:** NOT the WhatsApp call (that is the peak of crisis). It is the folded sweater — the moment she picks up a mundane object and the crisis recedes without announcement. Composition must protect this moment from premature relief and from explicit framing.

## Sophia
- Present only in digital mediation — the WhatsApp frame, the Nova Scotia wooden office, the lag.
- Her groundedness is her defining quality: tactile work, real soil, restoration projects along Peggy's Cove Road and the Chester peninsula.
- She is the story's emotional anchor and therefore must never be sentimentalized. Her face in the frame is enough. She does not deliver wisdom; she is simply, reliably, there.
- The asymmetry — one partner in soil, one in crisis — is the relationship's quiet truth. Do not resolve or comment on it.

## Sara (Swedish neighbor)
- Practical competence as a form of grace. She fixes the outflow with a twist-tie because she can see what the problem actually is.
- Her gender transition (eight months in, late-career, lives fully non-gender-conforming) is mentioned without commentary. It is context, not subject. No scene turns to examine it.
- Her former trade — professional welder — makes her mechanical literacy unremarkable to her and revelatory to Monique. The grace is in how little it costs Sara to give.

## GP (Algerian-born Oslo physician)
- Precision and compassion within institutional constraint. The 15-minute slot is an ethical commitment to throughput and equity, not a dismissal.
- Algerian-born, raised and educated in Oslo, fully integrated, erudite.
- Her face changing when the culture result arrives is the only moment of unguarded expression she permits herself in the story. Everything else is logged, bounded, professional.
- She is, in a sense, what Monique aspires to be: an expert who stays the expert. The contrast is structural and must not be stated.

## Interiority note
The reader must never be told what Monique feels about her father. The productive mystery of the story is exactly that withheld interior. Grief is metabolized — into abscess, into protocol, into packing — and never named. If composition reaches for a tender flashback to fill the gap, it has broken the story. The gap is the point.
