# READER-STATE — Dissolution Disequilibrium

A model of what the reader knows, expects, and may be productively surprised by. Updated as composition proceeds. State below is at INIT (before any scene is written).

## Current understanding (after init)
A grief sociologist, returning from her father's deathbed in Halifax, contracts MRSA in Oslo. The story will track her medical and psychological crisis and its resolution. The reader knows she is an expert in grief; the reader will quickly sense the irony that she is now the subject rather than the analyst.

## Expectations the reader brings
- Medical procedural detail — diagnoses, dosages, protocols, swabs.
- The partner (Sophia) as an emotional counterweight to the crisis.
- Institutional friction — the clinic, the registry, the landlady, the workplace.
- Some form of recovery by the end.

## Plausible defiance paths (where the story productively breaks expectation)
- **The recovery is not cathartic — it is mechanical.** No breakthrough, no insight that heals. A twist-tie, a wire pipe cleaner, an espresso cutoff. The reader expecting a climax of understanding gets a schedule instead.
- **Grief is never named as such, only metabolized.** The reader braced for a scene of mourning gets an abscess, a protocol, a load of laundry. The father never speaks; there is no deathbed valediction beyond Scene 0's silence.
- **The final scene is about a sweater, not about her father.** The reader expecting the story to "land" on grief finds it landing on packing. The closure is a gesture, not a statement.

## Productive mystery (deliberately withheld)
What has Monique actually felt about her father's death? The reader will not be told directly. The withheld interior is the engine of the story's restraint. The reader infers everything from the displacement — abscess, protocol, the folded sweater.

## Accidental confusion risk (to pre-empt on the page)
- **MSIS registry.** Non-Norwegian readers need a brief, grounded establishment of what it is and why being entered into it carries weight — the body documented as a public-health vector. One or two clauses of context, not a lecture.
- **The Aduro 9 brand name.** Needs one line of grounding so the reader understands it is a wood stove, and that the building's newly repaired chimneys make it the centerpiece of a restoration she failed to use.
- **EES (European Entry/Exit System).** The closing statistic needs just enough context that the reader grasps the 1-in-7 transit-cancellation risk and Monique's clean counter-reading of it.

## Reader-feeling target at close
Relief, arriving unannounced. If the last page makes the reader feel the crisis has dissolved without the narrator saying so — if the folded sweater carries it — the reader-state has been managed correctly.
