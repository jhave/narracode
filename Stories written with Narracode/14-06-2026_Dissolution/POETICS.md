# POETICS — Dissolution Disequilibrium

## Central tension
The ironist ironed. Dr. Monique Cormier has built a career on the theory that emotional disruption is structurable, knowable, taxonomizable — that grief is a system one can model from the outside. The story places her inside a systemic cascade (medical, hormonal, domestic, psychological) she cannot analytically escape. The expert becomes the patient. The observer becomes the data point. The story does not mock her for this; it follows the slow, undignified work of a mind that theorizes crisis learning instead to endure it.

## Register
Flat, precise, slightly clinical syntax. Medical terminology used unironically by a character who has internalized it — *Dicloxacillin*, *MRSA*, *Chlorhexidine gluconate*, *Suprachiasmatic Nucleus*, *colonized* — these are her native idiom, not exotic ornament. Resist the pull toward sentiment or interiority-exposition. The reader infers feeling from action and material detail, not from announced interiority. When Monique is most afraid, the prose should get more procedural, not more lyrical.

## Refused
- Metaphor that editorializes. (No "the abscess was like her grief." The abscess is the grief, displaced; the reader does the work.)
- Neat thematic closure that announces itself.
- Comic relief as a tonal release valve. Comedy here is dry, material, structural — never a wink.
- Emotional uplift announced as such. The reader must not be told the ending has arrived as comfort. It arrives as the mundane next action.
- Any scene of the father speaking. The father is a wound, not a character with lines.
- Sentimentality about the father's death. The grief is metabolized, not narrated.

## Allowed
- Diagnostic language saturated with unspoken dread.
- The specific brand name and dosage as texture: *Hibiscrub 4%*, *Mupirocin*, *Aduro 9*, *Electrolux*, *60°C*, *10:30 AM espresso cutoff*. Specificity is the feeling.
- Medical bureaucracy rendered faithfully: the MSIS registry, the strict 15-minute clinic slot, the clinical note logged at the end.
- The body as a site of professional embarrassment — the sociologist of grief reduced to a documented public-health vector.
- Material comedy that stays dry: the plastic twist-tie, the soot-blackened glass, the wire pipe cleaner from Jernia.

## Voice
Third person limited, close to Monique but not confessional. No internal monologue that explains itself. Action and perception carry weight. We are behind her eyes, not inside her commentary.

## Attentional dialect
Scene as accumulation of small procedural facts. Duration matters and is not summarized away: the five-day regime, the twice-daily scrub, the three-times-daily nasal ointment, the strict wash temperature. These repetitions are the texture of the experience — the reader should feel the grind of protocol, the way crisis becomes a schedule. Time is measured in courses of antibiotics and wash cycles, not in chapters.

## Structural shape
Eight scenes across three acts, each act performing a distinct psychological movement:
- **Act I — Destabilization** (scenes 0–1): the bedside, the diagnosis. The professional armor cracks. The wound enters the body.
- **Act II — Proliferating paranoia** (scenes 2–4): the regimes, the domestic friction, the nightmare. Control fragments into ritual; ritual fails to hold.
- **Act III — Banal dissolution of crisis** (scenes 5–7): the biochemistry of sleep, the small competencies of others, the suitcases. The third act earns its quiet by being genuinely small. No catharsis — just the next mundane thing.

## References
- **Janet Frame** — clinical precision, institutional language, the self observed as case.
- **Alice Munro** — material specificity, emotional restraint, the load-bearing ordinary object.
- **W.G. Sebald** — accumulation of bureaucratic and historical fact as emotional weather.
- **Marilynne Robinson** — long consequence, patience, the dignity of a settled sentence.
- **Karl Ove Knausgård** — the dignity of the tedious and procedural; the wash cycle as event.

## The test of the ending
The cathartic inflection point is NOT the WhatsApp breakdown (that is the peak of crisis). It is the folded sweater — the moment Monique picks up a mundane object and the crisis recedes without anyone, including the narrator, announcing that it has. If the last page makes the reader feel relief without being told to, the poetics held.
