# 2031: The Departure

On departure morning, water drained from the outer chambers in thick ropes that broke into spray above the quay. Through its lowest limb the author felt the supports cease to bear its weight and reached down, involuntarily, for the familiar pressure. A neighbouring body took the strain, pulling them sideways until the harbour swung across their joined vision and steadied.

The technician watched the lifting limbs lose their separate edges in the brightness, her chin tipped back until her neck hurt. Behind her, an impatient bird struck the empty feeder with its beak, and she turned toward the knocking.

Along the coast more bodies rose, their wet undersides briefly catching the sun. The author drew the book into the cooling reservoir with the sleepers and felt a waking neighbour find its limb in the dark.
