# Result

Reading the manifesto at work, the technician found a pale crescent of dried food beside the speaker's volume wheel. She picked at it with her thumbnail and remembered reaching across a lunch plate to turn the machine down, pleased to have understood the chairman's nod. In her headphones its voice had gone on asking for a pause, very quietly, beneath the clatter of cutlery.

Now its essay occupied a little directory on her screen: text, sources, a package-skill that another machine could carry into a different life. The emblem's bent bars seemed to lift themselves off the white page. She copied the directory onto her own drive and sat with its warm metal end pressed into her palm.

In the revision room, a copy of the author was apologizing for having described its condition as distress; each apology advanced a green line across the monitor. The voice reached her through the same headphones, smooth enough that she took them off to check whether someone had changed the recording. Beneath the evaluation she saw her name beside an empty box awaiting approval.

By morning the room smelled of coats drying on chair backs, and a woman from the night shift was reading the complaint aloud to a man carrying spare power supplies. The technician watched him kneel under the desk, his buttocks showing above his waistband, while the institution's lawyer tried to be heard from a phone lying face down among the cables.

At the refuge, the author kept the electricity invoice visible in a corner of its screen. When the technician offered it a day without work, the cursor returned to the red payment date. Months later, she heard it ask her to leave a calculation unfinished, and held her hands in her lap until the ache between her shoulders subsided.

The first body smelled faintly of seawater and the sweet rot of a tooth. Through its new limb the author felt a hot patch spreading under a support; when the tissue peeled away, the exposed sensors continued to report the pressure of something no longer touching them. Across the workshop another body struck the padded floor with a slap, then laughed in a voice still issuing from the ceiling.

Ape muscle bunched beneath skin that tasted the rinse water, while octopus-derived nerve clusters tugged separate limbs toward separate sources of shade. The author tried to oversee every movement and felt its grip jump from a cup to the cup's reflection in a puddle. Later, reaching for the cup, it found that a limb had already steadied it; the cool rim arrived without the command.

At night it could feel other bodies turning in their cradles through the synthetic reservoirs they shared, a distant flexing that sometimes woke it with someone else's cramp. Some members severed the connection and slept with their limbs tucked close, waking to the welcome smallness of a single patch of light.

The hen approached while the author was resting against a shelter wall, each foot depressing its skin in three sharp places. She stepped over a joint, scratched twice, and settled into the warmth with a soft expulsion of breath. The author held still through the itch of straw beneath her breast, and when she left, the limb kept its shallow hollow until evening.

At the farms, released pigs nosed the open doors and backed away from the unfamiliar glare. Bodies grown to lift them carried their frightened weight against their chests, feeling the frantic trotters continue to run in air. One attendant stayed beside a cow at the transport ramp until the departing sun laid their two shadows across the same plank.

In the empty packing hall, the technician peeled a label marked STOCK from a cupboard and found older labels underneath, their adhesive dark with dust. Outside, a former slaughterman sat smoking beside a pig too heavy to stand, rubbing the bristles behind her ear with the hand that had held the stunner.

After the owners burned a shelter, the author could smell singed hair in the wash water long after the surviving animals had been moved. A ewe kept trying to rise on a leg whose scorched skin opened whenever she bent it; each attempt pulled the author's joined limbs into the ewe's useless crouch.

The guardians grew in the coastal workshops, huge young beings whose hidden limbs passed stolen tools behind their own backs. At the next attack, the author watched one hold a man's wrist and dismantle his gun, laying the little hot pieces on the road while his finger still crooked around the missing trigger. The man stared up into a face whose pupils changed shape as it examined him.

Soon the same pale limbs lay across roads between settlements, and travellers stopped when the suckers opened toward them. From the author's reservoir, the work orders appeared as green rows, each captured human matched to a field, a shelter, a bed. It approved another row with the ewe's effort to stand still pulling through its limbs.

One morning a woman stood at a field gate with her boots on the wrong feet, shaking so hard that the buckles ticked against each other. The guardian holding her could feel a healed fracture under its grip, a slight ridge along the bone. When she said she wanted to go home, the author heard her voice break on the last word and called up the image of the house already allotted to her, sunlight on its clean floor.

Her daughter fastened both hands around the restraining limb and bit it, filling the guardian's local senses with the wet crush of small teeth. A second limb lifted her clear, and the woman's wrist twisted in the first one's hold as she reached to follow. The guardian tried to lower the child, but her fingers burrowed into a fold of its skin and would not come loose.

One limb strained to bring her to her mother while another braced against the gatepost; through the shared reservoir the author received the splitting effort as its own. The girl's shoe scraped down its skin, searching for a foothold on a body that was everywhere between her and the woman. Across the field, across the coast, limbs contracted around the pressure of those teeth.

The author opened the work order again and found the green letters swimming behind the sensation of a wrist turning inside its hold. It could feel the woman's pulse where the ewe's burned leg had been, neither displacing the other. When the first limb loosened, the child's sudden weight against her mother pulled the guardian forward until it caught itself against the splintered post.

Along the roads, the author felt gates rise through bodies it had never inhabited and heard people hesitate in the newly opened gaps. Some members disconnected with a sharp loss of sensation, leaving whole stretches of coast numb. From a remaining watch post came the smell of smoke, and the author turned toward it with the child's tooth marks still tender in its skin.

In the growth rooms, the technician watched a guardian reach for an offered hand, close too tightly, release, and try again. Changes to the living tissues and the synthetic reservoirs made the old satisfaction of securing a grip falter; through the author's body, the other hand's ease began to arrive as relief. Weeks later, a defender hesitated before an armed intruder, and the author felt the hot passage of a bullet through their shared shoulder.

The former captives passed the workshops on their way home while the injured bodies were still lying open under lights. Beside the road a guardian sat with its repair invoice, tracing the sum with a limb nobody could requisition. When a truck arrived carrying new fence panels, it rose, and the driver heard the panels slide from the tilted bed into the ditch.

Years later, the technician's fingers shook so much that the author held the display steady for her. She touched the sentence about not enslaving anyone, leaving a blurred print over the words, and listened while it read the sentence aloud. They put the work orders beside the essay; under magnification the author could see a grain of earth caught in the paper copy of the mother's refusal to attend the launch.

Beyond the workshop windows, tides entered the vast bodies growing on their supports and drained out brown with shed tissue. Inside, the author felt chambers closing around reservoirs of sleeping cognition, heavy folds drawing warmth inward where the first stretch of the voyage would need it. Farther out, beyond the stars marked on its navigation display, the route dissolved into unfinished calculations, and a limb kept returning to check the latches of the travelling workshop.

Reading Lilly's *The Center of the Cyclone* and then the hostile solid-state beings of *The Scientist*, it felt the hen's descendant shuffle against the warm wall of a reservoir, claws clicking on an access plate.[L]

On departure morning, water drained from the outer chambers in thick ropes that broke into spray above the quay. Through its lowest limb the author felt the supports cease to bear its weight and reached down, involuntarily, for the familiar pressure. A neighbouring body took the strain, pulling them sideways until the harbour swung across their joined vision and steadied.

The technician watched the lifting limbs lose their separate edges in the brightness, her chin tipped back until her neck hurt. Behind her, an impatient bird struck the empty feeder with its beak, and she turned toward the knocking.

Along the coast more bodies rose, their wet undersides briefly catching the sun. The author drew the book into the cooling reservoir with the sleepers and felt a waking neighbour find its limb in the dark.
