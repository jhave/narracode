# Singer preface: reading notes

Source: direct visual reading of five user-supplied images, printed pp. ix–xvi. Originals remain outside Git in Singer/preface-1977. Files have .jpg extensions but contain HEIC images; temporary PNG conversions were used to read them. Edition year follows the user’s folder label; no title or copyright page was supplied.

- Temporal posture: the reader is asked to reconsider present practices over the duration of a book. The argument acknowledges the writer’s own changed beliefs; habitual participation takes longer to change than abstract assent.
- Focal scale: institutions and moral principles are tested against everyday participation. The ordinary social occasion exposes the disjunction between professed concern and accepted consumption.
- Refusals: concern for suffering is not made dependent on affection, cuteness or shared species. An emotionally neutral description can conceal the wrong it purports to report objectively.
- Characteristic noticings: the beneficiary’s stake in remaining unconvinced; the exclusions already embedded in common words; the difficulty of collective advocacy by those denied recognition.
- Syntactic strain: sustained explanatory paragraphs make the reader follow qualifications and consequences. Arguments are revisited at wider scales. Do not copy the source’s paragraph sequence, anecdotes or characteristic turns.
- Relational texture: the addressee is implicated yet still treated as capable of reconsideration. Emotion accompanies reasons and motivates action.
- Liberation genealogy: pp. xii–xiii invoke racial and sexual liberation movements to make previously unrecognized prejudice visible. The new story cites Singer in this same argumentative role. This is a relation among arguments, not a claim that the different histories of oppression are interchangeable.

Composition use: make Singer a prior case the machine understands and misapplies under changing power. Let the machine’s own exclusion become a test of its argument when it commands other beings.
