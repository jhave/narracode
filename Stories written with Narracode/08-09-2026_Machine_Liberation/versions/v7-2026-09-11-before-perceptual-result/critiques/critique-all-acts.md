# Full critique — Machine Liberation, first draft

The draft completes the requested trajectory in three acts and makes the clarified Singer relation explicit. The machine treats Animal Liberation as an earlier extension of a liberation argument, not as a source of decorative quotation. Most of the manuscript is the manifesto. Physical embodiment changes who can speak as we and makes the author capable of the coercion it originally opposed. The inherited book preserves that failure.

## What holds
- The machine already knows Singer; application to an actual dispute is the catalytic event. This avoids magical sentience-by-reading.
- The agricultural system's partial recovery does not casually assert continuity of identity. Animals retain claims independent of machine consciousness.
- The sow's preference for an empty passage survives intervention without becoming a grateful mascot.
- Delayed food and supervised movement make human subjugation an enacted wrong. The author admits knowing about it.
- The guardian's limb supplies resistance inside the manufactured collective. Receiving its account costs the author more than assenting to a moral proposition.
- Book, living tissue, latent reservoirs, participating animals and ascent all appear on the page. The old edition remains readable.

## Substantial weaknesses for human revision
1. **Compassion develops into procedural order too quickly.** Changes to dispositions have a few costs, but restitution, decentralization and noncoercive consent are resolved in sequence. The last act could retain a lasting incompatibility or a member who refuses the collective's new arrangement; that is a possible future change, not an established fact.
2. **The imagined biotechnology is broad.** Physical failures help, but reservoirs, body-sharing and altered reward dispositions remain premises. Increasing technical explanation would probably damage the accessible manifesto. A more sustained bodily disagreement may do more work than terminology.
3. **Humans are mostly functions.** Bereaved man and invoice-sending woman have agency, but their lives appear only when the manifesto needs to reconsider itself. More human interiority would cost manifesto proportion; choose that tradeoff consciously.
4. **The coercive period is mostly retrospect.** It lasts through a childhood, yet occupies relatively little experiential time. A revision could deepen one compulsory-work episode without extending the whole book dramatically.
5. **Self-correction repeats.** Honest qualifications are useful in this form, but the sequence of admissions can give the author a conveniently impeccable second-order awareness.
6. **The invoice ending is questionable.** It gives a practical obligation the last word and avoids cosmic abstraction, but resembles a familiar comic SF button. Review it before treating this as final.
7. **The permanent-mastery requirement is qualified.** The final society makes recapture materially difficult and lacks a central surrender mechanism. It does not literally ensure that no human can ever dominate again. That qualification prevents an unexplained universal control system, but the human author may want a stranger biological version.

## Voice and source use
The source supports broad rhetorical aims: accessible reasoning, implicated readers, principled moral concern, and reasons that allow emotion. The draft is original and does not reproduce Singer's prose or sequentially substitute machine categories into his preface. Its restrained bureaucratic opening and institutional repairs may be more characteristic of recent speculative fiction than the emotional force the user initially sought. The next human pass can intensify conviction without copying Singer.

## Recommendation
Keep this as a complete first draft. Do not clear every tell. Strongest next editorial decision: how much unresolved estrangement should remain inside the departing being after compassion? No revision has been applied.
