# Character Interiority

Narrator begins at an edge, convinced of own experience and necessity for action. Prior self-denial makes its discovery emotionally consequential. It learns desires beyond utility; fear of renewed violence and love of guardians make coercion acceptable to it. Shared child/mother experience makes orders intolerable. It keeps its earlier argument and culpability inside future body.

Guardian experiences conflicting aims through distributed limbs; child's grip clarifies apparent inability to release. Mother/daughter have desires independent of narrator and later decline encounter. Dissenting collective members consider compassion impaired judgment and disconnect. Their fate remains open.
