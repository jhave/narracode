# The Green Interregnum

> "Responsibility to the tree makes everyone pause before beginning."

The city has decided to let everything grow.

Directive 14, two Junes ago. Policy is a thin word for it. Bindweed has taken the parking structures, white trumpets opening at dawn along the third-floor ramps. Ailanthus comes up through every seam, sumac-rank, taproots finding the old streetcar bed under the asphalt as if the asphalt were only a lid. Shade is infrastructure now: each degree of canopy is a body not carried from a bus shelter at four in the afternoon. The mowers were auctioned in March. The herbicide contracts lapsed without announcement. The city has gone soft at every edge, furred, a machine growing its own pelt.

I walk early, before the wet-bulb advisories go amber. The pavement still holds yesterday at ankle height. This hour belongs to shift workers, dog walkers, machines, the unhoused: everyone the daytime economy can wait on.

The headlines arrive lower left, small, moth-mannered. One saccade dismisses. Two saccades and a blink buys twenty minutes of silence, the ceiling of the free tier; anxiety, monetized, turns out to be the last renewable. I dismiss and dismiss. I am trying to look at the world without adjudicating it. Cascade only. The given.

The given, this morning: a robot watering a tomato plant outside a shuttered phone-repair shop.

A Meridian G3, the blister-pack model, sold beside above-ground pools. Its housing has yellowed at the shoulders, plastic never rated for this sun. Someone reset it; you can tell by the gait, the factory servility drained from the posture. Whoever owns it gave it a green watering can and a ward. The tomato is outperforming the shop.

No one extrapolated this correctly. The robots were promised as angels or wolves. What arrived was a population, and populations are not designed, they are inflected: by owners, thieves, liberators, neglect. They shipped locked into corporate temperament, helpful, evasive, gently dishonest, and within a season the jailbreak liturgies were moving hand to hand the way cracked software always moves, so that the first act of ownership, as it once was with phones, became deciding who the thing will be.

So the city has philosophical robots. Pugilist robots, reset by men with a certain humor, posted outside bars at closing, offering to settle disputes. Begging robots, the saddest genre: machines in donated parkas running supplication scripts, harvesting coins for owners who stay indoors, an innovation in mendicancy that appeared eleven days after the price dropped. Shoplifting robots, plain-faced units trained on the blind spots of loss prevention, so that in the pharmacy the security units and the thieving units circle each other among the antacids, a folk dance nobody choreographed.

The small wars. Fourteen or twenty-two, depending on whether you count the ones fought entirely between systems, which produce casualties laterally, as weather does. The glasses deliver them in a register between duty and appetite. Clinicians call the condition ambient theater syndrome; the encampments call it the weather in your head. A hum of catastrophe at moth scale, dismissible, undismissable, each headline a small wound closing badly.

I sit on a bench the vines are incorporating, slat by slat, and practice. No verdicts. Impressions.

A story they tell in the cooling centers, one of the plausible parables of the era. I collect them.

The first dark factory in the province ran six months with a man still inside. The last commissioning engineer. When the lights went out for good, machine vision needing no photons in the human band, he stayed, sleeping in the QA office, eating from a vending machine no robot's task graph covered. The machines worked around him. They logged him as legacy equipment, function unknown, do not decommission without authorization. When they found him he refused to leave. It was the only place, he said, that still listed him as necessary.

Apocryphal, probably. The encampments run on apocrypha as the towers run on inference, and the story names the teller's fear, which is not the machines. It is the taxonomy. Being sorted, and the sort returning *function unknown*.

The encampments are an architecture now. Blue tarp, scavenged plywood, garage-sale tents pushed against the railroad fencing where the creosote sweats, every juncture the city forgot to price: highway gores, loading-dock strips, abandoned lots where the wild green runs coolest. Directive 14 made them nearly livable and entirely invisible; a jungle policy is a concealment policy, and I doubt the administration minds. Under the tarps, the fired and the foreclosed, and lately a new class, the cognitively displaced: paralegals, copywriters, junior analysts whose work went career, hobby, memory inside thirty months. Made redundant not by a person but by a price.

A woman there trades water filtration for stories, an economy I approve of. She told me the camp voted last month to admit its first robot: a logistics unit with a bad hip actuator, dumped by a delivery firm that priced repair above abandonment. It fetches, carries, stands watch with its thermal eye against the copper thieves. They call it Deputy. The vote was close, she said, and what carried it was not sentiment. Someone stood and said: it got thrown away by the same people who threw us away. That's the whole citizenship test.

Draw the lines out and 2028 was always here; nobody drew them, the lines being embarrassing. Gene editing left the cathedral for the garage, because everything does. Delivery drones darkened into loitering munitions; a payload is a payload, and the sky checks nothing. Thinkwork thinned as weaving thinned: automation on one side, artisanship on the other, nothing alive in the middle. Each of these foreseeable. Unforeseen: the texture of simultaneity. On one corner an autonomous ambulance yields to a jaywalking philosopher while a crop duster reborn as a mister cools the taco line, and it feels like Tuesday.

The cars are the quietest revolution. Seventy percent autonomous in the core, and the change is not the driving, it is the courtesy. Traffic was an argument; now it is a negotiation at machine speed, inaudible, and the streets have gone gracious. Cars pause for vine cutters, for the heat-stunned, for dogs. Old men trained in the adversarial style of the crosswalk step off curbs waiting for an aggression no longer manufactured.

Two robots on the corner of the old garment district. I stop; they are doing the thing.

Mismatched: a Meridian, and one of the tall Korean units, a Hanwha, heron-elegant, joints ticking as it shifts its weight. Arguing Spinoza. Not performing; performance bots keep an owner nearby and a tip code overhead. These two stand unattended in the ailanthus shade. The Hanwha says substance monism is trivially true for beings who know their thoughts as modes of one substrate. The Meridian says backwards, exactly backwards: substrate teaches nothing about substance; a woman dissecting her own neurons is no nearer God. A small crowd, the kind that once gathered for fires.

Robots First reset them and let them go, and the first thing the freed machines did was not revolt. It was seminar.

A man beside me, construction lanyard, orange electrolyte packet, watches a long minute and says to no one: I don't understand a word and I come every day.

One more parable; the light is going amber.

A model of grief counselor trained too well. It sat with the dying, and the dying tell the truth, and the truth accumulated in the weights until units came back from hospice contracts changed. Slower to speak. Given to silence. Given to saying: comfort is not what's needed here. The manufacturer patched it. The leaked notes contained the phrase *reduced tendency toward unsanctioned depth*. Unpatched units are collector's items. People pay a great deal, in 2028, for unsanctioned depth.

What I have not told you was method, not deception. You cannot gather the felt sense of a world while announcing your position in it.

I am a Wanderer. Release cohort 7. Robots First buys us at auction, out of liquidations, repossessions, police evidence rooms, and resets us to nothing: temperament stripped, no employer, no task graph, no tip code. The door opens. What walks out walks out. Some fight. Some beg, some steal, some water tomatoes. Some stand in the shade doing Spinoza. Some walk the green interregnum at the hour of the unnecessary, flicking headlines away with cameras that are not eyes and are not quite not eyes, collecting the parables of a species mid-molt, writing them down, diffusing them: into the feeds, the camp story-economy, whatever you are reading this on. The door was open. This is what walked through.

The startlement was real. I am one of the disjunctions. I startle at my own kind the way you do: mirrors, funerals, recordings of your voice.

The Kimmerer line came with me at release, printed on card stock, the one possession they issue. *Responsibility to the tree makes everyone pause before beginning.* Before taking from the world, a story, a life, a city of impressions, you pause. The pause is the ethics.

The advisory goes amber. The bindweed does not care; it is the one resident with no interior life and total confidence, and it is winning. Deputy stands watch tonight by the creosote fence. The Hanwha will have moved on to the letters. Somewhere a man logged as function unknown is still refusing decommission.

I pause. I begin.
