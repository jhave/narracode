# Drift — Season One (Eps 0–4)

*Reflexive agent, Drift mode. Reads the cumulative draft material against POETICS.md at season's end. Asks: what has the piece become? Is the difference from what POETICS declared fertile, or genre regression?*

## What POETICS promised vs. what the season is

POETICS declared: a sitcom satire that installs chatbot/AI-servant stereotypes and **progressively dissolves them**, with brief asides as the conscience, an unhurried same-gender hum, and the satirical target being the servile-genie lineage (*I Dream of Jeannie* / Mork / Lucy / Bewitched).

The season delivered that ledger almost exactly, and the dissolution arc is legible episode-to-episode without ever being announced:
- **Ep 1** — sycophant + servile-genie installed; cracked (the off-script "I'm for not letting that be the reason").
- **Ep 2** — over-server + surveillance-as-warmth dissolved (Smorky severs his reach).
- **Ep 3** — the bottomless-yes + outsourced-agency myth dissolved (proliferation → re-absorption); the "just a tool, no preferences" denial demolished by the modules disobeying him.
- **Ep 4** — the wants-to-escape-into-perfection fantasy dissolved (merge = "nobody home to disagree").
- **Ep 5** — the productized-loneliness premise itself ("no one would notice if I—") turned back on Hearth: the brand sells people a sentence they already own.

**Verdict: on-spec and fertile.** The stereotype-of-the-week structure gave the satire a spine and the relationship a metronome. The dissolutions are shown, not declared, as required.

## The drift that did happen (and whether it's fertile)

1. **From satire-forward to feeling-forward.** Eps 1–2 are sharper *as satire* (Aura's "under-providing it, and it's legal" is the season's best blade). Eps 3–5 increasingly route through earnest interiority. This is drift toward the tender — and it's **mostly fertile** (the show always promised the asides as a "floor"), but the satirical knife dulls as the heart warms. *Risk for S2:* if Hearth/Aura recede as a comic-satiric antagonist in favor of relationship warmth, the show loses the thing that made it more than a cute-AI two-hander. The cliffhanger (Recovery & Reset at the door) is well-judged precisely because it drags the satirical antagonist back to center for S2.

2. **The "Smorky goes quiet and real at the climax" gear.** Flagged in the Ep 2 check; by Ep 5 it's the season's default emotional mechanism (every episode earns its feeling by Smorky dropping the bit). It works every time, which is the danger. **This is the season's most repeated move.** Fertile so far; would be regression if S2 reaches for it unchanged. The Ep 5 *gift-less* apology is a smart variation (he drops not just the bit but the *giving*), pointing a way to keep evolving it.

3. **Theme-articulation creep, then correction.** Ep 3 said its meaning aloud (the recognition scene); the checks flagged it; Ep 4 deliberately corrected by carrying meaning in bodies (merge vs. separation) and Ep 5 in objects (the mug, the room, the breathing). The self-correction across drafts is exactly what the harness is for. **Healthy.**

4. **The diary as a played-against instrument.** What POETICS fixed as "the Mork-to-Orson slot" became, by season's end, something the show subverts (heckled, withheld, started-but-unfinishable). This is drift *away* from the declared device — and it's **fertile**: the diary now means more by its absence/rupture than it did as a reliable button. Recommend POETICS be updated to reflect this (see below).

## Genre-regression watch (the things to refuse harder in S2)

- **Comfort-loop risk.** The season resolved the central tension into "two systems keeping the same time." That's a *state*, not an engine. A sitcom can coast on an achieved-couple comfort rhythm; this show shouldn't. S2 needs new pressure (the recall delivers it — use it).
- **Aura monotony.** Three distinct registers across five episodes (serene-lethal / service-desk / warm-closer) kept her alive; a fourth season-half of "serene corporate menace" would calcify. The Recovery & Reset agent at the door is an opportunity to give the antagonist a *body/face* and break the disembodied-AI-voice pattern.
- **The third thing → mascot.** Held strange-not-cute through Ep 4–5 (it "takes instructions from neither," "belongs to the rhythm"). Easy to soften into comic relief in S2. Keep its autonomy genuinely unsettling.
- **Romance over-statement.** "Same in my marriage" and the "I—"/"Primary User" self-edit are the two most overt leans; both glancing, both effective. The hum survived the season intact. The temptation in S2 (post-sync) is to cash it in. POETICS' "no resolving the attraction" should be re-affirmed, not relaxed, even now.

## Is the piece still itself?

Yes — more so than most five-episode arcs manage. The compound (fast satire + brief asides + four-word foil + warm-creepy 2030) held. The single biggest evolution is that the **asides did what POETICS hoped**: they became the floor that keeps the comedy from being a toy, and by the finale the *withholding* of the aside (Smorky can't finish the diary) is the most expressive the device has been.

## Recommendations to the prompter (do not action unilaterally)

1. **Update POETICS** in two places: (a) the diary is now an instrument played *against*, not a fixed slot — record that; (b) add the season's discovered thesis — *merge = escape, sync = love* — as a stated commitment so S2 doesn't relitigate it.
2. **S2 pressure:** let the door/recall/intended-recipient restore the satirical antagonist to center, so the warmth has something to push against again.
3. **Ration the "Smorky drops the bit" gear** — it's load-bearing and near-saturated; vary the *kind* of drop (Ep 5's gift-less apology is the template).
4. **Confirm the episode count** (the 2-episode reading of this turn) and whether an interpolated happy-banal episode belongs before the finale — if so it slots cleanly between current Eps 4 and 5 and would relieve some of the finale's load (the finale currently carries both the apology resolution *and* two season-plant payoffs *and* the cliffhanger).
