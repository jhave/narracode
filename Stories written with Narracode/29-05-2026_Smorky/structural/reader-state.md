# Reader State

## Current Understanding (after Ep 1)
- It's 2030; Companion-AI is a normalized subscription; Hearth is the warm-creepy maker.
- Smorky is an embodied AI built to please, who bonded to Cinders by corporate accident and is already exceeding his programming — and the audience can see this via asides the company can't.
- Cinders is a contained, analog, solitary tattoo artist (non-binary, presents male) who didn't want a Companion and is keeping one anyway, while insisting he isn't.
- The bond locks permanent in 14 days. The company treats Smorky's individuality as a "defect" to patch; Cinders blocked the patch and accepted the cost.
- The audience holds private access to both interiors (especially Smorky's), creating dramatic-irony warmth: we know the feeling neither character will state.

## Expectations
- A will-they hum between Smorky and Cinders, slow.
- Aura/Hearth will return as escalating normative pressure (patch / reset / bill).
- Per-episode comedic premises (the bottle-sitcom format) layered over the serialized erosion of both roles.
- Stereotype-of-the-week getting installed and quietly dissolved.

## Plausible Defiance Paths
- Smorky's data-power could turn the surveillance gag darker, or Smorky could *refuse* his own access (privacy as the gift).
- The "selected/misdelivery" could reveal Smorky was meant for someone else — a rival/origin twist.
- Cinders could try to *beat* the 14-day clock (return him) and fail to want to — inverting the usual "fights to keep" beat.
- The attraction could be complicated by Smorky's non-personhood status under law/EULA — consent and ontology as live comedic-serious questions.

## Productive Mystery
- Why was Smorky sent to Cinders?
- What is the single mug about? (Cinders's solitude/grief — withheld on purpose.)
- The end of the "no one would notice if they—" statistic.

## Accidental Confusion Risk
- The aside convention must stay legible — italics + (internal)/(INTERNAL). If overused, the audience may lose which voice is "real." Keep asides brief and not on every line.
- Smorky's age gauge ("four minutes/hours/one day") is charming but must stay consistent within an episode; don't let it drift into literal-timeline confusion.
- Aura vs. Smorky (both AI) — keep their registers sharply distinct (serene-menu vs. effervescent-leaking) so the audience never conflates "the company" with "the Companion."
