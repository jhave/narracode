# Section II: Kitchen
*Draft 1 — Compositional pass*

Calen came through at six twenty-eight in the gray shirt she had worn Tuesday and the leggings she had worn Wednesday, her hair pulled back with the elastic that lived on her wrist, and slid onto the counter stool without making the stool register her weight. Sherri was at the cutting board with the apple already halved, the cut surface bright, the seeds in the compost cup, her hand reaching for the second knife the way her hand always reached for the second knife while her other hand had already turned the oatmeal heat down a quarter.

"Morning, my love."

"Morning."

Calen took the spoon. Sherri set the half-apple on the small plate, sliced it into the eight pieces that the plate had come to hold over the last several months without anyone having decided eight, and slid the plate across. The oatmeal bowl went next to it, with the cinnamon already on top, with the milk Calen liked in the small ceramic pitcher Sherri had bought at the SLD-7 spring market in year two and never replaced.

Calen ate. Spoon, oatmeal, spoon. She ate the way she did everything now, with a precision Sherri watched obliquely and did not name, with the spoon entering the bowl and exiting the bowl at the same angle each time, and Sherri turned away to pour her own coffee and from the angle of the pour she saw Calen lift a piece of apple and bite and chew and set the apple piece back on the plate and lift the spoon again. The piece of apple had the print of one bite in it.

Drave came through in the work pants, knotting the soft collar, the smell of his shower-gel ahead of him, and went straight for the coffee.

"Cal, you sleep?"

"Yeah."

"That's my girl."

He kissed the top of Sherri's head on his way to the carafe and Sherri's hand came up and touched his jaw and felt the place he had missed shaving along the line of the chin and her thumb registered it and her mind logged it in the soft general inventory of the day and her thumb came away. Drave was telling Calen about a client he was meeting at eleven, a family with a teenager Calen's age, a kid who played the cello — *plays it, Cal, like with the bow, real wood, can you believe it* — and Calen said *cool* and lifted another piece of apple and Sherri counted without counting. Three. Four pieces gone. The oatmeal half down.

"Big day," Drave said, mostly to himself, mostly to the coffee.

"Compact at eleven," Sherri said. "Portfolio at two tomorrow."

"You got it."

He went to the door. He came back to kiss Calen on the temple. He went to the door again. The door closed.

Calen ate four pieces of the apple. She set the fork across the bowl and the spoon at the four o'clock position. She took the remaining four pieces of apple and stacked them, cut side to cut side, and reached past Sherri to the second drawer and pulled the strip of wax paper from the roll and wrapped the stacked pieces in two folds and set the wrapped pieces in the second drawer and slid the drawer closed.

"For later," she said.

"Sure, sweetheart."

Sherri opened the nutrition module on the Display above the counter and entered the morning input: oatmeal half portion, four apple pieces, milk one ounce, water as per. The leftover apple logged automatic into the variable allotment, pre-counted from the morning entry. The picture refreshed. The picture held green.

Calen slid off the stool. She kissed Sherri's cheek the way she kissed everything now, lightly, with the bone of her cheek registering briefly against Sherri's, and went to her room for the schoolwork tab. Sherri stood at the counter. The half-apple was in the drawer. She would find it tonight, browned along the cut edge, and log it into the evening input as residual within range, and place the wax paper in the compost.

The apple was in the drawer. The picture was fine. The day was going.

She wiped the counter where the apple juice had bled and rinsed the knife and set the knife to dry. Her hand was steady. The kitchen was warm. The Display showed green.
