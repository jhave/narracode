# Section VI: Revolt
*Draft 1 — Compositional pass*

At five eleven Sherri closed the Display. She did not minimize it. She did not put it to sleep. She closed the tab. The Display went to the soft slate-gray of its dormant state, the small Meridian Regional logo at the bottom right glowing faintly the way it had glowed at the bottom right of every screen in her household since the regional administrative consolidation in year two of the marriage.

She sat at the counter. The kitchen was the kitchen at five eleven in May. The east window had given up the morning light hours ago and the west window now held the late sun on the upper third of the wall and the lower two-thirds were in the deepening warm of the room beginning to go evening. The Bright was visible above the southern roofs. The towers were lit along their upper tiers, the lower tiers already in their own shadow, the line of light moving slowly down them the way it moved every evening between five and six-thirty.

She made tea. She made it from the loose mint Drave had brought home from the Sunday market in March, which lived in the jar at the back of the spice shelf, which was a thing she did because she liked the smell of it crushed between her fingers and because the loose leaves took five minutes to steep, and the five minutes were the five minutes. She put the kettle on. She crushed the leaves into the strainer. She poured the water and watched the leaves turn.

She sat with the tea. The Display was closed. The Bright was lit. Calen's keys were going down the hall, slower now, with longer pauses between the runs. *Tk tk.* Long pause. *Tk tk tk tk.* The score was being written. The mint smelled the way it smelled.

She thought about the ok level.

The thought came in the form it came in when the cascade was not running. Flat. Single-track. The ok level was 74.3. The ok level was a composite. The ok level was financial-health-civic-environmental-developmental. The ok level was the thing the system rewarded the household for maintaining. The ok level was what the household had built itself around for the eight years of the southern quarter and the four years of the Compact and the eleven years of Drave at Kessler & Norde and the fourteen years of Calen.

She thought about what the ok level was optimizing for, exactly.

The answer arrived. It arrived in the form a flat declarative arrives in: complete, present, unspectacular, ordinary. *The ok level is optimizing for the continuation of the ok level.* That was the answer. There was a second part. *And the people whose ok level continues are the people who continue, and the people whose ok level drops below seventy are the Quintards in SLD-9 by the end of summer.* And a third part. *And Calen's hands are doing the only thing they do that the ok level cannot count, and so the ok level is optimizing Calen out of the room she is in.* The answer was three sentences. The answer had been three sentences for a long time. The answer was the kind of thing that, once known, was simply the thing.

The mint had cooled. She drank it.

The keys went *tk tk tk*. The Bright was lower on the towers now. Forty-seven minutes had passed since Sherri had closed the Display. She had drunk a cup of tea. She had sat at the counter. She had thought a thing she had thought before, in pieces, every evening for some indefinite stretch of evenings, and tonight the thing had arrived assembled.

A sentence passed through her like a little wind. *I know.* It passed.

She opened the Display.

The ok level was 74.3. The orientation was in twenty days. The application revision was due Thursday at six. The Compact participation index had logged the morning attendance and the late-evening community-garden sign-up window was open until ten. Drave's commission band was tracking mid. Calen's daily aggregate was within range. The picture was within range.

She added herself to the garden sign-up for Saturday at nine. The Display logged the entry. The participation index ticked.

The day was going.
