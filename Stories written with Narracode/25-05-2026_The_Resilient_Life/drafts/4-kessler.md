# Section IV: Kessler
*Draft 1 — Compositional pass*

The comm pinged at twelve forty-one. Sherri was at the kitchen counter with the leftover lemon bar broken on a small plate and a cup of mint and the Display open to the application revision and the cursor in the extracurricular section. She tapped the call.

Drave's face came up against the gray of the Kessler & Norde meeting-room wall, his good shirt on, the small bracket-pin of the agency at the collar, his face flushed in the way it flushed when he was between meetings and pleased with himself.

"Babe. Hi. Two minutes. Just checking in."

"Hi, love. How's the day."

"Day's good. Quintard family. The eleven o'clock. Going well. They're doing the thing where the husband wants to fight it and the wife already knows. You know that thing."

"I know that thing."

"I'm walking them through. We're getting them to the place where they can see it as a lifestyle rebalancing, you know, the zone-adjacent options in SLD-9, the air filtration is honestly comparable, the schools are a tier down but the daughter is sixteen, she's almost out, and Quintard, the husband, he keeps saying *we built this life* and I'm sitting there with the comp printout and the equity-bridge offer and I'm just listening, you know, I'm just letting him say it. That's the thing. They have to say it."

"You're good at that."

"It's the listening. It's mostly the listening. Anyway. The commission is going to clear on this one if I can get the paperwork back by Friday. We're looking at solid mid-band. I wanted you to know."

"Mid-band is great, love."

"How was the Compact?"

"It was good. I spoke. Branda was — Branda. Joane Reyes-Albright asked about Calen."

"And you said?"

"I said wonderful."

"Because she is."

"Because she is."

"Okay. Two minutes is up. Kiss Cal for me. Love you."

"Love you."

The comm closed. Sherri sat at the counter with her hand still on the Display. The lemon bar was the same shape on the plate. The mint had cooled by a degree.

The Quintard family. The husband saying *we built this life*. Sherri's mind went where it went under that phrase — to a kitchen she had not been in, a kitchen with the morning light landing on it and the cup warming on the counter and the Display showing whatever number the Display had been showing for that family before it stopped showing the right number, and the husband at the wall in his robe at five forty-seven the way Sherri stood at the wall in her robe at five forty-seven, and the wife already knowing what the husband had not yet let himself know, and Drave in his good shirt at the meeting-room wall with the equity-bridge offer and the comp printout and the patient listening, doing the work he was good at, doing it well, doing it kindly. The Quintards would be in SLD-9 by the end of summer. The husband would say it had been the right move within a year. The wife had already said it. The daughter was sixteen, almost out. The line on Drave's commission held.

Four seconds. The mind landed where it landed.

She loved Drave. She loved that his face had been flushed against the gray wall. She loved that he had called her between meetings to say mid-band. His line was the structure under everything. His line was the reason the southern quarter was the southern quarter and the school zone was the school zone and the application was an application Calen could actually submit. His line was love made into a number that came in steady.

She turned back to the Display. The ok level was 74.3. The extracurricular cursor blinked. The application waited.

A sentence passed through her like a little wind. *Drave will make commission on a family being moved out of their kitchen at five forty-seven.* It passed. She put her hand on the trackpad. She typed *independent creative practice* into the extracurricular field and stopped and read the words and the words sat on the screen and the form accepted them, provisional, awaiting institutional affiliation.

The mint was cold. She drank it anyway. The day was going.
