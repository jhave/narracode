The risk is the moment tipping into editorial — Sherri seeing too clearly, the prose pushing the answer too hard.

Reading the section: the three-part answer ("The ok level is optimizing for the continuation of the ok level...") sits at the edge of the editorial. Each of the three sentences is precise and present-tense and stripped — but the cumulative weight of three sentences arriving as "the answer" risks the Carlin moment becoming a Carlin paragraph, which is not what Carlin does. The wind should pass; here it lingers slightly.

## Carlin check
This is the section's central question. The three-part answer is the deepest articulation in the story so far — the only moment where Sherri's clarity assembles into a sentence pattern that names the structure. The risk: it's *too* articulated for the Carlin register. Carlin's lines are devastating because they're short and they pass. The three-part answer is short, but it's three sentences in a row, and they don't pass quickly enough.

However: there's a structural defense for this. Section VI is the revolt section. It is the one section where the wind should be larger — not a storm, but a longer pass. The Carlin register can swell here without breaking, because the section's structural function permits it.

The closing landing "*I know.*" is the right Carlin move — flat, two words, acceptance, then the Display opens. This is the wind. Good.

But: I want to reconsider the three-part answer in revision. Possible move: keep the three sentences but make the third one shorter — strip it down. The third sentence currently has "and so the ok level is optimizing Calen out of the room she is in," which is the strongest line and the most Carlin-precise. Could land harder if the syntax stripped further.

4. **Closing.** "The day was going." is the right Carlin landing for this section — it echoes Section II and IV but here it carries the full weight of the revolt-absorbed. Keep.
