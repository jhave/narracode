At five forty-seven Sherri Holum was already at the kitchen wall, robe loose at the waist, hair still flat at the back from the pillow, the cup warming her palm before she had decided to drink from it, and the Display was on. The ok level was 74.3. It had been 74.3 at five forty-seven yesterday and 74.3 at five forty-seven the day before, which Sherri took as the cumulative effect of the lentil dinner Monday, the additional twenty-two minutes Drave had logged in the Wellness module on Tuesday, the Compact volunteer hour she had banked Sunday afternoon weeding the tomato bed in the community garden, and the steady current of Calen's morning biometrics, which the household sensor in the bathroom mirror had registered between six twelve and six fourteen daily without missing a reading for forty-one consecutive days. The margin was 2.3 above the threshold and 3.7 below the next band. The application sat secure inside that margin like a small clean dish inside a larger clean dish.

The orientation was in twenty days.

She opened the timeline. The portfolio review window was Thursday at two. The essay revision was due to the Academy intake portal by Friday at six. The Compact quarterly was Thursday at eleven, which she had already pre-cleared with the orientation prep slot at one, so the day would hold, the day would hold if she moved the application revision into the morning and pushed the kitchen inventory to Wednesday and let the Wellness check ride on the weekly aggregate instead of the daily, which she could allow herself because his line was holding, Drave's commission band was tracking at the median for Kessler & Norde Tier Two, the family was within the Range, and her picture — Calen's picture — was within range, within the green, within the soft band at the center of the chart Sherri had pinned to the top of the Display because looking at it was the first thing she did after the ok level.

Calen's picture: 17.2 BMI, which was inside the green; iron at 11.4, low-end green; sleep at six hours forty minutes average over the trailing seven days, which the Display had flagged twice in the last month with a gentle yellow advisory and which Sherri had addressed by adjusting the dim-cycle on the bedroom ambient light and which had bumped the average to seven hours two days running before settling back to the six forty figure that the Display now seemed to accept as Calen's natural variant, the way a doctor learns to accept that a child runs slightly cool. The picture was fine. The picture was within range.

Drave was asleep. He slept with his mouth slightly open and one hand under the pillow and the other across his chest the way he had slept since the second year of the marriage, which Sherri had noticed and noted and never raised, because it was his, it was the shape of him sleeping, and that was a thing she got to have without entering it anywhere.

The east window held the sky in the gray-blue of before-light, and the Bright was visible above the low roofs of the southern quarter — the towers catching the first weather of sun behind them, the windows beginning to take it. The Bright was clear today. Sherri saw it and saw the route through it: orientation, conditional, first year, secondary track, placement, life. Route open. Ok level holding. The cup was warm. The family was fine.

A sentence passed through her like a little wind. The number was the number. It passed. She moved to the lunch tab. The wax paper was in the second drawer. The half-apple from last night was still in there, wrapped, the cut surface beginning to brown along the edge. Sherri opened the Display's nutrition module and logged the leftover into today's variable allotment and closed the drawer in her mind and then physically closed the drawer in the kitchen and the cup was at her mouth and the coffee was hot and the day had started.

The Compact agenda loaded. His line refreshed. Calen's overnight biometrics ticked across the chart in the gentle pale green Sherri had selected three years ago from the Display's color palette. The Bright was lit now along the upper tier. The kitchen was warm. The ok level was 74.3.

Sherri seized the day.

# Section II: Kitchen Draft 1 — Compositional pass

Calen came through at six twenty-eight in the gray shirt she had worn Tuesday and the leggings she had worn Wednesday, her hair pulled back with the elastic that lived on her wrist, and slid onto the counter stool without making the stool register her weight. Sherri was at the cutting board with the apple already halved, the cut surface bright, the seeds in the compost cup, her hand reaching for the second knife the way her hand always reached for the second knife while her other hand had already turned the oatmeal heat down a quarter.

"Morning, my love."

"Morning."

Calen took the spoon. Sherri set the half-apple on the small plate, sliced it into the eight pieces that the plate had come to hold over the last several months without anyone having decided eight, and slid the plate across. The oatmeal bowl went next to it, with the cinnamon already on top, with the milk Calen liked in the small ceramic pitcher Sherri had bought at the SLD-7 spring market in year two and never replaced.

Calen ate. Spoon, oatmeal, spoon. She ate the way she did everything now, with a precision Sherri watched obliquely and did not name, with the spoon entering the bowl and exiting the bowl at the same angle each time, and Sherri turned away to pour her own coffee and from the angle of the pour she saw Calen lift a piece of apple and bite and chew and set the apple piece back on the plate and lift the spoon again. The piece of apple had the print of one bite in it.

Drave came through in the work pants, knotting the soft collar, the smell of his shower-gel ahead of him, and went straight for the coffee.

"Cal, you sleep?"

"Yeah."

"That's my girl."

He kissed the top of Sherri's head on his way to the carafe and Sherri's hand came up and touched his jaw and felt the place he had missed shaving along the line of the chin and her thumb registered it and her mind logged it in the soft general inventory of the day and her thumb came away. Drave was telling Calen about a client he was meeting at eleven, a family with a teenager Calen's age, a kid who played the cello — plays it, Cal, like with the bow, real wood, can you believe it — and Calen said cool and lifted another piece of apple and Sherri counted without counting. Three. Four pieces gone. The oatmeal half down.

"Big day," Drave said, mostly to himself, mostly to the coffee.

"Compact at eleven," Sherri said. "Portfolio at two tomorrow."

"You got it."

He went to the door. He came back to kiss Calen on the temple. He went to the door again. The door closed.

Calen ate four pieces of the apple. She set the fork across the bowl and the spoon at the four o'clock position. She took the remaining four pieces of apple and stacked them, cut side to cut side, and reached past Sherri to the second drawer and pulled the strip of wax paper from the roll and wrapped the stacked pieces in two folds and set the wrapped pieces in the second drawer and slid the drawer closed.

"For later," she said.

"Sure, sweetheart."

Sherri opened the nutrition module on the Display above the counter and entered the morning input: oatmeal half portion, four apple pieces, milk one ounce, water as per. The leftover apple logged automatic into the variable allotment, pre-counted from the morning entry. The picture refreshed. The picture held green.

Calen slid off the stool. She kissed Sherri's cheek the way she kissed everything now, lightly, with the bone of her cheek registering briefly against Sherri's, and went to her room for the schoolwork tab. Sherri stood at the counter. The half-apple was in the drawer. She would find it tonight, browned along the cut edge, and log it into the evening input as residual within range, and place the wax paper in the compost.

The apple was in the drawer. The picture was fine. The day was going.

She wiped the counter where the apple juice had bled and rinsed the knife and set the knife to dry. Her hand was steady. The kitchen was warm. The Display showed green.

# Section III: Compact Draft 1 — Compositional pass

The Compact met in the long room above the cooperative pharmacy, the room that had once been a real estate office and still had the soft beige carpet and the artificial planters in the corners, and Sherri arrived at ten fifty-three with the printed agenda and the soft-print Compact lanyard around her neck and the small folder of the quarterly civic-participation summary that she would file at the front desk and the pleasant warmth at the back of her throat that meant she was glad to be here. The Stewardship Circle of SLD-7 was thirty-eight households at full enrollment. Thirty-one had attended the spring quarterly. Sherri had attended thirty-one of the last thirty-two quarterlies, which the Compact recorded and which fed into the participation index, which fed into the ok level, which was 74.3, and Sherri had a settled feeling about it as she nodded to Marien Polk at the door and signed in on the tablet and took the seat in the second row by the aisle that she had taken every quarter for four years.

Branda Halling opened the meeting at eleven exactly. Sherri loved Branda. Branda had organized the tomato bed in the community garden and the school-zone advocacy filing in the second year and the senior-mobility shuttle integration last fall and Branda spoke with the warmth and the cadence of a person who believed that what she was doing was good and that good was a thing one assembled by showing up and writing the minutes and bringing the lemon bars. The agenda moved through the quarterly metrics — Wellness Rating up to 7.41, garden yield tracking ahead, mobility fleet uptime at 99.2 — and Sherri felt each number land in her own chest as a small confirmation that the place she had chosen for her family was a place that worked.

When Branda opened the floor for the open civic moment, Sherri raised her hand and Branda nodded and Sherri stood.

"I just want to say," Sherri said, and she heard her voice take the shape it took when she was speaking from the part of herself that meant it, "I have been thinking about what this Compact has built. Eight years ago when Drave and I moved into the southern quarter I drove around the district on a Sunday afternoon and I saw the garden and I saw the shuttle stop and I saw the school zone signage and I thought, this is a place where my family can become itself. And we have. Calen is fifteen weeks from her Academy submission. Drave's commission line is stable. We are above the threshold. We did this together, every one of us, every quarterly we showed up to, every hour we put in the garden, every meeting we logged. SLD-7 is what it is because we made it what it is. The southern quarter is the proof. The application is the proof. Thank you, Branda. Thank you all."

The room made the small sound a room makes when a thing has been said well. Branda smiled and made a note. Marien Polk caught Sherri's eye on her way back to the seat and pressed her hand to her own chest in the way that meant she had felt it too. Sherri sat down. Her face was warm. The settled feeling at the back of her throat had deepened into something almost physical, something she would have described, had she been asked, as a kind of belonging.

The agenda moved on. The garden subcommittee gave its report. The school-zone signage refresh was approved. The lemon bars were brought out at eleven forty.

In the break, Joane Reyes-Albright came over with her plate. Joane's son Marcus was a year ahead of Calen at the school. Marcus had applied to the Academy last cycle. Marcus was at the Academy now.

"Sherri, the application — how's Calen doing?"

"She's doing wonderfully. The portfolio is almost there. The essay is in the third revision. The extracurricular section is — we're framing the coding piece, the music coding she does, it's a really specific kind of independent creative work, and the Academy intake portal is — well, you know the portal. We're getting there."

"Marcus had the violin. The violin was easy."

"It's a different framing for sure. But Calen is so clear about what she wants to do, and the Academy values that articulation in the intake, so we are — we are confident."

"Of course you are. You're Sherri." Joane laughed. Joane meant it. Sherri laughed back and meant it.

Joane drifted to the lemon bars. Sherri stood by the planter with her own bar half-eaten in her hand. The Display was in her bag. She did not open it. She did not need to. The number was the number.

A sentence passed through her like a little wind. Calen codes alone in the dark and the form has no field for it. It passed. Branda was tapping the agenda again. The break was ending. The mobility fleet item was next. Sherri took her seat.

The meeting closed at twelve seventeen. Sherri signed out on the tablet. The participation index logged.

In the elevator down, Marien Polk said, "That was beautiful, what you said." Sherri said, "Oh, Marien, thank you, it just came out." The elevator opened on the lobby. The sun was on the planters. The day was going.

# Section VI: Revolt Draft 1 — Compositional pass

At five eleven Sherri closed the Display. She did not minimize it. She did not put it to sleep. She closed the tab. The Display went to the soft slate-gray of its dormant state, the small Meridian Regional logo at the bottom right glowing faintly the way it had glowed at the bottom right of every screen in her household since the regional administrative consolidation in year two of the marriage.

She sat at the counter. The kitchen was the kitchen at five eleven in May. The east window had given up the morning light hours ago and the west window now held the late sun on the upper third of the wall and the lower two-thirds were in the deepening warm of the room beginning to go evening. The Bright was visible above the southern roofs. The towers were lit along their upper tiers, the lower tiers already in their own shadow, the line of light moving slowly down them the way it moved every evening between five and six-thirty.

She made tea. She made it from the loose mint Drave had brought home from the Sunday market in March, which lived in the jar at the back of the spice shelf, which was a thing she did because she liked the smell of it crushed between her fingers and because the loose leaves took five minutes to steep, and the five minutes were the five minutes. She put the kettle on. She crushed the leaves into the strainer. She poured the water and watched the leaves turn.

She sat with the tea. The Display was closed. The Bright was lit. Calen's keys were going down the hall, slower now, with longer pauses between the runs. Tk tk. Long pause. Tk tk tk tk. The score was being written. The mint smelled the way it smelled.

She thought about the ok level.

The thought came in the form it came in when the cascade was not running. Flat. Single-track. The ok level was 74.3. The ok level was a composite. The ok level was financial-health-civic-environmental-developmental. The ok level was the thing the system rewarded the household for maintaining. The ok level was what the household had built itself around for the eight years of the southern quarter and the four years of the Compact and the eleven years of Drave at Kessler & Norde and the fourteen years of Calen.

She thought about what the ok level was optimizing for, exactly.

The answer arrived. It arrived in the form a flat declarative arrives in: complete, present, unspectacular, ordinary. The ok level is optimizing for the continuation of the ok level. That was the answer. There was a second part. And the people whose ok level continues are the people who continue, and the people whose ok level drops below seventy are the Quintards in SLD-9 by the end of summer. And a third part. And Calen's hands are doing the only thing they do that the ok level cannot count, and so the ok level is optimizing Calen out of the room she is in. The answer was three sentences. The answer had been three sentences for a long time. The answer was the kind of thing that, once known, was simply the thing.

The mint had cooled. She drank it.

The keys went tk tk tk. The Bright was lower on the towers now. Forty-seven minutes had passed since Sherri had closed the Display. She had drunk a cup of tea. She had sat at the counter. She had thought a thing she had thought before, in pieces, every evening for some indefinite stretch of evenings, and tonight the thing had arrived assembled.

A sentence passed through her like a little wind. I know. It passed.

She opened the Display.

The ok level was 74.3. The orientation was in twenty days. The application revision was due Thursday at six. The Compact participation index had logged the morning attendance and the late-evening community-garden sign-up window was open until ten. Drave's commission band was tracking mid. Calen's daily aggregate was within range. The picture was within range.

# Section VII: Renewal Draft 1 — Compositional pass

It was Calen, behind the door, making something by hand, in the dark, in 2051, in a world where the AI wrote everything in milliseconds, in a city where every surface generated its own text. Her daughter sat in the dark and typed music letter by letter into a machine that did not know it was supposed to do this for her already.
