# Substep 1.6 — Validation

**Model: Opus 4.6.** The failure mode is reporting a pass that was not verified.

**Output:** `corpus/validation.md`

**Step 1 is not complete until this passes. Step 2 must not begin before it.**

---

## Objective

Establish that the extracted corpus contains what it claims to contain, by checking it against ground truth that exists independently of the extractor.

---

## Why this exists

Every check so far is internal — the extractor agreeing with itself. Internal consistency does not detect a systematically inverted provenance label or a segmenter splitting on the wrong boundary. Those produce a clean-looking corpus that is wrong. This substep is the only external check in Step 1.

---

## Ground truth available

1. **The `Interim Edge` §1 edit.** `plans/2026-07-30_ai-tells-benchmark.md` §6 states that thirteen operations were extracted from this pair by hand. That is a documented count against a known snapshot pair (`v2-...-tell-scan` → `v3-...-jhave-section1`).

2. **The registry examples.** `master_ai_tells.md` quotes specific before/after spans with provenance dated 2026-07-30 — for example `Two minutes forty, and he stood there for all of it.` → the bare number. These exact pairs should appear in the corpus.

3. **The measured rates.** The benchmark doc reports em-dash at 5.6 per 1,000 in v1 and 3.7 in v4, and `the way {you,a,one}` at 1.3 and 0.2. These are independently computable from the drafts and must reproduce.

4. **The 40 `edit-observations.md` files.** Prose descriptions of edits. Not directly comparable, but each describes changes that should correspond to extracted pairs in the same snapshot.

---

## Method

### A. Hand-check a sample

Draw **30 pairs at random** with a fixed seed. For each, open both source drafts and verify by reading:

- the `before` text appears verbatim in the from-snapshot draft
- the `after` text appears verbatim in the to-snapshot draft
- the `op` label matches what actually happened
- the pair is a real edit, not a segmentation artefact

Report as a table with a pass/fail per pair and a written note on every failure. **Do not sample only high-confidence pairs.** Stratify: 10 from confidence ≥0.8, 10 from 0.5–0.8, 10 from 0.30–0.5.

### B. Reproduce the documented counts

- Extract pairs for the `Interim Edge` §1 transition. Compare the count to the documented thirteen. An exact match is not required — hand-counting and opcode-counting differ — but the order of magnitude must agree. **If it emits 2 or 60, something is wrong.**
- Recompute em-dash and `the way X` rates on v1 and v4 drafts. These must reproduce to within rounding.

### C. Check the registry spans

For at least five before/after pairs quoted in `master_ai_tells.md`, confirm the corresponding pair exists in `edit_pairs.jsonl`. Report any that are missing and why (wrong snapshot, below confidence gate, segmentation split).

### D. Provenance spot-check

Take 10 pairs whose provenance came from `source: name-marker` rather than jhave confirmation. Read the before and after text. Ask: **is the after text plausibly a human edit of the before text?** If any pair looks like machine-to-machine drift, the ledger is wrong for that story and 1.2 must be revisited.

This is the one place reading prose for provenance is permitted, because it is a check rather than an input, and a failure sends work back to 1.2 rather than writing a label.

### E. Symmetry check

Count pairs where `after` is longer than `before` versus shorter. A corpus of line edits should skew toward shortening. **A strong skew toward lengthening suggests the pair direction is inverted** — machine and human sides swapped — which would invert every displacement vector in Step 2 and is the single most damaging undetected error available.

---

## Output — `corpus/validation.md`

- Sample table from A, 30 rows, stratified, with pass/fail and notes
- Documented-count comparison from B, with actual numbers
- Registry-span check from C, listing hits and misses
- Provenance spot-check from D, with a verdict per pair
- Length-symmetry figures from E
- **A clear overall verdict: PASS or FAIL**, with the specific failures if FAIL

---

## Do not

- Report a pass rate without the underlying table.
- Sample only high-confidence pairs.
- Adjust the extractor to make validation pass, then report the validation as clean. If the extractor changed, re-run validation on a fresh sample and say that it was re-run.
- Treat "close enough" on the length-symmetry check as a pass. An inverted corpus is the failure that Step 2 cannot detect and that would produce a confident wrong conclusion.

---

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] 30 stratified pairs hand-checked, table present in the output
- [ ] ≥28 of 30 pass
- [ ] `Interim Edge` §1 count is within the same order of magnitude as thirteen
- [ ] em-dash and `the way X` rates reproduce the benchmark doc figures
- [ ] ≥4 of 5 registry spans located, with explanations for any misses
- [ ] Length symmetry skews toward shortening
- [ ] Explicit PASS or FAIL verdict recorded

## Report honestly

If validation fails, say which substep it sends work back to, and stop. Do not proceed to Step 2 with a failing corpus — Step 2's whole purpose is a go/no-go decision on the project, and it cannot make that decision on data that has not been verified.
